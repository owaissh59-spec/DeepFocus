## 4.1 DISPENSING — DEFINITIONS & SCOPE

📌 **DISPENSING** — Dispensing is the **art and science of preparing, packing, labelling and
supplying a medicine to a patient (or to the person acting on the patient's behalf) in accordance
with a prescription of a Registered Medical Practitioner**, together with the counselling needed
for its correct use.

📌 **DISPENSING PHARMACY** — that branch of pharmacy which deals with the **selection,
compounding, dispensing and supply of medicines** on the basis of a prescription.

📌 **COMPOUNDING** — the act of **combining two or more ingredients** (weighing, measuring, mixing)
to prepare a medicine.

📌 **EXTEMPORANEOUS DISPENSING** — preparation of a medicine **at the time of demand**, for a
**particular patient**, according to a prescription, in a **small quantity**, for **immediate use**.

📌 **BULK COMPOUNDING** — preparing a **large quantity** of a preparation in advance for stock, to
be dispensed later.

⚠️ **TRAP** — *Dispensing is not the same as compounding.* **Compounding is only one step of
dispensing.** Dispensing also includes interpreting the prescription, checking, labelling, packing,
pricing, record-keeping and counselling.

🔁 **COMPARE — Extemporaneous vs Bulk compounding**

| Feature | **Extemporaneous** | **Bulk** |
|---|---|---|
| For whom | **One named patient** | Stock / many patients |
| When prepared | **On demand** | In advance |
| Quantity | Small (a few doses) | Large |
| Shelf-life needed | Short (days) | Long (needs preservative & stability data) |
| Record | Prescription register | Batch manufacturing record |
| Label | Patient's name & directions | Batch no., mfg./ exp. date |

### ⭐ Types of dispensing set-ups
1. **Community/ Retail (chemist & druggist) pharmacy** — dispenses against prescriptions of the
   public.
2. **Hospital pharmacy** — in-patient (unit dose, ward stock, floor stock) & out-patient dispensing,
   IV admixture, TPN, satellite pharmacy.
3. **Dispensary of a clinic/ dispensary of a doctor**.
4. **Industrial dispensing** — factory/ ESI dispensaries.
5. **Ayurvedic/ Unani/ Homoeopathic dispensing**.

## 4.2 THE DISPENSING PROCEDURE — GENERAL DIRECTIONS ⭐⭐

📌 The classical sequence is a **10-step chain**. Learn the order.

```
 1. RECEIVE the prescription
 2. READ & CHECK it completely (legality, completeness, dose, incompatibility)
 3. QUERY the prescriber if there is any doubt
 4. NUMBER & record it (prescription register)
 5. PLAN the preparation (calculations, order of mixing, method)
 6. COLLECT the ingredients & apparatus; CHECK every label THREE times
 7. WEIGH / MEASURE accurately and COMPOUND
 8. Select the CONTAINER and PACK
 9. LABEL correctly (+ auxiliary/ cautionary labels)
10. FINAL CHECK, PRICE, and DELIVER with COUNSELLING; FILE the prescription
```

### Step-by-step "general directions"

**(1) Receiving the prescription**
* Receive courteously; do not show doubt or alarm before the patient.
* Never dispense a prescription that is **not signed** by a Registered Medical Practitioner
  (RMP) when the drug is a Schedule H/ H1/ X drug.

**(2) Reading & checking** — check that the prescription bears:
date, patient's name/age/sex/address, the drug(s), strength, dosage form, quantity, directions,
prescriber's name, registration number, address & **signature in ink**.
⭐ For a **Schedule X** drug the prescription must be **in duplicate**, one copy **retained by the
pharmacist for 2 years**.

**(3) Doubt/ query** — if the writing is illegible, the dose looks wrong, the drug is contraindicated
or an incompatibility exists, **contact the prescriber**. ⚠️ **Never guess, never alter a
prescription on your own.** Any change agreed with the prescriber must be **written on the
prescription and initialled with the date**.

**(4) Numbering & recording** — every dispensed prescription is given a **serial number**, entered
in the **prescription register/ record book** (kept **2 years** for Schedule H, **2 years** for
Schedule X duplicate copies), and the same number appears on the label.

**(5) Planning & calculations** — write down the working formula for the total quantity, decide the
order of mixing, check the **maximum dose**, check **incompatibilities**, choose the vehicle,
preservative, and the container.

**(6) Collecting ingredients — the "three-check" rule** ⭐
> **Read the label of every container: (i) when you take it from the shelf, (ii) when you weigh or
> measure from it, and (iii) when you return it to the shelf.**
* Never use an **unlabelled** container.
* Never dispense from a container whose label is **doubtful, damaged or defaced**.
* Keep the **stopper** in the hand (or on its side, never face down on the bench).
* Pour from the side **opposite the label** so the label is not spoiled by drips.

**(7) Weighing, measuring & compounding** — see §4.4 and §4.5.

**(8) Packing & container selection** — see §4.6.

**(9) Labelling** — see §4.7.

**(10) Final check, pricing & delivery**
* Re-check the finished product against the prescription: **right patient, right drug, right
  strength, right quantity, right form, right label**.
* ⭐ The **"5 Rights" (or 7 Rights) of dispensing**: **Right patient, right drug, right dose,
  right route/ dosage form, right time**, (+ right documentation, right counselling).
* Price it, deliver it, and **counsel the patient**: how much, how often, when (before/ after food),
  how long, how to store, what to avoid, what side-effects to expect,
  **"shake the bottle", "for external use only"** etc.
* **File/ return** the prescription as required by law.

### ⭐ GOOD DISPENSING PRACTICE (GDP) — the golden rules

1. **Accuracy above speed** — an inaccurate medicine is a poison.
2. **Cleanliness** — clean bench, clean apparatus, clean hands, dry glassware.
3. **Order & method** — one prescription at a time on the bench; put away each ingredient before
   taking the next.
4. **Never dispense from memory** — always read the label & the prescription.
5. **Check three times** (shelf → weighing → return).
6. **Never taste** an unknown substance; smell cautiously.
7. **Wash & dry apparatus immediately** after use to avoid cross-contamination.
8. **Use the right size of apparatus** — never weigh a very small quantity on a large balance, never
   measure a small volume in a large measure.
9. **Do not use metal spatulas** with oxidising/ corrosive substances (iodine, silver salts,
   tannins, mercurials) → use **porcelain, horn, plastic or rubber**.
10. **Keep poisons separate**, in distinctive containers, in a locked cupboard.
11. **Record everything**; date & initial every entry.
12. **Personal hygiene & professional appearance**; do not eat/ smoke at the dispensing bench.

## 4.3 THE DISPENSING DEPARTMENT — PREMISES & APPARATUS

### ⭐ Minimum requirements (Schedule N spirit)
* Area of a pharmacy **not less than 6 m² (about 60–65 sq ft)**, with adequate ventilation,
  lighting, water supply, drainage; **10 m²** if a dispensing counter plus storage is included
  (state rules vary).
* Separate, clean **dispensing bench** with a **smooth, impervious, washable top**;
  good **north light**/ shadow-free illumination.
* **Cupboards & shelves** for stock, a **refrigerator (2–8 °C)** for thermolabile drugs, a
  **locked poison cupboard**, and a **narcotic safe**.
* A **sink with running water**, waste-bin, and a fire extinguisher.
* The name-board must display the name of the **registered pharmacist** on duty.

### ⭐ APPARATUS OF THE DISPENSING BENCH

| Group | Items |
|---|---|
| **Weighing** | **Class A (Class I) prescription/ dispensing balance**, Class B balance, counter balance, analytical balance, **weight box (metric)**, forceps/ tongs (never touch weights with fingers), weighing papers/ watch glasses/ butter paper |
| **Measuring** | **Conical measures**, **cylindrical measures**, graduated pipettes, burettes, measuring flasks, **droppers/ dropping bottles (medicine dropper)**, minim measures/ minim glass, syringes, **spoons: teaspoon 5 mL, dessertspoon 8 mL, tablespoon 15 mL** |
| **Mixing & size reduction** | **Mortars & pestles** (glass, porcelain, **Wedgwood** — rough, best for grinding; metal), **spatulas** (steel, horn, plastic, rubber), **ointment slab/ tile (glass or porcelain)**, ointment mill, capsule filler, suppository moulds, pill machine, pill tile |
| **Heating & evaporation** | Bunsen burner/ spirit lamp/ hot plate, water bath, evaporating dish, beaker, crucible, tripod & wire gauze, sand bath |
| **Filtering & transferring** | Funnels, filter paper, cotton wool, muslin, retort stand, glass rods, stirring rods, glass funnels, Buchner funnel |
| **Glassware** | Beakers, flasks, test tubes, stoppered bottles, wide- and narrow-mouth bottles, watch glasses, desiccator |
| **Packing & finishing** | Bottles (plain, ribbed, amber), jars, collapsible tubes, cartons, corks/ screw caps, **labels** (white, red-bordered), sealing wax, capping machine, powder papers, cachet machine, pill boxes, wrapping paper, string |
| **Reference** | **IP, NFI, BP, Martindale**, dose tables, prescription register, poison register |

⭐ **Which mortar for what?**

| Mortar | Use |
|---|---|
| **Glass** | **Staining, corrosive, oxidising & liquid** substances (iodine, dyes, silver nitrate, potassium permanganate); easy to clean; **not for grinding hard solids** |
| **Porcelain (glazed)** | General mixing, trituration of crystals; smooth |
| **Wedgwood (unglazed, rough)** | ⭐ **Best for reducing hard crystalline substances to a fine powder** — the rough surface gives greater grinding action; may stain |
| **Metal (iron/ brass)** | Very hard substances (bark, roots); ⚠️ never for oxidising/ tannin/ mercurial substances |

⭐ **Which spatula for what?**
* **Stainless steel spatula** — general use with ointments & powders.
* ⚠️ **Horn, plastic, rubber or porcelain spatula** — for **iodine, tannic acid, salicylic acid,
  mercury salts, silver nitrate, phenol** and other substances that **react with or corrode metal**.
* **Rubber spatula (policeman)** — scraping the last of an ointment from a slab/mortar.

## 4.4 WEIGHING ⭐⭐

📌 **WEIGHING** is the determination of the **mass** of a substance by comparison with standard
weights, using a balance.
📌 A **BALANCE** is an instrument that compares an unknown mass with a known mass; it works on the
**principle of moments (levers)** — the classical dispensing balance is an **equal-arm lever
balance**.

### 4.4.1 Terminology of a balance ⭐

| Term | 📌 Definition |
|---|---|
| **Capacity (maximum load)** | The **largest weight** that can be placed on one pan without damaging the balance |
| **Sensitivity (sensitivity requirement, SR)** | ⭐ The **smallest weight that will produce a perceptible (1 division/ 1 mm) deflection** of the pointer at a given load — the **lower** the SR value, the **more** sensitive the balance |
| **Accuracy** | Nearness of the result to the **true value** |
| **Precision (reproducibility)** | Agreement between **repeated** weighings |
| **Rider** | A small wire weight moved along the graduated beam for fine adjustment |
| **Arrestment/ Beam-arrest knob** | Locks the beam to protect the knife-edges when loading/ unloading |
| **Knife-edge & bearing (planes)** | The agate/ steel pivots — the **most delicate part** |
| **Pointer & scale** | Indicate the equilibrium/ rest point |
| **Levelling screws & plumb bob/ spirit level** | To set the balance horizontal |
| **Zero adjustment (balancing screw)** | To bring the pointer to zero when unloaded |
| **Tare** | A counterpoise to offset the weight of a container/ paper |
| **Minimum weighable quantity (MWQ)** | ⭐ The **smallest quantity that can be weighed** on a given balance within the accepted percentage of error |

### 4.4.2 🔢 CLASS A (Class I) PRESCRIPTION BALANCE — the numbers to memorise

| Specification | Value |
|---|---|
| **Sensitivity requirement (SR)** | ⭐ **6 mg** (i.e. 6 mg causes 1 division deflection) — with no load, and with a **10 g** load on each pan |
| **Maximum capacity** | ⭐ **120 g** (some texts: 60 g per pan) |
| **Minimum weighable quantity** | ⭐ **120 mg** |
| **Accepted maximum error in dispensing** | ⭐ **5 %** |
| **Class B balance — SR** | **30 mg**; MWQ **650 mg**; capacity 120 g |

⭐ **THE MINIMUM WEIGHABLE QUANTITY FORMULA**
```
                    Sensitivity Requirement × 100
MWQ  =  ─────────────────────────────────────────────
                Acceptable percentage of error
```
🔢 **Worked example (the classic):**
SR = 6 mg, acceptable error = 5 % →
`MWQ = (6 × 100)/5 = 120 mg`.
Hence, **never weigh less than 120 mg on a Class A prescription balance.**

🔢 **Second example:** For a Class B balance (SR = 30 mg) with 5 % error →
`MWQ = (30 × 100)/5 = 600 mg` (often quoted as 650 mg).

🔢 **Third example:** If a 3 % error is acceptable and SR = 6 mg →
`MWQ = (6 × 100)/3 = 200 mg`.

📌 **Percentage error** =
```
        Error (difference between desired and actual quantity)
     ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────  × 100
                     Quantity desired
```
🔢 *Example:* You wish to weigh 60 mg on a Class A balance; the possible error is 6 mg →
% error = (6/60) × 100 = **10 %** → **unacceptable** (> 5 %) → therefore use the **aliquot method**.

### 4.4.3 ⭐ THE ALIQUOT METHOD (of weighing) — for quantities below the MWQ

📌 **DEFINITION** — The **aliquot method** is a technique in which a **quantity smaller than the
minimum weighable quantity** is obtained accurately by **weighing a larger, weighable amount of the
drug, diluting it with an inert diluent to a known total weight, and then weighing out an aliquot
(a definite fraction) of that mixture** which contains the required small quantity of drug.

⭐ **THE THREE STEPS**
1. **Multiple factor** — decide how many times bigger the weighable amount must be:
   `Multiple factor = MWQ ÷ quantity actually required`.
2. **Weigh** MWQ of the drug and dilute it with diluent to
   `MWQ × multiple factor` total weight (so that **each MWQ of the mixture contains the required
   dose**).
3. **Weigh the aliquot** = MWQ of the mixture → it contains the required quantity of drug.

🔢 **WORKED EXAMPLE 1 (weighing)**
*Weigh 20 mg of atropine sulphate on a Class A balance (MWQ 120 mg), using lactose as diluent.*
* Multiple factor = 120 ÷ 20 = **6**
* Weigh **120 mg atropine sulphate**; add lactose to make **120 × 6 = 720 mg** of mixture.
* **Weigh 120 mg of this mixture** → it contains 120/6 = **20 mg of atropine sulphate.** ✔

🔢 **WORKED EXAMPLE 2**
*Weigh 5 mg of a potent drug; MWQ = 120 mg.*
* Multiple factor = 120 ÷ 5 = **24**
* Weigh 120 mg drug + lactose to **120 × 24 = 2880 mg (2.88 g)**.
* Weigh **120 mg** of the mixture = **5 mg drug**. ✔

⚠️ **TRAP** — In the aliquot method, both the **quantity weighed of the drug** and the
**quantity of the aliquot taken** must be **≥ the MWQ**. Diluent commonly used = **lactose**
(also starch, sucrose, mannitol, kaolin, light kaolin, calcium phosphate).

📌 **Trituration (as a dilution)** — a **1 in 10 dilution** of a potent drug with lactose kept
ready-made for the same purpose.

### 4.4.4 ⭐ RULES / GENERAL DIRECTIONS FOR WEIGHING

1. Place the balance on a **firm, level, vibration-free bench**, away from draughts, direct
   sunlight, heat and corrosive fumes; **level it** with the levelling screws & spirit level/ plumb.
2. **Arrest the beam** before placing or removing anything from the pans; release it gently.
3. **Check the zero (rest point)** before every weighing; adjust with the balancing screw.
4. Always **use the pan on the correct side** — traditionally **weights on the LEFT pan** and
   the **substance on the RIGHT pan** (so the right hand is free to add the substance);
   with a single-pan/ digital balance simply **tare** the container.
5. Never place a substance **directly on the pan** — use **counterpoised weighing papers,
   butter paper, glazed paper, a watch glass or a tared container** on **both** pans.
6. **Handle weights only with forceps/ tongs**, never with the fingers (moisture, grease & corrosion
   change their mass). Keep the weight box **closed** and each weight in its own recess.
7. Add weights in **descending order** (largest first) and place them in the **centre** of the pan.
8. Add the substance in **small portions with a spatula** until balance is achieved;
   ⚠️ never add or remove material while the beam is released.
9. **Never weigh a quantity below the minimum weighable quantity** — use the **aliquot method**.
10. **Never exceed the maximum capacity** of the balance.
11. Weigh **hygroscopic, deliquescent, volatile & efflorescent** substances **quickly**, in a
    **closed/ tared container** (e.g. a stoppered weighing bottle) — never on open paper.
12. Weigh **liquids, semisolids, oily, sticky, corrosive & staining** substances in a
    **tared container** (never on paper).
13. Clean the pans and the surrounding area after every weighing; **remove spilt material at once**.
14. When finished, **remove the load, arrest the beam, cover the balance**, and return the weights
    to the box.
15. Check the balance **periodically against standard/ calibrated weights**; get it **verified &
    stamped** as required by the Weights & Measures Act.
16. **Do not weigh** a substance and a *different* substance on the same paper; use a fresh paper.

### 4.4.5 ⚠️ SOURCES OF ERROR IN WEIGHING

| Source | Explanation / remedy |
|---|---|
| **Unequal arms/ worn knife-edges** | Use the **method of double weighing (Gauss/ Borda)** — weigh, then interchange pan & weights, take the mean |
| **Inaccurate/ corroded weights** | Verify against standard weights; never handle with fingers |
| **Balance not levelled** | Level with screws before use |
| **Draught, vibration, heat, magnetism** | Use a draught-shield/ closed case; firm bench |
| **Buoyancy of air** | Significant only in analytical work |
| **Moisture absorption/ evaporation** | Weigh quickly in a **closed container** |
| **Static charge** | Wipe with an anti-static cloth; avoid plastic |
| **Weighing paper not counterpoised** | Always place an identical paper on the other pan |
| **Parallax while reading the pointer** | Read the pointer **straight on, at eye level** |
| **Weighing below the MWQ** | Use the **aliquot method** |
| **Residue left on pan/ spatula** | Clean between weighings; use separate spatulas |

## 4.5 MEASURING (of liquids) ⭐⭐

📌 **MEASURING** is the determination of the **volume** of a liquid with the aid of a graduated
apparatus.

### 4.5.1 Apparatus for measuring & its selection

| Apparatus | Description | Best for | Accuracy |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Conical measure** | Cone-shaped, graduated, wide mouth, easy to pour & clean, stands firmly | ⭐ **General dispensing of moderate to large volumes; viscous liquids** | **Less accurate** — because the diameter changes with height, so a small error in reading the meniscus means a **large volume error** near the top |
| **Cylindrical measure** | Straight-sided graduated cylinder | ⭐ **More accurate measuring**; small & moderate volumes | **More accurate** — uniform diameter, graduations far apart |
| **Graduated (measuring) pipette** | Delivers small volumes | **Small volumes (1–10 mL)**, potent liquids | High |
| **Burette** | Delivers variable, accurate volumes | Titrations, accurate small volumes | High |
| **Volumetric flask** | One mark, "to contain" | Making up to a **final volume** | Highest |
| **Dropper (medicine dropper)/ dropping bottle** | Delivers drops | **Very small doses, potent liquids (tinctures), paediatric drops, eye/ ear/ nose drops** | Depends on calibration |
| **Minim measure/ minim glass** | Old imperial small measure | Minims | Moderate |
| **Syringe (oral dosing syringe)** | Graduated barrel | Paediatric & accurate small volumes | High |
| **Household measures** | Spoons & cups given to the patient | Home administration only | Low |

⭐ **RULE OF APPARATUS SELECTION:** Always choose the **smallest measure that will hold the
required volume**; and **never measure a volume less than one-fifth (20 %) of the capacity of the
measure** (a 100 mL conical measure should not be used for less than 20 mL).

⚠️ **TRAP — Conical vs Cylindrical measure:** the **cylindrical measure is the MORE ACCURATE**
of the two; the **conical measure is more convenient** (easy pouring, stirring, cleaning) and is
preferred for **viscous liquids**.

🔢 **HOUSEHOLD MEASURES — memorise**

| Household measure | Metric equivalent |
|---|---|
| **1 teaspoonful (tsp)** | ⭐ **5 mL** (approx. 1 fluid drachm) |
| **1 dessertspoonful** | ⭐ **8 mL** (some texts 10 mL) |
| **1 tablespoonful (tbsp)** | ⭐ **15 mL** (½ fl oz) |
| **1 wineglassful** | **60 mL** (2 fl oz) |
| **1 teacupful** | **120 mL** (4 fl oz) |
| **1 tumblerful/ glassful** | **240 mL** (8 fl oz) |
| **1 breakfast cupful** | **240–250 mL** |
| **1 drop (water, standard dropper)** | ⭐ **≈ 0.05 mL → 20 drops = 1 mL** |
| **1 minim** | **0.06 mL** (1 mL ≈ 16.9 ≈ 17 minims) |

⚠️ **TRAP — "1 drop = 1 minim" is only approximately true for water.** The **actual volume of a
drop depends on the surface tension, viscosity & density of the liquid and on the diameter of the
dropper orifice**; therefore a dropper must be **calibrated for each liquid**.
🔢 Approximate drops per mL: **Water 20, Alcohol/ Tinctures ~ 40–60 (alcohol has low surface
tension → smaller drops), Chloroform ~ 60, Glycerin ~ 30, Fixed oils ~ 40–45**.

📌 **Calibration of a dropper** — count the number of drops needed to deliver a measured volume
(e.g. 1 mL or 2 mL) of the **actual liquid** three times, take the mean, then calculate
`drops required = (dose in mL × drops counted) ÷ volume measured`.

### 4.5.2 ⭐ READING THE MENISCUS

📌 The **meniscus** is the curved upper surface of a liquid in a narrow container.

* ⭐ For **water and most aqueous liquids** (which **wet** glass), the meniscus is **CONCAVE** →
  read the **LOWEST point (bottom) of the meniscus**.
* ⭐ For **mercury** (which does **not** wet glass), the meniscus is **CONVEX** → read the
  **TOP (upper) point**.
* Read with the **eye at the same level as the meniscus** to avoid **parallax error**;
  the measure must be **vertical on a level surface** (⚠️ never held up in the air at an angle).
* Reading from **above** gives a **falsely high** volume; from **below**, a **falsely low** volume.
* Dark liquids: read the **upper edge** if the bottom is not visible, and note this.

### 4.5.3 ⭐ RULES / GENERAL DIRECTIONS FOR MEASURING

1. Use a **clean, dry** measure; select the **smallest suitable size**; never use it for less than
   **one-fifth of its capacity**.
2. Place the measure on a **flat, level surface** and read at **eye level**; read the
   **bottom of the meniscus**.
3. Hold the bottle so the **label is in the palm of the hand (uppermost)** so that any liquid
   running down does not spoil the label; pour from the side **away from the label**.
4. **Remove the stopper with the little finger** of the pouring hand and **keep it in the hand**;
   never lay a stopper face-down on the bench.
5. Pour steadily, down the **inside wall/ down a glass rod**, avoiding splashing & air bubbles.
6. **Wipe the neck** of the stock bottle with a clean cloth after pouring, and replace the stopper
   at once.
7. **Allow viscous liquids** (glycerin, syrups, liquid paraffin, tragacanth mucilage) **to drain
   completely**; better still, **weigh viscous liquids instead of measuring** them, or **rinse the
   measure** with the vehicle and add the rinsings to the product.
8. **Very small volumes (< 1 mL, or less than one-fifth of the smallest measure)** should be
   measured with a **calibrated pipette, burette, syringe or dropper**, or dealt with by the
   **aliquot method of measuring**.
9. **Volatile liquids** (ether, chloroform, alcohol, volatile oils) must be measured **quickly** in
   a **cool place, away from flame**, and covered — to prevent loss by evaporation.
10. Measure liquids **at room temperature** (glassware is calibrated at 20 °C/ 27 °C);
    never measure a hot liquid.
11. **Never return** surplus measured liquid to the stock bottle.
12. Add liquids to the vehicle in the **correct order of mixing** (see Ch. 12); do not measure
    incompatible liquids in the same measure without washing.
13. Use **separate measures** for different types of liquids (e.g. one for oils/ tars, one for
    aqueous liquids), and wash immediately after use.
14. **"To contain" vs "to deliver"** — volumetric flasks *contain* the stated volume;
    pipettes & burettes *deliver* it. Do not confuse them.

### 4.5.4 ⭐ THE ALIQUOT METHOD OF MEASURING

📌 Used when the volume required is **too small to be measured accurately** (below one-fifth of the
smallest available measure).
**Principle:** measure a **larger, accurately measurable volume** of the liquid, **dilute it with a
suitable diluent to a known total volume**, then **measure an aliquot** of that dilution containing
the required amount.

🔢 **WORKED EXAMPLE**
*You must dispense 0.2 mL of a potent tincture, and the smallest accurate measure available reads
2 mL.*
* Measure **2 mL of the tincture** and dilute with alcohol (or the appropriate vehicle) to
  **20 mL** (a 1 in 10 dilution).
* Measure **2 mL of this dilution** → it contains **0.2 mL of the tincture.** ✔

⚠️ The **diluent must be compatible & pharmaceutically acceptable** (alcohol, water,
chloroform water, syrup), and the **extra volume of diluent must be allowed for** in the final
volume of the preparation.

### 4.5.5 ⚠️ SOURCES OF ERROR IN MEASURING

| Source | Remedy |
|---|---|
| **Parallax** (eye not level with meniscus) | Read at eye level on a level bench |
| **Wrong part of the meniscus read** | Read the **bottom** for aqueous liquids |
| **Using too large a measure for a small volume** | Use the smallest measure; observe the one-fifth rule |
| **Drainage/ adhesion of viscous liquids** | Allow to drain; rinse with vehicle; or **weigh instead** |
| **Evaporation of volatile liquids** | Measure quickly, keep covered & cool |
| **Temperature difference (expansion)** | Measure at room temperature |
| **Wet or dirty measure diluting the liquid** | Use a dry, clean measure |
| **Air bubbles/ froth** | Pour slowly down the wall; let bubbles rise |
| **Uncalibrated dropper assumed to give 20 drops/mL** | **Calibrate the dropper for that liquid** |
| **Graduations worn off/ inaccurate glassware** | Use verified, stamped Class A glassware |

🔁 **COMPARE — WEIGHING vs MEASURING (which to use?)**

| Situation | Preferred |
|---|---|
| Solids, semisolids, ointment bases, waxes | **Weigh** |
| Aqueous liquids, vehicles, tinctures, syrups (ordinary volumes) | **Measure** |
| ⭐ **Viscous liquids** — glycerin, liquid paraffin, castor oil, syrups, mucilages, tars, oils | ⭐ **WEIGH** (drainage makes measuring inaccurate) |
| ⭐ **Volatile liquids** — ether, chloroform, alcohol | **Measure quickly** (weighing loses material by evaporation) |
| Very small volumes of potent liquids | **Calibrated dropper/ pipette or aliquot method** |
| Corrosive liquids (strong acids) | Measure in a **glass measure**, never pipette by mouth |

🔢 **Converting weight ↔ volume:**
```
Volume (mL) = Weight (g) ÷ Specific gravity        Weight (g) = Volume (mL) × Specific gravity
```
🔢 *Example:* 25 mL of glycerin (sp. gr. 1.26) weighs 25 × 1.26 = **31.5 g**.
🔢 *Example:* 20 g of chloroform (sp. gr. 1.47) occupies 20 ÷ 1.47 = **13.6 mL**.

## 4.6 CONTAINERS, CLOSURES & PACKING ⭐

📌 **Container** — the article that holds or is intended to hold the drug and is or may be in
**direct contact** with it. The **closure** is part of the container.

### ⭐ Pharmacopoeial classes of container (definitions to learn verbatim)

| Container | 📌 Definition |
|---|---|
| **Well-closed container** | Protects the contents from **extraneous solids** and from **loss** of the article under normal handling |
| **Air-tight (tightly-closed) container** | Impermeable to solids, liquids and **gases/ vapour** under normal handling; can be **re-closed** air-tight |
| **Hermetically-sealed container** | ⭐ **Impervious to air or any other gas**; **cannot be re-opened without destroying the seal** — e.g. a **fused-glass ampoule** |
| **Light-resistant container** | Protects the contents from the effect of **light** — **amber-coloured glass** or an opaque wrapper |
| **Tamper-evident container** | Fitted with a device that reveals any interference |
| **Single-dose container** | Holds **one dose**; e.g. ampoule |
| **Multi-dose container** | Holds a quantity for **more than one dose**; e.g. a multi-dose vial with a rubber closure |
| **Child-resistant container** | Difficult for a child to open |
| **Pressurised container** | Withstands internal pressure; e.g. **aerosol** |
| **Sealed container** | Closed by fusion |

⭐ **TYPES OF GLASS (IP/USP)** — a favourite question

| Type | Composition | Use |
|---|---|---|
| **Type I — Borosilicate (neutral) glass** | Boron oxide replaces much of the alkali → **highest hydrolytic resistance, lowest alkali leaching** | ⭐ **All parenterals, especially alkali-sensitive & aqueous injections; ampoules & vials; water for injection** |
| **Type II — Treated (de-alkalised/ sulphur-treated) soda-lime glass** | Surface treated with SO₂ | ⭐ **Aqueous parenterals with pH < 7 (acidic & neutral); infusion bottles** |
| **Type III — Regular soda-lime glass** | Untreated | ⭐ **Non-aqueous parenterals (oily injections), powders for injection; dry solids** |
| **Type IV (NP) — General purpose soda-lime glass** | Untreated, lowest resistance | ⭐ **NOT for parenterals** — for **oral & topical** products (liquids, tablets, ointments) |

⭐ Glass is tested by the **powdered glass test** & the **water-attack test**;
**amber glass** protects against **light below 470 nm**.

⭐ **Choice of container in dispensing (classical, high-yield)**

| Preparation | Container |
|---|---|
| **Mixtures, syrups, elixirs, solutions (oral liquids)** | **Plain (smooth) narrow-mouth glass or plastic bottle** with a screw cap |
| ⭐ **Liniments, lotions, applications, all "FOR EXTERNAL USE" liquids** | ⭐ **RIBBED/ FLUTED (grooved) bottle** — recognisable **by touch in the dark**, so it cannot be swallowed by mistake |
| **Light-sensitive liquids** | **Amber/ dark-coloured bottle** |
| **Ointments, creams, pastes** | **Wide-mouth glass/ plastic jar or collapsible aluminium/ plastic tube** (tube = better protection, less contamination) |
| **Powders (bulk)** | Wide-mouth jar; **divided powders** → **powder papers** (double wrapped) or **cachets/ capsules** |
| **Effervescent powders/ granules, hygroscopic powders** | ⭐ **Air-tight, wide-mouth container** with a **desiccant**; sealed sachets |
| **Volatile & inflammable liquids (ether, chloroform, spirits)** | **Tightly-closed, cool, dark; away from flame** |
| **Tablets & capsules** | Well-closed wide-mouth container, strips, blisters |
| **Suppositories & pessaries** | Wrapped in **metal foil/ waxed paper**, in a **cardboard box, stored in a cool place (< 25 °C or refrigerated)** |
| **Injections (aqueous)** | **Type I/ II glass ampoule or vial, hermetically sealed** |
| **Eye drops** | **Sterile, amber glass or plastic dropper bottle, ≤ 10 mL**, with preservative |
| **Aerosols/ sprays** | **Pressurised container** |
| **Poisons & Schedule X** | Distinctive container, locked cupboard |

⭐ **Closures** — cork (⚠️ porous, absorbs, contaminates — must be lined), **screw caps (plastic/
metal with a liner)**, rubber closures for vials (⚠️ may **adsorb preservatives** & **leach
extractives**), glass stoppers (for corrosive/ volatile liquids), **child-resistant closures**,
crimped aluminium seals, roll-on & dropper closures.
⭐ **Liners/ wads** — pulp-board with a facing of **paper, foil, plastic (PVC), or waxed paper** to
give a seal and prevent contact with the closure.

⭐ **Packing rules**
* The container must be **clean, dry, of the correct size** — filled so there is a **small
  head-space** (never over-filled, never half-empty).
* **Never re-use** a container returned by a patient without cleaning & sterilising, and never for
  another drug.
* Fragile & liquid preparations must be **wrapped and boxed**.
* Suppositories & ointments must not be exposed to heat during packing.

## 4.7 LABELLING ⭐⭐

📌 The **label** is the pharmacist's *only* means of instructing the patient once the medicine has
left the pharmacy. **An unlabelled or wrongly-labelled medicine is dangerous.**

### ⭐ Particulars on a dispensed label

1. **Name & address of the pharmacy/ dispensary** (and phone number).
2. **Prescription serial number** and **date of dispensing**.
3. **Name of the patient** (⭐ and "for the child of…" where relevant).
4. **Name of the preparation/ drug and its strength** (as prescribed).
5. **Total quantity** supplied.
6. ⭐ **Directions for use** — the **dose, frequency, route, time in relation to food, duration**;
   written in **clear, simple language the patient understands** (English + local language),
   **never in Latin or in abbreviations**.
7. **Auxiliary/ cautionary labels** (see below).
8. **Storage instructions** ("Store in a cool, dark place", "Keep refrigerated, do not freeze").
9. **Expiry/ "use before" date** for the extemporaneous product.
10. ⭐ **"KEEP ALL MEDICINES OUT OF THE REACH OF CHILDREN."**
11. **Name/ initials of the dispensing pharmacist**.
12. For manufactured products: **batch number, manufacturing & expiry date, manufacturer's name &
    licence number, net content, Rx/ ℞ symbol**, and the **Schedule H/ H1/ X warning**.

### ⭐ COLOUR & POSITION OF LABELS — classical rules

| Label | Colour/ form | Applied to |
|---|---|---|
| **Internal (oral) medicines** | ⭐ **WHITE label with black print** | Mixtures, syrups, elixirs, solutions, powders, tablets |
| ⭐ **External use** | ⭐ **RED label** (or white with a **red border/ red print**) bearing **"FOR EXTERNAL USE ONLY"** | Liniments, lotions, applications, paints, ointments, gargles, ear/ nose drops, inhalations |
| **Poisons** | **Red or distinctly marked "POISON"**, often with a skull symbol | Poison-schedule items |
| **Schedule H drugs** | Must bear the symbol **"Rx"** and the warning **"To be sold by retail on the prescription of a Registered Medical Practitioner only"** | Prescription drugs |
| **Schedule H1** | ⭐ **Rx in RED, within a box with a RED border**, plus the extra warning about antibiotic resistance | Specified antibiotics, anti-TB & habit-forming drugs |
| **Schedule X** | **"NRx"** in red + the Schedule X warning | Narcotics/ psychotropics |
| **Schedule G** | ⭐ **"Caution: it is dangerous to take this preparation except under medical supervision"** | Schedule G drugs |

### ⭐ AUXILIARY / CAUTIONARY LABELS — match the label to the product

| Auxiliary label | Applied to |
|---|---|
| ⭐ **"SHAKE THE BOTTLE (WELL) BEFORE USE"** | ⭐ **ALL suspensions (diffusible & indiffusible), emulsions, lotions with insoluble matter, mixtures with a sediment** |
| **"FOR EXTERNAL USE ONLY"** | Liniments, lotions, applications, paints, ointments |
| **"NOT TO BE TAKEN"** | Gargles, mouthwashes, douches, inhalations, enemas |
| **"NOT TO BE SWALLOWED — for gargling only; dilute before use"** | ⭐ **Gargles & mouthwashes (concentrated)** |
| **"Dilute before use"** | Concentrated gargles, concentrated infusions, some antiseptics |
| **"Do not apply to broken/ raw skin"** | Counter-irritant liniments |
| **"Avoid contact with eyes"** | Keratolytics, counter-irritants |
| **"Warm/ soften before use", "Store in a cool place"** | Suppositories, pessaries, ointments |
| **"Insert into the rectum"/ "Insert into the vagina"** | ⭐ **Suppositories / Pessaries** — plus **"NOT TO BE TAKEN BY MOUTH"** |
| **"Inhale the vapour"/ "Add 1 teaspoonful to 500 mL of hot water and inhale the vapour"** | ⭐ **Inhalations** |
| **"To be taken with/ after food"** | Irritant drugs — aspirin, NSAIDs, iron, metronidazole |
| **"To be taken on an empty stomach/ ½–1 h before food"** | Ampicillin, tetracycline, antacids as directed |
| **"Do not take milk/ antacids/ iron with this medicine"** | Tetracyclines, fluoroquinolones |
| **"Avoid alcohol"** | Metronidazole, antihistamines, sedatives, disulfiram |
| **"May cause drowsiness — do not drive or operate machinery"** | Antihistamines, sedatives, hypnotics |
| **"Complete the full course"** | Antibiotics |
| **"This medicine may colour the urine (red/ orange/ brown)"** | Rifampicin, phenazopyridine, senna |
| **"Swallow whole — do not chew or crush"** | Enteric-coated & sustained-release tablets |
| **"Dissolve/ disperse in water before taking"** | Effervescent & dispersible tablets |
| **"Suck/ dissolve slowly in the mouth"** | Lozenges, pastilles |
| **"Protect from light"** | Photolabile drugs |
| **"Discard … days after opening"** | Eye drops (⭐ **discard 4 weeks after opening**; hospital single-patient use), reconstituted syrups |
| **"Keep refrigerated — do not freeze"** | Insulin, vaccines, reconstituted antibiotic syrups, suppositories |
| **"Caution: flammable — keep away from fire"** | Collodions, spirits, ether |

⚠️ **TRAP** — **"Shake well before use"** is required on **emulsions and suspensions**, but is
**NOT** required on a **true solution** (a clear mixture) — the classic examination discriminator.

⚠️ **TRAP — Gargles & mouthwashes carry "NOT TO BE SWALLOWED", and are labelled with a RED
(external) label** even though they are used in the mouth/ throat.

### ⭐ Rules of good labelling
* Type or write **legibly in ink**; ⭐ **never in pencil**; never over-write or erase.
* **One label = one medicine.** Never leave old labels on a re-used container.
* Place the label on the **front of the bottle**, **squarely & neatly**, on the **flat side**;
  cover with **transparent tape/ varnish** if it may become wet or greasy.
* Directions must be **complete and quantitative** — ⚠️ *"as directed", "use as before" and
  "take as required" are NOT acceptable directions.*
* Write **quantities in words as well as figures** where confusion is possible
  ("Two (2) 5 mL spoonfuls").
* Use the **metric system**; avoid decimals where possible (write **500 mg**, not 0.5 g);
  ⭐ always put a **zero before a decimal point (0.5 mL)** and **never a trailing zero (5.0 mL)**
  — a misread decimal is a classic ten-fold overdose.
* Check the finished label against the prescription **before** it is stuck on, and again at the
  final check.

## 4.8 PRICING, RECORDS & THE PHARMACIST'S RESPONSIBILITIES

* ⭐ **Pricing** of an extemporaneous preparation = **cost of ingredients + cost of the container &
  label + a professional (dispensing) fee** (+ overheads/ profit margin).
* **Records to maintain:** prescription/ dispensing register, **poison register**,
  **Schedule X duplicate prescriptions (retain 2 years)**, purchase & sale records (retain
  **2 years**), controlled-drug register, temperature log of the refrigerator.
* ⭐ **Only a registered pharmacist** may compound & dispense a medicine on the prescription of an
  RMP (**Pharmacy Act, 1948, Sec. 42**); contravention is punishable.
* The pharmacist must **not substitute** one brand/ drug for another without the prescriber's
  consent (except where generic substitution is legally permitted).
* **Professional secrecy** — the contents of a prescription are **confidential**.
* **Refuse to dispense** (and refer back to the prescriber) if: the prescription is
  **unsigned/ forged**, the **dose exceeds the maximum**, the drug is **contraindicated** for the
  patient, there is a **serious incompatibility**, or the prescription is for a
  **Schedule X drug without the legal formalities**.

## 4.9 CHAPTER 4 — RAPID REVISION SHEET

* **Dispensing** = preparing + packing + labelling + supplying a medicine against a prescription;
  **compounding is only one step of it**.
* **Extemporaneous** = for one patient, on demand, small quantity, short shelf-life.
* Dispensing steps: **receive → read/check → query → number & record → plan → collect (check
  label 3 times) → weigh/measure & compound → pack → label → final check, price, deliver, counsel**.
* **THREE-CHECK RULE:** read the label when taking from the shelf, when using, and when returning.
* **5 Rights:** right patient, drug, dose, route/form, time.
* **Wedgwood mortar** = best for grinding hard crystals; **glass mortar** = staining/ corrosive/
  liquid substances; **horn/ plastic spatula** = iodine, tannins, mercurials, silver nitrate.
* **Sensitivity requirement (SR)** = smallest weight giving a **1-division deflection**;
  lower SR = more sensitive.
* 🔢 **Class A balance: SR = 6 mg, capacity 120 g, MWQ = 120 mg, allowed error 5 %.**
  **Class B: SR = 30 mg, MWQ ≈ 600–650 mg.**
* 🔢 **MWQ = (SR × 100) ÷ acceptable % error.**
* 🔢 **% error = (error ÷ quantity desired) × 100.**
* **Aliquot method:** multiple factor = MWQ ÷ required quantity; weigh MWQ of drug, dilute to
  (MWQ × factor), then weigh MWQ of the mixture. Diluent = **lactose**.
* **Weights on the left pan, substance on the right; handle weights with forceps; add weights in
  descending order; arrest the beam while loading.**
* Never place a substance directly on the pan → **counterpoised papers or a tared container**;
  weigh **hygroscopic/ volatile/ liquid/ sticky** substances in a **closed, tared container**.
* **Cylindrical measure is MORE accurate; conical measure is more convenient (viscous liquids).**
* **Never use a measure for less than one-fifth of its capacity**; always use the smallest
  suitable measure.
* **Read the BOTTOM of the meniscus at eye level** (mercury: read the top). Reading from above →
  falsely high volume.
* 🔢 **1 tsp = 5 mL · 1 dessertspoon = 8 mL · 1 tbsp = 15 mL · wineglass = 60 mL · teacup = 120 mL ·
  tumbler = 240 mL · 1 drop ≈ 0.05 mL (20 drops = 1 mL) · 1 minim = 0.06 mL.**
* **A dropper must be calibrated for each liquid** — drop volume depends on surface tension,
  viscosity, density & orifice size.
* ⭐ **WEIGH viscous liquids** (glycerin, liquid paraffin, syrup, mucilage, tars);
  ⭐ **MEASURE volatile liquids** quickly (ether, chloroform, alcohol).
* 🔢 **Volume = weight ÷ specific gravity.**
* **Hermetically sealed = impervious to gases, cannot be reopened (ampoule).**
  **Air-tight = impermeable to solids, liquids & gases, re-closable.**
  **Well-closed = keeps out extraneous solids & prevents loss.**
* **Glass: Type I borosilicate = all parenterals; Type II treated soda-lime = acidic/neutral
  aqueous parenterals; Type III = oily injections & dry powders; Type IV (NP) = oral & topical
  only.**
* ⭐ **RIBBED/ FLUTED bottle + RED label + "FOR EXTERNAL USE ONLY"** for liniments, lotions,
  applications; **plain bottle + WHITE label** for internal medicines.
* ⭐ **"SHAKE WELL BEFORE USE"** → emulsions & suspensions (**not** true solutions).
* **Gargles/ mouthwashes → "NOT TO BE SWALLOWED / dilute before use"**;
  **Inhalations → "add 1 teaspoonful to 500 mL hot water and inhale the vapour"**;
  **Suppositories → "insert into the rectum, not to be taken by mouth, store in a cool place"**.
* **Eye drops: discard 4 weeks after opening.**
* **Always write 0.5 mL (leading zero) and never 5.0 mL (no trailing zero).**
* **Schedule X prescription: in duplicate, one copy retained 2 years.**
* Only a **registered pharmacist** may dispense (**Pharmacy Act 1948, Sec. 42**).
