## 1.1 THE WORD "DRUG"

📌 **DEFINITION (Etymology)**
The word **drug** is derived from the French word **"Drogue"** — meaning *a dry herb*.
(Some texts trace it to the Dutch/Low German **"Droog"** = dry.)
⭐ Examiners ask: *"Drug is derived from which word?"* → **Drogue (French) = dry herb.**

📌 **DEFINITION (Pharmacological)**
A **drug** is any substance or product that is used or intended to be used to **modify or explore
physiological systems or pathological states** for the benefit of the recipient (WHO, 1966).

📌 **DEFINITION (Drugs & Cosmetics Act, 1940 — Sec. 3(b), India)**
"Drug" includes —
1. All medicines for **internal or external** use of human beings or animals, and all substances
   intended to be used for or in the **diagnosis, treatment, mitigation or prevention** of any
   disease or disorder in human beings or animals.
2. Such substances (other than food) intended to **affect the structure or any function** of the
   human body, or intended to be used for the destruction of **vermin or insects** which cause
   disease in human beings or animals.
3. All substances intended for use as a **component** of a drug, including empty gelatin capsules.
4. Such **devices** intended for internal or external use in diagnosis, treatment, mitigation or
   prevention of disease, as notified by the Central Government.

📌 **DEFINITION (Cosmetic — Sec. 3(aaa))**
Any article intended to be rubbed, poured, sprinkled or sprayed on, introduced into or otherwise
applied to the human body for **cleansing, beautifying, promoting attractiveness or altering the
appearance**, and includes any article intended for use as a component of a cosmetic.

⚠️ **TRAP** — Soap is *excluded* from the definition of a cosmetic under the Act.

📌 **DEFINITION (Crude drug)**
A **crude drug** is a naturally occurring, unrefined substance of vegetable, animal or mineral
origin, subjected to no processing other than **collection and drying**.

🧠 **MEMORY HOOK — the 5 "must-know" definitions:** Drug → Crude drug → Pharmacognosy →
Pharmacy → Pharmacopoeia.

| Term | Definition | Etymology |
|---|---|---|
| **Pharmacognosy** | Study of crude drugs of natural (plant, animal, mineral) origin | Gk. *pharmakon* = drug + *gnosis* = knowledge |
| **Pharmacy** | Art & science of selecting, preparing, compounding, dispensing and storing drugs | Gk. *pharmakon* = drug/medicine |
| **Pharmacology** | Study of drugs and their actions on living systems | *pharmakon* + *logos* = study |
| **Pharmacodynamics** | What the **drug does to the body** | — |
| **Pharmacokinetics** | What the **body does to the drug** (ADME) | — |
| **Posology** | Science of **dosage** / dose determination | Gk. *posos* = how much + *logos* |
| **Pharmacopoeia** | Legally recognised official book of drug standards | Gk. *pharmakon* + *poiein* = to make |
| **Formulary** | Book of formulae/collection of prescriptions (e.g. National Formulary of India) | L. *formula* |
| **Materia Medica** | "Materials of medicine" — older term for the study of source, preparation & use of drugs | L. |
| **Therapeutics** | Application of remedies for the treatment of disease | Gk. *therapeuein* |
| **Toxicology** | Study of poisons — detection, action, treatment | Gk. *toxikon* = poison |
| **Dispensing** | Preparation and supply of medicine to a patient against a prescription | L. *dispensare* = weigh out |
| **Compounding** | Combining two or more ingredients to make a medicine | — |

⭐ **EXAM POINT** — **Father of Pharmacognosy** → *C. A. Seydler* coined the term (1815);
**Father of Medicine** → *Hippocrates*; **Father of Pharmacology** → *Rudolf Buchheim* /
*Oswald Schmiedeberg*; **Father of Indian Pharmacology** → *Ram Nath Chopra*;
**Father of Pharmacognosy in India** → *T. E. Wallis* (widely printed); **Father of Botany** →
*Theophrastus*; **Father of Materia Medica** → *Dioscorides* (*De Materia Medica*);
**Father of Toxicology** → *Paracelsus* (also called Father of Chemotherapy/Iatrochemistry);
**Father of Antisepsis/aseptic surgery** → *Joseph Lister*; **Father of Chemotherapy (modern)** →
*Paul Ehrlich*.

## 1.2 "KINDS OF DRUGS" — ALL THE CLASSIFICATION SCHEMES

⭐ This heading in the syllabus means: *"In how many ways can drugs be classified?"*
Objective papers pick a random one of these eight schemes. Learn all eight.

### Scheme 1 — Classification by SOURCE / ORIGIN

```
                            DRUGS
                              │
   ┌────────────┬─────────────┼─────────────┬──────────────┐
 NATURAL     SYNTHETIC   SEMI-SYNTHETIC  BIOLOGICAL   BIOTECHNOLOGICAL
   │                                     (biologicals)   (r-DNA)
   ├── Plant (vegetable)
   ├── Animal
   ├── Mineral
   ├── Microbial (fermentation)
   └── Marine
```

| Source | Explanation | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **Plant / Vegetable** | Largest single source of crude drugs | Digitalis (leaf), Opium (latex), Senna, Rauwolfia (root), Cinchona (bark), Castor oil (seed), Acacia, Tragacanth |
| **Animal** | Glands, organs, secretions, whole animals | Cod liver oil, Shark liver oil, Insulin (pancreas), Thyroid, Heparin (lung/intestinal mucosa), Gelatin, Honey, Beeswax, Wool fat (lanolin), Musk, Cochineal, Cantharides, Spermaceti, Pepsin, Trypsin, Vitamin A & D, Placental extract |
| **Mineral** | Inorganic substances of earth | Kaolin, Bentonite, Talc, Sulphur, Borax, Boric acid, Alum, Magnesium sulphate, Sodium chloride, Iodine, Calamine, Liquid paraffin & Petrolatum (mineral/petroleum origin), Bismuth salts, Ferrous sulphate |
| **Microbial** | Fungi, bacteria, actinomycetes — via fermentation | Penicillin (*Penicillium chrysogenum/notatum*), Streptomycin (*Streptomyces griseus*), Chloramphenicol (*S. venezuelae*), Tetracycline (*S. aureofaciens*), Erythromycin (*S. erythreus*), Griseofulvin, Ergot alkaloids (*Claviceps purpurea*), Lovastatin |
| **Marine** | Marine flora & fauna | Agar & Alginates (seaweeds), Carrageenan, Cod/shark liver oil, Cephalosporin C (marine fungus *Cephalosporium acremonium*), Cytarabine (sponge-derived model), Spongothymidine |
| **Synthetic** | Made wholly in the laboratory | Aspirin, Paracetamol, Sulphonamides, Chloroquine, Chloramphenicol (now synthetic), Barbiturates, Phenytoin |
| **Semi-synthetic** | Natural nucleus chemically modified | Ampicillin/Amoxicillin (from 6-APA), Cephalexin (from 7-ACA), Ethinyl-oestradiol, Homatropine, Diacetylmorphine (heroin), Bromoscopolamine, Dihydroergotamine |
| **Biotechnological / r-DNA** | Genetically engineered | Human insulin, Human growth hormone, Interferons, Erythropoietin, Hepatitis-B vaccine, Streptokinase, tPA |

🧠 **MEMORY HOOK for natural sources — "PAM-MM"** → **P**lant, **A**nimal, **M**ineral,
**M**icrobial, **M**arine.

⚠️ **TRAP** — *Liquid paraffin, hard paraffin, soft paraffin (petrolatum) and paraffin wax are of
MINERAL (petroleum) origin — not animal, not vegetable.* Whereas **wool fat (lanolin), beeswax,
spermaceti and cod liver oil are ANIMAL.** **Carnauba, candelilla and cocoa butter are VEGETABLE.**

⭐ **EXAM POINT — Organised vs Unorganised**

| Feature | Organised drug | Unorganised drug |
|---|---|---|
| Cellular structure | **Present** | **Absent** |
| Identification | By **microscopy** (histology) | By **physical & chemical** tests |
| Nature | Direct part of plant/animal | Product/exudate/extractive |
| Examples | Senna leaf, Cinchona bark | Acacia gum, Opium, Beeswax |

🧠 **MEMORY HOOK** — "**Organ**ised = you can see the **organ**/cells."

⭐ Other legal "kinds of drugs":
* **New drug** — a drug not used in the country to any significant extent, or a new claim/route/
  dosage; treated as new for **4 years** from approval.
* **Spurious drug** — imitates or is a substitute for another drug, or bears a false label of
  another manufacturer (fraudulent).
* **Misbranded drug** — coloured/coated/powdered so as to conceal damage, or false/misleading label.
* **Adulterated drug** — contains filthy/putrid substance, unhygienically prepared, harmful colour
  or container.
* **Patent & proprietary medicine** — formulation of a licensed manufacturer under a brand name.
* **Bulk drug / API** — the active pharmaceutical ingredient in bulk.
* **Generic drug** — marketed under its non-proprietary (official) name.
* **Orphan drug** — for a rare disease, commercially unattractive.
* **Essential drug** — WHO/ National List of Essential Medicines (NLEM), satisfies priority
  healthcare needs.
* **OTC drug** — sold without prescription.
* **Habit-forming drug** — listed in Rule 76 (e.g. barbiturates, codeine).
* **Prohibited/banned FDC** — fixed-dose combinations banned under Sec. 26A.

## 1.3 CHARACTERISTICS OF DRUGS

📌 **DEFINITION** — The *characteristics* of a drug are the sum of its **organoleptic,
morphological, physical, chemical and biological properties**, which together are used to
**identify** the drug, **judge its quality**, and **detect adulteration**.

🧠 **MEMORY HOOK for the 5 characteristics/evaluation heads — "MOPCB"**
**M**orphological → **O**rganoleptic → **P**hysical → **C**hemical → **B**iological.
(Often taught as: Organoleptic/Morphological, Microscopical, Physical, Chemical, Biological.)

### A. ORGANOLEPTIC CHARACTERS (sensory — "evaluation by sense organs")

| Character | What is noted | Illustrative examples |
|---|---|---|
| **Colour** | Natural colour of surface & fracture | Turmeric — yellow; Saffron — orange-red; Gentian violet — violet; Potassium permanganate — dark purple; Ferrous sulphate — pale bluish-green; Copper sulphate — blue; Sulphur — yellow; Calamine — pink; Iodine — greyish-black lustrous |
| **Odour** | Aromatic, terebinthinate, alliaceous, balsamic, odourless | Asafoetida — strong **alliaceous** (garlic-like); Clove — aromatic; Turpentine — terebinthinate; Peru & Tolu balsam — balsamic vanilla-like; Valerian — unpleasant/ disagreeable |
| **Taste** | Bitter, sweet, saline, acidic, acrid, astringent, mucilaginous, tasteless | Quinine/Gentian/Chirata — intensely **bitter**; Liquorice — **sweet**; Alum & Catechu — **astringent**; Magnesium sulphate — **saline bitter**; Sodium chloride — saline; Capsicum & Ginger — **pungent/acrid**; Acacia — **mucilaginous**; Starch — tasteless |
| **Texture / Touch** | Smooth, rough, gritty, oily, greasy, unctuous | Talc — smooth soapy; Kaolin — earthy, adheres to tongue; Liquid paraffin — oily; Wool fat — unctuous |
| **Sound / Fracture** | Snap, crack, brittle, fibrous, horny, splintery | Cinchona bark — **fibrous** fracture; Cinnamon — **short**; Quillaia — **splintery**; Liquorice — coarsely fibrous; Gum acacia — brittle, glassy |
| **Appearance/shape** | Quilled, channelled, curved, recurved, compound, single | Cinnamon — **quilled/compound quill**; Cinchona — quills/channelled; Senna — lanceolate leaflets with **asymmetric base** |

⭐ **EXAM POINT** — Organoleptic evaluation is also called **evaluation by "organs of sense"**;
it is the **simplest, quickest, least precise** method, and always the **first** step.

### B. MORPHOLOGICAL / MICROSCOPICAL CHARACTERS

| Feature | Significance |
|---|---|
| **Size & shape** | Measured with stage micrometer; e.g. starch grain size distinguishes maize, wheat, potato, rice starch |
| **Stomata & stomatal index/number** | Anisocytic (Cruciferous — Datura, Belladonna), Paracytic (Rubiaceous — Senna, Coca), Diacytic (Caryophyllaceous — Mentha, Vasaka), Anomocytic (Ranunculaceous — Digitalis, Buchu) |
| **Trichomes** | Covering (non-glandular) vs glandular; uni/multicellular, stellate, T-shaped |
| **Calcium oxalate crystals** | Rosette (Senna, Cascara), prismatic, acicular/raphides (Squill, Ipecac), microsphenoidal, sandy |
| **Vein-islet number / veinlet termination number / palisade ratio** | **Leaf constants** — used to identify entire & powdered leaf drugs |
| **Lignified elements** | Fibres, tracheids, vessels, sclereids, stone cells |
| **Starch, aleurone grains, oil globules, inulin** | Cell inclusions |

🔢 **NUMBERS TO MEMORISE (leaf constants — classical values)**

| Drug | Palisade ratio | Stomatal index | Vein-islet no. |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Senna (Alexandrian)** | 5.5–15.0 | ~10–15 | ~25 |
| **Digitalis purpurea** | 3.7–4.2 (–5.4) | 2.5–4.5 | 2–5 |
| **Belladonna** | 6–10 | ~20 (upper 2–5) | ~2–7 |

⭐ **Palisade ratio** = average number of palisade cells beneath **one** upper epidermal cell.
⭐ **Stomatal index** = [S/(E+S)] × 100, where S = number of stomata, E = number of epidermal cells.
⭐ **Vein-islet number** = number of vein-islets per **mm²** in the central part of the lamina.
⭐ **Veinlet termination number** = number of veinlet terminations per mm².

### C. PHYSICAL CHARACTERISTICS (physical constants)

| Constant | Meaning / use | Typical exam values |
|---|---|---|
| **Solubility** | Nature & extent of dissolution in solvents | Volatile oils — soluble in alcohol; Gums — water-soluble, alcohol-**insoluble**; Resins — alcohol-soluble, water-**insoluble**; Balsams — partly both |
| **Specific gravity (relative density)** | Mass of substance / mass of equal volume of water at same temp | Water = 1.000; Alcohol (ethanol) ≈ 0.79; Glycerin ≈ 1.26; Chloroform ≈ 1.47–1.48; Liquid paraffin 0.83–0.89; Castor oil 0.945–0.965; Clove oil ≈ 1.04–1.06 (**heavier than water**); Turpentine oil ≈ 0.86 |
| **Melting point** | Sharp for pure chemicals | Cocoa butter (theobroma oil) **30–35 °C** (m.p. below body temp — key for suppositories); Beeswax 62–65 °C; Hard paraffin 50–57 °C; White soft paraffin (melting range) 38–56 °C; Wool fat 38–44 °C |
| **Boiling point** | For liquids | Water 100 °C; Alcohol 78.4 °C; Ether 34.6 °C; Chloroform 61 °C |
| **Optical rotation** | Rotation of plane-polarised light (polarimeter); +dextro / –laevo | Peppermint oil **laevo**-rotatory; Turpentine oil dextro; Quinine laevo; Sucrose dextro (+66.5°) |
| **Refractive index** | Ratio of velocity of light in air to that in the substance (Abbé refractometer) | Water 1.333; Volatile oils 1.45–1.55; Fixed oils ~1.46–1.48 |
| **Viscosity** | Resistance to flow | Glycerin, liquid paraffin high; controls pourability of mixtures |
| **Moisture content (LOD)** | Loss on drying — checks over-hydration & mould risk | Usually **< 5 %**; crude drugs must be dried to < 8–14 % |
| **Ash values** | **Total ash** (all inorganic), **acid-insoluble ash** (silica/sand → detects earthy adulteration), **water-soluble ash**, **sulphated ash** | Acid-insoluble ash of Ginger NMT 2 %; Senna total ash NMT 12 % |
| **Extractive values** | Alcohol-soluble, water-soluble, ether-soluble extractives — measure of active constituents | Low value = exhausted/adulterated drug |
| **Volatile oil content** | By Clevenger apparatus / hydro-distillation | Clove NLT 15 % v/w; Cinnamon bark ~1 % |
| **Swelling index** | Volume in mL occupied by 1 g of drug after swelling in water for 4 h | **Isapghula NLT 40**; Linseed ~4; Agar & Tragacanth high |
| **Foaming index** | Froth height — for saponin-containing drugs | Quillaia, Liquorice, Senega |
| **Bitterness value** | Reciprocal of the dilution still bitter, expressed in units per g relative to quinine HCl | Gentian ≥ 10 000; Quassia ≥ 3 500 |
| **Haemolytic index** | Saponin content measured by RBC lysis relative to standard Senega | Saponin drugs |
| **Iodine value** | g of iodine absorbed by 100 g fat → **degree of unsaturation** | Linseed oil very high (drying oil), Castor 82–90, Coconut low (~7–10) |
| **Acid value** | mg KOH needed to neutralise free fatty acids in 1 g → **rancidity indicator** | Should be low & is limited in pharmacopoeias |
| **Saponification value** | mg KOH needed to saponify 1 g fat → **inverse of molecular weight** of glycerides | Castor 176–187; Coconut 250–264 |
| **Ester value** | Sap. value – Acid value | For oils & waxes |
| **Acetyl value** | mg KOH to neutralise acetic acid from 1 g acetylated fat → hydroxyl groups | **Castor oil high** (ricinoleic –OH) |
| **Unsaponifiable matter** | Fraction not saponified | Liquid paraffin is fully unsaponifiable |
| **Fluorescence** | Under UV | Quinine — blue fluorescence in acid; Ergot & Aloe show characteristic fluorescence |
| **Deliquescence** | Absorbs so much atmospheric moisture that it **liquefies** | Calcium chloride, Zinc chloride, Potassium/Sodium hydroxide, Ferric chloride, Ammonium nitrate, Pepsin, Trypsin |
| **Efflorescence** | **Loses** water of crystallisation to air and becomes powdery | Sodium carbonate (Na₂CO₃·10H₂O), Ferrous sulphate, Magnesium sulphate, Sodium sulphate (Glauber's salt), Alum, Atropine sulphate, Codeine phosphate, Citric acid |
| **Hygroscopicity** | Absorbs moisture without liquefying | Glycerin, Sorbitol, PEG, Ammonium chloride |
| **Sublimation** | Solid → vapour → solid on heating, without melting | **Camphor, Menthol, Thymol, Iodine, Naphthalene, Benzoic acid, Salicylic acid, Ammonium chloride, Sulphur (partly)** |
| **Volatility** | Evaporates at room temperature | Alcohol, ether, chloroform, volatile oils, camphor |

⚠️ **TRAP — Efflorescent vs Deliquescent vs Hygroscopic**
* **Efflorescent** → **gives out** water → becomes **powdery** (Na₂CO₃·10H₂O).
* **Deliquescent** → **takes in** water → becomes **liquid** (CaCl₂).
* **Hygroscopic** → takes in moisture but **stays solid/semi-solid** (glycerin, NH₄Cl).
🧠 "Efflor**escent** = water **exits**; **D**eliquescent = **D**issolves itself."

⚠️ **TRAP — Volatile vs Fixed oil**

| Feature | Volatile (essential) oil | Fixed oil / fat |
|---|---|---|
| Volatility | Volatile, evaporates | Non-volatile |
| Grease stain on paper | **Temporary** (disappears on warming) | **Permanent** |
| Steam distillation | **Possible** | Not possible |
| Saponification | Not saponified (generally) | **Saponified** by alkali |
| Rancidity | Resinify/oxidise | **Become rancid** |
| Chemistry | Terpenes, sesquiterpenes, phenols, aldehydes | Glycerides of fatty acids |
| Odour | Strongly odorous | Nearly odourless |
| Examples | Clove, peppermint, eucalyptus, turpentine | Castor, olive, arachis, cod-liver |

### D. CHEMICAL CHARACTERISTICS

* **Nature of the active constituent** — alkaloid, glycoside, tannin, resin, volatile oil, etc.
* **Assay** — quantitative determination of active principle: acid–base, non-aqueous, complexometric,
  iodimetric, gravimetric, colorimetric/spectrophotometric, chromatographic (HPLC/GC).
* **Limit tests** — for chloride, sulphate, iron, arsenic, heavy metals, lead.
* **Identification tests** — colour reactions (see Ch. 2 & Ch. 13).
* **pH / acidity–alkalinity**, **loss on ignition**, **water content (Karl Fischer)**.
* **Chemical incompatibility potential** (see Ch. 8).

### E. BIOLOGICAL CHARACTERISTICS (Bioassay)

📌 **Bioassay** = estimation of the **potency** of a drug by observing its effect on **living
matter** (animal, isolated tissue, micro-organism), compared with a **standard preparation**.

Used when chemical assay is impossible/unreliable:
* **Digitalis** — pigeon/guinea-pig, cat method (emesis in pigeon; cardiac arrest).
* **Insulin** — rabbit blood-sugar method / mouse convulsion.
* **Oxytocin** — isolated rat uterus, fowl blood pressure.
* **Adrenaline** — rabbit intestine / cat blood pressure.
* **Histamine** — guinea-pig ileum.
* **Vitamin D** — rat line test (antirachitic).
* **Antibiotics** — cup-plate / turbidimetric microbial assay.
* **Vaccines & sera** — LD₅₀, ED₅₀ methods; **pyrogen test (rabbit)**; **sterility test**.
* **Toxicity test** — LD₅₀ (Litchfield & Wilcoxon; Reed–Muench).
* **Types of bioassay** — Qualitative vs Quantitative; **Direct** (Digitalis in cat) vs
  **Indirect**; Graded response; All-or-none; **Matching**; **Bracketing**; **Interpolation**;
  **Multiple-point (2-, 3-, 4-point)**.

## 1.4 EVALUATION OF DRUGS (Standardisation)

📌 **DEFINITION** — Evaluation of a drug means confirming its **identity**, determining its
**quality & purity**, and **detecting the nature of adulteration**.

⭐ **Five methods (learn the order):**
1. **Organoleptic / Morphological** — sensory, macroscopic.
2. **Microscopical** — histology, cell contents, leaf constants, powder analysis.
3. **Physical** — solubility, m.p., ash, extractive, moisture, refractive index, optical rotation,
   swelling & foaming index, viscosity, specific gravity.
4. **Chemical** — qualitative tests, assays, limit tests.
5. **Biological** — bioassay, toxicity, antimicrobial activity, pyrogen & sterility test.

🧠 **MEMORY HOOK — "O-M-P-C-B"** ("**O**ld **M**an **P**icks **C**rude **B**ark").

### Adulteration & Substitution

📌 **Adulteration** = intentional or unintentional substitution of a drug wholly or partly with an
inferior, spurious, defective or harmful substance.

| Type of adulteration | Description | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Substitution with substandard commercial variety** | Similar-looking cheaper drug | Indian senna for Alexandrian senna; Japanese mint for peppermint |
| **Substitution with exhausted drug** | Active constituent already extracted | Exhausted ginger, clove (oil removed), gentian, fennel |
| **Substitution with artificially manufactured substance** | Synthetic look-alike | Paraffin wax blocks coloured as beeswax; artificial invert sugar for honey |
| **Substitution with inferior/foreign organic matter** | Other plant parts or unrelated matter | Senna stalks & pods with leaves; ergot-free rye; papaya seeds for pepper |
| **Harmful adulterants** | Waste/harmful material | Limestone, white oil, glass pieces, iron filings, mud |
| **Adulteration of powders** | Cheap powdered filler | Powdered bark/dextrin/starch in powdered drugs; brick powder in chilli/turmeric |
| **Addition of inseparable matter (sophistication)** | Cannot be removed by sifting | Water sprinkling to increase weight; sand & stone in gums |
| **Vitiation (deterioration)** | Loss of quality by age, moisture, heat, light, insects | Rancid oils, mouldy drugs, faded flowers |
| **Inferiority** | Genuine but of poor quality | Under-mature or wrongly collected drug |
| **Spoilage** | Microbial/insect attack | Weevils in cereal drugs |

🧪 **Common bazaar adulteration detection (frequently asked)**
* **Turmeric powder** + metanil yellow → add conc. HCl to aqueous extract → **violet/pink** colour.
* **Chilli powder** + brick powder/sand → shake in water → grit settles; red colour separates.
* **Asafoetida** adulterated with starch/soapstone → **iodine gives blue** (starch);
  ash residue high.
* **Honey** adulterated with sugar syrup → **Fiehe's test** (resorcinol + HCl → cherry-red for
  invert sugar/HMF).
* **Beeswax** with paraffin → melting point & saponification value change.
* **Silver leaf (chandi ka warq)** with aluminium → aluminium burns into grey-black ash ball.
* **Coconut oil** with other oils → freeze test / cool below 24 °C.
* **Milk** with starch → iodine blue; with water → lactometer.
* **Ghee/butter** with vanaspati → **Baudouin test** (furfural + HCl → **pink/crimson**, detects
  sesame oil marker).
* **Mustard oil** with argemone oil → **nitric acid test** (brownish-red).

## 1.5 DETERIORATION, STORAGE & SHELF-LIFE OF DRUGS

### Causes of deterioration

| Factor | Effect | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Air (O₂)** | Oxidation | Vitamin C, adrenaline, fixed oils turning rancid, ferrous salts → ferric |
| **Moisture / humidity** | Hydrolysis, caking, mould, effervescence | Aspirin → salicylic acid + acetic acid; effervescent powders; ampicillin |
| **Light** | Photolysis, discolouration | Silver salts, chlorpromazine, riboflavin, phenothiazines, sodium nitroprusside |
| **Heat** | Increased reaction rate, melting, loss of volatiles | Suppositories melt, vitamins degrade, insulin denatures |
| **Micro-organisms** | Spoilage, fermentation | Syrups, mucilages, infusions (short shelf-life!) |
| **Insects & rodents** | Physical destruction, contamination | Crude vegetable drugs |
| **Enzymes** | Autolysis, hydrolysis of glycosides | Digitalis leaf must be dried below 60 °C to kill enzymes |
| **Trace metals** | Catalyse oxidation | Ascorbic acid + Cu²⁺/Fe³⁺ |
| **pH** | Acid/base catalysed hydrolysis | Penicillin unstable in acid; procaine hydrolyses |
| **Time / age** | Loss of potency | Fixed shelf life (Schedule P) |

### Signs of a deteriorated drug ("recognition of spoilage")
* Change in **colour** (faded/darkened), **odour** (rancid, sour, mouldy), **taste**.
* **Caking, clumping, mottling, softening, liquefaction** of solids.
* **Cloudiness, precipitate, sediment, gas, cracking of emulsion**, mould growth in liquids.
* **Loss of effervescence**; **cracked/leaking/swollen** containers; **crystal growth**.
* Ointments: **separation of liquid (bleeding)**, rancid smell, granular feel.

### General storage rules (pharmacopoeial terminology)

| Term | Temperature |
|---|---|
| **Deep freezer** | –20 °C to –10 °C (below –18 °C) |
| **Cold / refrigerator** | **2 °C – 8 °C** |
| **Cool place** | **8 °C – 25 °C** |
| **Room temperature** | 15 °C – 30 °C (IP: 15–25 °C) |
| **Warm** | 30 °C – 40 °C |
| **Excessive heat** | above 40 °C |

🔢 **NUMBER TO MEMORISE** — **Cold storage = 2–8 °C** (vaccines, insulin, sera, ergometrine,
oxytocin, suppositories, most eye drops after opening).

⭐ **Storage practice**
* **Light-sensitive** → **amber/ dark coloured, light-resistant** container.
* **Moisture-sensitive/deliquescent** → **well-closed, air-tight** container, in a dry place;
  add **desiccant** (silica gel).
* **Volatile & sublimable** (camphor, iodine, ether, spirit) → **tightly/hermetically closed**,
  cool place, away from flame.
* **Inflammable** (ether, alcohol, collodion) → cool, away from fire, separate flame-proof store.
* **Oxidising agents** (KMnO₄, chlorates, peroxides) stored **away from reducing/organic matter**
  (fire hazard).
* **Poisons & Schedule X** → separate **locked** cupboard marked "POISON", register maintained.
* **Narcotics** → double-locked safe.
* **Vegetable crude drugs** → dry, cool, dark, insect-proof (fumigation with e.g. carbon
  tetrachloride/ chloroform classically; today gamma irradiation/ ETO).
* **Antibiotics, biologicals, vaccines** → cold chain, never frozen (except as directed).
* **Rubber/plastic-incompatible** drugs → glass only.

📌 **Shelf-life (expiry date)** — the time during which a drug retains **not less than 90 % (or
95 % for some) of its labelled potency** under the stated storage conditions. In India, expiry is
governed by **Schedule P**.

⭐ **Preparations with very short shelf-life (extemporaneous)**
* **Infusions — 12 hours** (fresh); concentrated infusions keep longer.
* **Mucilages, decoctions — 24 hours to a few days** (require preservative).
* **Emulsions & suspensions (unpreserved)** — a few days.
* **Aqueous extemporaneous mixtures** — usually labelled to be used within **7–14 days**.

## 1.7 CHAPTER 1 — RAPID REVISION SHEET

* Drug ← French **"Drogue"** = dry herb.
* Sources: **P**lant, **A**nimal, **M**ineral, **M**icrobial, **M**arine, Synthetic,
  Semi-synthetic, Biotechnological.
* **Organised** drug = has cells (identified microscopically); **Unorganised** = no cells
  (identified by physical/chemical tests).
* **Antiseptic** → living tissue; **Disinfectant** → inanimate object.
* **Mydriatic** dilates; **Miotic** constricts.
* **Emollient** = external softener; **Demulcent** = internal soother.
* **Vermifuge** expels; **Vermicide** kills.
* **Carminative** relieves flatulence; **Refrigerant** cools and quenches thirst.
* **Efflorescent** loses water (Na₂CO₃·10H₂O, FeSO₄, MgSO₄, alum, citric acid);
  **Deliquescent** absorbs water & liquefies (CaCl₂, ZnCl₂, NaOH, KOH, FeCl₃).
* **Sublimable**: camphor, menthol, thymol, iodine, benzoic acid, salicylic acid, NH₄Cl,
  naphthalene.
* **Volatile oil** → temporary grease stain, steam-distillable; **Fixed oil** → permanent stain,
  saponifiable, becomes rancid.
* Evaluation = **O**rganoleptic, **M**icroscopic, **P**hysical, **C**hemical, **B**iological.
* **Acid-insoluble ash** detects **sand/earthy adulteration**.
* **Swelling index of Isapghula ≥ 40**.
* **Iodine value** → unsaturation; **Acid value** → rancidity; **Saponification value** → inverse
  of molecular weight; **Acetyl value high** → **castor oil**.
* **Cold storage = 2–8 °C**; **cool place = 8–25 °C**.
* **Infusion shelf-life = 12 hours**.
* **Schedule H** = prescription drug, **X** = narcotic (retain copy 2 years), **N** = minimum
  pharmacy equipment, **M** = GMP, **P** = shelf-life, **Y** = clinical trials.
* IP **1955**; NFI **1960**; *International Pharmacopoeia* by **WHO** (not legally enforceable).
* **Bioassay** = potency measured on living tissue against a standard.
