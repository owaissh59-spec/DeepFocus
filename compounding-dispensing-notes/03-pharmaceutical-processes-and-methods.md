## 3.1 WHAT THE SYLLABUS MEANS HERE

📌 **PHARMACEUTICAL PROCESS** — any physical or chemical operation carried out on a drug or
excipient in order to convert it into a **finished, stable, elegant and accurately dosed
medicinal product**.

📌 **UNIT OPERATION** — a **basic physical step** that is common to many manufacturing processes
(e.g. size reduction, mixing, drying). "Unit operation" = *physical* change;
"**unit process**" = *chemical* change (nitration, esterification, oxidation).

⭐ **THE MASTER LIST — memorise this skeleton first, then fill in details**

| # | Unit operation | Purpose in one line |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Size reduction (comminution)** | Make particles smaller |
| 2 | **Size separation (sieving/ classification)** | Sort particles by size |
| 3 | **Mixing (blending)** | Make a uniform mass |
| 4 | **Drying (desiccation)** | Remove moisture |
| 5 | **Evaporation** | Concentrate by removing solvent as vapour |
| 6 | **Distillation** | Separate & purify liquids by boiling + condensing |
| 7 | **Sublimation** | Solid → vapour → solid |
| 8 | **Extraction** | Remove desired constituent with a solvent (menstruum) |
| 9 | **Filtration & clarification** | Separate solids from liquids |
| 10 | **Centrifugation** | Separate by centrifugal force |
| 11 | **Crystallisation** | Obtain pure solid from solution |
| 12 | **Sterilisation** | Destroy all micro-organisms |
| 13 | **Heat transfer** | Move heat (conduction/ convection/ radiation) |
| 14 | **Humidification/ dehumidification & air conditioning** | Control moisture in air |
| 15 | **Emulsification & suspension** | Disperse one phase in another |
| 16 | **Compression/ granulation/ coating** | Tablet manufacture steps |

🧠 **MEMORY HOOK — "Small Sorted Mixtures Dry, Evaporate, Distil, Sublime, Extract, Filter,
Centrifuge, Crystallise, Sterilise."**

## 3.2 SIZE REDUCTION (COMMINUTION)

📌 **DEFINITION** — Size reduction, also called **comminution, grinding, milling, pulverisation
or trituration**, is the mechanical process of **breaking a substance into smaller particles**
without altering its chemical nature.

### ⭐ Objectives / advantages of size reduction

1. **Increases surface area** → faster **dissolution** & better **bioavailability**
   (e.g. griseofulvin, spironolactone).
2. Increases **rate of extraction** of active constituents from crude drugs.
3. Improves **mixing/ blending uniformity** of powders.
4. Improves **drying rate** (larger surface).
5. Permits **accurate dosing** of potent drugs (uniform small particles).
6. Improves **flow, texture & elegance** (dusting powders, ointments, suspensions).
7. Reduces **sedimentation rate** in suspensions (Stokes' law → smaller r, slower settling).
8. Aids **absorption** & smoothness of topical products (no grittiness).

### ⚠️ Disadvantages / limitations
* **Heat generation** → degrades thermolabile drugs (waxes, gums, vitamins).
* **Contamination** from mill wear (metal).
* **Loss of drug** as dust; **dust hazard/ explosion** (e.g. starch, sulphur).
* Excessive fines → **poor flow, caking, aggregation**.
* Change in **polymorphic form/ crystallinity**.
* Segregation & static charge.

### Mechanisms (forces) of size reduction ⭐

| Force | Mechanism | Mill using it |
|---|---|---|
| **Impact (hammering)** | Sharp instantaneous blow | **Hammer mill**, disintegrator |
| **Compression (crushing)** | Slow steady pressure | **End-runner mill, Edge-runner mill**, roller mill |
| **Attrition (rubbing/ shearing)** | Rubbing between surfaces | **Colloid mill, roller mill, disc/ attrition mill** |
| **Cutting** | Sharp blade | **Cutter mill**, guillotine |
| **Impact + Attrition** | Both together | **Ball mill, fluid energy mill** |

🧠 "**I-C-A-C**": **I**mpact, **C**ompression, **A**ttrition, **C**utting.

### Factors affecting size reduction
* **Hardness** (Mohs' scale) & **abrasiveness**.
* **Toughness/ elasticity/ friability** — tough materials resist; brittle break easily.
* **Fibrous nature** (needs cutting, not impact).
* **Moisture content** — ⭐ dry grinding needs **< 5 % moisture**; wet grinding **> 50 %**.
  Intermediate moisture causes **caking/ balling**.
* **Softening/ melting point** (heat-sensitive → cool, or use cryogenic grinding with
  **liquid nitrogen/ dry ice**).
* **Stickiness, hygroscopicity, thermolability, purity required**.
* **Bulk density** & feed rate; **ratio of feed size to product size**.

### 🔢 Laws of size reduction (energy laws)

| Law | Statement | Formula (E = energy) |
|---|---|---|
| **Rittinger's law** | Energy required ∝ **new surface area produced** — best for **fine grinding** | E = K_R (1/d₂ − 1/d₁) |
| **Kick's law** | Energy required ∝ **log of size reduction ratio** — best for **coarse grinding** | E = K_K log(d₁/d₂) |
| **Bond's law** | Work is proportional to the **square root of the surface : volume ratio** — intermediate; introduces **Work Index** | E = 2K_B (1/√d₂ − 1/√d₁) |

🧠 **"Rittinger = fine (Ritt for Really tiny), Kick = coarse, Bond = in Between."**

### 📋 MILLS — the exam table

| Mill | Principle/ force | Feed & product size | Suitable for | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Mortar & Pestle** | Attrition + compression (hand trituration) | — | Small-scale dispensing | Glass, porcelain, Wedgwood (rough — best for grinding), metal |
| **Cutter mill** | **Cutting** by rotating knives | Coarse | **Fibrous, tough, dry & crude vegetable drugs**, roots, barks | Screen controls size |
| **Roller mill / Triple roller mill** | **Compression + attrition** between rollers | — | **Ointments, pastes, creams, cocoa, colours** | Reduces grittiness |
| **Hammer mill** | **Impact** (high-speed swinging hammers) | 5–10 mm feed → 20–300 mesh | **Almost any material — brittle, crystalline, fibrous, sticky (with cooling)** | Very versatile; screen size sets product size; high heat |
| **Ball mill** | **Impact + Attrition** — balls tumble in a rotating cylinder | Coarse → **very fine (as low as 10 µm)** | Hard, abrasive, brittle drugs; **can be used for wet & dry, sterile & closed grinding** | ⭐ **Critical speed** = speed at which the balls are held to the wall by centrifugal force → **no grinding**. Optimum = **50–80 % (≈ 2/3) of critical speed**. Balls 30–50 % of mill volume |
| **Fluid energy mill (micronizer/ jet mill)** | **Impact + attrition** by high-velocity air/ inert gas stream; particles collide with each other | → **1–20 µm (micronising)** | **Thermolabile & very fine powders; sterile & aseptic milling; antibiotics, corticosteroids** | ⭐ **No moving parts → no heat, no contamination**; expansion of gas cools the chamber |
| **Colloid mill** | **Shear/ attrition** between a fixed stator & high-speed rotor | → colloidal size | **Suspensions, emulsions, ointments (wet milling)** | For liquids/ slurries only |
| **End-runner mill** | **Compression + shear** (steel pestle in revolving mortar) | Moderate | Crystalline & brittle drugs; small-scale | "Mechanical mortar & pestle" |
| **Edge-runner mill** | **Compression + shear** (heavy stone/ metal wheels revolving on a bed) | Moderately fine | **Tough, fibrous & crystalline drugs; sticky masses** | Two heavy rollers |
| **Disintegrator/ Attrition (Disc) mill** | Attrition between two grooved discs | Fine | Cereals, dry drugs | |
| **Pin/ Universal mill** | Impact & attrition between pins | Fine | Versatile | |
| **Vibration mill** | Impact & attrition, vibrating balls | Very fine | Hard materials | 60–80 % filled |
| **Cryogenic grinding** | Brittleness induced with **liquid N₂ / dry ice** | Fine | **Thermolabile, waxy, gummy, volatile-oil-rich drugs** | Preserves volatile oil |

⭐ **HIGH-YIELD ONE-LINERS**
* **Best mill for a thermolabile drug / for micronising / for sterile products → Fluid energy
  mill.**
* **Best mill for fibrous crude vegetable drugs → Cutter mill.**
* **Mill that can grind under closed & sterile conditions and produce colloidal fineness →
  Ball mill.**
* **Mill for ointments & pastes → Triple roller mill / Colloid mill.**
* **Most versatile/ general purpose mill → Hammer mill.**
* **Critical speed formula:** `Nc = 1 / (2π) × √(g / (R − r))` (revolutions per second),
  where R = radius of mill, r = radius of ball.

### Classical (dispensing-bench) size-reduction terms ⭐⭐

| Term | 📌 Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Trituration** | Continuous rubbing/ grinding of a solid in a mortar with a pestle to reduce its size **or** to mix powders |
| **Levigation** | Reducing particle size by grinding a solid **with a small amount of a liquid (levigating agent)** in which it is insoluble, to make a smooth paste — e.g. zinc oxide + liquid paraffin/ glycerin for ointments |
| **Pulverisation by intervention** | Size reduction with the help of an **additional volatile substance that is later removed** — e.g. **camphor + a few drops of alcohol/ ether** ("spirit of wine"), **iodine + ether**; also making a fine powder of a gummy substance |
| **Elutriation** | Separating fine from coarse particles by suspending in a stream of **water or air**, fine ones being carried over |
| **Comminution** | The general term for size reduction |
| **Granulation** | Building **small particles into larger free-flowing granules** (wet or dry) |
| **Spatulation** | Mixing small quantities of powders on a **tile/ slab with a spatula** — used for **eutectic/ liquefiable mixtures** (no pressure, no trituration) |
| **Sifting/ Bolting** | Passing powders through a **sieve or bolting cloth** to mix & separate |
| **Tumbling** | Mixing powders by rotating them in a closed container |
| **Geometric dilution** | ⭐ Method of mixing a **small quantity of a potent drug** with a large quantity of diluent by **doubling up** at each stage |

## 3.3 SIZE SEPARATION (SIEVING, CLASSIFICATION)

📌 **DEFINITION** — Size separation (also **classification, screening, sifting, grading**) is the
unit operation in which **particles of a mixture are separated into two or more fractions
according to their size**.

### Objectives
* To obtain a powder of the **official degree of fineness**.
* To ensure **uniform particle size** → uniform dosage, good flow & mixing.
* To **remove oversize/ fines** & recycle them.
* To determine **particle size distribution** (sieve analysis).

### ⭐ Methods of size separation

| Method | Principle | Examples/ equipment |
|---|---|---|
| **Sieving (screening)** | Particles pass through mesh apertures by **gravity/ agitation** | Standard sieves, vibratory sieve shaker, oscillating/ reciprocating sieve, rotary/ trommel sieve, **agitation sieve, brushing sieve, centrifugal sieve** |
| **Sedimentation (in liquid)** | Different **settling velocities (Stokes' law)** | Andreasen pipette, elutriation tank |
| **Elutriation** | **Upward** flow of fluid carries fines away | Air/ water elutriator |
| **Air separation (cyclone/ classifier)** | Centrifugal + gravitational force in an air stream | **Cyclone separator**, double-cone classifier, air separator |
| **Magnetic separation** | Removes iron contamination | Magnetic separators |
| **Flotation** | Difference in wettability/ density | Froth flotation |

🔢 **Stokes' Law (used in sedimentation & suspension stability)**
```
v = d² (ρs − ρl) g / 18 η
```
v = sedimentation velocity, d = particle diameter, ρs = density of solid, ρl = density of liquid,
g = gravity, η = viscosity.
⭐ Sedimentation is **slowed** by ↓ particle size, ↑ viscosity, ↓ density difference.

⭐ **Higher sieve number → smaller aperture → finer powder.**

### Standards of sieves & sieve analysis
* Sieves are made of **wire cloth (brass, bronze, stainless steel)** woven, not twilled;
  IP specifies aperture size, **wire diameter, approximate screening area & tolerances**.
* **Sieve analysis** — a **nest/ stack of sieves** arranged with the **coarsest on top** and a
  **collecting pan at the bottom**; shaken for a fixed time (e.g. 20 min); the retained weight on
  each sieve gives the **particle size distribution**.
* Results expressed as **cumulative % oversize/ undersize** curves; **arithmetic & geometric
  mean diameter**.
* Sieving of **hygroscopic** powders is done in a **dry (dehumidified) room**;
  **air-jet sieving** is used for very fine powders.

## 3.4 MIXING (BLENDING)

📌 **DEFINITION** — **Mixing** is the process in which **two or more ingredients in a separate or
roughly mixed condition are treated so that each particle of one lies as nearly as possible in
contact with a particle of the other**.

📌 **Positive mixture** — components mix spontaneously & irreversibly (miscible liquids, gases);
no energy needed.
📌 **Negative mixture** — components tend to separate (suspensions, emulsions); needs continuous
energy input.
📌 **Neutral mixture** — components neither mix spontaneously nor separate (pastes, ointments,
mixed powders).

### Objectives of mixing
Uniform distribution of active drug (content uniformity), initiation of physical/ chemical
reactions, dispersion of a phase (emulsification/ suspension), promotion of heat & mass transfer,
and elegant appearance.

### Mechanisms of mixing (solids)
1. **Convective mixing** — transfer of **groups of particles** to another location (ribbon
   blender).
2. **Shear mixing** — slipping of **layers** past one another.
3. **Diffusive mixing** — random motion of **individual particles** (tumbling mixers).

⚠️ **Segregation (demixing)** occurs when particles differ in **size, shape or density**;
**percolation** of fines and **trajectory segregation** are common causes.

### 📋 Mixing equipment

| Class | Equipment | Best for |
|---|---|---|
| **Solid–solid (powder) mixers** | **Double-cone blender, Twin-shell (V-cone) blender, Cube/ drum tumbler, Ribbon blender, Sigma blade mixer, Planetary mixer, Nauta (conical screw) mixer, Fluidised bed mixer** | Dry powders & granules |
| **Liquid mixers (low viscosity)** | **Propeller (axial), Turbine (radial), Paddle** agitators; air jet; **baffled tanks** to prevent vortex | Solutions, syrups, low-viscosity suspensions |
| **Semisolid mixers** | **Sigma blade (Z-blade), Planetary, Triple roller mill, Colloid mill** | Ointments, creams, pastes |
| **Homogenisers/ emulsifiers** | **Silverson mixer emulsifier, colloid mill, ultrasonic homogeniser, high-pressure (valve) homogeniser** | Emulsions & fine suspensions |
| **Small-scale dispensing** | **Mortar & pestle (trituration), spatula on tile (spatulation), sieving, tumbling in a bag/ bottle** | Extemporaneous powders |

⭐ **Rules for mixing powders on the dispensing bench**
1. Use a **mortar & pestle** for coarse or hard powders; a **tile & spatula** for eutectics.
2. Mix in **ascending order of bulk density**, i.e. begin with the powder of **smallest bulk**
   (never put the heavy powder in first — it stays at the bottom).
3. Always use **geometric dilution** for **potent (small-quantity) drugs**.
4. **Colour**ed/ dye-like ingredients are **sandwiched** between portions of the diluent so that
   the colour acts as an **indicator of complete mixing**.
5. Avoid **excessive pressure** on liquefiable/ eutectic mixtures and explosive mixtures.
6. **Sift** through a sieve (no. 44/60) after mixing to break aggregates, then remix.
7. Mix the **smallest quantity first** with an **equal quantity** of the other.

📌 **GEOMETRIC DILUTION (Doubling-up)** — worked example:
*Prepare 60 g of a powder containing 60 mg of a potent drug.*
Step 1: Triturate **60 mg drug + 60 mg diluent** = 120 mg.
Step 2: add **120 mg** diluent = 240 mg → Step 3: + 240 mg = 480 mg → continue doubling
(0.96 g → 1.92 g → 3.84 g → 7.68 g → 15.36 g → 30.72 g → 60 g).
⭐ The principle: **at every stage add a quantity of diluent equal to the quantity already in the
mortar.**

### Evaluation of mixing
* **Mixing index / index of mixing**, **coefficient of variation (CV)** of sampled potency.
* **Standard deviation** of drug content of random samples; the **lower** the SD, the better.
* Use of a **sampling thief** at different points & depths.
* **Scale of scrutiny** — the smallest amount of the mix that will be examined (for tablets, one
  tablet weight).

## 3.5 DRYING (DESICCATION)

📌 **DEFINITION** — **Drying** is the removal of **small amounts of a liquid (usually water)**
from a material by the **application of heat**, so that the liquid is removed as **vapour**.

⚠️ **TRAP — Drying vs Evaporation:**
* **Drying** removes a *small* amount of liquid → the **product is a SOLID**.
* **Evaporation** removes a *large* amount of liquid → the **product is a LIQUID (concentrate)**.

### Objectives of drying
Prevent hydrolysis & microbial growth (stability), reduce bulk & transport cost, improve flow of
granules, allow accurate weighing, make grinding possible, produce a definite crystalline/ powder
form, meet pharmacopoeial moisture limits.

### ⭐ Moisture terminology

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Bound moisture** | Water held in chemical/ physical combination — exerts **vapour pressure lower** than pure water; hard to remove |
| **Unbound (free) moisture** | Water in excess of equilibrium moisture content; exerts **full vapour pressure** |
| **Equilibrium Moisture Content (EMC)** | Moisture retained by the solid when in **equilibrium** with air of a given temp & humidity — **cannot be removed** at those conditions |
| **Free moisture** | Total moisture − EMC → **the moisture that CAN be removed** |
| **Critical Moisture Content (CMC)** | Moisture content at the point where the **constant-rate period ends** and the falling-rate period begins |
| **Loss on drying (LOD)** | % weight lost on drying, based on **wet weight** |
| **Moisture content (dry basis)** | Based on **dry weight** |

### ⭐ Drying rate curve — the three periods
```
Rate
 │        ┌──────────┐  ← B–C  CONSTANT RATE period
 │       /            \        (free surface water evaporates;
 │  A–B /              \        surface temp = wet-bulb temp)
 │ warm-up             \  C–D  FIRST FALLING RATE
 │                      \      (surface partially dry)
 │                       \ D–E SECOND FALLING RATE
 │                        \    (internal diffusion controls)
 └──────────────────────────── Moisture content →
                    ↑ CMC at point C
```
⭐ In the **constant-rate period**, drying is controlled by **external conditions (air temp,
humidity, velocity)**. In the **falling-rate period**, it is controlled by **internal movement
(diffusion) of moisture** within the solid.

### 📋 DRYERS — the exam table

| Dryer | Principle | Suitable for | Key exam point |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Tray (shelf/ compartment/ truck) dryer** | Hot air circulated over material on trays (convection) | Granules, crude drugs, powders, small batches | Simple & cheap; **long drying time (12–24 h), non-uniform**; labour-intensive |
| **Fluidised bed dryer (FBD)** | Hot air blown **upwards** through the bed → particles float ("fluidised") | ⭐ **Granules for tablets** | Very **rapid (20–40 min), uniform, thermal efficiency high**; risk of **attrition & static charge**; can also granulate & coat |
| **Vacuum dryer / Vacuum tray & rotary vacuum** | Drying **below atmospheric pressure** → lower boiling point | **Thermolabile, hygroscopic, oxidisable drugs; solvent recovery** | Low temperature, no oxidation; expensive |
| **Drum (film/ roller) dryer** | Thin film of liquid dried on a heated rotating drum, scraped off | Slurries, pastes, milk, yeast, starch | Continuous, contact drying |
| **Spray dryer** | Liquid **atomised into a hot air chamber** → droplets dry instantly into spherical particles | ⭐ **Thermolabile liquids: antibiotics, vaccines, enzymes, milk, blood products, spray-dried lactose** | ⭐ **Contact time is only a few seconds** → drug stays cool by evaporative cooling; gives free-flowing spherical granules |
| **Freeze dryer (Lyophiliser)** | ⭐ **Sublimation of ice** under high vacuum, below the **triple point** | ⭐ **Highly thermolabile & sterile products: vaccines, sera, antibiotics, plasma, enzymes, hormones, bacterial cultures, blood products** | Stages: **Freezing → Primary drying (sublimation) → Secondary drying (desorption)**. Product is **porous, rapidly & completely soluble**, moisture ~ 1 %. **Most expensive & slowest**, but preserves activity |
| **Microwave/ dielectric dryer** | Internal heating by microwaves (2450 MHz) | Granules, small batches | Rapid, uniform, no surface case-hardening |
| **Infrared/ radiant dryer** | Radiation from IR lamps | Thin layers, coatings | Surface drying |
| **Turbo/ rotary dryer** | Rotating shell with hot gas | Free-flowing granules, crystals | Continuous |
| **Desiccator (chemical drying)** | Moisture absorbed by a **desiccant** | Small quantities, crystals | Desiccants: **silica gel, calcium chloride, phosphorus pentoxide, conc. H₂SO₄, calcium oxide, alumina** |
| **Sun/ air drying (natural)** | Solar heat | Crude vegetable drugs | Cheap; risk of contamination & loss of volatile oil |

⭐ **DRYING OF CRUDE DRUGS — special rules**
* **Digitalis, Belladonna, Ergot** — dry rapidly **below 60 °C** to **destroy enzymes** and
  preserve glycosides.
* **Volatile-oil-containing drugs (clove, peppermint, cinnamon)** — dry in **shade at low
  temperature** to avoid loss of oil.
* **Gentian & Vanilla** — deliberately **slow-dried/ fermented** to develop constituents.
* **Cinchona, Senna** — dried in **sun/ artificial heat**.
* **Aloe, Opium** — dried by **evaporation of juice/ latex**.
* Final moisture usually **< 5 % (up to 8–14 % for some)**.

🧠 **MEMORY HOOK — "Which dryer for a thermolabile drug?"**
> **Liquid & thermolabile → Spray dryer; extremely thermolabile & sterile → Freeze dryer;
> solid & thermolabile → Vacuum dryer; granules → Fluidised bed dryer.**

## 3.6 EVAPORATION

📌 **DEFINITION** — **Evaporation** is the removal of a **large amount of solvent (usually water)**
from a solution or suspension **by conversion into vapour**, so as to obtain a **concentrated
liquid** product (the concentrate/ extract).

### Factors affecting the rate of evaporation
* **Temperature** (↑ temp → ↑ rate) and **vapour pressure**.
* **Surface area exposed** (↑ area → ↑ rate).
* **Pressure** (↓ pressure → ↓ boiling point → suitable for thermolabile drugs).
* **Removal of vapour** (air velocity/ vacuum, humidity).
* **Viscosity, concentration & film thickness**; scale/ crust formation; foaming.
* **Heat transfer coefficient** & agitation.

⭐ **Boiling point elevation** — as the solution concentrates, its **boiling point rises**;
predicted by **Dühring's rule** (BP of solution vs BP of water is a straight line).

### 📋 Evaporators

| Equipment | Notes |
|---|---|
| **Evaporating dish / pan (steam-jacketed pan)** | Simple, open, small-scale; large surface; used for **extracts, syrups** |
| **Evaporating still** | Closed; permits **solvent recovery** (tinctures, alcohol) |
| **Vacuum evaporator (vacuum still)** | ⭐ Lower temperature → **thermolabile** drugs; faster; recovers solvent |
| **Short-tube (calandria) / Vertical tube evaporator** | Natural circulation; standard industrial |
| **Long-tube (climbing/ falling film) evaporator** | Rapid, single pass, **short contact time** — heat-sensitive liquids |
| **Forced-circulation evaporator** | Pump-driven, for **viscous & scaling** liquids |
| **Multiple-effect evaporator** | ⭐ Vapour of one effect is the **heating medium** of the next → **great steam economy** |
| **Thermo-compression (vapour recompression)** | Vapour compressed & reused |
| **Rotary/ Wiped (agitated) thin-film evaporator** | Very viscous & heat-sensitive materials |
| **Rotary vacuum evaporator (lab)** | Small-scale concentration of extracts |

⭐ In multiple-effect evaporators, the **pressure decreases** and the **boiling point falls** from
the first to the last effect.

## 3.7 DISTILLATION

📌 **DEFINITION** — **Distillation** is the process of **converting a liquid into its vapour by
heating, and then condensing the vapour back to a liquid**, in order to **separate or purify** it.

⚠️ **Distillation vs Evaporation:** in **distillation the VAPOUR is collected (condensed) and is
the product**; in **evaporation the vapour is discarded and the RESIDUE is the product**.

### ⭐ Types of distillation

| Type | Principle | Used for | Examples |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Simple distillation** | Single vaporisation–condensation | Liquids with **widely different boiling points**; volatile from non-volatile | **Distilled water (Purified Water IP)** |
| **Fractional distillation** | Repeated vaporisation–condensation in a **fractionating column** | Liquids with **close boiling points** | Separation of **alcohol–water**, petroleum fractions, coal tar |
| **Steam distillation** | Steam passed through the material; **immiscible volatile substance distils below 100 °C** with the steam | ⭐ **Volatile (essential) oils** from crude drugs; heat-sensitive, water-immiscible substances | **Clove oil, peppermint oil, eucalyptus oil, turpentine, aromatic waters** |
| **Vacuum (reduced-pressure) distillation** | Boiling point lowered by reducing pressure | **Thermolabile** liquids | Vitamins, glycerin, fixed-oil fractions |
| **Molecular distillation** | Very **high vacuum (10⁻³ mm Hg)**; molecules travel to a nearby cold surface without collision | ⭐ **Vitamins A & E, hormones, high-molecular-weight thermolabile oils** | Distance between evaporator & condenser < mean free path |
| **Destructive distillation** | Heating in the **absence of air**; substance decomposes | **Wood tar, coal tar, birch tar, animal charcoal, methyl alcohol from wood** | Product is a decomposition mixture |
| **Extractive & azeotropic distillation** | A third component alters relative volatility | Breaking **azeotropes** | Alcohol–water azeotrope (**95.6 % ethanol, bp 78.15 °C**) broken with benzene/ cyclohexane |
| **Compound/ cohobation** | Distillate returned & re-distilled from fresh drug | Concentrated aromatic waters (classical) | — |
| **Flash distillation** | Liquid flashes into vapour at reduced pressure | Petroleum | — |

🔢 **NUMBERS** — Purified water is stored **not more than 24 h** after collection for most uses;
**Water for Injection** must be **freshly distilled, pyrogen-free**, collected in **sterile,
neutral-glass** containers; **first portion of distillate is discarded**.

⭐ **Distillation apparatus (parts)** — still/ boiler (retort), still-head, condenser
(**Liebig, coil/ worm, reflux**), receiver, thermometer, **splash-head/ baffle to prevent
entrainment**.
⭐ **Aromatic waters** are made by **steam distillation** or by the **solution/ trituration method**
(with talc as a distributing agent).

📌 **RAOULT'S LAW** — the partial vapour pressure of a component ∝ its **mole fraction** in the
liquid. ⭐ Ideal mixtures obey it; deviation gives **azeotropes** (constant-boiling mixtures which
**cannot be separated by simple fractional distillation**).

## 3.8 SUBLIMATION

📌 **DEFINITION** — **Sublimation** is the process in which a **solid, on heating, is converted
directly into vapour without passing through the liquid state**, and the vapour on cooling
condenses back into a **solid (sublimate)**.

⭐ **Sublimable substances (memorise):** **Camphor, Menthol, Thymol, Iodine, Naphthalene,
Benzoic acid, Salicylic acid, Ammonium chloride, Sulphur (partly), Caffeine, Arsenious oxide,
Hexamine, Anthracene, Solid CO₂ (dry ice)**.

* **Uses:** purification (camphor, iodine), **identification test** for balsams & benzoic acid,
  and **freeze-drying** is sublimation of **ice under vacuum**.
* **Apparatus:** sublimation is done in a **shallow dish with an inverted funnel/ watch glass**,
  or industrially in **vacuum sublimation chambers**.
* **Triple point** — the temperature & pressure at which solid, liquid & vapour coexist;
  sublimation occurs **below** the triple point.

## 3.9 EXTRACTION ⭐⭐ (very high-yield)

📌 **DEFINITION** — **Extraction** is the process of **removing the soluble (desired) constituents
of a crude drug with a suitable solvent**, leaving behind the insoluble inert (marc).

📌 **Menstruum** = the **solvent** used for extraction.
📌 **Marc** = the **inert, exhausted, insoluble residue** of the drug left after extraction.
📌 **Miscella** = the extract solution (solvent + dissolved constituents).
📌 **Percolate** = the liquid that trickles out of a percolator.
📌 **Colature** = the strained liquid obtained after straining.
📌 **Despumation** = removal of scum/ frothy impurities from a liquid.
📌 **Garbling** = removal of foreign & unwanted parts from a crude drug after collection.
📌 **Exhausted drug** = drug from which active constituents have already been removed.

### ⭐ Choice of menstruum

| Menstruum | Extracts | Note |
|---|---|---|
| **Water** | Gums, mucilages, sugars, salts, glycosides, tannins, some alkaloidal salts | Cheap; but **supports microbial growth** → needs preservative & has short shelf-life |
| **Alcohol (ethanol)** | Alkaloids (bases), resins, volatile oils, glycosides, tannins, chlorophyll | **Self-preserving above ~ 20 % v/v (≥ 12–20 %)**; the standard menstruum of tinctures |
| **Alcohol–water (hydro-alcoholic)** | Widest range — most tinctures & fluid extracts | Strength chosen for each drug (e.g. 45 %, 60 %, 70 %, 90 %) |
| **Glycerin** | Tannins, prevents oxidation/ precipitation, sweet & preservative | Used in **glycerin-containing extracts** |
| **Ether/ Chloroform/ Acetone/ Petroleum spirit** | Fixed & volatile oils, resins, alkaloidal bases | Volatile, inflammable; used for **oleoresins** |
| **Acidified water/ alcohol** | Converts alkaloids to **water-soluble salts** | Dilute HCl/ acetic acid |
| **Alkalinised solvent** | Frees alkaloidal **bases** | Ammonia, lime |
| **Diluted acetic acid** | Vinegars (acetum) | Squill vinegar |

### 📋 METHODS OF EXTRACTION ⭐⭐

| Method | 📌 Definition & procedure | Used for | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Maceration** | Drug is **soaked in the whole of the menstruum in a closed vessel for a stated period (usually 7 days for organised, 2–7 days for unorganised), with occasional shaking**, then strained, the marc pressed & the volume adjusted | **Unorganised drugs, drugs whose active constituents are readily soluble; drugs that swell too much for percolation** | **Tincture of Orange peel, Tincture of Squill, Tincture of Lemon, Tincture of Tolu, Tincture of Benzoin, Compound Tincture of Benzoin, Tincture of Myrrh, Tincture of Ginger (weak)** |
| — **Simple maceration** | Whole menstruum at once, 7 days, press marc | Organised drugs | Tincture of Squill |
| — **Maceration with adjustment** *(for unorganised drugs)* | Drug + menstruum, no pressing of marc (no marc), filtered & volume adjusted | Unorganised (gums, resins, balsams) | **Tincture of Benzoin, Tincture of Tolu** |
| — **Multiple/ Circulatory maceration** | Menstruum divided into portions used successively → more complete extraction | Concentrated preparations | **Concentrated infusions, liquid extracts** |
| — **Digestion** | ⭐ **Maceration with GENTLE HEAT (about 35–40 °C, up to 50 °C)**, so long as the active constituents are not damaged | Drugs with **tough/ hard tissue**; increases solvent efficiency | **Compound tincture of Benzoin (some texts), Mother tinctures** |
| **Percolation** | ⭐ Drug packed in a **percolator (a narrow, cone-shaped vessel)** and the menstruum allowed to **flow slowly downward through it**, continuously exposing fresh drug to fresh solvent | ⭐ **Organised drugs & potent drugs; the official method for most tinctures & liquid extracts** | **Tincture of Belladonna, Tincture of Nux vomica, Tincture of Cinchona, Tincture of Ipecac, Tincture of Digitalis, Liquid extract of Liquorice** |
| — Steps of percolation | ⭐ **(1) Size reduction → (2) Imbibition/ wetting (moistening with menstruum, keep 4 h in a closed vessel) → (3) Packing of the percolator → (4) Maceration in the percolator (24 h, outlet closed) → (5) Percolation proper (rate controlled) → (6) Final adjustment of volume** | — | 🧠 "**S-I-P-M-P-A**" |
| — Rate of percolation | 🔢 **Slow: 1 mL/min · Moderate: 1–3 mL/min · Rapid: 3–5 mL/min** (per 1000 g of drug) | — | Slow percolation gives a **more complete extraction** |
| — **Reserved percolation** | ⭐ **First ¾ of the percolate is RESERVED**; the rest is percolated further, concentrated by evaporation, then **added to the reserved portion** and adjusted to volume — the reserved portion is never heated, so **heat-labile & volatile constituents are protected** | ⭐ **Liquid extracts, potent & aromatic drugs** | **Liquid Extract of Belladonna, Liquid extract of Ipecac** |
| — **Continuous hot percolation (Soxhlet)** | Solvent is **boiled, condensed, percolates through drug, then siphons back** to the flask — solvent is reused | ⭐ **Small quantity of solvent, exhaustive extraction; not for thermolabile drugs** | **Oleoresins, fixed oil determination, alkaloid extraction** |
| — **Counter-current extraction** | Drug & solvent move in **opposite directions** through a series of vessels | Industrial, very efficient | — |
| **Infusion** | 📌 A **dilute aqueous solution** of the readily soluble constituents of a crude drug, prepared by **macerating the drug with COLD or BOILING water for a SHORT time** | Drugs with readily soluble/ heat-labile constituents | **Fresh Infusion of Senna, Infusion of Gentian (cold), Infusion of Digitalis** |
| — **Fresh infusion** | 🔢 **10 g drug + 100 mL water (1 in 10 or 5 %)**, macerated **15–30 min**, strained. ⭐ **Shelf-life only 12 hours** | — | Prepared as required |
| — **Concentrated infusion** | 🔢 **Prepared 8 times the strength** of the fresh infusion, by **maceration with alcohol (20–25 %) as a preservative**; **1 part diluted with 7 parts water gives a fresh infusion** | — | **Concentrated infusion of Gentian, of Orange, of Quassia** |
| **Decoction** | 📌 The drug is **BOILED with water (usually 10 min) and strained while hot** | **Hard, woody drugs, barks, roots** whose constituents are heat-stable & water-soluble | **Decoction of Cinchona (classical)**; Ayurvedic **kwath/ kadha** |
| **Hot continuous extraction** | See Soxhlet above | — | — |
| **Aqueous alcoholic fermentation** | Drug fermented with water & jaggery | **Ayurvedic Asava & Arishta** | Draksharishta |
| **Modern methods** | **Supercritical fluid extraction (SFE with CO₂), ultrasound-assisted (sonication), microwave-assisted (MAE), enzyme-assisted, phytonic (HFC solvent) extraction** | Thermolabile & high-purity extracts | Volatile oils, caffeine decaffeination |
| **Enfleurage / Expression** | ⭐ Non-solvent methods for volatile oils: **enfleurage** = absorption of the perfume of flowers in fat; **expression (cold pressing)** = mechanical squeezing | Delicate flower oils; **citrus peel oils (lemon, orange)** | **Lemon oil by expression** |
| **Ecuelle à piquer** | Pricking & pressing of citrus peel | Citrus oils | — |

🔁 **COMPARE — MACERATION vs PERCOLATION** ⭐

| Feature | **MACERATION** | **PERCOLATION** |
|---|---|---|
| Contact | Drug soaked in **whole** menstruum, **static** | Menstruum **flows continuously** through drug |
| Equilibrium | Extraction stops at **equilibrium** → incomplete | Fresh solvent always meets drug → **complete/ exhaustive** |
| Time | **Long (2–7 days)** | Shorter, controlled |
| Apparatus | Closed vessel/ jar | **Percolator** (conical, tapering) |
| Suited to | **Unorganised drugs**, drugs that swell, cheap drugs | **Organised & potent drugs** |
| Marc | Pressed (except with adjustment) | Not pressed; washed |
| Efficiency | Lower | **Higher** |
| Examples | Tinct. Benzoin, Tinct. Squill, Tinct. Orange | Tinct. Belladonna, Tinct. Nux vomica, Liquid extracts |

🔁 **COMPARE — INFUSION vs DECOCTION**

| Feature | **INFUSION** | **DECOCTION** |
|---|---|---|
| Water temperature | **Cold or boiling water poured on drug** | Drug **boiled** in water |
| Duration | **15–30 min** (short) | **10–15 min of boiling** |
| Heat applied during process | **No continued boiling** | **Continuous boiling** |
| Drugs | Soft, heat-labile (leaves, flowers) | **Hard, woody (roots, barks)** |
| Strength | 1 in 10 (5–10 %) | Usually 1 in 20 concentrated to a set volume |
| Shelf life | ⭐ **12 hours** | Very short (use fresh) |

⭐ **CLASSICAL GALENICAL STRENGTHS — memorise!** 🔢

| Galenical | Strength |
|---|---|
| **Tincture (simple/ non-potent drug)** | **1 in 5 (20 % w/v)** — e.g. Tincture of Ginger, Orange, Lemon, Benzoin (1 in 5) |
| **Tincture (potent drug)** | **1 in 10 (10 % w/v)** — e.g. Tinct. Belladonna, Nux vomica, Digitalis, Opium, Ipecac, Stramonium |
| **Liquid (fluid) extract** | ⭐ **1 in 1** — 1 mL ≡ 1 g of the drug |
| **Fresh infusion** | **1 in 10** |
| **Concentrated infusion** | **8 times** the fresh infusion (1 : 7 dilution) |
| **Syrup (simple syrup IP)** | **66.7 % w/w sucrose** (≈ 85 % w/v; 667 g sucrose in 1000 g syrup) |
| **Spirit** | Alcoholic/ hydro-alcoholic solution of a **volatile** substance, usually **5–10 %** |
| **Aromatic water** | **Saturated** aqueous solution of a volatile oil (≈ 0.2 %) |
| **Elixir** | Sweetened, aromatic, **hydro-alcoholic** clear liquid |
| **Glycerin (Glycerite)** | Solution in **glycerin** (e.g. Glycerin of Boric acid, Glycerin of Starch) |
| **Vinegar (Acetum)** | Solution in **dilute acetic acid** |
| **Linctus** | **Viscous, sweet, demulcent** oral liquid for cough, taken undiluted, sipped slowly |
| **Mucilage of Acacia** | **35 % w/v** |

| **Mouthwash** | Aqueous solution for rinsing the **mouth** |
| **Douche** | Aqueous solution directed against a **body cavity** (eye, nose, vagina) |
| **Enema** | Liquid **rectal** administration (evacuant/ retention) |
| **Collodion** | **Pyroxylin (nitrocellulose)** in ether–alcohol; leaves a flexible film |
| **Draught** | A single-dose (usually 50 mL) mixture |

⚠️ **TRAP — "1 in 1" is the LIQUID EXTRACT.** Tinctures are 1 in 5 (simple) or 1 in 10 (potent).
🧠 **"Potent = 10, Plain = 5, Liquid extract = 1."**

## 3.10 FILTRATION & CLARIFICATION

📌 **DEFINITION** — **Filtration** is the process of **separating suspended solid particles from a
liquid or gas by passing it through a porous medium (filter) that retains the solid**.

📌 **Filtrate** = the clear liquid that passes through. 📌 **Cake/ residue** = the retained solids.
📌 **Clarification** = removal of a **small amount** of solid to make a liquid sparkling clear.
📌 **Straining** = coarse separation through a cloth/ muslin.

### ⭐ Mechanisms of filtration
1. **Straining/ sieving** — particles larger than the pore are held on the surface.
2. **Impingement** — particles strike the medium and adhere.
3. **Entanglement** — particles trapped in the mesh of fibres (cotton wool, filter pad).
4. **Attractive (electrostatic/ adsorption) forces** — fine particles adsorbed on the medium
   (asbestos, charcoal, sintered glass, membrane).
5. **Autofiltration** — the **deposited cake itself acts as the filter medium**.

### 🔢 Factors affecting filtration rate — **POISEUILLE'S / DARCY'S equation**
```
        A × ΔP × t
V  =  ───────────────
        η × L × R
```
Rate ∝ **area of filter (A)**, **pressure difference (ΔP)**;
Rate ∝ 1/**viscosity (η)**, 1/**thickness of cake (L)**, 1/**resistance (R)**.

⭐ Hence, to **increase** the filtration rate: increase area & pressure (or apply vacuum),
**reduce viscosity by heating**, keep the cake thin, use a **filter aid**, and pre-treat/ flocculate
the slurry.

### Filter media & filter aids

| Filter media | Filter aids |
|---|---|
| **Filter paper (Whatman), cotton wool, muslin/ flannel cloth, glass wool, asbestos pads, sintered glass & metal, porcelain (unglazed), ceramic candles, membrane filters (cellulose acetate/ nitrate), woven wire, synthetic fabrics** | ⭐ **Kieselguhr (diatomaceous earth), Talc, Purified siliceous earth, Charcoal, Paper pulp, Bentonite, Fuller's earth, Asbestos, Perlite** |

⭐ **Filter aid** = an inert, insoluble, porous powder added to a slurry (or pre-coated on the
filter) to **prevent the pores being clogged by gelatinous/ slimy particles**, thereby increasing
the filtration rate. ⚠️ **Never use a filter aid when the substance to be filtered off is the
wanted product**, or when it may **adsorb the active drug** (e.g. alkaloids on charcoal/ talc).

### 📋 Filtration equipment

| Equipment | Principle & use |
|---|---|
| **Filter funnel + filter paper** | Small-scale/ dispensing |
| **Flannel bag / conical strainer** | Straining of mixtures & syrups |
| **Buchner funnel / Sintered glass with vacuum flask** | **Vacuum filtration**, small scale |
| **Filter press (plate-and-frame)** | Batch, high pressure; **viscous liquids, syrups, extracts**; large area in small space; labour-intensive |
| **Rotary drum (continuous vacuum) filter** | Continuous, large volumes of slurry |
| **Leaf filter (Sweetland/ Kelly/ Vallez)** | Enclosed pressure filter; solvent-recovery friendly |
| **Meta filter (edge filter)** | Rings on a rod; **needs a filter aid (kieselguhr)**; used for **viscous liquids & clarifying syrups**; ⭐ no filter medium needed, easy to clean, withstands high pressure |
| **Cartridge filter** | Disposable cartridge; clarification |
| **Membrane filter** | ⭐ **0.22 µm = sterilising (bacteria-proof) grade; 0.45 µm = for bioburden testing**; used for **sterility testing & sterilisation of thermolabile solutions** |
| **Seitz filter (asbestos pad)** | Classical sterile filtration |
| **Candle filters** — **Chamberland–Pasteur (porcelain)**, **Berkefeld (kieselguhr)**, **Mandler** | Classical bacterial filtration |
| **Sparkler filter** | Small-scale clarification of syrups |
| **HEPA filter** | ⭐ Air filtration — removes **≥ 99.97 % of particles of 0.3 µm**; used in **LAF (laminar air flow) units & clean rooms** |

🔢 **REMEMBER:** **0.22 µm (0.2 µm) membrane = sterilising filter**; **HEPA = 0.3 µm at 99.97 %**;
Clean-room **Grade A/ Class 100** for aseptic filling.

## 3.11 CENTRIFUGATION

📌 **DEFINITION** — **Centrifugation** is the separation of two immiscible phases (or a solid from
a liquid) by the application of a **centrifugal force** many times greater than gravity.

🔢 **Centrifugal force** `F = m ω² r = m v²/r`; **Relative Centrifugal Force (RCF/ "g" value)**
`= 1.118 × 10⁻⁵ × r (cm) × N² (rpm)`.

| Type | Use |
|---|---|
| **Perforated-basket (filter/ centrifugal) centrifuge** | Removing mother liquor from **crystals**, drying of wet solids |
| **Non-perforated basket / Tubular bowl** | **Clarification of liquids**, separating emulsions |
| **Disc-bowl (Alfa-Laval) centrifuge** | Continuous **separation of two immiscible liquids** (cream from milk, oil–water) |
| **Supercentrifuge & Ultracentrifuge** | Very fine particles, **colloids, proteins, viruses, molecular weight determination** |
| **Laboratory (swinging-bucket/ angle-head) centrifuge** | Blood, sediment volume of suspensions |
| **Cyclone separator (gas)** | Removing dust from an air stream |

⭐ Pharmaceutical uses: separation of **crystals** from mother liquor, **clarification of syrups &
injections**, **breaking or testing emulsions (accelerated stability)**, **sedimentation-volume
studies of suspensions**, **removing water from wet granules**, **plasma from blood**.

## 3.12 CRYSTALLISATION

📌 **DEFINITION** — **Crystallisation** is the formation of **solid crystals of a definite
geometric form** from a **supersaturated** solution or melt — used for **purification** and to
obtain a product of defined **size, shape & purity**.

### Steps / requirements
1. **Supersaturation** — achieved by (a) **cooling**, (b) **evaporating solvent**,
   (c) adding a **miscible non-solvent (salting out)**, (d) **chemical reaction**, (e) altering pH.
2. **Nucleation** — formation of nuclei (**primary** — homogeneous/ heterogeneous;
   **secondary** — induced by existing crystals/ seeding/ agitation).
3. **Crystal growth** — deposition of solute on nuclei.

⭐ **Rules of crystal size:**
* **Rapid cooling + vigorous agitation + high supersaturation → many nuclei → SMALL crystals.**
* **Slow cooling + no agitation + low supersaturation → few nuclei → LARGE crystals.**
* **Seeding** with crystals of the desired form controls polymorph & size.

### Key terms
* **Mother liquor** — the liquid remaining after crystals are separated.
* **Water of crystallisation** — water chemically combined in the crystal
  (e.g. **CuSO₄·5H₂O, Na₂CO₃·10H₂O, MgSO₄·7H₂O, FeSO₄·7H₂O, Alum·12H₂O**).
* **Efflorescence** — loss of water of crystallisation to air.
* **Polymorphism** — same substance, **different crystal forms**, with different melting points,
  solubility & **bioavailability** (e.g. **chloramphenicol palmitate — only polymorph B is
  active/absorbable**; cocoa butter α, β, β′, γ forms; sulphathiazole).
* **Pseudopolymorphism** — solvates/ hydrates (e.g. ampicillin anhydrous vs trihydrate).
* **Habit** — external shape (tabular, prismatic, acicular/needle, plate).
* **Amorphous form** — no lattice; **more soluble & faster dissolving but less stable** than
  crystalline form. ⭐ **Novobiocin: amorphous form is absorbed, crystalline is not.**

**Crystallisers:** tank (batch) crystalliser, **Swenson–Walker (continuous, scraped-surface)**,
Krystal (Oslo) vacuum crystalliser, agitated batch vacuum crystalliser.

## 3.13 STERILISATION ⭐⭐

📌 **DEFINITION** — **Sterilisation** is the process by which **all forms of living
micro-organisms, including bacterial spores and viruses, are completely destroyed or removed**
from an article.

⚠️ **Key distinctions (very frequently asked)**

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Sterilisation** | **Complete** destruction/ removal of **ALL** micro-organisms **including spores** |
| **Disinfection** | Destruction of **vegetative pathogens** (not necessarily spores), on **inanimate** objects |
| **Antisepsis** | Prevention of growth of micro-organisms on **living tissue** |
| **Sanitisation** | Reducing microbial numbers to a safe public-health level |
| **Asepsis** | Techniques that **prevent the entry** of micro-organisms |
| **Bacteriostatic** | **Inhibits** growth (reversible) |
| **Bactericidal** | **Kills** bacteria (irreversible) |
| **Sporicidal** | Kills spores |
| **Pasteurisation** | Partial sterilisation of liquids: **63 °C/ 30 min (holder/ LTLT)** or **72 °C/ 15–20 s (flash/ HTST)** |
| **Tyndallisation** | **Intermittent (fractional) sterilisation** — **100 °C for 20–60 min on 3 successive days** |
| **Depyrogenation** | Destruction of **pyrogens** — needs **dry heat 250 °C/ 30 min** (or 180 °C/ 3–4 h) |

### 📋 THE CLASSIFICATION OF STERILISATION METHODS

```
                        STERILISATION
                              │
   ┌──────────┬───────────────┼────────────┬────────────┐
 PHYSICAL   CHEMICAL      MECHANICAL   RADIATION    ASEPTIC
   │           │          (Filtration)      │      TECHNIQUE
 ┌─┴───┐   Gaseous &        0.22 µm     Ionising &
DRY   MOIST  liquid                     Non-ionising
HEAT   HEAT  agents
```

### A. DRY HEAT STERILISATION 🔢

| Method | Conditions | Uses |
|---|---|---|
| **Hot air oven** ⭐ | **160 °C for 2 h** (also 170 °C/ 1 h; 180 °C/ 30 min) | **Glassware, metal instruments, forceps, scissors, syringes, powders (talc, sulphur, ZnO), oils, fats, waxes, paraffin, glycerin, dry chemicals** |
| **Depyrogenation tunnel** | **250 °C for 30 min** | Destroying **pyrogens** on glass ampoules/ vials |
| **Flaming** | Direct flame | Inoculating loops, mouths of tubes |
| **Incineration** | Burning to ash | **Contaminated dressings, animal carcasses, disposables** |
| **Red heat** | Until red-hot | Wire loop, forceps tips, spatula |
| **Infrared conveyor** | 180 °C tunnel | Syringes, glass |

⭐ **Mechanism of dry heat = OXIDATION / protein denaturation & charring**, and it is
**LESS efficient than moist heat** (needs higher temp & longer time) because **dry air is a poor
conductor and there is no latent heat**.

### B. MOIST HEAT STERILISATION 🔢

| Method | Conditions | Uses |
|---|---|---|
| **Autoclave (saturated steam under pressure)** ⭐⭐ | **121 °C at 15 psi (1.05 kg/cm²) for 15 min** (also **115 °C/ 10 psi/ 30 min**; **134 °C/ 30 psi/ 3 min**) | ⭐ **Aqueous injections, IV fluids, dressings, surgical instruments, rubber closures, culture media, glassware, gowns, filters** |
| **Boiling** | 100 °C for 20–30 min (**not sterilisation** — spores survive) | Instruments (emergency disinfection) |
| **Tyndallisation** | 100 °C, 20–60 min × **3 successive days** | Media & solutions containing **heat-labile** components (with spore germination in between) |
| **Steam at 100 °C (Koch's/ Arnold's steamer)** | Free-flowing steam 90 min | Media, egg/serum media |
| **Low-temperature steam + formaldehyde (LTSF)** | **73 °C + formaldehyde** | Heat-sensitive rubber, plastics |
| **Pasteurisation** | 63 °C/30 min or 72 °C/15 s | **Milk, vaccines** |

⭐ **Mechanism of moist heat = COAGULATION & DENATURATION of proteins by hydrolysis** —
efficient because of the **latent heat of condensation of steam** (steam gives up **~ 540 cal/g**
on condensing) and the high **heat penetration**.

⚠️ **TRAP — Why is AIR removed from an autoclave?** Because **air is a poor conductor**; entrapped
air **lowers the temperature at a given pressure** and prevents steam penetration → sterilisation
failure. Hence the **air is displaced downward** ("downward displacement autoclave") or removed by
a **vacuum pump (pre-vacuum autoclave)**.

⭐ **AUTOCLAVE CONTROLS/ INDICATORS**

| Type | Example |
|---|---|
| **Physical** | Thermocouple, temperature–pressure chart recorder, **Browne's tube (green spot at 121 °C)** |
| **Chemical** | **Autoclave (Bowie–Dick) tape**, chemical indicator strips |
| **Biological** ⭐ | **Bacillus stearothermophilus (Geobacillus stearothermophilus) spores** for **moist heat/ autoclave**; **Bacillus subtilis var. niger (globigii)** for **dry heat & ethylene oxide**; **Bacillus pumilus** for **ionising radiation**; **Clostridium sporogenes** for anaerobic |

🧠 **MEMORY HOOK — biological indicators:** "**S**tearothermophilus = **S**team;
**S**ubtilis = **S**earing dry heat & **E**TO; **P**umilus = **R**adiation."

🔢 **F₀ VALUE** — the **equivalent time in minutes at 121 °C** delivered to a product;
pharmacopoeias require **F₀ ≥ 8 (usually 12)** for terminally sterilised aqueous products.
🔢 **D-value** — time required to reduce the microbial population by **90 % (1 log cycle)** at a
given temperature.
🔢 **Z-value** — the **temperature change** needed to alter the D-value by **1 log cycle**
(**≈ 10 °C** for moist heat).
🔢 **Sterility Assurance Level (SAL)** — probability of a non-sterile unit = **10⁻⁶**.

### C. RADIATION STERILISATION

| Type | Source/ wavelength | Mechanism | Uses |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Ionising — Gamma rays** ⭐ | **Cobalt-60** (also Caesium-137); dose **2.5 Mrad = 25 kGy** | Ionisation → free radicals → DNA damage | ⭐ **Disposable plastic syringes, catheters, sutures, gloves, surgical dressings, prepacked heat-labile items, some powders & ointments** |
| **Ionising — Electron beam (cathode rays)** | Electron accelerator | Same | Surface sterilisation, thin packs; **low penetration but fast** |
| **Non-ionising — UV light** | **240–280 nm; most effective ≈ 253.7 nm (254 nm)** from a **mercury vapour lamp** | Pyrimidine dimer formation in DNA | ⭐ **Surface & air sterilisation of aseptic rooms, LAF benches, water; POOR penetration → NOT a true steriliser** |
| **Infrared** | Heat radiation | Dry heat | Conveyor sterilisation of syringes |
| **Microwave** | 2450 MHz | Dielectric heating | Small-scale, moist materials |

⚠️ **TRAP** — **UV light is only a surface disinfectant** (cannot penetrate glass, water depth or
paper); **gamma radiation penetrates the final sealed pack** and is therefore the method of choice
for **disposables**.

### D. GASEOUS (CHEMICAL) STERILISATION

| Agent | Conditions | Uses | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Ethylene oxide (ETO)** ⭐ | **~ 500–1000 mg/L, 45–63 °C, 30–60 % RH, 4–6 h**; usually diluted with CO₂/ N₂ (e.g. **Carboxide 10 % ETO + 90 % CO₂**) | **Heat-labile plastics, catheters, sutures, powders, prosthetics, delicate instruments, syringes** | **Alkylating agent** (alkylates DNA/protein). ⚠️ **Flammable, explosive, mutagenic & carcinogenic**; needs **moisture** to work; requires long **degassing/ aeration** to remove residues |
| **Formaldehyde (gas/ fumigation)** | 500 mL formalin/ 1000 ft³, 12–24 h; or LTSF | **Rooms, aseptic areas, filters, wool, blankets** | Irritant; neutralised with **ammonia** |
| **Beta-propiolactone (BPL)** | 0.2 % | Fumigation of rooms, vaccines | Carcinogenic |
| **Hydrogen peroxide vapour / Plasma** | Low temperature | Isolators, endoscopes, clean rooms | No toxic residue |
| **Peracetic acid** | 0.2–0.35 % | Isolators, endoscopes | — |
| **Ozone** | — | Water, surfaces | — |

### E. FILTRATION (MECHANICAL) STERILISATION
* ⭐ **Membrane filter of 0.22 µm** removes bacteria; **0.1 µm** for mycoplasma;
  **viruses are NOT removed** (except by ultrafiltration).
* Used for **thermolabile solutions**: **insulin, vaccines, sera, enzymes, hormones, vitamins,
  antibiotic solutions, eye drops, LVPs** and for **air (HEPA)**.
* Classical filters: **Seitz (asbestos pad), sintered glass, Chamberland–Pasteur porcelain candle,
  Berkefeld (kieselguhr) candle, Mandler**.
* **Bubble-point test** verifies membrane integrity.

### F. ASEPTIC TECHNIQUE
📌 A method by which a **sterile product is prepared and filled in a sterile environment from
already-sterilised components**, when the product **cannot itself be terminally sterilised**.
* Requires **clean rooms (Grade A/B, Class 100 laminar air flow)**, HEPA-filtered air,
  sterile garments, gloves, validated disinfection, **media-fill validation**.
* Used for **antibiotic powders for injection, insulin, biologicals, ophthalmic ointments**.

### G. CHOICE OF METHOD — the decision table ⭐

| Product | Method of choice |
|---|---|
| **Aqueous injections, IV fluids, dressings, rubber closures** | **Autoclave, 121 °C/ 15 min** |
| **Glassware, metal instruments, powders, oils, fats, waxes, paraffin, glycerin** | **Dry heat (hot air oven), 160 °C/ 2 h** |
| **Thermolabile solutions (insulin, vaccines, enzymes, eye drops)** | **Membrane filtration (0.22 µm) + aseptic filling** |
| **Disposable plastics, syringes, catheters, sutures, gloves** | **Gamma radiation (2.5 Mrad)** |
| **Plastics & delicate instruments that cannot take radiation** | **Ethylene oxide** |
| **Clean-room air** | **HEPA filter + UV** |
| **Ampoule glass — pyrogen removal** | **Dry heat 250 °C/ 30 min** |
| **Culture media containing heat-labile sugars** | **Tyndallisation** |

⭐ **STERILITY TEST (IP)** — carried out by **membrane filtration** or **direct inoculation** into
**Fluid Thioglycollate Medium (for anaerobes & aerobes, 30–35 °C)** and
**Soyabean Casein Digest Medium (for fungi & aerobes, 20–25 °C)**, incubated **14 days**.
⭐ **PYROGEN TEST** — **rabbit method** (rise in body temperature) and the *in vitro*
**LAL (Limulus Amoebocyte Lysate) test** for bacterial endotoxins.
⭐ **Pyrogens** are **lipopolysaccharides** from the cell wall of **Gram-negative bacteria**;
they are **water-soluble, non-volatile, heat-stable** and are **NOT removed by autoclaving** —
they are removed by **dry heat 250 °C**, **activated charcoal**, **ultrafiltration**, or
**anion-exchange resins**.

## 3.14 HEAT TRANSFER & ALLIED OPERATIONS

📌 **Heat transfer** is the flow of heat from a hotter to a cooler body. Three modes:

| Mode | Mechanism | Pharmaceutical example |
|---|---|---|
| **Conduction** | Through a solid, molecule to molecule, no bulk movement | Heating an evaporating pan; hot plate |
| **Convection** | By actual movement of the heated fluid (natural or forced) | Hot-air oven, tray dryer, steam-jacketed pan |
| **Radiation** | Electromagnetic waves, no medium required | Infrared dryer, sun drying |

🔢 **Fourier's law (conduction):** `Q = k A (ΔT)/L`.
🔢 **Newton's law of cooling / convection:** `Q = h A ΔT`.
🔢 **Stefan–Boltzmann (radiation):** `Q = σ A T⁴`.

* **Heat exchangers**: **shell-and-tube, double-pipe, plate, coil, jacketed vessel**.
* **Steam** is the commonest heating medium (**latent heat ≈ 540 cal/g**); others: hot water,
  hot oil, Dowtherm, electricity.
* **Humidification** — adding moisture to air; **dehumidification** — removing it.
  🔢 **Dry-bulb temperature** = actual air temperature; **wet-bulb temperature** = temperature of
  evaporative equilibrium; **dew point** = temperature at which air becomes saturated;
  **relative humidity (RH)** = (actual vapour pressure/ saturated vapour pressure) × 100.
  Read from the **psychrometric (humidity) chart**.
* ⭐ **RH control is critical** for **effervescent granules, hygroscopic powders, capsules
  (35–45 % RH)**, and **tablet compression**.

## 3.15 📖 GLOSSARY OF CLASSICAL PHARMACEUTICAL OPERATIONS ⭐⭐
*(The old "pharmaceutical processes and methods" list — pure one-mark material.
Learn every definition.)*

| Operation | 📌 Definition |
|---|---|
| **Solution** | Dissolving a solute in a solvent to form a homogeneous, molecularly dispersed system |
| **Trituration** | Rubbing/ grinding a substance in a mortar with a pestle (to reduce size or to mix) |
| **Levigation** | Grinding a solid with a small amount of liquid in which it is insoluble, to a smooth paste |
| **Pulverisation by intervention** | Size reduction with the aid of a volatile solvent that is subsequently removed (camphor + alcohol) |
| **Spatulation** | Mixing powders on a tile with a spatula, without pressure |
| **Sifting/ Bolting** | Passing powders through a sieve or bolting cloth |
| **Tumbling** | Mixing by rotating powders in a closed container |
| **Geometric dilution** | Mixing a potent drug with diluent by successive doubling |
| **Elutriation** | Separating fine from coarse particles by a stream of water or air |
| **Decantation** | Pouring off the clear supernatant liquid, leaving the sediment behind |
| **Colature** | The strained liquid; **straining** through cloth/muslin |
| **Expression** | Obtaining liquid by **mechanical pressure/ squeezing** (fixed oils, citrus peel oils, juice) |
| **Clarification** | Making a liquid sparkling clear by removing a small amount of suspended matter |
| **Despumation** | Removal of scum/ frothy impurities from the surface of a boiling liquid |
| **Defecation** | Removal of impurities from a liquid by allowing them to settle |
| **Filtration** | Separating solids from a liquid by a porous medium |
| **Evaporation** | Removing a large amount of solvent as vapour to give a concentrated liquid |
| **Desiccation / Drying** | Removing small amounts of moisture to give a dry solid |
| **Exsiccation** | ⭐ Removal of the **water of crystallisation** by heat, e.g. **exsiccated ferrous sulphate, exsiccated alum, dried sodium sulphate** |
| **Efflorescence** | Spontaneous loss of water of crystallisation to the atmosphere |
| **Deliquescence** | Absorption of atmospheric moisture until the solid liquefies |
| **Calcination** | ⭐ Heating a substance **strongly, in the presence of air**, to drive off volatile matter/ water & to bring about decomposition — e.g. **calcined magnesia (MgO from MgCO₃), calcined calamine, lime from limestone** |
| **Ignition** | Heating to redness, generally to constant weight (as in ash determination) |
| **Torrefaction** | ⭐ **Roasting** a drug until it is brown & brittle — e.g. **roasted coffee, torrefied rhubarb, roasted asafoetida** |
| **Carbonisation** | Converting organic matter to charcoal by heating without air |
| **Destructive distillation** | Heating in the absence of air so that the substance decomposes, and collecting the products (wood tar, coal tar) |
| **Sublimation** | Solid → vapour → solid on heating, without melting |
| **Fusion** | ⭐ **Melting together** two or more substances (with or without heat) — the basis of **suppository & ointment preparation**, and of **eutectic mixtures** |
| **Deflagration** | Rapid combustion with sparks/ flame, e.g. heating nitrates with charcoal |
| **Lixiviation** | ⭐ **Leaching** — extracting a **soluble salt from a mixture by washing with water** and evaporating (e.g. obtaining potash from wood ash) |
| **Elixation/ Digestion** | Extraction with the aid of gentle heat |
| **Maceration** | Soaking a drug in the whole of the menstruum in a closed vessel |
| **Percolation** | Slow downward passage of a menstruum through a packed column of the drug |
| **Infusion** | Short maceration of a drug with cold/ boiling water |
| **Decoction** | Boiling a drug with water and straining while hot |
| **Precipitation** | Formation of an insoluble solid from solution by a chemical reagent or a non-solvent |
| **Salting out** | Precipitating a substance by adding a large amount of a soluble salt/electrolyte |
| **Crystallisation** | Formation of geometrically definite solids from a supersaturated solution |
| **Granulation** | Converting fine powders into free-flowing granules (wet or dry) |
| **Slugging** | ⭐ **Dry granulation** by compressing powder into large tablets ("slugs") and re-milling them |
| **Roller compaction** | Dry granulation by passing powder between rollers |
| **Emulsification** | Dispersing one immiscible liquid in another with the aid of an emulgent |
| **Homogenisation** | Reducing globule size in an emulsion to make it uniform & stable |
| **Nascent soap method** | Forming the emulsifier (a soap) *in situ* — e.g. **olive oil + lime water → calcium oleate** (**Carron oil/ Lime liniment**) |
| **Levigation (in ointments)** | See above — removes grittiness |
| **Adsorption** | Retention of a substance on the surface of a solid (charcoal, kaolin) |
| **Absorption** | Uptake of one substance **into** the bulk of another |
| **Dialysis** | Separating crystalloids from colloids through a semi-permeable membrane |
| **Osmosis / Reverse osmosis** | Solvent flow through a semi-permeable membrane; RO used for **water purification** |
| **Ion exchange (demineralisation)** | ⭐ Removing ions by cation & anion exchange resins → **de-ionised (demineralised) water** |
| **Deionisation vs Distillation** | Deionised water is free of **ions** but may contain **pyrogens & organics**; distilled water is free of both |
| **Saponification** | Hydrolysis of a fat/oil by alkali to give **soap + glycerol** |
| **Esterification** | Acid + alcohol → ester |
| **Hydrolysis** | Splitting of a molecule by water |
| **Oxidation & Reduction** | Loss/ gain of electrons; important in drug degradation |
| **Fermentation** | Chemical change brought about by micro-organisms/ enzymes (alcohol, antibiotics, asava-arishta) |
| **Maturation/ Ageing** | Storage to allow desirable changes (tinctures, spirits) |
| **Garbling** | Removing foreign & unwanted parts from a collected crude drug |
| **Blanching** | Brief exposure to hot water/steam to inactivate enzymes |
| **Dehydration** | Complete removal of water |
| **Insufflation** | Blowing a powder into a body cavity |
| **Nebulisation/ Atomisation** | Breaking a liquid into a fine spray of droplets |
| **Comminution** | General term for size reduction |
| **Trituration (2nd meaning)** | ⭐ A **1 in 10 dilution of a potent drug with lactose** (a "trituration" in homoeopathy & dispensing) |
| **Attenuation/ Potentisation** | Serial dilution & succussion (homoeopathy) |
| **Percolate/ Marc/ Menstruum/ Miscella** | See §3.9 |
| **Sterilisation** | Complete destruction of all micro-organisms including spores |
| **Depyrogenation** | Destruction/ removal of pyrogens |
| **Lyophilisation** | Freeze-drying — sublimation of ice under vacuum |
| **Micronisation** | Reducing particles to 1–10 µm (fluid energy mill) |
| **Coating** | Applying a layer (sugar, film, enteric) to a tablet |
| **Encapsulation** | Enclosing a drug in a shell (hard/ soft gelatin) |
| **Microencapsulation** | Coating minute particles/ droplets with a polymer film |
| **Compression** | Forming a tablet under pressure |
| **Ampoule sealing** | Tip-sealing or pull-sealing by fusion of glass |

## 3.16 CHAPTER 3 — RAPID REVISION SHEET

* **Unit operation = physical change; unit process = chemical change.**
* **Rittinger → fine grinding (new surface); Kick → coarse (log of ratio); Bond → in between
  (work index).**
* **Fluid energy mill** = micronising, thermolabile, sterile, **no moving parts**.
  **Ball mill** = impact + attrition, works at **50–80 % of critical speed**, can grind wet/ dry &
  closed/ sterile. **Hammer mill** = impact, most versatile. **Cutter mill** = fibrous crude drugs.
  **Colloid & triple roller mill** = ointments/ emulsions. **Edge & end runner** = compression +
  shear.
* **Dry grinding needs < 5 % moisture; wet grinding > 50 %.**
* **Sieve number = apertures per linear inch;** higher number = finer powder.
* **IP powder grades: Coarse 10/44, Mod. coarse 22/60, Mod. fine 44/85, Fine 85, Very fine 120;
  Microfine 90 % < 50 µm; Superfine 90 % < 10 µm.**
* **Positive/ negative/ neutral mixtures**; **convective, shear, diffusive** mixing;
  **geometric dilution** for potent drugs; mix in **ascending order of bulk density**.
* **Drying → product is a solid; Evaporation → product is a liquid.**
* **EMC cannot be removed; CMC ends the constant-rate period.**
* **Spray dryer** = thermolabile liquids, contact time a few seconds.
  **Freeze dryer (lyophiliser)** = sublimation of ice under vacuum, for vaccines/ sera/ antibiotics.
  **FBD** = tablet granules, fast & uniform. **Vacuum dryer** = thermolabile solids.
* **Steam distillation → volatile oils; molecular distillation → vitamins A & E;
  destructive distillation → tar; azeotrope of alcohol–water = 95.6 %.**
* **Menstruum = solvent; Marc = exhausted residue.**
* **Maceration 7 days (static, unorganised drugs); Percolation (continuous, organised & potent
  drugs).** Percolation steps: **size reduction → imbibition → packing → maceration 24 h →
  percolation → adjustment.** Rates: slow 1, moderate 1–3, rapid 3–5 mL/min.
* **Reserved percolation protects heat-labile constituents (reserve ¾ of percolate).**
* **Soxhlet = continuous hot percolation, small solvent volume.**
* **Tincture: simple 1 in 5, potent 1 in 10. Liquid extract 1 in 1. Fresh infusion 1 in 10, keeps
  12 h. Concentrated infusion = 8× strength (dilute 1 + 7). Simple syrup = 66.7 % w/w sucrose.
  Mucilage of acacia 35 % w/v.**
* **Liniment = rubbed on; Lotion = dabbed on; Paint = brushed on; Gargle = diluted for throat.**
* **Filtration rate ∝ area × pressure ÷ (viscosity × cake thickness × resistance).**
* **Filter aid (kieselguhr/ talc) prevents clogging — never use if the solid is the product.**
* **0.22 µm membrane = sterilising filter; HEPA = 99.97 % of 0.3 µm; Meta filter needs kieselguhr.**
* **Rapid cooling + agitation → small crystals; slow cooling → large crystals.**
* **Polymorphism affects solubility & bioavailability (chloramphenicol palmitate form B).**
* **Autoclave: 121 °C, 15 psi, 15 min (moist heat = coagulation of protein).
  Hot air oven: 160 °C, 2 h (dry heat = oxidation).
  Depyrogenation: 250 °C, 30 min.
  Gamma radiation: 2.5 Mrad (25 kGy), Cobalt-60, for disposables.
  UV: 254 nm, surface only.
  ETO: alkylating agent, needs moisture, for heat-labile plastics.**
* **Biological indicators: B. stearothermophilus → autoclave; B. subtilis → dry heat & ETO;
  B. pumilus → radiation.**
* **F₀ ≥ 8–12; D-value = 1 log reduction; Z-value ≈ 10 °C; SAL = 10⁻⁶.**
* **Pyrogens = lipopolysaccharides of Gram-negative bacteria; heat-stable; removed by dry heat
  250 °C, charcoal, ultrafiltration; detected by rabbit test & LAL test.**
* **Sterility test media: Fluid Thioglycollate (30–35 °C) & Soyabean Casein Digest (20–25 °C),
  14 days.**
* **Exsiccation = removal of water of crystallisation. Calcination = strong heating in air.
  Torrefaction = roasting. Lixiviation = leaching a soluble salt with water.
  Fusion = melting together. Despumation = removing scum. Colature = strained liquid.
  Expression = squeezing out liquid. Decantation = pouring off supernatant.**
