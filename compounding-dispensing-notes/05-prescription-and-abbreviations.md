# 📝 THE PRESCRIPTION
### How prescriptions are written · Prescription reading · Handling · Abbreviations

## 5.1 DEFINITION & LEGAL STATUS

📌 **DEFINITION** — A **prescription** is a **written order** from a **Registered Medical
Practitioner (RMP)**, or other **properly licensed practitioner** (dentist, veterinarian), to a
**pharmacist**, to **compound and dispense** a specific medication for a **specific patient**,
together with **directions for its use**.

📌 In law it is a **legal document**. It establishes a **three-way professional relationship**
between the **prescriber**, the **pharmacist** and the **patient**.

⭐ **KEY LEGAL POINTS**
* Only a **Registered Medical Practitioner** (or registered dentist/ veterinary surgeon within
  their field) may write a prescription for a **Schedule H / H1 / X** drug.
* Only a **Registered Pharmacist** may **compound and dispense** it —
  **Pharmacy Act 1948, Section 42**.
* A prescription is **confidential** — professional secrecy must be maintained.
* The prescription belongs to the **patient**, but for **Schedule X** drugs the pharmacist must
  **retain one copy for 2 years**.
* Sale/ dispensing records must be kept for **2 years** (Drugs & Cosmetics Rules).
* ⚠️ A prescription is **NOT** valid if it is **unsigned**, **undated**, **forged/ altered**, or
  written by an unqualified person.

📌 **Refill/ Repeat (RP)** — a prescription may be repeated only if the prescriber has authorised it
(e.g. "Rep. 3" or "**NR / non rep.**" = do not repeat). ⚠️ **Schedule X and narcotic prescriptions
must NEVER be repeated.**

## 5.2 PARTS OF A PRESCRIPTION ⭐⭐

📌 A complete prescription has **SEVEN parts**. This is one of the most frequently examined
enumerations in the whole syllabus.

```
┌──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│ Dr. A. B. Sharma, M.B.B.S., M.D.        Regn. No. 12345      │  ① HEADING /
│ Consulting Physician                                          │     SUPERSCRIPTION
│ 12, Civil Lines, Lucknow   Ph. 0522-xxxxxxx                  │     (part of it)
│──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────│
│ Name: Mr. Ram Kumar      Age: 45 yrs   Sex: M                │  ② PATIENT'S
│ Address: 5, Mall Road                Date: 17-09-2026        │     PARTICULARS + DATE
│──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────│
│ ℞                                                             │  ③ SUPERSCRIPTION (Rx)
│                                                               │
│   Ammonium chloride            .......... 6 g       ← Basis   │  ④ INSCRIPTION
│   Sodium citrate               .......... 3 g       ← Adjuvant│     (the BODY /
│   Liquorice liquid extract     .......... 8 mL      ← Corrective│    ingredients)
│   Chloroform water    ............... to 100 mL     ← Vehicle │
│                                                               │
│   Fiat mistura.  Mitte 100 mL                                │  ⑤ SUBSCRIPTION
│                                                               │     (directions to the
│                                                               │      PHARMACIST)
│   Sig.: One 5 mL spoonful to be taken three times a day      │  ⑥ SIGNATURA / TRANSCRIPTION
│         after food.                                           │     (directions to the PATIENT)
│──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────│
│ Refill: 0 (non rep.)                    (Signature in ink)   │  ⑦ RENEWAL INSTRUCTIONS +
│                                          Dr. A. B. Sharma     │     SIGNATURE of prescriber
└──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

### 📋 THE SEVEN PARTS — explained

| # | Part | 📌 What it contains |
|---|---|---|
| **1** | **Date** | The date on which the prescription was written. ⭐ Essential to judge whether the prescription is **current**, to detect misuse (an old narcotic prescription), and for records |
| **2** | **Name, age, sex, weight & address of the patient** | ⭐ **Age and weight are essential for children & the elderly** so that the pharmacist can **verify the dose**. The address identifies the patient & is needed for narcotic records |
| **3** | **Superscription** — the symbol **℞ (Rx)** | ⭐ Derived from the **Latin "Recipe" = "Take thou / You take"**; a contraction of the Latin imperative. Traditionally also regarded as a **prayer/ dedication to Jupiter (♃)**, the Roman god of healing, seeking a blessing on the recipe. It **introduces the prescription order** |
| **4** | **Inscription** (the **body** of the prescription) | ⭐ **The most important part** — the **names and quantities of the ingredients**. Classically it has **four components**: **Basis, Adjuvant (Adjuvans), Corrective (Corrigent), Vehicle (Excipient/ Vehiculum)** |
| **5** | **Subscription** | ⭐ The **directions to the PHARMACIST** — the dosage form to be made, the number of doses & the total quantity. E.g. **"Fiat mistura", "M. et ft. pulv. mitte tales doses no. XII", "Ft. ung. Mitte 30 g"** |
| **6** | **Signatura (Signa / Sig. / Transcription)** | ⭐ The **directions to the PATIENT** — dose, frequency, route, time & duration, to be transferred onto the **label**. E.g. "**Sig.: 5 mL t.d.s. p.c.**" |
| **7** | **Renewal (refill) instructions + Prescriber's signature, address & registration number** | Whether & how often the prescription may be repeated (**Rep./ NR**), and the prescriber's **signature in ink** — ⭐ without the signature the prescription is **invalid** |

🧠 **MEMORY HOOK for the 7 parts — "Date, Patient, Rx, Ingredients, Pharmacist, Patient-directions,
Signature."**
Or: **"D-P-S-I-S-S-S"** → **D**ate, **P**atient particulars, **S**uperscription, **I**nscription,
**S**ubscription, **S**ignatura, **S**ignature (+ refill).

### ⭐⭐ THE FOUR PARTS OF THE INSCRIPTION (the classic question)

| Component | 📌 Definition / role | Example (in the mixture above) |
|---|---|---|
| **1. BASIS (Base)** | ⭐ **The chief/ principal active drug** — the one intended to produce the main therapeutic effect | **Ammonium chloride** (expectorant) |
| **2. ADJUVANT (Adjuvans)** | ⭐ A substance that **assists, enhances or supplements the action of the basis** (a second active drug acting in the same direction) | **Sodium citrate** (also expectorant) |
| **3. CORRECTIVE (Corrigent/ Corrigens)** | ⭐ A substance that **corrects or counteracts an undesirable property of the basis** — its unpleasant **taste, odour, colour**, irritancy or side-effect | **Liquorice liquid extract** (masks the salty, unpleasant taste); also flavours, sweeteners, colours, antacids added to reduce gastric irritation |
| **4. VEHICLE (Excipient/ Vehiculum/ Diluent)** | ⭐ The **inert medium** that **dissolves, suspends or dilutes** the other ingredients and **brings the preparation to the required volume/ bulk**, and makes it convenient to take | **Chloroform water** (also water, syrup, alcohol, glycerin; for powders — **lactose**; for ointments — the base) |

🧠 **MEMORY HOOK — "BAC-V"** → **B**asis, **A**djuvant, **C**orrective, **V**ehicle.

⚠️ **TRAP** — The **adjuvant helps the basis**; the **corrective corrects a defect of the basis**.
The **vehicle is pharmacologically inert**.

## 5.3 TYPES / KINDS OF PRESCRIPTIONS

| Type | Description | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Precompounded (pre-compounded/ "brand") prescription** | ⭐ Prescriber orders a **ready-made, already manufactured product** by its **proprietary or official name**; the pharmacist only counts/ pours & labels — **no compounding** | "Tab. Paracetamol 500 mg — 10 tablets" or "Syp. Crocin 60 mL" |
| **Extemporaneous (magistral) prescription** | ⭐ Prescriber writes out the **individual ingredients & quantities**, which the pharmacist must **weigh, measure & compound** | The ammonium chloride mixture above |
| **Official/ Pharmacopoeial prescription** | Orders an **official preparation by its pharmacopoeial name** | "Mist. Carminativa 100 mL", "Ung. Zinci Oxidi 30 g" |
| **Verbal/ Oral order** | Spoken order (telephone, ward round) — ⚠️ must be **reduced to writing at once and countersigned**; ⭐ **NOT permitted for Schedule X/ narcotic drugs** | Emergency in hospital |
| **Standing order** | A pre-approved written protocol for a defined situation in a hospital/ ward | Ward stock protocol |
| **Institutional/ Hospital prescription** | Written on the **in-patient case sheet/ medication chart**; may be a **unit-dose order** | In-patient chart |
| **Repeat prescription** | Authorised for repetition a stated number of times | "Rep. × 3" |

## 5.4 HOW A PRESCRIPTION SHOULD BE WRITTEN ⭐
### (the rules the prescriber must follow — and which the pharmacist must check)

1. Write **legibly, in ink**, on a **printed prescription form** bearing the prescriber's name,
   qualifications, **registration number** and address; ⚠️ **never in pencil**.
2. Enter the **date** and the patient's **full name, age (and weight for a child), sex and address**.
3. Write **one prescription per patient**; do not mix two patients on one form.
4. Use the ℞ symbol, then the drugs, **one drug per line**.
5. ⭐ Prefer the **generic (non-proprietary/ official) name**; use **capital/ block letters** for
   drug names to prevent misreading.
6. State the **strength, dosage form and total quantity** unambiguously.
7. ⭐ **Use the METRIC system only.** Avoid the apothecary system, and avoid decimals where
   possible.
   * ⭐ Always write a **leading zero**: `0.5 mL` — never `.5 mL`.
   * ⭐ **Never write a trailing zero**: write `5 mg`, never `5.0 mg` (a missed decimal point
     causes a **10-fold overdose**).
   * Prefer **micrograms written out in full** ("micrograms"), not "µg" or "mcg" — ⚠️ "µg" can be
     misread as "mg" (**1000-fold error**).
   * Write **units** in full, not "U" (can be read as a zero).
8. Give **complete, quantitative directions** (dose, frequency, route, timing relative to food,
   duration). ⚠️ **"As directed", "use as before", "SOS" alone are inadequate.**
9. **Avoid ambiguous, non-standard or "dangerous" abbreviations** (see §5.7).
10. **Do not leave blank space** between the last drug and the signature (prevents additions).
11. State whether the prescription **may be repeated** and how often.
12. ⭐ **Sign in ink**, with the prescriber's **registration number**.
13. For **Schedule X/ narcotic** drugs: write **in duplicate**, spell out the **quantity in words
    as well as figures**, and never leave the total open.
14. Cross out any error, rewrite, and **initial and date the correction** — never overwrite.

📌 **THE CLASSICAL "ORDER OF WRITING" IN AN EXTEMPORANEOUS PRESCRIPTION** —
**Basis → Adjuvant → Corrective → Vehicle**, i.e. the active drugs first and the vehicle
("**ad 100 mL**") last.

## 5.5 PRESCRIPTION READING & HANDLING ⭐⭐
### (the pharmacist's side — the classic "handling of prescription" question)

📌 **HANDLING OF A PRESCRIPTION** is the systematic procedure a pharmacist follows from the moment
the prescription is received until the medicine is delivered.

### ⭐ THE SEVEN STEPS OF HANDLING A PRESCRIPTION

| # | Step | What is done |
|---|---|---|
| **1** | **RECEIVING** | Receive the prescription **courteously & confidently** from the patient. ⚠️ Never show surprise, doubt or alarm in front of the patient; never criticise the prescriber |
| **2** | **READING & CHECKING** | Read the **whole** prescription carefully, **twice**. Check: legality (signature, date, RMP), completeness of all 7 parts, correct drug name & strength, **dose against the maximum dose**, dosage form, quantity, **incompatibilities**, patient's age vs dose, duplication & interactions. ⚠️ **Never dispense from a partly-read prescription** |
| **3** | **LABELLING** *(prepare the label now)* | Write/ type the label **before** compounding, so the directions are transcribed accurately from the signatura while the prescription is in front of you |
| **4** | **COLLECTING / NUMBERING & PRICING** | Give the prescription a **serial number**, enter it in the register, collect the ingredients & apparatus (**check each label 3 times**), and price it |
| **5** | **COMPOUNDING / PREPARING (dispensing proper)** | Do the **calculations**, weigh & measure accurately, follow the correct **order of mixing**, prepare the dosage form, and **transfer to the container** |
| **6** | **PACKING & PRESENTATION** | Choose the correct **container** (plain vs ribbed), affix the label (white vs red) **squarely**, add **auxiliary labels**, wrap/ box neatly |
| **7** | **RECHECKING & DELIVERY** | ⭐ **Final check** of the finished medicine against the prescription (right patient/drug/dose/form/label), then deliver with **patient counselling**; file/ return the prescription and complete the records |

🧠 **MEMORY HOOK — "R-R-L-C-C-P-R"**: **R**eceive, **R**ead, **L**abel, **C**ollect & number,
**C**ompound, **P**ack, **R**echeck & deliver.

### ⭐ Difficulties in reading prescriptions — and how to resolve them

| Difficulty | Correct action |
|---|---|
| **Illegible handwriting** | Read the whole prescription for **context** (dose, form, directions often identify the drug); compare with the strength & the patient's condition; ⭐ **if still in doubt, TELEPHONE/ CONTACT THE PRESCRIBER**. ⚠️ **Never guess** |
| **Look-alike drug names** (see §5.7) | Confirm with the prescriber; use the strength & indication as a cross-check |
| **Non-standard/ unknown abbreviation** | Consult reference books; ask the prescriber |
| **Overdose / dose exceeds the maximum** | ⭐ **Do not dispense.** Contact the prescriber. If the prescriber **confirms** the higher dose deliberately, the classical rule is that the prescriber should **write the dose in words and underline it, or write "!" or "sic"** — otherwise refuse |
| **Underdose (sub-therapeutic)** | Query the prescriber |
| **An ingredient is missing / a quantity is missing** | Query the prescriber; ⚠️ never supply your own quantity |
| **An incompatibility is present** | Identify its type (physical/ chemical/ therapeutic — Ch. 8); **correct it only if it can be corrected without changing the therapeutic effect**, otherwise **consult the prescriber** and **record the change** |
| **Obvious clerical error** (e.g. "mg" written for "g") | Confirm with the prescriber |
| **Out-of-stock drug / brand** | ⚠️ **Do not substitute** another drug or brand on your own authority; inform the patient & contact the prescriber |
| **Suspected forgery or alteration** | Check the signature & register; ⭐ **refuse and report**; note the details |
| **Prescription for a Schedule X drug without the legal formalities** | **Refuse to dispense** |
| **Unsigned prescription** | **Refuse** (except as permitted for a genuine emergency, when the prescriber must sign later) |
| **Patient asks what the medicine is for / for a repeat of someone else's prescription** | Counsel appropriately; ⚠️ maintain **professional secrecy** and do not dispense a prescription-only medicine without a valid prescription |

⚠️ **THE GOLDEN RULE OF PRESCRIPTION READING**
> **NEVER guess, NEVER assume, NEVER alter a prescription on your own initiative.
> When in doubt — CONSULT THE PRESCRIBER, and record what was agreed, with the date and your
> initials.**

### ⭐ Prescription errors — classification

| Type of error | Example |
|---|---|
| **Prescribing error** | Wrong drug, wrong dose, wrong route, illegible writing, drug interaction, contraindicated drug, incomplete order |
| **Dispensing error** | Wrong drug/ strength selected, wrong quantity, wrong calculation, wrong label/ directions, wrong patient, contamination, incorrect compounding |
| **Administration error** | Wrong time, wrong route, missed dose, wrong technique (patient/nurse level) |
| **Transcription error** | Copying mistake from the chart to the label |
| **Omission error** | Dose or drug left out |

⭐ **Common causes of dispensing errors:** look-alike/ sound-alike names, look-alike packaging,
**decimal-point errors**, misread abbreviations, interruptions & workload, dispensing from memory,
failure to check the label three times, and failure to do a final check.

## 5.7 ⚠️ DANGEROUS ABBREVIATIONS & LOOK-ALIKE NAMES
### (the "error-prevention" section — increasingly examined)

⭐ **ABBREVIATIONS THAT SHOULD NEVER BE USED**

| Do NOT write | Because it is misread as | Write instead |
|---|---|---|
| **U** (for units) | 0, 4, cc → 10-fold overdose | **"units"** |
| **IU** | IV or 10 | **"international units"** |
| **µg** | mg (**1000-fold error**) | **"microgram"** |
| **o.d.** (meaning once daily) | *oculus dexter* = right eye | **"once daily"** |
| **q.d.** | q.i.d. (4 times) | **"daily"** |
| **q.o.d.** | q.d. / q.i.d. | **"every other day"** |
| **.5 mg** (no leading zero) | 5 mg (**10-fold overdose**) | **0.5 mg** |
| **5.0 mg** (trailing zero) | 50 mg (**10-fold overdose**) | **5 mg** |
| **cc** | u (units) | **mL** |
| **AZT, MTX, HCT, 6-MP** (drug-name acronyms) | Different drugs | **Full drug name** |
| **@** | 2 or 0 | **"at"** |
| **/ (slash)** | the numeral 1 | **"per"** |
| **gr** (grain) | g (gram) | **use metric — mg or g** |
| **SS** | 55 or ss (a half) | **"one-half" or "sliding scale"** |
| **TIW / BIW** | three times a day | **"3 times weekly"** |
| **Apothecary symbols (℥ ℨ ♏)** | Various | **Metric units** |

⭐ **LOOK-ALIKE / SOUND-ALIKE (LASA) DRUG NAMES — classic pairs**

| Pair | Note |
|---|---|
| **Chlorpromazine / Chlorpropamide** | Antipsychotic vs antidiabetic |
| **Chlorpheniramine / Chlorpromazine** | Antihistamine vs antipsychotic |
| **Prednisone / Prednisolone** | Different potency |
| **Dopamine / Dobutamine** | Different haemodynamics |
| **Hydralazine / Hydroxyzine** | Antihypertensive vs antihistamine |
| **Clonidine / Clonazepam / Klonopin** | Antihypertensive vs antiepileptic |
| **Quinine / Quinidine** | Antimalarial vs antiarrhythmic |
| **Digoxin / Digitoxin** | Different half-lives |
| **Methotrexate / Metolazone / Metronidazole** | Wholly different |
| **Cefotaxime / Cefoxitin / Ceftriaxone / Cefuroxime** | Cephalosporins |
| **Amoxicillin / Ampicillin** | Similar but distinct |
| **Glibenclamide / Glipizide / Gliclazide** | Sulphonylureas |
| **Ranitidine / Rifampicin (Ranit-/ Rifa-)** | Distinct |
| **Losartan / Lovastatin** | Distinct |
| **Tramadol / Trazodone / Toradol** | Distinct |
| **Vinblastine / Vincristine** | ⚠️ Cytotoxics — **fatal if interchanged** |
| **Epinephrine / Ephedrine** | Distinct potency |
| **Fluoxetine / Fluvoxamine / Fluphenazine** | Distinct |

## 5.8 WORKED EXAMPLES — READING A PRESCRIPTION ⭐

### Example 1 — an extemporaneous mixture
```
℞
   Potassium citrate           .......... 20 g
   Sod. bicarb.                .......... 10 g
   Syr. Zingiberis             .......... 20 mL
   Aq. chlorof.            ad  .......... 200 mL
   M. ft. mist.
   Sig.: 15 mL t.d.s. p.c. c. aq.
```
**Reading:** Mix and make a **mixture** of 200 mL containing potassium citrate 20 g, sodium
bicarbonate 10 g and syrup of ginger 20 mL, made up to volume with chloroform water.
**Label:** *"Take **15 mL (one tablespoonful) three times a day after food, with water**."*
**Analysis:** Basis = potassium citrate (+ sodium bicarbonate as adjuvant);
Corrective = syrup of ginger (flavour & carminative); Vehicle = chloroform water.
**Labels:** white label; **"Shake well"** not needed (a true solution).
**Number of doses:** 200 ÷ 15 = **13 doses** (≈ 4½ days at t.d.s.).

### Example 2 — divided powders
```
℞
   Aspirin                     .......... 300 mg
   Sod. bicarb.                .......... 200 mg
   Lactose                     .......... q.s.
   M. ft. pulv. Mitt. tal. dos. no. XII
   Sig.: One powder q. 6 h. p.c. p.r.n. pain
```
**Reading:** Mix and make a powder; **send 12 such doses**; each powder contains aspirin 300 mg +
sodium bicarbonate 200 mg with sufficient lactose as diluent.
**Label:** *"Take **one powder every six hours after food, when required for pain**."*
**Total to weigh:** aspirin 300 mg × 12 = **3.6 g**; sodium bicarbonate 200 mg × 12 = **2.4 g**.

### Example 3 — an ointment
```
℞
   Salicylic acid              .......... 3 %
   Sulphur praecip.            .......... 6 %
   Ung. emulsificans        ad .......... 30 g
   M. ft. ung.
   Sig.: Applic. loc. aff. o.n.  Ad us. ext.
```
**Reading:** Make **30 g of an ointment** containing 3 % salicylic acid (**0.9 g**) and 6 %
precipitated sulphur (**1.8 g**) in emulsifying ointment (**27.3 g**).
**Label:** *"Apply to the affected part **every night**."* + ⭐ **"FOR EXTERNAL USE ONLY"**
(**RED label**, wide-mouth jar/ tube).

### Example 4 — an "external" liquid
```
℞
   Ol. terebinth.              .......... 20 mL
   Camphor                     .......... 5 g
   Ol. oliv.                ad .......... 100 mL
   M. ft. lin.  Sig.: Ad part. dolent. c. frictione.  Ad us. ext.
```
**Reading:** Make **100 mL of a liniment**; apply to the painful parts **with friction (rubbing)**.
**Container/label:** ⭐ **RIBBED (fluted) bottle, RED label, "FOR EXTERNAL USE ONLY", "Shake well"**
(camphor + oils), and **"Do not apply to broken skin"**.

## 5.9 CHAPTER 5 — RAPID REVISION SHEET

* **Prescription** = written order of an RMP to a pharmacist to compound & dispense a medicine for
  a **named patient**; it is a **legal document**.
* ⭐ **Seven parts:** ① **Date** ② **Patient's name, age, sex, address** ③ **Superscription (℞)**
  ④ **Inscription (body — the ingredients)** ⑤ **Subscription (directions to the PHARMACIST)**
  ⑥ **Signatura / Sig. (directions to the PATIENT)** ⑦ **Refill instructions + prescriber's
  signature**.
* ⭐ **℞ = "Recipe" = "Take thou"**; also regarded as a dedication to **Jupiter**, god of healing.
* ⭐ **Inscription has four components: BASIS (chief drug) → ADJUVANT (helps the basis) →
  CORRECTIVE (corrects taste/odour/side-effect) → VEHICLE (inert diluent to volume).**
* **Subscription = to the pharmacist; Signatura = to the patient.** ⚠️ Do not interchange them.
* **Precompounded prescription** = ready-made product; **extemporaneous** = ingredients to be
  compounded.
* ⭐ **Handling steps: Receive → Read & check → Label → Collect/number/price → Compound →
  Pack → Recheck & deliver.**
* ⚠️ **Never guess an illegible prescription; never alter it; CONTACT THE PRESCRIBER and record the
  change with date & initials.**
* **Overdose above the maximum dose → do not dispense; confirm with the prescriber.**
* **Schedule X prescription: in duplicate, quantity in words, one copy retained 2 years, never
  repeated.**
* **Only a registered pharmacist may dispense — Pharmacy Act 1948, Sec. 42.**
* 🔤 **Frequency:** o.d. = once daily · b.d./b.i.d. = twice · **t.d.s./t.i.d. = 3 times** ·
  **q.d.s./q.i.d. = 4 times** · q.q.h. = every 4 hours · o.m. = every morning · o.n. = every night ·
  **h.s. = at bedtime** · alt. die = alternate days.
* 🔤 **Timing:** **a.c. = before food · p.c. = after food** · **p.r.n. = when required** ·
  **s.o.s. = if necessary** · **stat. = immediately** · ut dict. = as directed ·
  **q.s. = sufficient quantity** · ad lib. = freely.
* 🔤 **Route:** p.o. = by mouth · s.l. = sublingual · **p.r. = per rectum · p.v. = per vaginam** ·
  **a.d. = right ear · a.s./a.l. = left ear · a.u. = both ears** ·
  **o.d. = RIGHT EYE (in eye Rx!) · o.s./o.l. = left eye · o.u. = both eyes** ·
  **gutt. = drops** · **ad us. ext. = for external use** ·
  **ad part. dolent. = to the painful part**.
* 🔤 **Instructions:** **M. = mix · Ft./Fiat = let it be made · M. et ft. = mix and make ·
  Mitte = send · D.t.d. = give such doses · Div. in p. aeq. = divide into equal parts ·
  Sig. = label · Agit. ante sum. = shake before taking · aa/ana = of each · ad = up to ·
  ss = a half · sec. art. = according to the art · non rep. = do not repeat ·
  in vitro nig. = in an amber bottle**.
* 🔤 **Forms:** Mist. = mixture · Tinct. = tincture · Ung. = ointment · Lin. = liniment ·
  Lot. = lotion · Pig. = paint · Emp. = plaster · Pulv. = powder · Inf. = infusion ·
  Dec. = decoction · Mucil. = mucilage · Neb. = spray · Vap. = inhalation ·
  Haust. = draught · Garg. = gargle · Collut. = mouthwash · Collyr. = eye lotion ·
  Aq. dest. = distilled water · Co./Comp. = compound · Dil. = dilute · Fort. = strong ·
  Levis = light · Ponderosus = heavy · Recens = fresh.
* ⚠️ **Dangerous:** never write **U, IU, µg, cc, @, .5, 5.0, o.d. for once-daily, gr for gram.**
* ⚠️ **"gr" = GRAIN (65 mg), "g" = GRAM** — a 15-fold difference.
* ⚠️ **LASA pairs:** vinBLAStine/vinCRIStine, predniSONE/prednisoLONE, DOPamine/DOBUTamine,
  chlorpromazine/chlorpropamide, quinine/quinidine, carbamazepine/carbimazole.
