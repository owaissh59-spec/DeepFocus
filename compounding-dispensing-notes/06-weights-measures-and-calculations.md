## 6.1 THE THREE SYSTEMS OF WEIGHTS & MEASURES

📌 Three systems have been used in pharmacy:

| System | Used for | Status |
|---|---|---|
| **1. METRIC (SI / decimal) system** | ⭐ **The only legally recognised system in India** (Standards of Weights & Measures Act) — used in IP, BP, USP | **Current** |
| **2. IMPERIAL / APOTHECARIES' system** | Old British pharmaceutical system — grains, drachms, ounces, minims | **Obsolete** (but examined!) |
| **3. AVOIRDUPOIS system** | Commercial weighing of goods — ounces & pounds | Commercial |

⭐ **Why the metric system is preferred:** it is **decimal** (all conversions are by powers of 10),
**simple, universal, less liable to error**, and legally required.

---

### 6.1.1 ⭐ THE METRIC SYSTEM

📌 **Fundamental units:** **metre (length), gram (mass), litre (volume)**.

🔢 **METRIC WEIGHT (MASS)**

| Unit | Symbol | Equivalent |
|---|---|---|
| **1 kilogram** | kg | **1000 g** |
| **1 hectogram** | hg | 100 g |
| **1 decagram** | dag | 10 g |
| **1 GRAM** | **g** | **1 g = the base unit** |
| **1 decigram** | dg | 0.1 g |
| **1 centigram** | cg | 0.01 g |
| **1 milligram** | mg | **0.001 g** → **1 g = 1000 mg** |
| **1 microgram** | µg (mcg) | **0.000001 g** → **1 mg = 1000 µg** |
| **1 nanogram** | ng | 0.001 µg |

🧠 **MEMORY LADDER:** **kg → g → mg → µg → ng**, each step **× 1000**.
🔢 **1 kg = 1000 g = 1 000 000 mg = 1 000 000 000 µg.**

🔢 **METRIC VOLUME (CAPACITY)**

| Unit | Symbol | Equivalent |
|---|---|---|
| **1 kilolitre** | kL | 1000 L |
| **1 LITRE** | **L** | **1000 mL** |
| **1 decilitre** | dL | 100 mL |
| **1 centilitre** | cL | 10 mL |
| **1 millilitre** | mL | **0.001 L** |
| **1 microlitre** | µL | 0.001 mL |

⭐ **1 mL = 1 cubic centimetre (cc/ cm³)** — for practical purposes identical.
⭐ **1 mL of WATER at 4 °C weighs exactly 1 g** → hence **1 L of water = 1 kg**.
⚠️ **TRAP** — this weight–volume identity holds **only for water**. For any other liquid,
`weight = volume × specific gravity`.

🔢 **METRIC LENGTH:** 1 m = 100 cm = 1000 mm; 1 cm = 10 mm; 1 mm = 1000 µm; 1 µm = 1000 nm;
**1 Å (Angstrom) = 10⁻¹⁰ m = 0.1 nm**.

**20 grains = 1 scruple · 3 scruples = 1 drachm · 8 drachms = 1 ounce · 12 ounces = 1 pound.**

🔢 **IMPERIAL FLUID (LIQUID) MEASURE**

| Unit | Symbol | Equivalent |
|---|---|---|
| **60 minims (♏)** | fℨ | **1 fluid drachm** |
| **8 fluid drachms (480 minims)** | f℥ | **1 fluid ounce** |

🧠 **MEMORY CHAIN — "60-8-20-8"**:
**60 minims = 1 fluid drachm · 8 fl drachms = 1 fluid ounce · 20 fl oz = 1 pint ·
8 pints = 1 gallon.**

⚠️ **TRAP — the IMPERIAL vs U.S. pint!**
* ⭐ **1 Imperial (British) pint = 20 fluid ounces = 568 mL** (often taken as **≈ 570 mL** or, in

* **1 U.S. pint = 16 fluid ounces = 473 mL.**
* **1 Imperial gallon = 4546 mL (≈ 4.5 L)**; **1 U.S. gallon = 3785 mL (≈ 3.8 L)**.

🔢 **AVOIRDUPOIS WEIGHT** (commercial)

| Unit | Equivalent |
|---|---|
| **1 ounce (av.)** | **437.5 grains = 28.35 g** |
| **16 ounces** | **1 pound (av.) = 7000 grains = 453.6 g** |
| **14 pounds** | 1 stone |
| **112 pounds** | 1 hundredweight (cwt) |
| **20 cwt** | 1 ton |

⚠️ **TRAP — the two "ounces" and two "pounds" are DIFFERENT!**

| | **Apothecary/ Troy** | **Avoirdupois** |
|---|---|---|
| **1 ounce** | **480 grains = 31.1 g** | **437.5 grains = 28.35 g** |
| **1 pound** | **5760 grains (12 oz) = 373.2 g** | **7000 grains (16 oz) = 453.6 g** |

🧠 **MEMORY HOOK** — "**The apothecary's ounce is HEAVIER (480 gr), but his pound is LIGHTER
(5760 gr).**" *(He has only 12 ounces to the pound, not 16.)*
🧠 **"A pound of feathers (av., 7000 gr) is heavier than a pound of gold (troy, 5760 gr) —
but an ounce of gold is heavier than an ounce of feathers."**

### 6.2.1 ⚠️ EXACT vs PRACTICAL (APPROXIMATE) EQUIVALENTS

📌 **A very frequently examined distinction.**

| Purpose | Which equivalents to use |
|---|---|
| ⭐ **Calculating a formula/ converting a prescription for compounding** (i.e. when the *quantity of drug* matters) | ⭐ **EXACT equivalents** (1 gr = 64.8 mg; 1 fl oz = 28.4 mL; 1 oz = 31.1 g) |
| ⭐ **Prescribing/ dispensing DOSES & directions to the patient**, and for **container sizes** | ⭐ **PRACTICAL (approximate) equivalents** (1 gr = 65 mg; 1 fl oz = 30 mL; 1 tsp = 5 mL) |

⭐ **THE COMMON "PRACTICAL" SET used in dispensing** 🔢

| Practical equivalence | |
|---|---|
| **1 grain ≈ 65 mg** | **1 g ≈ 15 grains** |
| **1 minim ≈ 0.06 mL** | **1 mL ≈ 17 minims** |
| **1 fluid drachm ≈ 4 mL** | **1 fluid ounce ≈ 30 mL** |
| **1 ounce ≈ 30 g** | **1 pint ≈ 500 mL (600 mL in older texts)** |
| **1 teaspoonful = 5 mL** | **1 tablespoonful = 15 mL** |
| **1 dessertspoonful = 8 mL** | **1 drop ≈ 0.05 mL (20 drops = 1 mL)** |

🔢 **WORKED EXAMPLE — converting an old prescription**
*Convert:* `℞ Sod. bicarb. gr. x; Aq. ad f℥ iv` *into metric.*
* gr x = 10 grains × 64.8 = **648 mg (0.648 g)** [exact] or 10 × 65 = **650 mg** [practical].
* f℥ iv = 4 fluid ounces × 28.4 = **113.6 mL** [exact] or 4 × 30 = **120 mL** [practical].
→ Metric prescription: **Sodium bicarbonate 650 mg, water to 120 mL.**

## 6.3 ⭐⭐ PERCENTAGE STRENGTHS & RATIO STRENGTH

### 6.3.1 THE FOUR KINDS OF PERCENTAGE — learn the definitions exactly

| Expression | 📌 Definition | Used for | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| **% w/w** (weight in weight) | ⭐ **Number of GRAMS of solute in 100 GRAMS of the product** | ⭐ **Solid in solid, or semisolid** — ointments, creams, pastes, powders; also very viscous liquids | 2 % w/w salicylic acid ointment = 2 g in 100 g of ointment |
| **% w/v** (weight in volume) | ⭐ **Number of GRAMS of solute in 100 mL of the product** | ⭐ **Solid dissolved/ dispersed in a liquid** — the commonest in pharmacy: injections, lotions, mixtures, syrups | 0.9 % w/v NaCl = 0.9 g in 100 mL |
| **% v/v** (volume in volume) | ⭐ **Number of mL of solute in 100 mL of the product** | ⭐ **Liquid in liquid** — alcohol, volatile oils, glycerin solutions | 70 % v/v alcohol = 70 mL alcohol in 100 mL |
| **% v/w** (volume in weight) | ⭐ **Number of mL of solute in 100 GRAMS of the product** | ⭐ **Liquid in a solid/ semisolid** — rare; e.g. a volatile oil in an ointment or a powder | 2 % v/w coal tar in an ointment |

⚠️ **GOLDEN RULE:** *When a pharmacopoeial preparation says only **"per cent"** without a
qualifier:*
* ⭐ for **solutions of a solid in a liquid** → **% w/v**
* ⭐ for **solutions of a liquid in a liquid** → **% v/v**
* ⭐ for **mixtures of solids & semisolids** → **% w/w**
* ⭐ for **gases** → **% v/v**

🔢 **THE ONE FORMULA THAT SOLVES EVERYTHING**
```
              Quantity of active ingredient
Strength % = ───────────────────────────────── × 100
                Total quantity of product
```
And its rearrangement:
```
Quantity of ingredient  =  (Strength % × Total quantity) ÷ 100
```

🔢 **WORKED EXAMPLE 1**
*How much potassium permanganate is needed to make 500 mL of a 0.05 % w/v solution?*
`= (0.05 × 500)/100 = 0.25 g = 250 mg` ✔

🔢 **WORKED EXAMPLE 2**
*What is the percentage strength of a solution containing 6 g of boric acid in 300 mL?*
`= (6/300) × 100 = 2 % w/v` ✔

🔢 **WORKED EXAMPLE 3 (w/w)**
*How much sulphur is present in 60 g of a 6 % w/w ointment?*
`= (6 × 60)/100 = 3.6 g of sulphur` (+ 56.4 g of base) ✔

🔢 **WORKED EXAMPLE 4 (v/v)**
*How much 90 % alcohol is required to make 250 mL of a 45 % v/v solution?*
`Alcohol needed = (45 × 250)/100 = 112.5 mL of absolute alcohol equivalent`
`Volume of 90 % alcohol = (112.5 × 100)/90 = 125 mL` → dilute to 250 mL with water ✔

### 6.3.2 ⭐ RATIO STRENGTH

📌 **Ratio strength** expresses a **very dilute** concentration as **1 part of the active ingredient
in a stated number of parts of the total product** — written as **1 : x** or "1 in x".

🔢 **CONVERSION FORMULAE**
```
                        100                                    100
Ratio strength 1 : x  →  ─── % ;        Percentage p %  →  1 : ───
                          x                                     p
```

🔢 **CONVERSION TABLE (memorise these!)**

| Ratio strength | Percentage | Ratio strength | Percentage |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1 : 10** | 10 % | **1 : 1000** | ⭐ **0.1 %** |
| **1 : 20** | 5 % | **1 : 2000** | 0.05 % |
| **1 : 50** | 2 % | **1 : 5000** | 0.02 % |
| **1 : 100** | 1 % | **1 : 10 000** | ⭐ **0.01 %** |
| **1 : 200** | 0.5 % | **1 : 100 000** | 0.001 % |
| **1 : 500** | 0.2 % | **1 : 1 000 000** | ⭐ **0.0001 % = 1 ppm** |

⭐ **Classic pharmaceutical ratio strengths:**
**Adrenaline injection 1 : 1000 (= 1 mg/mL)**; adrenaline for anaphylaxis **1 : 10 000** dilution;
**Potassium permanganate lotion 1 : 5000 to 1 : 10 000**; **Benzalkonium chloride 1 : 1000**;
**Lignocaine with adrenaline 1 : 80 000 (dental)**.

🔢 **WORKED EXAMPLE 5**
*Express 1 : 2500 as a percentage.* → `100/2500 = 0.04 % w/v` ✔
🔢 **WORKED EXAMPLE 6**
*Express 0.25 % as a ratio strength.* → `100/0.25 = 400` → **1 : 400** ✔
🔢 **WORKED EXAMPLE 7**
*How much KMnO₄ is needed for 2 litres of a 1 : 5000 solution?*
1 : 5000 = 1 g in 5000 mL → for 2000 mL = `2000/5000 = 0.4 g = 400 mg` ✔

### 6.3.3 PARTS PER MILLION (ppm) & other expressions
🔢 **1 ppm = 1 part in 10⁶ = 0.0001 % = 1 mg per litre (for dilute aqueous solutions) = 1 µg/mL.**
* **mg/mL ↔ %:** ⭐ **1 % w/v = 10 mg/mL** (and **1 mg/mL = 0.1 %**).
* **mg % (mg per 100 mL)** — used for blood levels: 100 mg % = 1 mg/mL.
* **Molarity (M)** = moles of solute per **litre of solution**.
* **Molality (m)** = moles of solute per **kg of solvent**.
* **Normality (N)** = gram-equivalents per litre. `N = M × basicity/ acidity (valency)`.
* **Mole fraction** = moles of a component ÷ total moles.

🔢 **WORKED EXAMPLE 8**
*A 5 % w/v dextrose infusion — how many mg/mL and how many g in a 500 mL bottle?*
5 % w/v = **50 mg/mL**; in 500 mL = **25 g of dextrose** ✔

## 6.4 ⭐⭐ DILUTION & CONCENTRATION

📌 **The principle of dilution:** *when a preparation is diluted, the **amount of active ingredient
stays the same** — only the total quantity changes.*

🔢 **THE UNIVERSAL DILUTION EQUATION**
```
          Q₁ × C₁  =  Q₂ × C₂
```
where Q = quantity (g or mL) and C = concentration (% or ratio strength).
🧠 **"Strong × its quantity = Weak × its quantity."**

🔢 **WORKED EXAMPLE 9**
*How much water must be added to 250 mL of a 10 % w/v solution to make it 4 % w/v?*
`Q₂ = (250 × 10)/4 = 625 mL final volume` → **water to add = 625 − 250 = 375 mL** ✔

🔢 **WORKED EXAMPLE 10**
*What is the strength when 100 mL of 1 : 200 solution is diluted to 500 mL?*
1 : 200 = 0.5 % → `C₂ = (100 × 0.5)/500 = 0.1 %` = **1 : 1000** ✔

🔢 **WORKED EXAMPLE 11 (concentrating)**
*How much of a 95 % v/v alcohol is needed to prepare 1 litre of 70 % v/v alcohol?*
`Q₁ = (1000 × 70)/95 = 736.8 mL` of 95 % alcohol, then **water to 1000 mL** ✔
⚠️ **Note:** because alcohol & water **contract on mixing**, you must **make up to volume**, not
simply add 263 mL of water.

🔢 **WORKED EXAMPLE 12 (dilution of a solid/ ointment)**
*How much white soft paraffin must be added to 30 g of a 10 % w/w sulphur ointment to reduce it to
2 % w/w?*
`Q₂ = (30 × 10)/2 = 150 g` → **paraffin to add = 150 − 30 = 120 g** ✔

### ⭐ DILUTION OF ALCOHOL & "PROOF SPIRIT"

📌 **Proof spirit** — the old British standard: a spirit which at **51 °F (10.6 °C)** weighs
**exactly 12/13ths of an equal volume of water**.
🔢 **Proof spirit contains ⭐ 57.1 % v/v (49.24 % w/w) of absolute alcohol.**
* **"Over proof (O.P.)"** = stronger than proof; **"Under proof (U.P.)"** = weaker than proof.
* 🔢 **100 volumes of proof spirit = 57.1 volumes of absolute alcohol.**
* 🔢 **Absolute alcohol = 100 % = 175.25° proof (i.e. 75.25 % O.P.).**
* 🔢 **Rectified spirit = 90 % v/v alcohol** (IP); **Absolute alcohol = 99.5 % v/v**;
  **Industrial methylated spirit = 95 % alcohol + 5 % wood naphtha**;
  **Surgical spirit** = methylated spirit + castor oil + methyl salicylate + diethyl phthalate.
* 🔢 **Dilute alcohols of the IP: 90 %, 70 %, 45 %, 25 %, 20 % v/v.**

🔢 **WORKED EXAMPLE 13**
*Convert 70 % v/v alcohol into degrees proof.*
`Degrees proof = (% v/v × 100)/57.1 = (70 × 100)/57.1 = 122.6° proof` → i.e. **22.6 % over proof** ✔

🔢 **Percentage w/w ↔ v/v for alcohol** (needs specific gravity):
```
% v/v = (% w/w × sp. gr. of the solution) ÷ sp. gr. of absolute alcohol (0.7936)
```

## 6.5 ⭐⭐ ALLIGATION

📌 **ALLIGATION** is an arithmetical method of solving problems that involve **mixing two or more
preparations of different strengths** of the **same** substance.

### A. ALLIGATION MEDIAL
📌 **Alligation medial** is used to find the **strength (average/ mean) of a MIXTURE** made by
mixing **known quantities of known strengths**.
🔢 **Method:** multiply each quantity by its strength, add the products, divide by the total
quantity.

🔢 **WORKED EXAMPLE 14**
*What is the strength of a mixture of 300 mL of 40 % w/v, 200 mL of 20 % w/v and 500 mL of 10 % w/v
solutions?*
```
300 × 40 = 12 000
200 × 20 =  4 000
500 × 10 =  5 000
Total   1000 mL   21 000
Strength = 21 000 ÷ 1000 = 21 % w/v ✔
```

### B. ALLIGATION ALTERNATE ⭐⭐
📌 **Alligation alternate** is used to find the **PROPORTION in which two (or more) preparations of
known strength must be mixed** to obtain a preparation of a **required intermediate strength**.

⭐ **THE CROSS (X) METHOD — the classic layout**
```
     Higher strength (H)              (D − L) parts of the HIGHER
                        ╲          ╱
                         Desired (D)
                        ╱          ╲
     Lower strength (L)               (H − D) parts of the LOWER
```
🔢 **Rule:** *Write the higher and lower strengths on the left, the desired strength in the middle;
subtract **diagonally** (always smaller from larger); the difference obtained on each diagonal is
the number of parts of the strength on the **same row**.*

🔢 **WORKED EXAMPLE 15**
*In what proportion must 20 % and 5 % ointments be mixed to make a 10 % ointment?*
```
     20 %                     (10 − 5) = 5 parts of 20 %
          ╲              ╱
            10 %
          ╱              ╲
      5 %                     (20 − 10) = 10 parts of 5 %
```
→ **Ratio = 5 : 10 = 1 : 2** → mix **1 part of the 20 % with 2 parts of the 5 %.** ✔
*Check (alligation medial):* (1×20 + 2×5)/3 = 30/3 = 10 % ✔

🔢 **WORKED EXAMPLE 16 (with a total quantity)**
*Prepare 900 g of a 12 % w/w ointment from 25 % and 5 % ointments.*
```
     25 %              (12 − 5)  =  7 parts of 25 %
          ╲        ╱
            12 %
          ╱        ╲
      5 %              (25 − 12) = 13 parts of  5 %
   Total = 7 + 13 = 20 parts
```
* 25 % ointment = (7/20) × 900 = **315 g**
* 5 % ointment = (13/20) × 900 = **585 g** ✔ (total 900 g)

🔢 **WORKED EXAMPLE 17 (diluent = 0 %)**
*How much white soft paraffin (0 %) must be mixed with a 20 % ointment to make 500 g of a 6 %
ointment?*
```
     20 %              (6 − 0)  =  6 parts of 20 %
          ╲        ╱
             6 %
          ╱        ╲
      0 %              (20 − 6) = 14 parts of 0 % (base)
   Total = 20 parts
```
* 20 % ointment = (6/20) × 500 = **150 g**
* Paraffin base = (14/20) × 500 = **350 g** ✔

🔢 **WORKED EXAMPLE 18 (100 % as one component)**
*In what proportion must pure (100 %) salicylic acid be mixed with a 2 % ointment to make a 10 %
ointment?*
```
    100 %              (10 − 2) =  8 parts of 100 % (pure acid)
          ╲        ╱
            10 %
          ╱        ╲
      2 %              (100 − 10) = 90 parts of 2 % ointment
```
→ **8 : 90 = 4 : 45** ✔

⚠️ **TRAP** — Alligation alternate works **only if the desired strength lies BETWEEN the two
available strengths**. You cannot make a 30 % ointment from a 20 % and a 5 % ointment.

## 6.6 ⭐⭐ HOW TO CALCULATE DOSES

### 6.6.1 Basic dose arithmetic

🔢 **THE THREE WORKHORSE FORMULAE**
```
① Number of doses  =  Total quantity ÷ Size of one dose

② Total quantity   =  Number of doses × Size of one dose

③ Size of one dose =  Total quantity ÷ Number of doses
```

🔢 **WORKED EXAMPLE 19**
*How many 5 mL doses are there in a 150 mL bottle, and how long will it last at t.d.s.?*
`150 ÷ 5 = 30 doses`; at 3 doses/day → `30 ÷ 3 = 10 days` ✔

🔢 **WORKED EXAMPLE 20**
*A patient must take 250 mg t.d.s. for 7 days. How many 125 mg tablets are needed?*
Per dose = 250/125 = 2 tablets; per day = 2 × 3 = 6; for 7 days = **42 tablets** ✔

🔢 **WORKED EXAMPLE 21 (drug in a volume of mixture)**
*A mixture contains 300 mg of a drug in each 10 mL dose. How much drug is in the 200 mL bottle?*
`(300 mg ÷ 10 mL) × 200 mL = 6000 mg = 6 g` ✔

🔢 **WORKED EXAMPLE 22 (the classic "dose from a % solution")**
*How many mL of a 2 % w/v solution will provide a 40 mg dose?*
2 % w/v = 20 mg/mL → `40 ÷ 20 = 2 mL` ✔

🔢 **WORKED EXAMPLE 23 (injection strength)**
*An ampoule is labelled "gentamicin 80 mg in 2 mL". How many mL give 60 mg?*
`(60/80) × 2 = 1.5 mL` ✔

🔢 **THE UNIVERSAL "DOSE FROM STOCK" FORMULA**
```
                    Dose required
Volume to give  =  ───────────────  ×  Volume of the stock
                   Stock strength
```

### 6.6.2 ⭐⭐ PAEDIATRIC (CHILD) DOSE FORMULAE — memorise all of them

| Formula | 🔢 Equation | Based on | When used |
|---|---|---|---|
| ⭐ **YOUNG'S RULE** | **Child dose = Adult dose × [ Age (yr) ÷ (Age + 12) ]** | **Age** | ⭐ Children **1–12 years** |
| ⭐ **DILLING'S RULE** | **Child dose = Adult dose × [ Age (yr) ÷ 20 ]** | **Age** | Children up to **20 years** |
| ⭐ **COWLING'S RULE** | **Child dose = Adult dose × [ (Age at next birthday) ÷ 24 ]** | **Age** | Children |
| ⭐ **FRIED'S RULE** | **Infant dose = Adult dose × [ Age in MONTHS ÷ 150 ]** | **Age in months** | ⭐ **Infants < 1–2 years** |
| ⭐ **CLARK'S RULE** | **Child dose = Adult dose × [ Weight in POUNDS ÷ 150 ]** | **Body weight** | ⭐ Any child (more reliable than age) |
| **Clark's (metric form)** | **Child dose = Adult dose × [ Weight in kg ÷ 68 ]** *(some texts 70)* | Body weight | — |
| ⭐ **BSA (Body Surface Area) RULE** | **Child dose = Adult dose × [ BSA of child (m²) ÷ 1.73 m² ]** | **Surface area** | ⭐ **The MOST ACCURATE method** — cytotoxics, neonates |
| **Catzel's / Percentage method** | Uses a table of % of adult dose against age/weight | — | Quick reference |
| **Bastedo's rule** | Child dose = Adult dose × [(Age + 3) ÷ 30] | Age | Older texts |
| **Augsberger's rule** | Child dose = Adult dose × [(4 × age in yr + 20) ÷ 100] | Age | Older texts |
| **Gaubius' rule** | Fractions by age (¼ at 4 yr, ⅓ at 7 yr, ½ at 14 yr…) | Age | Historic |
| **Body-weight (mg/kg) method** | **Dose = mg per kg × body weight (kg)** | Weight | ⭐ **The method actually used in modern practice** |

🔢 **NUMBER TO MEMORISE — the standard adult reference values:**
**Adult weight = 150 lb (≈ 68 kg or 70 kg); Adult BSA = 1.73 m²; Adult age reference = 20–24 years
(depending on the rule).**

🧠 **MEMORY HOOK for the denominators — "12, 20, 24, 150, 150, 1.73":**
**Young → +12** · **Dilling → 20** · **Cowling → 24** · **Fried → 150 (months)** ·
**Clark → 150 (pounds)** · **BSA → 1.73**.
🧠 "**F**ried is for **F**ants (in**F**ants) — in **months**; **C**lark uses **C**lark's **weight**
in pounds — both over **150**."

🔢 **WORKED EXAMPLE 24 — Young's rule**
*Adult dose 500 mg; child aged 8 years.*
`500 × 8/(8 + 12) = 500 × 8/20 = 200 mg` ✔

🔢 **WORKED EXAMPLE 25 — Dilling's rule**
*Adult dose 500 mg; child aged 8 years.*
`500 × 8/20 = 200 mg` ✔ *(coincides with Young's at 8 years)*

🔢 **WORKED EXAMPLE 26 — Cowling's rule**
*Adult dose 600 mg; child is 5 years old (so next birthday = 6).*
`600 × 6/24 = 150 mg` ✔

🔢 **WORKED EXAMPLE 27 — Fried's rule**
*Adult dose 300 mg; infant aged 9 months.*
`300 × 9/150 = 18 mg` ✔

🔢 **WORKED EXAMPLE 28 — Clark's rule**
*Adult dose 400 mg; child weighs 50 lb.*
`400 × 50/150 = 133.3 mg` ✔

🔢 **WORKED EXAMPLE 29 — BSA rule**
*Adult dose 750 mg; child's BSA = 0.62 m².*
`750 × 0.62/1.73 = 268.8 ≈ 269 mg` ✔

🔢 **WORKED EXAMPLE 30 — mg/kg method**
*Amoxicillin 25 mg/kg/day in 3 divided doses for a 12 kg child; syrup is 125 mg/5 mL.*
Daily dose = 25 × 12 = 300 mg → per dose = 100 mg → volume = (100/125) × 5 = **4 mL t.d.s.** ✔

🔢 **BSA can be estimated** from **Du Bois** or **Mosteller's** formula:
```
Mosteller:  BSA (m²) = √[ height (cm) × weight (kg) ÷ 3600 ]
```
🔢 *Worked:* A child 100 cm tall weighing 16 kg → √(100 × 16/3600) = √0.444 = **0.67 m².**

⚠️ **TRAP** — Paediatric rules are **only approximations**. They must **NOT** be used for
**digoxin, cytotoxics, anticoagulants, aminoglycosides or anticonvulsants** — these are dosed on
**body weight or BSA with therapeutic drug monitoring**. Also, children are **not small adults**:
neonates have immature enzymes and **need proportionately smaller** doses, whereas older children
sometimes metabolise faster and need proportionately **larger** mg/kg doses.

### 6.6.3 ⭐ GERIATRIC & OTHER DOSE ADJUSTMENTS
* ⭐ Elderly patients generally need a **reduced dose** (reduced renal & hepatic function, lower
  lean body mass, altered receptor sensitivity) — often **½ to ¾** of the adult dose.
* **Renal impairment** → dose reduced in proportion to **creatinine clearance**
  🔢 **Cockcroft–Gault:** `CrCl = [(140 − age) × weight (kg)] ÷ (72 × serum creatinine mg/dL)`
  (× **0.85 for females**).
* **Obese patients** → dose on **ideal or adjusted body weight** for lipid-insoluble drugs.
* **Loading dose** 🔢 `= Vd × desired plasma concentration`;
  **Maintenance dose** 🔢 `= Clearance × desired concentration × dosing interval ÷ F`.

## 6.7 ISOTONIC SOLUTIONS ⭐

📌 **ISOTONIC** — a solution having the **same osmotic pressure as body fluids (blood, tears)**;
📌 **HYPOTONIC** — lower osmotic pressure → water enters cells → **haemolysis** of red cells;
📌 **HYPERTONIC** — higher osmotic pressure → water leaves cells → **crenation/ shrinkage**.

🔢 **NUMBERS TO MEMORISE**

| Reference | Value |
|---|---|
| ⭐ **Isotonic sodium chloride (normal saline)** | **0.9 % w/v NaCl** |
| ⭐ **Isotonic dextrose** | **5 % w/v** |
| ⭐ **Isotonic boric acid** | **1.9 % w/v** |
| ⭐ **Isotonic sodium bicarbonate** | 1.39 % w/v |
| ⭐ **Freezing point of blood/ tears** | **− 0.52 °C** |
| ⭐ **Osmotic pressure of blood** | ≈ **7 atm**; osmolarity ≈ **285–310 mOsm/L** |
| **pH of blood / tears** | 7.4 / 7.4 |
| ⭐ **NaCl equivalent of NaCl itself (E value)** | **1.0** |

📌 **SODIUM CHLORIDE EQUIVALENT (E value)** — the **weight of sodium chloride that produces the
same osmotic effect as 1 g of the drug**.

🔢 **METHOD 1 — THE SODIUM CHLORIDE EQUIVALENT METHOD**
```
NaCl required (g) = [ 0.9 × Volume (mL) ÷ 100 ]  −  ( Weight of drug × its E value )
```
🔢 **WORKED EXAMPLE 31**
*Make 100 mL of an isotonic solution of 1 % w/v atropine sulphate (E = 0.13).*
* NaCl needed for 100 mL isotonic = 0.9 g
* Osmotic contribution of the drug = 1 g × 0.13 = 0.13 g of "NaCl equivalent"
* **NaCl to add = 0.9 − 0.13 = 0.77 g** ✔

🔢 **WORKED EXAMPLE 32**
*30 mL of a 2 % w/v solution of a drug whose E = 0.20.*
* NaCl for isotonicity of 30 mL = (0.9 × 30)/100 = 0.27 g
* Drug present = (2 × 30)/100 = 0.6 g → NaCl equivalent = 0.6 × 0.20 = 0.12 g
* **NaCl to add = 0.27 − 0.12 = 0.15 g** ✔

🔢 **METHOD 2 — THE FREEZING-POINT (CRYOSCOPIC) METHOD**
```
                    0.52  −  ( ΔTf of the drug at its given % )
% w/v of NaCl  =  ───────────────────────────────────────────────
                        0.576   (the ΔTf of 1 % w/v NaCl)
```
🔢 **WORKED EXAMPLE 33**
*A 1 % solution of a drug lowers the freezing point by 0.15 °C. What % of NaCl is needed to make a
1 % solution of this drug isotonic?*
`(0.52 − 0.15)/0.576 = 0.37/0.576 = 0.64 % w/v NaCl` ✔

🔢 **METHOD 3 — WHITE–VINCENT METHOD** (volume to make the drug isotonic, then dilute with an
isotonic vehicle)
```
V = W × E × 111.1
```
where V = volume (mL) of isotonic solution that can be made from W grams of the drug.
🔢 *Worked:* 0.3 g of a drug with E = 0.20 → V = 0.3 × 0.20 × 111.1 = **6.67 mL** of isotonic
solution; make the drug up to 6.67 mL with water, then dilute to the final volume with an
**isotonic diluting vehicle** (e.g. 0.9 % NaCl or Sörensen's buffer).

⭐ **Substances used to adjust tonicity:** **sodium chloride, dextrose, boric acid, sodium
bicarbonate, sodium citrate, potassium nitrate, mannitol, propylene glycol.**
⚠️ **Boric acid (1.9 %)** is preferred for **eye drops of alkaloidal salts, silver salts and
zinc salts** — because sodium chloride would **precipitate** them.

## 6.8 MILLIEQUIVALENTS (mEq) & ELECTROLYTE CALCULATIONS ⭐

📌 An **equivalent weight** = **atomic (or molecular) weight ÷ valency**.
📌 **1 milliequivalent (mEq)** = **1/1000 of a gram-equivalent weight**.

🔢 **THE FORMULAE**
```
                         Atomic / Molecular weight (mg)
Weight of 1 mEq (mg) =  ────────────────────────────────
                                  Valency

           Weight of substance (mg) × Valency
mEq  =  ────────────────────────────────────────
              Molecular weight (mg)
```

🔢 **STANDARD VALUES TO MEMORISE**

| Ion / salt | Atomic/ Mol. wt | Valency | **1 mEq =** |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Na⁺** | 23 | 1 | **23 mg** |
| **K⁺** | 39 | 1 | **39 mg** |
| **Ca²⁺** | 40 | 2 | **20 mg** |
| **Mg²⁺** | 24 | 2 | **12 mg** |
| **Cl⁻** | 35.5 | 1 | **35.5 mg** |
| **HCO₃⁻** | 61 | 1 | **61 mg** |
| **NaCl** | 58.5 | 1 | ⭐ **58.5 mg** |
| **KCl** | 74.5 | 1 | **74.5 mg** |
| **NaHCO₃** | 84 | 1 | ⭐ **84 mg** |
| **CaCl₂** | 111 | 2 | **55.5 mg** |

🔢 **WORKED EXAMPLE 34**
*How many mEq of Na⁺ are in 1 litre of 0.9 % w/v sodium chloride?*
0.9 % w/v = 9 g NaCl per litre = 9000 mg;
`mEq = 9000 ÷ 58.5 = 153.8 ≈ 154 mEq of Na⁺ (and 154 mEq of Cl⁻)` ✔
⭐ **Remember: normal saline contains ≈ 154 mEq/L each of Na⁺ and Cl⁻.**

🔢 **WORKED EXAMPLE 35**
*How many mg of KCl provide 20 mEq of potassium?*
`20 × 74.5 = 1490 mg = 1.49 g` ✔

🔢 **WORKED EXAMPLE 36**
*Express 4.2 % w/v sodium bicarbonate in mEq/L.*
4.2 % = 42 g/L = 42 000 mg → `42 000 ÷ 84 = 500 mEq/L` ✔

🔢 **OSMOLARITY**
```
                          weight (g/L) × 1000 × number of ions
mOsmol/L  =  ────────────────────────────────────────────────────
                          molecular weight (g)
```
🔢 *Worked:* 0.9 % NaCl → (9 × 1000 × 2)/58.5 = **308 mOsmol/L** ✔ (isotonic ≈ 285–310).

## 6.9 ⭐ ENLARGEMENT & REDUCTION OF FORMULAE

📌 Official formulae are usually given **for 1000 mL or 1000 g**. To dispense a different quantity
you must **scale the formula by a FACTOR**.

🔢 **THE FACTOR METHOD**
```
             Quantity required
Factor  =  ─────────────────────   ; then multiply EVERY ingredient by the factor
           Quantity in the formula
```

🔢 **WORKED EXAMPLE 37**
*The official formula of Compound Powder of Tragacanth makes 100 g:
Tragacanth 15 g, Acacia 20 g, Starch 20 g, Sucrose 45 g. Prepare 25 g.*
Factor = 25/100 = **0.25** →
Tragacanth **3.75 g**, Acacia **5 g**, Starch **5 g**, Sucrose **11.25 g** (total 25 g) ✔

🔢 **WORKED EXAMPLE 38**
*A formula for 1000 mL of a mixture contains Ammonium chloride 100 g, Liquorice liquid extract
50 mL, Chloroform water to 1000 mL. Prepare 150 mL.*
Factor = 150/1000 = **0.15** →
Ammonium chloride **15 g**, Liquorice liquid extract **7.5 mL**, Chloroform water **to 150 mL** ✔

🔢 **WORKED EXAMPLE 39 — formula given in "parts"**
*A formula reads: Zinc oxide 1 part, Starch 1 part, White soft paraffin 2 parts. Make 60 g.*
Total parts = 4 → 1 part = 60/4 = 15 g →
Zinc oxide **15 g**, Starch **15 g**, White soft paraffin **30 g** ✔

⭐ **RULES for enlargement/ reduction**
1. Apply the factor to **every** ingredient, including the vehicle if it is a fixed quantity.
2. If the vehicle is written as **"ad" (to make up to)**, do **not** multiply it — simply
   **make up to the new final volume**.
3. ⭐ **Always prepare a slight excess** (about **5–10 %**) of a liquid preparation to allow for
   losses in transfer & filtration — but **never of a potent/ narcotic drug**.
4. After scaling, **check that any quantity below the MWQ** is handled by the **aliquot method**
   (Ch. 4).
5. Round sensibly, but **never round a potent drug upward**.

## 6.10 DISPLACEMENT VALUE (an introduction — full treatment in Chapter 9)

📌 **DISPLACEMENT VALUE (DV)** — the **number of parts by weight of a drug that displaces
1 part by weight of the suppository base** (usually theobroma oil).

🔢 **THE WORKING FORMULA**
```
                        Weight of the drug in all the suppositories
Weight of base = (n × mould calibration) − ────────────────────────────
                                                Displacement value
```
🔢 **Quick example:** 12 suppositories of 1 g each containing 300 mg zinc oxide (DV = 4.7):
Total drug = 3.6 g; base displaced = 3.6/4.7 = 0.766 g;
Base required = 12 − 0.766 = **11.23 g**. ✔ *(Full worked treatment in Ch. 9.)*

## 6.11 MISCELLANEOUS CALCULATIONS THE PAPER LIKES

### A. Specific gravity ⭐
🔢 `Sp. gr. = weight of the substance ÷ weight of an equal volume of water`
🔢 **Weight (g) = Volume (mL) × Sp. gr.** and **Volume (mL) = Weight (g) ÷ Sp. gr.**

| Liquid | Sp. gr. |
|---|---|
| **Water** | **1.000** |
| **Alcohol (ethanol, absolute)** | **0.79** |
| **Rectified spirit (90 %)** | 0.834 |
| **Glycerin** | ⭐ **1.26** |
| **Chloroform** | ⭐ **1.47–1.48** |
| **Ether** | 0.72 |
| **Liquid paraffin** | 0.83–0.89 |
| **Castor oil** | 0.945–0.965 |
| **Olive/ arachis oil** | 0.91–0.92 |
| **Syrup (simple)** | ⭐ **1.315–1.33** |
| **Clove oil** | 1.04–1.06 (heavier than water) |
| **Mercury** | 13.6 |

🔢 *Worked:* What volume is occupied by 100 g of glycerin? `100 ÷ 1.26 = 79.4 mL` ✔
🔢 *Worked:* What is the weight of 30 mL of chloroform? `30 × 1.48 = 44.4 g` ✔

### B. Percentage of alcohol in a finished preparation
🔢 *A 200 mL mixture contains 30 mL of a tincture of 60 % v/v alcohol. What is the alcohol
percentage of the mixture?*
Absolute alcohol = 30 × 60/100 = 18 mL → `(18/200) × 100 = 9 % v/v` ✔

### C. Percentage & absolute error
🔢 `% error = (error ÷ quantity desired) × 100`
🔢 *Worked:* You intended 500 mg and actually weighed 520 mg → error 20 mg →
`(20/500) × 100 = 4 %` → **acceptable** (< 5 %). ✔

### D. Number of doses/ days' supply with a changing schedule
🔢 *A 100 mL bottle: 10 mL b.d. for 3 days, then 5 mL b.d. How long will it last?*
First 3 days use 20 × 3 = 60 mL; remaining 40 mL at 10 mL/day = 4 days → **total 7 days** ✔

### E. Drip-rate calculation (hospital dispensing) ⭐
```
                      Volume (mL) × Drop factor (drops/mL)
Drops per minute = ────────────────────────────────────────
                              Time in minutes
```
🔢 *Worked:* 500 mL over 4 hours with a 20 drops/mL set →
`(500 × 20)/(4 × 60) = 10 000/240 = 41.7 ≈ 42 drops/min` ✔
⭐ Standard drop factors: **macro-drip 15 or 20 drops/mL; micro-drip (paediatric) 60 drops/mL**.

### F. Reducing/ increasing a potency-based formula (units)
🔢 *Worked:* Insulin 100 units/mL — how many mL give 28 units? `28/100 = 0.28 mL` ✔

### G. Proof spirit & duty (classical)
🔢 *Worked:* How many mL of absolute alcohol are in 500 mL of proof spirit?
`500 × 57.1/100 = 285.5 mL` ✔

## 6.12 CHAPTER 6 — RAPID REVISION SHEET (the numbers you must be able to recite)

**METRIC**
* 1 kg = 1000 g; 1 g = 1000 mg; 1 mg = 1000 µg; 1 L = 1000 mL; **1 mL = 1 cc**;
  **1 mL of water = 1 g**.

**APOTHECARY**
* **20 grains = 1 scruple · 3 scruples = 1 drachm (60 gr) · 8 drachms = 1 ounce (480 gr) ·
  12 ounces = 1 pound (5760 gr)**
* **60 minims = 1 fl drachm · 8 fl drachms = 1 fl ounce · 20 fl oz = 1 pint · 8 pints = 1 gallon**

**CONVERSIONS**
* **1 grain = 64.8 mg (≈ 65 mg)** · **1 g = 15.43 grains (≈ 15)**
* **1 minim = 0.06 mL** · **1 mL = 16.9 ≈ 17 minims**
* **1 fl drachm = 3.55 mL (≈ 4)** · **1 fl oz = 28.4 mL (≈ 30)**
* **1 apoth. ounce = 31.1 g** · **1 avoirdupois ounce = 28.35 g**
* **1 avoirdupois pound = 453.6 g** · **1 troy pound = 373.2 g** · **1 kg = 2.2 lb**
* **1 Imperial pint = 568 mL** · **1 US pint = 473 mL** · **1 Imperial gallon = 4546 mL**
* **1 inch = 2.54 cm**
* **°F = (9/5 °C) + 32 · °C = 5/9 (°F − 32) · K = °C + 273 · both scales equal at −40°**
* **Body temp 37 °C = 98.6 °F**

**PERCENTAGE**
* **% w/w** = g in 100 g · **% w/v** = g in 100 mL · **% v/v** = mL in 100 mL ·
  **% v/w** = mL in 100 g.
* Solid in liquid → **w/v**; liquid in liquid → **v/v**; solid/semisolid mixtures → **w/w**.
* **1 % w/v = 10 mg/mL** · **1 : 1000 = 0.1 %** · **1 ppm = 0.0001 % = 1 mg/L = 1 µg/mL**.
* **Ratio 1 : x → % = 100/x** and **% p → ratio 1 : (100/p)**.

**DILUTION & ALLIGATION**
* **Q₁C₁ = Q₂C₂.**
* **Alligation medial** → find the **strength of a mixture**;
  **alligation alternate** → find the **proportion** to mix (cross/ X method, subtract diagonally).
* Desired strength must lie **between** the two available strengths.

**ALCOHOL**
* **Proof spirit = 57.1 % v/v alcohol**; absolute alcohol = **175.25° proof**;
  **rectified spirit = 90 % v/v**; absolute alcohol = 99.5 % v/v.
* `Degrees proof = (% v/v × 100) ÷ 57.1`.

**DOSES**
* **Young's = A × age/(age + 12)** · **Dilling's = A × age/20** ·
  **Cowling's = A × (age next birthday)/24** · **Fried's = A × months/150** ·
  **Clark's = A × lb/150 (or kg/68)** · **BSA = A × child BSA/1.73**.
* Adult reference: **150 lb (68–70 kg), BSA 1.73 m²**.
* **Mosteller BSA = √(ht cm × wt kg ÷ 3600)**.
* **Cockcroft–Gault CrCl = [(140 − age) × wt] ÷ (72 × S.Cr)**, × 0.85 for women.
* **Volume to give = (dose required ÷ stock strength) × stock volume.**

**ISOTONICITY**
* **0.9 % NaCl · 5 % dextrose · 1.9 % boric acid** are isotonic;
  **freezing point of blood = −0.52 °C**; ΔTf of 1 % NaCl = **0.576**.
* **NaCl to add = (0.9 × volume/100) − (weight of drug × E value)**.
* **White–Vincent: V = W × E × 111.1.**
* Hypotonic → **haemolysis**; hypertonic → **crenation**.

**mEq**
* **1 mEq = mol. wt ÷ valency (in mg)**; **NaCl 58.5 mg · NaHCO₃ 84 mg · KCl 74.5 mg ·
  Na⁺ 23 · K⁺ 39 · Ca²⁺ 20 · Mg²⁺ 12 · Cl⁻ 35.5 mg.**
* **Normal saline ≈ 154 mEq/L of Na⁺ and Cl⁻; 308 mOsmol/L.**

**OTHERS**
* **Factor = quantity required ÷ quantity in the formula**; do not multiply an "ad" vehicle.
* **Weight = volume × sp. gr.**; glycerin 1.26, chloroform 1.48, alcohol 0.79, syrup 1.32.
* **% error = (error ÷ desired quantity) × 100**; ≤ 5 % is acceptable.
* **Drops/min = (volume × drop factor) ÷ minutes**; macro 15–20, micro 60 drops/mL.
* **Displacement value:** base = (n × mould size) − (total drug ÷ DV).
