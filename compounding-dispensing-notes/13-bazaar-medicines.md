## 13.1 WHAT ARE "BAZAAR MEDICINES"? ⭐

📌 **DEFINITION**
> **"Ordinary bazaar medicines" are the simple, cheap, commonly available drugs and chemicals that
> are sold in the open market (bazaar) and are stocked in every dispensary and household — chiefly
> inorganic salts, simple organic chemicals, oils, and common crude drugs — which a pharmacist must
> be able to RECOGNISE on sight (by their organoleptic characters and simple tests), and whose
> DOSES and USES he must know.**

⭐ **WHY THIS TOPIC EXISTS IN THE SYLLABUS**
* These drugs are handled **every day** in a dispensary, often supplied **loose/ unlabelled** from
  the bazaar.
* ⚠️ Many of them are **look-alikes** (white crystalline powders) and **confusing them can be
  fatal** — e.g. **boric acid vs oxalic acid**, **Epsom salt vs oxalic acid**, **sodium bicarbonate
  vs sodium nitrite**, **alum vs sugar**.
* ⭐ Therefore the pharmacist must be able to **identify them by RECOGNITION (appearance, taste,
  odour, solubility, behaviour on heating)** and by **simple confirmatory tests**.

### ⭐⭐ HOW TO "RECOGNISE" A BAZAAR MEDICINE — the systematic scheme

| # | Step | What to observe |
|---|---|---|
| **1** | ⭐ **Physical state & form** | Powder / crystal / granule / lump / liquid / semisolid; crystalline shape (cubic, needle, prismatic, flaky, amorphous) |
| **2** | ⭐ **Colour** | White, coloured, lustrous, metallic |
| **3** | ⭐ **Odour** | Odourless / aromatic / pungent / ammoniacal / garlic-like / phenolic |
| **4** | ⭐ **Taste** *(⚠️ ONLY where safe — NEVER taste an unknown/ poisonous substance)* | Saline, bitter, sweet, astringent, sour, acrid, metallic, tasteless |
| **5** | ⭐ **Touch/ feel** | Smooth & soapy (talc), gritty, greasy, unctuous, adheres to the tongue (kaolin) |
| **6** | ⭐ **Solubility** | In **cold water, hot water, alcohol, ether, dilute acid, alkali** |
| **7** | ⭐ **Behaviour in air** | ⭐ **Efflorescent** (Na₂CO₃, FeSO₄, MgSO₄, alum) · **Deliquescent** (CaCl₂, ZnCl₂) · **hygroscopic** · **volatile/ sublimable** (camphor, menthol, iodine, NH₄Cl) |
| **8** | ⭐ **Behaviour on HEATING** | ⭐ **Sublimes** (camphor, iodine, NH₄Cl, benzoic acid) · **melts** · **chars/ smells of caramel** (sugars, gums) · **burns with a sooty flame** (resins) · **effervesces/ gives CO₂** (carbonates) · **evolves ammonia** (ammonium salts) · **swells & puffs up** (alum, borax → "burnt alum", "borax bead") · **leaves a residue** |
| **9** | ⭐ **Behaviour with DILUTE ACID** | ⭐ **Effervescence (CO₂) → CARBONATE/ BICARBONATE** · **rotten-egg smell (H₂S) → sulphide** · **brown fumes → nitrite** · **vinegar smell → acetate** |
| **10** | ⭐ **Reaction to litmus/ pH** | Acidic / alkaline / neutral |
| **11** | ⭐ **Flame test (cation)** | ⭐ **Sodium — golden yellow · Potassium — lilac/ violet · Calcium — brick red · Barium — apple green · Copper — blue-green · Strontium — crimson · Lead/ Zinc — bluish-white** |
| **12** | ⭐ **Confirmatory chemical test** | See the individual monographs below |

🧠 **MEMORY HOOK — "S-C-O-T-T-S: State, Colour, Odour, Taste, Touch, Solubility"** —
then **heat it, acidify it, and flame-test it.**

### ⭐⭐ THE MOST USEFUL "GROUP" IDENTIFICATION TESTS 🧪

| Radical/ group | 🧪 TEST | ⭐ Positive result |
|---|---|---|
| ⭐ **Carbonate/ Bicarbonate (CO₃²⁻/ HCO₃⁻)** | Add dilute HCl | ⭐ **Brisk EFFERVESCENCE — CO₂ turns lime water milky** |
| ⭐ **Sulphate (SO₄²⁻)** | Add **barium chloride** solution | ⭐ **WHITE precipitate, insoluble in HCl** |
| ⭐ **Chloride (Cl⁻)** | Add **silver nitrate** + dilute HNO₃ | ⭐ **WHITE curdy precipitate, soluble in ammonia** |
| ⭐ **Iodide (I⁻)** | Silver nitrate / chlorine water + starch | ⭐ **Yellow precipitate / BLUE colour with starch** |
| ⭐ **Bromide (Br⁻)** | Silver nitrate | Pale yellow precipitate |
| ⭐ **Nitrate (NO₃⁻)** | ⭐ **Brown-ring test** (FeSO₄ + conc. H₂SO₄) | ⭐ **BROWN RING at the junction** |
| ⭐ **Nitrite (NO₂⁻)** | Dilute acid | **Brown fumes (NO₂)**; starch-iodide paper turns blue |
| ⭐ **Borate (BO₃³⁻)** | ⭐ **Turmeric paper** moistened with the acidified solution | ⭐ **Turmeric paper turns REDDISH-BROWN, and GREEN-BLACK on adding alkali** |
| **Borate (2nd test)** | Burn with alcohol + conc. H₂SO₄ | ⭐ **GREEN-EDGED flame** |
| ⭐ **Phosphate (PO₄³⁻)** | Ammonium molybdate + HNO₃ | Canary-yellow precipitate |
| ⭐ **Acetate (CH₃COO⁻)** | Warm with dilute H₂SO₄ | **Smell of VINEGAR**; FeCl₃ → deep red |
| ⭐ **Sulphide (S²⁻)** | Dilute HCl | ⭐ **Rotten-egg smell (H₂S)**; blackens lead acetate paper |
| ⭐ **Ammonium (NH₄⁺)** | Warm with NaOH | ⭐ **Pungent AMMONIA gas — turns red litmus blue** |
| ⭐ **Ferrous (Fe²⁺)** | Potassium ferricyanide | ⭐ **Deep BLUE precipitate (Prussian blue)** |
| ⭐ **Ferric (Fe³⁺)** | Potassium thiocyanate / ferrocyanide | ⭐ **BLOOD-RED colour / blue precipitate** |
| ⭐ **Zinc (Zn²⁺)** | NaOH (excess) | White precipitate, **soluble in excess** |
| ⭐ **Aluminium (Al³⁺)** | NaOH (excess) | Gelatinous white precipitate, **soluble in excess** |
| ⭐ **Calcium (Ca²⁺)** | Ammonium oxalate | White precipitate; **brick-red flame** |
| ⭐ **Magnesium (Mg²⁺)** | NaOH | White precipitate, **insoluble in excess** |
| ⭐ **Copper (Cu²⁺)** | Ammonia | **Deep blue** solution |
| ⭐ **Lead (Pb²⁺)** | Potassium iodide/ dilute HCl | Yellow PbI₂ / white PbCl₂ |
| ⭐ **Starch** | Iodine | ⭐ **BLUE-BLACK** |
| ⭐ **Reducing sugar** | Fehling's/ Benedict's | Brick-red precipitate |
| ⭐ **Salicylate/ Phenol** | Ferric chloride | ⭐ **VIOLET/ purple colour** |
| ⭐ **Tannin** | Ferric chloride / gelatin | Blue-black / white precipitate |
| ⭐ **Oxidising agent (permanganate, chlorate, peroxide)** | KI–starch paper | Blue colour |

⚠️ ⭐ **DANGEROUS LOOK-ALIKES — THE "WHITE POWDER" TRAP TABLE**

| Look-alike pair | ⭐ How to tell them apart |
|---|---|
| ⭐ **Boric acid vs Oxalic acid** *(both white crystalline; ⚠️ oxalic acid is a POISON)* | ⭐ **Boric acid: turmeric paper → reddish-brown; green flame with alcohol; feels SOAPY/ slippery; taste faintly bitter.** ⭐ **Oxalic acid: STRONGLY ACID to litmus, sour, gives a WHITE precipitate with calcium chloride (calcium oxalate, insoluble in acetic acid); burns without residue** |
| ⭐ **Epsom salt (MgSO₄) vs Oxalic acid** ⚠️ *the classic fatal confusion* | ⭐ **Epsom salt: NEUTRAL, saline-bitter taste, gives a white ppt with BaCl₂ (sulphate) and a white ppt with NaOH insoluble in excess (Mg).** ⭐ **Oxalic acid: strongly ACID, poisonous** |
| ⭐ **Sodium bicarbonate vs Sodium nitrite** ⚠️ | ⭐ **Bicarbonate EFFERVESCES with acid (CO₂).** ⭐ **Nitrite gives BROWN FUMES with acid** |
| ⭐ **Alum vs Sugar vs Magnesium sulphate** | ⭐ **Alum: sweetish-ASTRINGENT taste, PUFFS UP on heating ("burnt alum"), Al³⁺ + sulphate tests.** ⭐ **Sugar: sweet, CHARS with a caramel smell.** ⭐ **MgSO₄: bitter-saline** |
| ⭐ **Borax vs Boric acid** | ⭐ **Borax is ALKALINE to litmus (soapy, sweetish-alkaline) and swells to a glassy "borax bead" on heating; boric acid is faintly ACID** |
| ⭐ **Zinc oxide vs Calamine** | ⭐ **Zinc oxide is WHITE; calamine is PINK (zinc oxide coloured with ferric oxide)** |
| ⭐ **Light vs Heavy magnesium carbonate/ oxide** | ⭐ **Same chemical — differ only in BULK DENSITY (the "light" grade occupies a much larger volume for the same weight)** |
| ⭐ **Precipitated vs Sublimed sulphur** | ⭐ **Precipitated ("milk of sulphur") is a very fine, pale, almost odourless powder; sublimed ("flowers of sulphur") is a coarser, gritty, bright-yellow crystalline powder** |
| **Talc vs Kaolin vs Starch** | ⭐ **Talc: smooth, soapy, gritty-free, does NOT adhere to the tongue.** **Kaolin: earthy, ADHERES to the tongue, earthy smell when damp.** **Starch: iodine → BLUE-BLACK** |
| **Potassium permanganate vs Iodine** | ⭐ **KMnO₄: dark purple crystals, dissolves in water giving a PURPLE solution.** **Iodine: greyish-black LUSTROUS scales, SUBLIMES giving violet vapour, blue with starch, barely soluble in water but soluble in KI solution** |

### 📋 TABLE C — BORON, IODINE, SULPHUR & OTHER ANTISEPTIC CHEMICALS ⭐

| Drug (synonym) | ⭐ RECOGNITION | ⭐ DOSE | ⭐ USES |
|---|---|---|---|

## 13.4 ⭐ ANTIDOTES & FIRST-AID MEASURES FOR COMMON BAZAAR POISONS ⚠️

| Poison | ⭐ Antidote / first aid |
|---|---|
| ⭐ **Iodine** | ⭐ **STARCH or flour suspension** (then gastric lavage), sodium thiosulphate |
| ⭐ **Phenol/ carbolic acid** | ⭐ **Wash the skin with ALCOHOL, GLYCERIN or vegetable OIL (not water alone)**; internally — olive oil, egg white, activated charcoal |
| ⭐ **Oxalic acid** | ⭐ **CALCIUM salts — lime water, calcium gluconate, chalk & milk** (⚠️ never a carbonate — CO₂) |
| ⭐ **Acids (corrosive)** | ⚠️ ⭐ **Do NOT induce vomiting or use a carbonate; give MILK, egg white, magnesium oxide/ milk of magnesia, water** |
| ⭐ **Alkalis (caustic)** | ⭐ **Dilute VINEGAR or lemon juice, milk, egg white**; do not induce vomiting |
| ⭐ **Copper & mercury salts** | ⭐ **Egg white/ milk (protein precipitates the metal), then emesis; dimercaprol (BAL)** |
| ⭐ **Arsenic** | ⭐ **Dimercaprol (BAL)**, freshly precipitated ferric hydroxide |
| ⭐ **Lead** | ⭐ **Calcium disodium EDTA, penicillamine, dimercaprol** |
| ⭐ **Iron (ferrous sulphate)** | ⭐ **DESFERRIOXAMINE** |
| ⭐ **Cyanide** | ⭐ **Amyl nitrite inhalation + sodium nitrite IV + SODIUM THIOSULPHATE** |
| ⭐ **Opium/ morphine** | ⭐ **NALOXONE**; potassium permanganate lavage (classical) |
| ⭐ **Organophosphate insecticide** | ⭐ **ATROPINE + pralidoxime** |
| ⭐ **Paracetamol** | ⭐ **N-ACETYLCYSTEINE** (or methionine) |
| ⭐ **Barbiturates/ sedatives** | Supportive care; forced alkaline diuresis |
| ⭐ **Alkaloids generally** | ⭐ **TANNIC ACID/ strong tea (precipitates them), activated charcoal, potassium permanganate 1:5000** |
| ⭐ **Any unknown ingested poison** | ⭐ **ACTIVATED CHARCOAL 50–100 g** (the universal adsorbent) + supportive care |

## 13.5 CHAPTER 13 — RAPID REVISION SHEET

**RECOGNITION SCHEME** — ⭐ **state → colour → odour → taste → touch → solubility → behaviour in air
(efflorescent/ deliquescent/ sublimable) → behaviour on heating → behaviour with dilute acid →
litmus → flame test → confirmatory test.**

**KEY GROUP TESTS** — ⭐ **Carbonate → effervescence with acid · Sulphate → white ppt with BaCl₂ ·
Chloride → white curdy ppt with AgNO₃ · Iodide → blue with starch · Nitrate → brown ring ·
Nitrite → brown fumes · Borate → turmeric paper reddish-brown + GREEN flame ·
Ammonium → ammonia gas with NaOH · Ferrous → blue with ferricyanide · Salicylate/ phenol →
VIOLET with FeCl₃ · Starch → blue-black with iodine.**
⭐ **Flame colours: Na golden-yellow · K lilac · Ca brick-red · Ba apple-green · Cu blue-green.**

**FATAL LOOK-ALIKES** — ⭐ **Boric acid vs OXALIC ACID · Epsom salt vs OXALIC ACID ·
sodium bicarbonate vs SODIUM NITRITE · alum vs sugar · borax (alkaline) vs boric acid (acid) ·
zinc oxide (white) vs calamine (pink) · talc (does not stick to the tongue) vs kaolin (sticks).**

**THE "MUST-KNOW" RECOGNITION POINTS**
* ⭐ **Castor oil — the ONLY fixed oil freely soluble in alcohol.**
* ⭐ **Liquid paraffin — mineral origin, does not become rancid, unsaponifiable; ⚠️ blocks vitamins
  A D E K & causes lipoid pneumonia.**
* ⭐ **Iodine — lustrous greyish-black scales, SUBLIMES with violet vapour, BLUE with starch,
  soluble only in KI solution.**
* ⭐ **Camphor — sublimes, gyrates on water, powdered by "pulverisation by intervention" with
  alcohol, EUTECTIC with menthol/ thymol/ phenol, DV 0.7.**
* ⭐ **Alum — PUFFS UP on heating ("burnt alum"), sweetish-astringent, styptic & water purifier.**
* ⭐ **Borax — swells to a glassy "borax bead", ALKALINE; Boric acid — soapy, faintly ACID,
  isotonic at 1.9 %, ⚠️ toxic to infants.**
* ⭐ **Potassium permanganate — purple lustrous crystals, EXTERNAL 1:5000–1:10 000,
  ⚠️ EXPLOSIVE with glycerin/ sugar/ charcoal.**
* ⭐ **Hydrogen peroxide — effervesces on tissue, store in an amber bottle with a VENTED cap.**
* ⭐ **Ferrous sulphate — pale bluish-green, efflorescent, oxidises to brown; 200 mg t.d.s. after
  food; ⚠️ fatal to children in overdose.**
* ⭐ **Copper sulphate — blue crystals, white on heating, deep blue with ammonia; ⚠️ poisonous.**
* ⭐ **Silver nitrate — turns black in light; caustic; use a horn spatula & amber bottle.**
* ⭐ **Sulphur — precipitated is fine & pale, sublimed is coarse & gritty; burns with a BLUE flame
  giving SO₂; INDIFFUSIBLE; 10 % ointment for SCABIES.**
* ⭐ **Kaolin ADHERES to the tongue; Talc is smooth & soapy and does NOT.**
* ⭐ **Light kaolin = diffusible; heavy kaolin = indiffusible.**
* ⭐ **Zinc oxide turns yellow on heating & white again on cooling; DV 4.7; indiffusible.**
* ⭐ **Glycerin — sweet, hygroscopic, sp. gr. 1.26, miscible with water & alcohol but NOT with
  ether/ fixed oils; ⚠️ EXPLOSIVE with permanganate.**
* ⭐ **Chloroform — sp. gr. 1.48 (heavier than water), non-inflammable; Ether — b.p. 34.6 °C,
  HIGHLY INFLAMMABLE.**
* ⭐ **Clove oil is HEAVIER than water and contains ~85 % eugenol; methyl salicylate & phenol give
  VIOLET with FeCl₃.**
* ⭐ **Isapghula swelling index ≥ 40.**
* ⭐ **Carron oil (Lime liniment) = LIME WATER + OLIVE OIL in EQUAL PARTS, for BURNS.**
* ⭐ **EUSOL = chlorinated lime + boric acid, for wound irrigation.**
* ⭐ **ORS = NaCl 2.6 + trisodium citrate 2.9 + KCl 1.5 + glucose 13.5 per litre.**
* ⭐ **Universal antidote = charcoal 2 : magnesium oxide 1 : tannic acid 1; in practice ACTIVATED
  CHARCOAL 50–100 g alone.**
* ⭐ **Iodine poisoning → STARCH. Phenol on the skin → ALCOHOL/ GLYCERIN/ OIL.
  Oxalic acid → CALCIUM (lime water). Cyanide → sodium thiosulphate + nitrites.
  Iron → desferrioxamine. Paracetamol → N-acetylcysteine. Morphine → naloxone.
  Organophosphate → atropine. Alkaloids → tannic acid/ strong tea.**
