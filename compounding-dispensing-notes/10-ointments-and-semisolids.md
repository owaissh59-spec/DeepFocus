## 10.1 DEFINITIONS — THE WHOLE SEMISOLID FAMILY ⭐⭐

📌 **OINTMENT (Unguentum)** — *(learn word-for-word)*
> **An ointment is a SEMISOLID preparation intended for EXTERNAL APPLICATION to the SKIN or MUCOUS
> MEMBRANES, in which the medicament is dissolved, suspended or emulsified in a GREASY
> (non-aqueous, oleaginous) BASE.** Ointments are **usually anhydrous or contain little water**,
> are **greasy, occlusive and emollient**, and do not normally contain more than **20 % of water &
> volatiles**.

### 📋 THE COMPARISON TABLE OF ALL SEMISOLIDS ⭐⭐

| Preparation | 📌 Definition | ⭐ Key distinguishing feature | Examples |
|---|---|---|---|
| ⭐ **OINTMENT** | Semisolid for external use with the drug in a **greasy base** | ⭐ **Greasy, occlusive, anhydrous (or ≤ 20 % water)**; **not water-washable** (unless emulsifying base) | Simple Ointment, Sulphur Ointment, Whitfield's Ointment |
| ⭐ **CREAM** | ⭐ A semisolid **EMULSION** for external use — either **oil-in-water (o/w, "aqueous cream")** or **water-in-oil (w/o, "oily cream")** | ⭐ **Contains a LARGE proportion of WATER; non-greasy or less greasy; easily spread; o/w creams are WATER-WASHABLE & "vanishing"** | Aqueous Cream, Zinc Cream, Cetomacrogol Cream, Hydrocortisone Cream |
| ⭐ **PASTE** | A semisolid containing a **HIGH proportion (usually 20–50 % or more) of finely powdered INSOLUBLE SOLIDS** in a greasy base | ⭐ **STIFF, very thick, LESS greasy, does NOT melt at body temperature, DOES NOT FLOW/ spread beyond where it is applied → an excellent PROTECTIVE & absorbent** | ⭐ **Zinc & Salicylic Acid Paste (Lassar's Paste)**, Zinc Compound Paste, Coal Tar Paste, Magnesium Sulphate Paste |
| ⭐ **JELLY / GEL** | A **transparent or translucent, non-greasy, semisolid** preparation made with a **water-soluble gelling agent** | ⭐ **Water-soluble, non-greasy, non-staining, washable, thixotropic** | K-Y Jelly, Ephedrine Jelly, Lignocaine Gel, Ichthammol Jelly, Carbomer gels |
| **CERATE** | An **unctuous preparation containing a large amount of WAX**, so stiff that it does not melt at body temperature but is soft enough to spread | Wax-rich, spreads without melting | Camphor cerate, Rose cerate |
| **GLYCEROGELATIN** | A **plastic mass of gelatin, glycerin, water & a medicament**, melted before application & applied with a brush; sets on the skin | ⭐ Melted before use; sets on cooling | **Zinc Gelatin (Unna's paste boot)** for varicose ulcers |
| **POULTICE (Cataplasm)** | Soft, moist, paste-like mass applied **HOT** to retain heat | Applied hot | Kaolin poultice |
| **PLASTER** | Solid/ semisolid **adhesive** mass on a **backing** | Adheres to the skin | Zinc oxide plaster (Ch. 9) |
| **LINIMENT** | **Liquid/ semi-liquid** applied **with friction** | Rubbed in | Camphor liniment |
| **LOTION** | **Liquid** applied **without friction** | Dabbed on | Calamine lotion |
| **OCULENTUM (Eye ointment)** | ⭐ **STERILE** ointment for the eye | ⭐ **Must be sterile & free from gritty particles** | Chloramphenicol eye ointment |

🔁 **THE THREE-WAY DISCRIMINATOR THE EXAMINER LOVES**

| | **OINTMENT** | **CREAM** | **PASTE** |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Water content** | **Low/ none (anhydrous)** | ⭐ **HIGH (it is an emulsion)** | Low |
| **Powder content** | Low (< 20 %) | Low | ⭐ **HIGH (20–50 %+)** |
| **Consistency** | Soft, unctuous | Soft, smooth | ⭐ **Very stiff** |
| **Greasiness** | ⭐ **Most greasy** | Least greasy (o/w) | Intermediate, less greasy |
| **Spreads/ flows on the skin** | Spreads readily | Spreads very easily | ⭐ **Does NOT flow — stays put** |
| **Washability** | Not water-washable | ⭐ **o/w cream is water-washable** | Not easily washable |
| **Cooling effect** | None | ⭐ **Cooling (water evaporates)** | None |
| **Main use** | Emollient, occlusive, drug vehicle | Cosmetically elegant vehicle, moist lesions | ⭐ **Protective barrier; absorbs discharge; for localised lesions** |

🧠 **MEMORY HOOK** — "**O**intment = **O**ily · **C**ream = **C**ontains water (and **C**ools) ·
**P**aste = **P**owder-packed & **P**rotective (stays **P**ut)."

## 10.2 ⭐ USES & ADVANTAGES OF OINTMENTS

⭐ **THERAPEUTIC PURPOSES (why an ointment is prescribed)**
1. ⭐ **Emollient** — softens & smooths the skin by **occlusion** (prevents water loss):
   soft paraffin, wool fat.
2. ⭐ **Protective** — a physical barrier against irritants, moisture, sun:
   zinc oxide, titanium dioxide, dimethicone (barrier cream for nappy rash & incontinence).
3. ⭐ **Vehicle for a locally-acting drug** — antiseptic, antibiotic, antifungal, keratolytic,
   anti-inflammatory (corticosteroid), local anaesthetic, parasiticide.
4. ⭐ **Vehicle for a systemically-absorbed (transdermal) drug** — glyceryl trinitrate ointment,
   hormone ointments.
5. **Counter-irritant/ analgesic** rubs — methyl salicylate, capsicum.
6. **Lubricant** — for instruments, catheters (jelly).
7. **Cosmetic** — cleansing, moisturising, sunscreen.

⭐ **FACTORS AFFECTING PERCUTANEOUS (through-the-skin) ABSORPTION**
* **Nature & concentration of the drug** — **lipid solubility & partition coefficient**
  (⭐ lipid-soluble, un-ionised drugs penetrate best), molecular size, concentration gradient.
* ⭐ **Nature of the base** — an **occlusive/ greasy base hydrates the skin & increases penetration**;
  the drug must be **released** from the base (an "affinity" that is too high retards release).
* ⭐ **Hydration of the skin** — a **hydrated stratum corneum is far more permeable**
  → **occlusive dressings & greasy bases increase absorption several-fold**.
* ⭐ **Condition & site of the skin** — **broken/ abraded/ inflamed skin absorbs much more**;
  absorption is greatest where the skin is **thin** (scrotum, face, eyelid, post-auricular) and
  least on the **palms & soles**; ⚠️ **infants' skin absorbs more (greater surface-area : weight
  ratio)**.
* **Rubbing (inunction)** and the **duration of contact & surface area**.
* **Temperature & blood flow**; **penetration enhancers** (DMSO, propylene glycol, urea, azone,
  surfactants).
* **Follicular route** — via hair follicles & sweat ducts (a minor pathway).

## 10.3 ⭐⭐ OINTMENT BASES

📌 **DEFINITION** — An **ointment base** is the **vehicle** in which the medicament is incorporated;
it determines the **consistency, spreadability, drug release, occlusiveness & washability** of the
product.

### ⭐ IDEAL PROPERTIES OF AN OINTMENT BASE
1. **Inert, non-irritant, non-sensitising, non-toxic.**
2. **Compatible** with the medicament and with the skin's pH.
3. Should **release the drug readily** at the site of application.
4. **Smooth, unctuous, easily applied & easily removed.**
5. **Physically & chemically stable** (no rancidity, no separation, no phase inversion).
6. **Good consistency** — neither too stiff nor too runny, over the range of climate temperatures.
7. Should be **able to absorb water/ aqueous secretions** where required.
8. Should **not stain** clothing; should be **cosmetically acceptable & odourless**.
9. Should be **capable of being sterilised** where necessary (eye ointments).

### 📋 THE MASTER BASE TABLE ⭐⭐

| Class | Composition & examples | ⭐ Properties | ⭐ Uses / notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| ⭐ **① OLEAGINOUS / HYDROCARBON (greasy) bases** | ⭐ **White & Yellow SOFT PARAFFIN (petrolatum/ vaseline)**, **Hard paraffin**, **Liquid paraffin**, **Paraffin Ointment**, ⭐ **SIMPLE OINTMENT BP**, vegetable oils, **Cerates**, **Plastibase** | ⭐ **Anhydrous, GREASY, highly OCCLUSIVE, EMOLLIENT; NOT water-washable; absorb only a small amount of water; chemically very stable (no rancidity, no hydrolysis)** | ⭐ **Best EMOLLIENT & OCCLUSIVE base — hydrates the skin and gives the GREATEST percutaneous absorption**; protective; vehicle for **water-sensitive drugs** (e.g. antibiotics, aspirin). ⚠️ Greasy, stains clothes, difficult to remove, **cannot absorb aqueous secretions** → not for exuding lesions |
| ⭐ **② ABSORPTION (emulsifiable/ "absorption") bases** | (a) **Anhydrous** — ⭐ **Wool fat (anhydrous lanolin), Wool alcohols Ointment, Hydrophilic Petrolatum, Simple Ointment BP (contains wool fat), beeswax + paraffin**; (b) **already containing water (w/o)** — ⭐ **Hydrous wool fat (Lanolin), Oily Cream, Cold Cream, Zinc Cream, Rose water ointment** | ⭐ **Anhydrous but can ABSORB LARGE AMOUNTS OF WATER/ aqueous solutions to form a w/o emulsion**; greasy but less than hydrocarbon; **not easily washed off**; ⭐ **emollient + occlusive** | ⭐ **Used when an AQUEOUS SOLUTION/ liquid must be incorporated into a greasy base**; good emollient for **dry, scaly skin**; ⚠️ **wool fat may cause CONTACT SENSITISATION (allergy)** and can become **rancid** |
| ⭐ **③ EMULSIFYING / WATER-REMOVABLE (o/w) bases** | ⭐ **EMULSIFYING OINTMENT BP** = ⭐ **Emulsifying wax 30 %, White soft paraffin 50 %, Liquid paraffin 20 %**; ⭐ **AQUEOUS CREAM BP** = ⭐ **Emulsifying ointment 30 %, Water 70 % (+ preservative)**; **Cetomacrogol emulsifying ointment (non-ionic), Cetrimide emulsifying ointment (cationic), Hydrophilic ointment USP** | ⭐ **o/w EMULSION → WATER-WASHABLE ("vanishing"), NON-greasy or less greasy, easily spread & removed, can be DILUTED with water, cosmetically elegant; allow exudate to escape** | ⭐ **The most widely used modern base**; for **hairy areas & large surfaces**; ⚠️ needs a **PRESERVATIVE** (microbial growth in the aqueous phase); ⚠️ ⭐ **less occlusive → less percutaneous absorption than a greasy base**; may be **incompatible** with drugs that react with the surfactant |
| ⭐ **④ WATER-SOLUBLE (greaseless) bases** | ⭐ **MACROGOL (PEG) OINTMENT** = ⭐ **PEG 4000 (or 3350) 40 % + PEG 400 60 %**; **Polyethylene glycol ointment USP**; **jellies of tragacanth, sodium alginate, methylcellulose, carbomer, starch–glycerin** | ⭐ **Completely WATER-SOLUBLE & washable, NON-greasy, non-staining, do not stain clothes, anhydrous or aqueous** | ⭐ Ideal where a **greaseless, easily removed** product is wanted; **good for hairy areas, lubricant jellies, and as a vehicle for water-soluble drugs**; ⚠️ **HYGROSCOPIC & dehydrating → may sting**; ⚠️ ⭐ **INCOMPATIBLE with phenols, benzocaine, aspirin, sulphonamides, silver salts, iodine, tannic acid, and with plastics**; poor emollient; limited water-absorbing capacity |

## 10.4 ⭐⭐ METHODS OF PREPARING OINTMENTS

📌 **THREE (four) METHODS** — the standard examination answer:

| Method | When used | Principle |
|---|---|---|
| ⭐ **1. FUSION (melting) method** | ⭐ When the base contains **several solid/ waxy ingredients of different melting points** (hard paraffin, beeswax, wool fat, cetostearyl alcohol, emulsifying wax, resins) | **MELT the ingredients together and stir until cold** |
| ⭐ **2. TRITURATION (mechanical incorporation/ levigation) method** | ⭐ When **insoluble powders** are to be incorporated into a **ready-made soft base** | **LEVIGATE the powder with a small quantity of the base (or a levigating liquid) and gradually incorporate the rest** |
| ⭐ **3. EMULSIFICATION method** | When the product is a **cream (an emulsion)** — an aqueous phase must be combined with an oily phase | **Heat both phases separately to the same temperature (~ 60–70 °C), add the internal phase to the external phase with continuous stirring, and stir until cold** |
| **4. Chemical reaction/ *in situ* method** | When the active substance is formed during preparation | e.g. **iodine ointment**; nascent soap formation |

### ⭐⭐ (1) THE FUSION METHOD — step by step

```
 ① Weigh all the ingredients
 ② MELT them in a porcelain dish/ water bath ⭐ in DESCENDING ORDER of melting point —
   i.e. the ingredient with the HIGHEST melting point FIRST (hard paraffin → beeswax →
   cetostearyl alcohol/ emulsifying wax → wool fat → soft paraffin → liquid paraffin)
 ③ Use the MINIMUM heat and the LOWEST temperature that will melt everything
 ④ Add any heat-labile or VOLATILE ingredient LAST, when the melt has cooled
   almost to setting point
 ⑤ ⭐ STIR GENTLY & CONTINUOUSLY UNTIL THE MASS IS COLD AND SET
 ⑥ If a gritty/ granular product results, ⭐ pass it through an OINTMENT MILL
   (or re-melt & re-stir)
 ⑦ Pack in a jar/ collapsible tube; label
```

⭐ **THE THREE "WHY" QUESTIONS OF THE FUSION METHOD**

| Question | ⭐ Answer |
|---|---|
| **Why melt in descending order of melting point?** | ⭐ The **highest-melting substance is melted first**, and the heat of that melt then melts the lower-melting ones — this **avoids overheating** the low-melting & heat-labile ingredients and **minimises the total heat applied** |
| **Why stir continuously until cold?** | ⭐ To keep the ingredients **uniformly mixed and prevent the high-melting waxes from SEPARATING/ CRYSTALLISING OUT in lumps as the mass cools**, and to produce a **smooth, homogeneous, non-granular** product |
| **Why not overheat?** | Overheating **volatilises** components, **degrades** heat-labile drugs, and causes **coarse crystals/ grittiness** on cooling |

⚠️ **Never stir so vigorously that air is beaten in** (an aerated ointment looks white & spongy and
oxidises).

### ⭐⭐ (2) THE TRITURATION / LEVIGATION METHOD — step by step

```
 ① Reduce the powder to a VERY FINE, IMPALPABLE powder (⭐ pass a no. 120–180 sieve);
   grittiness irritates the skin
 ② Place the powder on an OINTMENT SLAB/ TILE (glass or porcelain)
 ③ ⭐ LEVIGATE it with a SMALL QUANTITY of the base — or with a few drops of a
   LEVIGATING AGENT — using a STAINLESS-STEEL SPATULA, with firm shearing strokes,
   until a SMOOTH, GRIT-FREE PASTE is obtained
 ④ ⭐ Add the REST OF THE BASE GRADUALLY, in portions, by GEOMETRIC DILUTION
   (doubling up), working the mass with the spatula each time
 ⑤ Work with a "figure-of-eight"/ shearing motion, scraping the slab clean with the
   spatula, until uniform
 ⑥ Pack & label
```

⭐ **LEVIGATING AGENTS — a high-yield list**

| Levigating agent | Used with which base |
|---|---|
| ⭐ **Liquid paraffin (mineral oil)** | ⭐ **Oleaginous/ greasy bases** (soft paraffin, simple ointment) |
| ⭐ **Glycerin / Propylene glycol / Water** | ⭐ **Water-soluble & o/w (emulsifying) bases** |
| **A portion of the melted base itself** | Any base (the commonest & safest choice) |
| **Castor oil, olive oil, arachis oil** | Oleaginous bases |
| **Alcohol** (a few drops) | To dissolve/ wet camphor, menthol, iodine, resins |

🧠 **RULE:** ⭐ **The levigating agent must be MISCIBLE with the base** (and must not dissolve the
drug if a suspension is wanted).

⭐ **Apparatus:** **ointment slab/ tile (glass or porcelain)**, **stainless-steel spatula**
(⚠️ **use a HORN, PLASTIC or RUBBER spatula for iodine, tannic acid, salicylic acid, mercurials,
silver salts** — they react with metal), **mortar & pestle** (for small quantities/ liquids),
⭐ **OINTMENT MILL (triple roller mill)** for large-scale smoothing, **planetary/ sigma-blade mixer**
for bulk manufacture.

### ⭐ (3) THE EMULSIFICATION METHOD (for creams) — step by step

```
 ① Separate the ingredients into the OILY phase (waxes, paraffins, emulsifying wax,
   oil-soluble drugs & preservatives) and the AQUEOUS phase (water, water-soluble
   drugs, preservatives, humectants)
 ② ⭐ Heat BOTH phases SEPARATELY to the SAME temperature, about 60–70 °C
   (⭐ usually 5 °C above the melting point of the highest-melting component)
 ③ ⭐ Add the INTERNAL (dispersed) phase to the EXTERNAL (continuous) phase
   SLOWLY, with CONTINUOUS STIRRING
   • For an o/w cream → add the OIL phase to the WATER phase
   • For a w/o cream → add the WATER phase to the OIL phase
 ④ ⭐ STIR CONTINUOUSLY UNTIL COLD (or use a homogeniser/ Silverson mixer)
 ⑤ Add volatile & heat-labile ingredients (perfume, volatile oils) at ~ 40 °C
 ⑥ Make up any water lost by evaporation; homogenise; pack & label
```

⚠️ **Why must both phases be at the same temperature?** ⭐ If one phase is cooler, the **waxes
solidify prematurely at the interface**, giving a **lumpy, granular** cream and a **broken/ unstable
emulsion**.

## 10.5 ⭐⭐ INCORPORATING SPECIAL MEDICAMENTS INTO OINTMENTS
*(the exact counterpart of the suppository "special medicines" table — expect a question)*

| Nature of the medicament | ⭐ HOW TO INCORPORATE IT | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| ⭐ **Insoluble solid** | ⭐ Reduce to an **impalpable powder (no. 120–180 sieve)** → **LEVIGATE** with a little base or a levigating agent on a slab → incorporate the rest by **geometric dilution** | **Zinc oxide, calamine, sulphur, starch, boric acid, kaolin, titanium dioxide, hydrocortisone** |
| ⭐ **Solid soluble in the base (fat-soluble)** | ⭐ **Dissolve it in the melted base** (fusion), or triturate with a little **warmed** base | **Camphor, menthol, thymol, phenol, chloral hydrate, benzoic acid, salicylic acid (partly), volatile oils, coal tar** |
| ⭐ **Solid soluble in water** | ⭐ **Dissolve in the MINIMUM amount of water** and incorporate the solution: (a) into an **ABSORPTION base (wool fat)** which absorbs it, or (b) into the **aqueous phase of a cream**; ⚠️ **never simply add water to a hydrocarbon base** (it will not be absorbed and will "bleed") | **Sodium salts, potassium iodide, silver nitrate, zinc sulphate, morphine salts, ephedrine HCl** |
| ⭐ **Eutectic substances (liquefy on mixing)** | ⭐ **Mix them together FIRST and let the eutectic form deliberately**, then incorporate the liquid into the base; ⚠️ or **absorb** it (with a hardening wax/ absorbent) | ⭐ **Camphor + menthol · menthol + thymol · phenol + camphor** |
| ⭐ **Volatile substances** | ⭐ **Add LAST, at the LOWEST possible temperature** (below ~ 40 °C), and keep the container **covered**; then pack promptly in a **well-closed** container | **Volatile oils, camphor, menthol, chloroform, methyl salicylate** |
| ⭐ **Liquids (small volume)** | Stir directly into the base; a **greasy base holds only a small amount** — use an **absorption base (wool fat 10 %)** or **increase the wool fat/ emulsifying content** for larger volumes | **Ichthammol, coal tar solution, castor oil, balsam of Peru, tinctures (evaporate the alcohol first)** |
| ⭐ **Extracts & tinctures** | ⭐ **Evaporate the alcohol/ water on a water bath to a soft-extract consistency FIRST**, then incorporate — otherwise the base will not hold it | Belladonna extract, hamamelis |
| ⭐ **Substances that soften the base (lower its consistency)** | ⭐ **Add a HARDENING agent — hard paraffin, beeswax, white wax or cetostearyl alcohol** | **Phenol, chloral hydrate, camphor, coal tar, volatile oils, salicylic acid** |
| ⭐ **Substances that stain/ react with METAL** | ⭐ Use a ⭐ **HORN, PLASTIC, RUBBER or PORCELAIN spatula** and a **glass/ porcelain slab** | ⭐ **Iodine, tannic acid, salicylic acid, mercury salts (ammoniated mercury), silver nitrate, phenol, resorcinol, potassium permanganate** |
| ⭐ **Oxidising/ easily oxidisable substances** | Add an **antioxidant**; avoid metal contact & excessive heat; pack in a **collapsible tube** (excludes air) | Coal tar, resorcinol, hydroquinone |
| ⭐ **Thermolabile drugs** | ⭐ **Never use the fusion method for the drug itself** — prepare the base by fusion, cool it, then incorporate the drug by **trituration** | **Antibiotics, corticosteroids, enzymes, vitamins** |
| ⭐ **Potent drugs of very small dose** | Use a **trituration (1 in 10 with the base)** or the **aliquot method** | Corticosteroids, alkaloids |
| ⭐ **Gritty/ coarse products** | ⭐ Pass through an **OINTMENT MILL (triple roller mill)** or re-levigate | Any |

🧠 **THE FOUR-WAY RULE (same logic as suppositories):**
> **Soluble in the base → DISSOLVE. · Insoluble → LEVIGATE. · Water-soluble → minimum water into an
> ABSORPTION base or the aqueous phase. · Volatile/ heat-labile → LAST, COOL.**

## 10.7 CREAMS, PASTES, JELLIES & GELS — SPECIAL POINTS

### ⭐ CREAMS
* ⭐ **o/w (aqueous) creams** — external phase water → **non-greasy, water-washable, cooling,
  "vanishing"**; emulsifier = **emulsifying wax, cetomacrogol, SLS, triethanolamine soaps**;
  ⭐ **suitable for weeping/ moist lesions & hairy areas**.
* ⭐ **w/o (oily) creams** — external phase oil → **greasy, emollient, occlusive, NOT washable**;
  emulsifier = **wool fat, wool alcohols, beeswax–borax, sorbitan esters (Spans), calcium soaps**;
  ⭐ **suitable for dry, scaly skin** (cold cream, zinc cream, oily cream).
* ⭐ **Identifying the type of cream** — **dye test** (a water-soluble dye like amaranth colours the
  continuous phase of an **o/w** cream uniformly; an oil-soluble dye like Sudan III does so for a
  **w/o**); **dilution test** (an o/w cream is **dilutable with water**);
  **conductivity test** (⭐ **o/w conducts electricity; w/o does not**).
* ⭐ **HLB values:** ⭐ **o/w emulsifier HLB 8–18**; **w/o emulsifier HLB 3–6**;
  wetting 7–9; solubilising 15–18; antifoam 1–3.
* ⭐ **All creams containing water MUST contain a PRESERVATIVE** — **methyl & propyl parabens
  (0.2 % + 0.02 %), phenoxyethanol 1 %, chlorocresol 0.1 %, benzoic acid, benzalkonium chloride**.
* ⚠️ **Instability of creams:** **creaming, cracking (coalescence), phase inversion,
  bleeding (liquid separation), microbial spoilage, rancidity, "drying out" of the surface.**

### ⭐ PASTES
* ⭐ **Contain 20–50 % (or more) of insoluble powder** (zinc oxide, starch, calcium carbonate, talc,
  kaolin) → **very stiff**.
* ⭐ **Advantages:** **do NOT melt or flow at body temperature → remain exactly where applied**
  (good for **localised, well-demarcated lesions** such as psoriasis plaques);
  **absorb serous discharge**; **less occlusive than an ointment** (so they allow the skin to
  "breathe"); **protective barrier**; **can carry a high drug load**.
* ⚠️ **Disadvantages:** stiff & **difficult to apply & to remove** (⭐ remove with **arachis/ olive
  oil or liquid paraffin, not water**); can be uncomfortable & unsightly; may be **drying**.
* Prepared by the ⭐ **trituration/ levigation method**, often finished on an **ointment mill**.

### ⭐ JELLIES & GELS
* 📌 **Transparent/ translucent, non-greasy, semisolid** preparations containing a **gelling agent**
  in an **aqueous or hydro-alcoholic** medium.
* ⭐ **Gelling agents:** **tragacanth, sodium alginate, pectin, starch, gelatin, methylcellulose,
  sodium carboxymethylcellulose, hydroxypropyl methylcellulose (HPMC), carbomer (Carbopol),
  bentonite, magnesium aluminium silicate (Veegum), poloxamer, PVP.**
* ⭐ **Types:** **medicated jellies** (ephedrine, lignocaine, ichthammol), **lubricant jellies**
  (K-Y, catheter jelly), **spermicidal jellies (nonoxynol-9)**, **patch-test/ ECG electrode jellies**,
  **hair & cosmetic gels**.
* ⭐ **Advantages:** non-greasy, **water-washable, non-staining**, cooling, cosmetically elegant,
  **good for hairy areas, mucous membranes & eyes**, **thixotropic** (stiff at rest, flows on
  shearing).
* ⚠️ **Must contain a PRESERVATIVE**; may **dry out** → keep in a well-closed container;
  ⚠️ **carbomer gels must be neutralised** (with triethanolamine/ NaOH) to gel.
* ⭐ **Syneresis** — the **shrinking of a gel with the exudation of liquid**; **imbibition** — the
  taking up of liquid **without** an increase in volume; **swelling** — uptake **with** an increase in
  volume; **xerogel** — a dried gel (tragacanth ribbons, gelatin sheets, dry cellulose).

## 10.8 ⭐ EVALUATION, PACKING & LABELLING

⭐ **EVALUATION (QUALITY CONTROL) OF SEMISOLIDS**
1. ⭐ **Appearance, colour, odour, homogeneity & absence of GRITTINESS** (rub a little between the
   fingers/ on glass).
2. ⭐ **Consistency/ spreadability** — measured by a **penetrometer** or by the
   **spreadability (slip & drag) test**.
3. ⭐ **Rheology/ viscosity** — semisolids are **non-Newtonian, plastic & thixotropic**;
   they exhibit a **yield value**.
4. ⭐ **pH** — should be near the skin's pH (**4.5–6.5**).
5. **Drug content/ assay & content uniformity.**
6. ⭐ **Drug release/ diffusion studies** — **Franz diffusion cell**, agar-plate or membrane methods.
7. **Water number** — ⭐ the **maximum quantity of water that 100 g of a base can hold** at 20 °C
   (a measure of an absorption base's capacity).
8. ⭐ **Loss on drying / volatile content, minimum fill (net content), weight variation.**
9. ⭐ **Microbial limit test / sterility** (⭐ **eye ointments must be STERILE** and must also pass
   the **metal-particle test**).
10. **Stability testing** — freeze–thaw cycles, centrifugation, elevated temperature; check for
    **bleeding, phase separation, crystal growth, rancidity, colour change**.
11. **Irritancy/ patch test** (Draize test) & sensitisation.

⭐ **PACKING & STORAGE**
* ⭐ **Collapsible tubes (aluminium, tin, plastic/ laminate)** — the **best choice**: they
  **exclude air & moisture, prevent contamination by the patient's fingers**, are convenient, and
  allow easy dispensing of small amounts.
* **Wide-mouth glass or plastic jars** (amber for light-sensitive drugs) — used for larger
  quantities/ extemporaneous dispensing; ⚠️ **more liable to contamination**.
* ⭐ **Eye ointments** — **STERILE collapsible tubes of ≤ 5 g** with a **nozzle**.
* ⚠️ Store in a **cool place**, away from direct heat & sunlight; ⚠️ **do not freeze** (creams crack).
* ⚠️ **PEG bases must not be packed in polystyrene**; ⚠️ **coal tar & phenol attack some plastics**.

⭐ **LABELLING**
* ⭐ ⭐ **"FOR EXTERNAL USE ONLY"** — on a **RED (or red-bordered) label**.
* Name & strength of the preparation, **quantity**, **directions** ("Apply to the affected part
  twice daily"), **patient's name & date**, **"Keep out of the reach of children"**,
  **storage instructions**, expiry.
* Auxiliary labels as needed: **"Avoid contact with the eyes"** · **"Do not apply to broken skin"**
  (counter-irritants) · **"May stain clothing"** (coal tar, dithranol, iodine) ·
  **"Discard 4 weeks after opening"** (eye ointments) ·
  **"Wash hands after use"** · **"Store in a cool place"**.
* ⚠️ **Sterile products:** "Sterile" + the batch number + expiry.

## 10.9 CHAPTER 10 — RAPID REVISION SHEET

**DEFINITIONS**
* ⭐ **Ointment** = semisolid, external, drug in a **greasy base**, **anhydrous/ ≤ 20 % water**.
* ⭐ **Cream** = semisolid **EMULSION** (o/w or w/o) → contains **much water**.
* ⭐ **Paste** = **20–50 %+ insoluble powder** → **stiff, does not flow, PROTECTIVE**.
* ⭐ **Jelly/ Gel** = transparent, **non-greasy, water-soluble** gelling agent.
* ⭐ **Cerate** = wax-rich, does not melt at body temperature.
* ⭐ **Glycerogelatin** = melted before application, sets on the skin (**Unna's boot**).

**BASES (4 classes)**
1. ⭐ **Oleaginous/ hydrocarbon** (soft & hard paraffin, Simple Ointment) — **most greasy, most
   occlusive, greatest percutaneous absorption, most stable, NOT washable, absorbs little water**.
2. ⭐ **Absorption** (wool fat, wool alcohols ointment, hydrophilic petrolatum, oily cream) —
   ⭐ **anhydrous but absorbs LARGE amounts of water → for incorporating aqueous liquids**;
   ⚠️ wool fat **sensitises**.
3. ⭐ **Emulsifying/ water-removable (o/w)** (Emulsifying Ointment, Aqueous Cream) —
   ⭐ **water-washable, non-greasy, needs a PRESERVATIVE, less occlusive**.
4. ⭐ **Water-soluble/ greaseless** (**Macrogol/ PEG ointment**) — ⭐ **completely washable,
   non-staining**; ⚠️ **hygroscopic (stings), incompatible with phenols, benzocaine, aspirin,
   sulphonamides, silver salts, iodine, plastics**.

**FORMULAE TO MEMORISE** 🔢
* ⭐ **Simple Ointment = Wool fat 5 + Hard paraffin 5 + Cetostearyl alcohol 5 + Soft paraffin 85.**
* ⭐ **Emulsifying Ointment = Emulsifying wax 30 + White soft paraffin 50 + Liquid paraffin 20.**
* ⭐ **Emulsifying Wax = Cetostearyl alcohol 90 + Sodium lauryl sulphate 10.**
* ⭐ **Aqueous Cream = Emulsifying ointment 30 + Phenoxyethanol 1 + Water to 100.**
* ⭐ **Oily Cream = Wool alcohols ointment 50 + Water 50.**
* ⭐ **Wool Alcohols Ointment = Wool alcohols 6 + Hard paraffin 24 + White soft paraffin 10 +
  Liquid paraffin 60.**
* ⭐ **Macrogol Ointment = PEG 4000 40 + PEG 400 60.**
* ⭐ **Simple Eye Ointment = Liquid paraffin 10 + Wool fat 10 + Yellow soft paraffin 80 (STERILE).**
* ⭐ **Whitfield's Ointment = Benzoic acid 6 % + Salicylic acid 3 %.**
* ⭐ **Lassar's Paste = Zinc oxide 24 + Salicylic acid 2 + Starch 24 + White soft paraffin 50.**
* ⭐ **Zinc Compound Paste = Zinc oxide 25 + Starch 25 + Soft paraffin 50.**
* ⭐ **Sulphur Ointment = 10 % sulphur; Zinc Ointment = 15 % zinc oxide.**
* ⭐ **Glycerin of Starch = Starch 10 + Water 20 + Glycerin 70.**

**EMULSIFYING WAXES**
* ⭐ **Emulsifying wax BP → sodium lauryl sulphate → ANIONIC** (⚠️ not with cationic drugs).
* ⭐ **Cetrimide emulsifying wax → CATIONIC** (⚠️ not with anionic drugs).
* ⭐ **Cetomacrogol emulsifying wax → NON-IONIC → compatible with both.**

**METHODS OF PREPARATION**
* ⭐ **FUSION** — for bases with several waxy solids; ⭐ **melt in DESCENDING order of melting point,
  use minimum heat, add volatiles last, and STIR CONTINUOUSLY UNTIL COLD** (to prevent the waxes
  separating out and to avoid grittiness).
* ⭐ **TRITURATION/ LEVIGATION** — for insoluble powders; **fine powder (no. 120–180 sieve) →
  levigate on a slab with a spatula → geometric dilution**.
  ⭐ **Levigating agent must be MISCIBLE with the base: liquid paraffin for greasy bases;
  glycerin/ propylene glycol/ water for water-miscible bases.**
* ⭐ **EMULSIFICATION** — for creams; ⭐ **heat both phases separately to the SAME temperature
  (60–70 °C), add the internal phase to the external phase with constant stirring, stir till cold**
  (unequal temperatures → lumpy, unstable cream).
* ⭐ Grittiness is corrected with an **OINTMENT MILL (triple roller mill)**.

**SPECIAL MEDICAMENTS**
* ⭐ **Soluble in base → dissolve · Insoluble → levigate · Water-soluble → minimum water into an
  ABSORPTION base (wool fat) or the aqueous phase · Volatile/ thermolabile → add LAST when cool ·
  Eutectics (camphor + menthol) → let them liquefy first · Softening agents (phenol, chloral
  hydrate, coal tar) → add a HARDENING wax · Iodine, tannic acid, mercurials, silver salts →
  use a HORN/ PLASTIC spatula.**

**PERCUTANEOUS ABSORPTION**
* ⭐ Increased by **lipid solubility, an occlusive/ greasy base, skin hydration, broken or inflamed
  skin, thin skin (face/ scrotum), rubbing, larger area & longer contact, penetration enhancers**;
  ⚠️ **infants absorb more**.

**OTHER POINTS**
* ⭐ **o/w emulsifier HLB 8–18; w/o emulsifier HLB 3–6.**
* ⭐ **o/w cream conducts electricity & is dilutable with water; w/o does not.**
* ⭐ **All water-containing semisolids need a preservative** (parabens 0.2 + 0.02 %,
  phenoxyethanol 1 %, chlorocresol 0.1 %).
* ⭐ **Water number** = maximum water 100 g of a base can hold.
* ⭐ **Syneresis** = a gel shrinking & exuding liquid; **xerogel** = a dried gel.
* ⭐ **Eye ointments must be STERILE, in ≤ 5 g sterile tubes, free of metal particles, discarded
  4 weeks after opening.**
* ⭐ **Collapsible tubes are the best container** (exclude air & contamination).
* ⭐ **Label: "FOR EXTERNAL USE ONLY" on a RED label.**
* ⭐ **Pastes are removed with oil (arachis/ olive/ liquid paraffin), not water.**
* Semisolids are **non-Newtonian, plastic & thixotropic**, with a **yield value**;
  consistency measured by a **penetrometer**.
