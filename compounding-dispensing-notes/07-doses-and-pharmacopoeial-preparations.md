## 7.1 POSOLOGY & THE VOCABULARY OF DOSE

📌 **POSOLOGY** — the branch of medical/ pharmaceutical science that deals with **the dose of a
drug** (Gk. *posos* = how much + *logos* = study).

📌 **DOSE** — the **quantity of a drug or medicine prescribed to be taken at one time, or in a
stated period, to produce the desired therapeutic effect** in a patient of average build & age.

### ⭐⭐ TYPES OF DOSE — the master definition table

| Type of dose | 📌 Definition |
|---|---|
| **Single dose** | The quantity given **at one time** |
| **Daily dose** | The total quantity given in **24 hours** |
| **Total (course) dose** | The quantity given over the **whole course** of treatment |
| **Divided (fractional) dose** | The daily dose split into **smaller doses at intervals** |
| ⭐ **Minimum (threshold) dose** | The **smallest** amount that will produce a **detectable therapeutic effect** |
| ⭐ **Maximum dose** | The **largest** amount that can be given **without producing toxic effects** — ⚠️ the pharmacist must check every prescription against this |
| ⭐ **Therapeutic (usual/ medicinal) dose** | The dose that produces the **desired therapeutic effect** — lies between the minimum and the maximum |
| ⭐ **Toxic dose** | The amount that produces **harmful/ poisonous effects** |
| ⭐ **Lethal (fatal) dose** | The amount that causes **death** |
| ⭐ **LOADING (priming/ initial) dose** | A **large initial dose** given to achieve the **effective plasma concentration rapidly**; e.g. digoxin, sulphonamides, antimalarials, some antibiotics |
| ⭐ **MAINTENANCE dose** | The dose given **repeatedly** to **maintain** the therapeutic plasma level |
| **Prophylactic dose** | Given to **prevent** a disease (e.g. antimalarial prophylaxis, tetanus toxoid) |
| **Diagnostic dose** | Given as an aid to **diagnosis** (e.g. barium meal, radio-opaque contrast, tuberculin, histamine test) |
| **Booster dose** | A later dose to **reinforce immunity** |
| **Depot dose** | A single dose from a **long-acting (depot) preparation** giving slow, sustained release |
| **Standard/ Official dose** | The dose stated in the **pharmacopoeia** |
| ⭐ **ED₅₀ (median effective dose)** | Dose producing the desired effect in **50 %** of the population |
| ⭐ **LD₅₀ (median lethal dose)** | Dose that kills **50 %** of the test animals — a measure of **acute toxicity** |
| ⭐ **TD₅₀** | Dose producing toxicity in **50 %** |
| ⭐ **THERAPEUTIC INDEX (TI)** | 🔢 **TI = LD₅₀ ÷ ED₅₀** — a measure of the **safety margin** of a drug. ⭐ **The HIGHER the TI, the SAFER the drug.** ⚠️ **Low TI (narrow margin) drugs: digoxin, lithium, warfarin, phenytoin, theophylline, aminoglycosides, cytotoxics** — these need therapeutic drug monitoring |
| **Certain Safety Factor (CSF)** | 🔢 **LD₁ ÷ ED₉₉** — a stricter safety measure |
| **Margin of safety** | The range between the therapeutic and the toxic dose |

🧠 **THE DOSE LADDER — memorise the order:**
**Minimum (threshold) → Therapeutic → Maximum → Toxic → Lethal.**

## 7.2 ⭐⭐ FACTORS AFFECTING THE DOSE OF A DRUG

📌 A very frequently asked "list" question. Learn the **five groups**.

### GROUP A — Factors relating to the PATIENT

| Factor | Effect on dose |
|---|---|
| ⭐ **Age** | **Children need less** (immature enzymes, kidneys & blood–brain barrier); the **elderly need less** (reduced renal/ hepatic function). Use the paediatric formulae (Ch. 6) |
| ⭐ **Body weight & body surface area** | Dose ∝ weight/ BSA; obese & very thin patients need adjustment. **BSA is the most accurate basis** |
| ⭐ **Sex** | **Women generally need a slightly smaller dose** (lower body weight, higher proportion of fat); ⚠️ **special caution in pregnancy, lactation & menstruation** — teratogens, drugs excreted in milk, ecbolics & purgatives are avoided |
| **Species & race** | Genetic differences in metabolism (e.g. **slow vs fast acetylators of isoniazid**) |
| ⭐ **Idiosyncrasy** | ⭐ An **abnormal, unpredictable, genetically determined** reaction to a normal dose (e.g. **haemolysis with primaquine in G6PD deficiency**, malignant hyperthermia with halothane) |
| ⭐ **Allergy / hypersensitivity** | An immunologically mediated reaction (penicillin anaphylaxis) — **dose-independent** |
| ⭐ **Tolerance** | ⭐ **Progressively larger doses needed to produce the same effect** on repeated use — e.g. **morphine, barbiturates, alcohol, nitrates, ephedrine**. **Tachyphylaxis** = rapid tolerance after a few doses (ephedrine, amphetamine) |
| ⭐ **Cumulation** | ⭐ The drug is **eliminated more slowly than it is administered**, so it **accumulates** to toxic levels — e.g. **digitalis, heavy metals, emetine, bromides, chloroquine** |
| ⭐ **Habituation & addiction (drug dependence)** | Psychological/ physical dependence — morphine, barbiturates, alcohol |
| **Pathological state / disease** | ⚠️ **Liver disease** → reduced metabolism → **reduce dose**; ⚠️ **Kidney disease** → reduced excretion → **reduce dose**; fever, dehydration, hypoproteinaemia, thyroid state, GI disease (absorption) all alter dose |
| **Emotional/ psychological state** | Placebo response; anxiety alters requirement of analgesics & sedatives |
| **Genetic factors (pharmacogenetics)** | Enzyme polymorphisms (CYP2D6, G6PD, pseudocholinesterase) |
| **Diet & nutrition** | Food may ↓ or ↑ absorption; grapefruit juice inhibits CYP3A4; protein-rich diet ↑ metabolism |
| **Circadian rhythm (chronopharmacology)** | Corticosteroids best in the **morning**; antihistamines & hypnotics at **night** |

### GROUP B — Factors relating to the DRUG

| Factor | Effect |
|---|---|
| ⭐ **Route of administration** | ⭐ **Parenteral (IV) doses are SMALLER than oral doses** because oral drugs undergo **incomplete absorption + first-pass metabolism**. e.g. morphine oral 10–30 mg but IM 10 mg |
| ⭐ **Dosage form** | Solutions act faster than suspensions > capsules > tablets > coated/ SR tablets |
| **Physicochemical properties** | Particle size, solubility, polymorphic form, salt form, pKa, lipid solubility, degree of ionisation |
| **Bioavailability** | 🔢 **F = fraction of the administered dose reaching the systemic circulation unchanged**; IV F = 1 (100 %) |
| **Dose frequency & interval** | Determined by the **half-life (t½)** |
| **Purity & potency of the preparation** | Standardised/ assayed preparations only |
| **Age & storage of the preparation** | A deteriorated drug is under-potent |

### GROUP C — Factors relating to DRUG INTERACTION & COMBINATION
* ⭐ **Synergism** — the combined effect is **greater** than the sum of the individual effects.
  * **Additive (summation)** — 1 + 1 = 2, e.g. **aspirin + paracetamol**.
  * **True (super-additive) synergism/ potentiation** — 1 + 1 > 2, e.g.
    **sulphamethoxazole + trimethoprim**, **levodopa + carbidopa**,
    **penicillin + probenecid**, **adrenaline + local anaesthetic**.
* ⭐ **Antagonism** — one drug **opposes** the effect of another.
  * **Chemical antagonism** — they react chemically (**tannic acid + alkaloid**,
    **chelation: dimercaprol + arsenic; desferrioxamine + iron**).
  * **Physical antagonism** — adsorption (**activated charcoal + a poison**).
  * **Physiological/ functional antagonism** — opposite effects through **different receptors**
    (**adrenaline vs histamine**).
  * **Pharmacological/ competitive antagonism** — both act on the **same receptor**
    (**atropine vs acetylcholine; naloxone vs morphine; propranolol vs adrenaline**).
* ⭐ **Enzyme induction** → faster metabolism → **larger dose needed** —
  **phenobarbitone, phenytoin, rifampicin, carbamazepine, griseofulvin, smoking, alcohol
  (chronic)**.
* ⭐ **Enzyme inhibition** → slower metabolism → **smaller dose needed / toxicity** —
  **cimetidine, erythromycin, ketoconazole, metronidazole, allopurinol, isoniazid,
  chloramphenicol, grapefruit juice**.
* **Displacement from plasma protein binding** — aspirin displaces warfarin → bleeding.
* **Altered absorption** — antacids/ iron/ milk chelate tetracyclines & fluoroquinolones.
* **Altered renal excretion** — probenecid blocks penicillin secretion;
  alkalinisation of urine speeds salicylate excretion.

### GROUP D — Factors relating to the TIME & MANNER of administration
* **Before food (a.c.)** — for drugs whose absorption is reduced by food (ampicillin, tetracycline,
  levodopa) and for appetite stimulants/ bitters.
* **After food (p.c.)** — for **gastric irritants**: aspirin/ NSAIDs, iron, metronidazole,
  griseofulvin (fatty meal aids absorption), corticosteroids.
* **On an empty stomach/ at bedtime** — hypnotics, laxatives.
* **Duration of therapy & cumulative effect**; adherence/ compliance.

### GROUP E — ENVIRONMENTAL factors
Temperature & climate (sweating alters fluid & electrolyte requirement), altitude, occupational
exposure, smoking (**enzyme induction**), alcohol.

🧠 **MEMORY HOOK — the 12 classic "factors affecting dose" for a written answer:**
**A**ge, **B**ody weight/ BSA, **S**ex, **R**oute, **T**ime of administration, **T**olerance,
**I**diosyncrasy, **C**umulation, **D**isease (liver/kidney), **D**rug interaction/ synergism &
antagonism, **G**enetic factors, **E**nvironment.

## 7.3 ⭐ THE CLASSICAL DOSAGE FORMS & OFFICIAL PREPARATIONS — DEFINITIONS

📌 Learn each **definition + its official strength**, because the examiner asks both
*"Define a linctus"* and *"What is the strength of a fresh infusion?"*

### A. ORAL LIQUID PREPARATIONS

| Preparation | 📌 Definition | 🔢 Strength / dose |
|---|---|---|
| ⭐ **MIXTURE (Mistura)** | A **liquid oral preparation** containing one or more drugs **dissolved, suspended or dispersed** in a suitable vehicle, intended to be taken in **divided doses** | Dose usually **10–15 mL**; supplied 100–300 mL; ⚠️ **suspensions need "Shake well"** |
| ⭐ **DRAUGHT (Haustus)** | A mixture intended to be taken as a **SINGLE dose** | **Usually 50 mL** (some texts 30–50 mL) |
| ⭐ **SOLUTION (Solutio)** | A **clear, homogeneous, molecularly dispersed** liquid preparation of a solute in a solvent | — |
| ⭐ **SYRUP (Syrupus)** | A **concentrated, viscous, nearly saturated aqueous solution of sucrose** or another sugar; may be medicated or simply a flavoured vehicle | ⭐ **Simple Syrup IP = 66.7 % w/w sucrose** (≈ 85 % w/v; **667 g sucrose in 1000 g** of syrup). Sp. gr. ≈ **1.315–1.33**. Self-preserving because of high osmotic pressure |
| ⭐ **ELIXIR** | A **clear, sweetened, pleasantly flavoured HYDRO-ALCOHOLIC** liquid preparation for oral use | Alcohol 5–40 %; dose 5–15 mL. e.g. **Elixir of Piperazine Citrate, Paediatric Chloral Elixir** |
| ⭐ **LINCTUS** | ⭐ A **VISCOUS, SWEET, DEMULCENT** oral liquid used in the treatment of **COUGH**, to be **sipped slowly and taken UNDILUTED** so as to soothe the throat | Dose **5 mL** (⚠️ label: *"to be sipped slowly, undiluted"*). e.g. **Simple Linctus, Codeine Linctus, Squill Opiate Linctus** |
| ⭐ **TINCTURE (Tinctura)** | An **alcoholic or hydro-alcoholic solution** prepared from **vegetable drugs or chemical substances** | ⭐ **Simple/non-potent drugs = 1 in 5 (20 % w/v)**; ⭐ **potent drugs = 1 in 10 (10 % w/v)** |
| ⭐ **SPIRIT (Spiritus)** | An **alcoholic or hydro-alcoholic solution of a VOLATILE substance** | Usually **5–10 %**; e.g. **Spirit of Camphor 10 %, Aromatic Spirit of Ammonia, Spirit of Peppermint 10 %** |
| ⭐ **AROMATIC WATER (Aqua)** | A **clear, SATURATED aqueous solution of a volatile oil** or other aromatic/ volatile substance | ≈ **0.2 %**; e.g. **Peppermint Water, Chloroform Water (⭐ = 0.25 % v/v chloroform, i.e. Double-strength chloroform water diluted 1 : 1)**, Anise Water, Dill Water, Cinnamon Water |
| ⭐ **INFUSION (Infusum)** | A **dilute aqueous solution** of the readily soluble constituents of a crude drug, prepared by **macerating** the drug with **cold or boiling water for a SHORT time (15–30 min)** | ⭐ **Fresh infusion = 1 in 10 (10 % w/v); SHELF-LIFE 12 HOURS.** ⭐ **Concentrated infusion = 8× strength** (dilute **1 part with 7 parts water**), preserved with 20–25 % alcohol |
| ⭐ **DECOCTION (Decoctum)** | An aqueous preparation made by **BOILING** the drug with water (about 10 min) and **straining while hot** | Used for hard, woody drugs; very short shelf-life |
| ⭐ **LIQUID (FLUID) EXTRACT** | A **concentrated liquid preparation** of a vegetable drug, made so that **1 mL is equivalent to 1 g of the drug** | ⭐ **1 in 1**; e.g. Liquid Extract of Liquorice, of Belladonna, of Ipecacuanha, of Cascara |
| **DRY / SOFT EXTRACT** | The evaporated concentrate of a drug extract, of powder (dry) or ointment-like (soft) consistency | e.g. Belladonna Dry Extract |
| **EMULSION (Emulsio)** | A **biphasic** liquid preparation of two immiscible liquids, one dispersed as globules in the other, stabilised by an **emulgent** | ⭐ *"Shake well"*; e.g. **Liquid Paraffin Emulsion, Cod Liver Oil Emulsion, Castor Oil Emulsion** |
| **SUSPENSION** | A **biphasic** preparation of finely divided **insoluble solid** dispersed in a liquid | ⭐ *"Shake well"* |
| **MUCILAGE (Mucilago)** | A **thick, viscous, adhesive aqueous solution of a gum**, used as a suspending/ emulsifying agent | ⭐ **Mucilage of Acacia = 35 % w/v**; Mucilage of Tragacanth = **1.25 % w/v**; Mucilage of Starch ≈ 2 % |
| **GLYCERIN / GLYCERITE (Glycerinum)** | A solution of a medicament in **glycerin** | e.g. **Glycerin of Boric acid (11 % w/w), Glycerin of Starch, Glycerin of Thymol Compound, Glycerin of Tannic acid, Glycerin of Ichthammol** |
| **VINEGAR (Acetum)** | A solution of a drug in **dilute acetic acid** | e.g. Vinegar of Squill |
| **HONEY (Mel)** | A preparation with **honey** as the vehicle | e.g. **Honey of Borax (Borax + honey + glycerin)** — mouth paint for thrush |
| **OXYMEL** | A preparation of honey + acetic acid | Classical |
| **JULEP** | A sweetened, flavoured aqueous vehicle | Classical |
| **ORAL DROPS** | Concentrated liquid measured in drops | Paediatric vitamins |
| **ORAL POWDER / EFFERVESCENT GRANULES** | Dry preparation dissolved/ dispersed in water before use | e.g. **Effervescent Magnesium Sulphate, ORS** |

### B. PREPARATIONS FOR EXTERNAL APPLICATION

| Preparation | 📌 Definition | ⭐ Key point |
|---|---|---|
| ⭐ **LOTION (Lotio)** | A **liquid** preparation for **application to the skin WITHOUT FRICTION** (dabbed/ patted on, or applied on a dressing) | ⭐ RED label, "FOR EXTERNAL USE"; **often needs "Shake well"** (calamine lotion). Produces a **cooling** effect by evaporation |
| ⭐ **LINIMENT (Linimentum)** | A **liquid or semi-liquid** preparation for **application to the skin WITH FRICTION (rubbing)** | ⭐ Usually contains an oil/ soap/ alcohol; ⚠️ **NEVER applied to broken skin**; **counter-irritant**. Ribbed bottle, RED label |
| ⭐ **APPLICATION** | A liquid/ semi-liquid preparation applied to the skin, usually as a **parasiticide** | e.g. **Benzyl Benzoate Application (25 %)** for scabies |
| ⭐ **PAINT (Pigmentum)** | A liquid applied to the **skin or mucous membrane with a BRUSH**, leaving a film | e.g. **Compound Podophyllum Paint (15–25 %), Magenta Paint (Castellani's), Iodine Paint** |
| ⭐ **COLLODION (Collodium)** | A liquid preparation of **pyroxylin (nitrocellulose) in a mixture of ether & alcohol**, which on application **evaporates leaving a tough, flexible, protective film** | e.g. **Flexible Collodion (+ castor oil & colophony), Salicylic Acid Collodion (for corns/ warts)**. ⚠️ **Highly inflammable** |
| **CATAPLASM / POULTICE (Cataplasma)** | A **soft, moist, heated, paste-like** preparation applied to the skin to **retain heat** & relieve pain/ inflammation | e.g. **Kaolin Poultice, Linseed Poultice**; ⭐ applied **as hot as the patient can bear**, spread on a cloth |
| ⭐ **PLASTER (Emplastrum)** | A **solid or semisolid adhesive mass** spread on a **backing** (cloth/ plastic), which **adheres to the skin on warming** | See Ch. 9 |
| **DUSTING POWDER** | A finely divided powder for **external application** | ⭐ Must pass a **no. 120/ 180 sieve**; must NOT be applied to open wounds unless **sterile** |
| **EAR (Aural) DROPS** | Solution/ suspension instilled into the ear; vehicle = water, glycerin, propylene glycol, alcohol | e.g. Sodium bicarbonate ear drops |
| **NASAL DROPS / SPRAY** | Solution instilled into the nose; ⭐ vehicle should be **isotonic & near pH 5.5–7.5**, preferably **aqueous** (⚠️ **oily nasal drops may cause lipoid pneumonia**) | Ephedrine nasal drops 0.5–1 % |
| **EYE DROPS (Guttae ophthalmicae)** | ⭐ **STERILE, isotonic, buffered, particle-free** aqueous/ oily solution or suspension for the eye, containing a **preservative** (benzalkonium chloride 0.01 %, chlorhexidine, phenylmercuric nitrate 0.002 %) in multi-dose containers | ⭐ **Discard 4 weeks after opening**; single-dose units for eye surgery must be **preservative-free** |
| **EYE LOTION (Collyrium)** | A **sterile** aqueous solution for **bathing/ irrigating** the eye; ⭐ **must be preservative-free & discarded within 24 h of opening** | Sodium chloride eye lotion |
| **EYE OINTMENT (Oculentum)** | Sterile ointment for the eye, base = **Simple Eye Ointment Base (liquid paraffin 10 %, wool fat 10 %, yellow soft paraffin 80 %)** | Sterile |
| **GARGLE (Gargarisma)** | ⭐ An **aqueous solution used to treat the THROAT**, usually supplied **CONCENTRATED to be DILUTED with warm water before use** | ⭐ Labels: **"NOT TO BE SWALLOWED", "Dilute before use"** |
| **MOUTHWASH (Collutorium)** | An aqueous solution used for **rinsing the mouth** | "Not to be swallowed" |
| **THROAT PAINT** | Viscous (glycerin-based) liquid applied to the throat with a brush | **Compound Glycerin of Thymol, Mandl's paint** |
| **DOUCHE** | An aqueous solution directed **against a body cavity/ part** (eye, nose, throat, vagina) | Warm, isotonic |
| **ENEMA** | A liquid preparation given **rectally** | ⭐ **Evacuant enema** (e.g. **soap enema, glycerin, sodium phosphate**) — cleanses the bowel; ⭐ **Retention enema** (e.g. **arachis oil, corticosteroid, paraldehyde**) — held in for absorption or local effect. Given at **body temperature** |
| **SUPPOSITORY / PESSARY / BOUGIE** | See Ch. 9 | — |
| **INHALATION / NEBULA / SPRAY / INSUFFLATION** | See Ch. 11 | — |
| **OINTMENT / CREAM / PASTE / JELLY / GEL** | See Ch. 10 | — |

### C. SOLID ORAL DOSAGE FORMS

| Preparation | 📌 Definition | ⭐ Key point |
|---|---|---|
| ⭐ **POWDER (Pulvis)** | A **dry mixture of finely divided drugs** for internal or external use | **Bulk powder** (dispensed in a wide-mouth jar, dose measured by the patient — only for non-potent drugs like antacids & laxatives) vs **DIVIDED powder/ Chartula** (each dose wrapped separately in a powder paper — **essential for potent drugs**). Powder papers are folded by the **standard double-fold** method |
| **EFFERVESCENT POWDER/ GRANULES** | Contains an **acid (citric/ tartaric) + a bicarbonate**, which release CO₂ in water | ⚠️ Must be kept **absolutely dry, air-tight** |
| **CACHET** | A **rice-paper (starch) shell** enclosing an unpleasant powder; moistened & swallowed | Obsolete |
| **CAPSULE (Capsula)** | Drug enclosed in a **gelatin shell** — **hard (two-piece, for powders/ granules)** or **soft (one-piece, for liquids/ oils)** | ⭐ Hard capsule sizes: **000 (largest) → 5 (smallest)**; sizes 0, 1, 2 commonest |
| **TABLET (Tabella/ Compressi)** | A solid unit dosage form made by **compression** (or moulding) of powders/ granules | Types: uncoated, sugar-coated, film-coated, **enteric-coated**, effervescent, dispersible, chewable, sublingual, buccal, **sustained/ controlled release**, implant, vaginal |
| **PILL (Pilula)** | A **small, spherical mass** of drug + excipient, made by hand-rolling | Obsolete; excipients: liquorice powder, acacia, glucose, glycerin |
| **LOZENGE / TROCHE (Trochiscus)** | A **flavoured, hard, solid** dosage form to be **sucked slowly in the mouth** for a local effect on the mouth/ throat | Base = sugar + acacia |
| **PASTILLE (Pastillus)** | Like a lozenge but with a **soft, glycero-gelatin or glycerin–gelatin base** | Sucked slowly |
| **GRANULE** | Small agglomerated particles | For reconstitution |
| **IMPLANT / PELLET** | Sterile, small, sterile cylinder inserted subcutaneously for prolonged release | Hormones |
| **TABLET TRITURATE** | Small, moulded, rapidly soluble tablet made from a moist mass | For potent drugs |

### D. STERILE PREPARATIONS

| Preparation | 📌 Definition | ⭐ Key point |
|---|---|---|
| **INJECTION (Injectio)** | A **sterile, pyrogen-free** solution, suspension or emulsion, or a sterile powder for reconstitution, for **parenteral** administration | **SVP** (small volume, ≤ 100 mL) & ⭐ **LVP (large volume parenteral, > 100 mL — must be isotonic, pyrogen-free, and contain NO preservative or bacteriostat)** |
| **INFUSION (IV)** | Large-volume sterile aqueous solution given IV | Dextrose, saline, Ringer's |

## 7.5 ⭐ DOSAGE-FORM QUICK COMPARISONS (frequent discriminators)

🔁 **Mixture vs Draught** → **mixture = divided doses; draught = single 50 mL dose.**
🔁 **Syrup vs Elixir** → **syrup = aqueous, sugar-based, no/low alcohol; elixir = hydro-alcoholic,
clear, sweetened & aromatic.**
🔁 **Linctus vs Mixture** → **linctus is viscous, sweet, taken undiluted & sipped slowly for cough.**
🔁 **Tincture vs Liquid extract** → **tincture 1 in 5 or 1 in 10; liquid extract 1 in 1** (liquid
extract is therefore the more concentrated → smaller dose).
🔁 **Infusion vs Decoction** → **infusion = short maceration, no boiling; decoction = boiled.**
🔁 **Lotion vs Liniment** → **lotion applied WITHOUT friction; liniment applied WITH friction.**
🔁 **Emollient vs Demulcent** → **emollient softens SKIN (external); demulcent soothes MUCOUS
membrane (internal).**
🔁 **Gargle vs Mouthwash** → **gargle for the THROAT (usually diluted before use);
mouthwash for the MOUTH.**
🔁 **Collyrium vs Eye drops** → **collyrium = eye LOTION for bathing, preservative-free, discard in
24 h; eye drops = instilled, preserved, discard in 4 weeks.**
🔁 **Lozenge vs Pastille** → **lozenge = hard sugar-acacia base; pastille = soft glycero-gelatin
base.**
🔁 **Evacuant vs Retention enema** → **evacuant is expelled (cleanses bowel); retention is held for
absorption/ local action.**
🔁 **Bulk powder vs Divided powder** → **potent drugs MUST be dispensed as divided powders.**

## 7.6 CHAPTER 7 — RAPID REVISION SHEET

* **Posology = the study of dose.** Dose ladder: **minimum → therapeutic → maximum → toxic →
  lethal.**
* **Loading dose** = large initial dose to reach the effective level fast;
  **maintenance dose** keeps it there.
* 🔢 **Therapeutic Index = LD₅₀ ÷ ED₅₀; higher TI = safer.**
  **Narrow-TI drugs: digoxin, lithium, warfarin, phenytoin, theophylline, aminoglycosides.**
* **Factors affecting dose:** age, weight/BSA, sex (⚠️ pregnancy & lactation), route (⭐ parenteral
  dose < oral dose), dosage form, time relative to food, **tolerance, tachyphylaxis, cumulation,
  idiosyncrasy, allergy, habituation**, liver & kidney disease, **drug interaction (synergism/
  antagonism, enzyme induction/ inhibition)**, genetics, environment.
* **Cumulation** = elimination slower than administration (**digitalis, emetine, heavy metals,
  bromides**); **tolerance** = need for a larger dose on repetition (**morphine, barbiturates,
  nitrates**).
* **Enzyme inducers:** phenobarbitone, phenytoin, **rifampicin**, carbamazepine, griseofulvin,
  smoking. **Inhibitors:** cimetidine, erythromycin, ketoconazole, metronidazole, INH,
  chloramphenicol.
* 🔢 **STRENGTHS:** Tincture **1 in 5 (simple) / 1 in 10 (potent)** · Liquid extract **1 in 1** ·
  Fresh infusion **1 in 10, keeps 12 h** · Concentrated infusion **8×, dilute 1 + 7** ·
  Simple syrup **66.7 % w/w sucrose** · Mucilage of acacia **35 % w/v** ·
  Chloroform water **0.25 % v/v** (double strength 0.5 %) · Aromatic water **saturated (~0.2 %)** ·
  Spirit **5–10 %**.
* **Draught = single 50 mL dose. Linctus = 5 mL, viscous, sipped undiluted.**
* **Tincture of Iodine (weak) = Iodine 2.5 % + KI 2.5 % in 90 % alcohol; Lugol's (strong) =
  Iodine 5 % + KI 10 % in water.**
* **Compound Tincture of Benzoin = Friar's Balsam; inhalation 1 mL in 500 mL hot water.**
* **Paregoric = camphorated tincture of opium, dose 2–8 mL** (weak);
  **Laudanum = tincture of opium, dose 0.3–2 mL** (strong).
* **Dover's powder = Ipecac 10 % + Opium 10 % + Lactose 80 %; dose 300–600 mg.**
* **Compound tragacanth powder = 15 : 20 : 20 : 45.**
* **Gregory's powder = compound powder of rhubarb.**
* **ORS = NaCl 2.6 g + trisodium citrate 2.9 g + KCl 1.5 g + glucose 13.5 g per litre.**
* **Key doses:** Magnesium sulphate **5–15 g** · Castor oil **5–20 mL** · Liquid paraffin
  **10–30 mL** · Isapghula **4–16 g** · Ammonium chloride **0.3–2 g** · Ferrous sulphate
  **200 mg t.d.s.** · Paracetamol **500 mg–1 g (max 4 g/day)** · Atropine **0.6 mg** ·
  Adrenaline **0.5 mL of 1 : 1000 IM** · Male fern **3–6 mL (max 8)** · Activated charcoal
  **50–100 g** · Digoxin maintenance **0.125–0.25 mg**.
* **Eye drops: sterile, isotonic, preserved, discard 4 weeks after opening.
  Eye lotion (collyrium): preservative-free, discard 24 h.**
* **LVP must be isotonic, pyrogen-free and contain NO preservative.**
* **Potent drugs are dispensed as DIVIDED powders, never as a bulk powder.**
