## 12.1 DEFINITION & CHARACTERISTICS

📌 **DEFINITION (learn word-for-word)**
> **A MIXTURE (Mistura) is a LIQUID PREPARATION FOR ORAL (internal) USE, in which one or more
> medicaments are DISSOLVED, SUSPENDED or DISPERSED in a suitable aqueous VEHICLE, and which is
> intended to be taken in DIVIDED DOSES.**

⭐ **KEY CHARACTERISTICS OF A MIXTURE**
* ⭐ **For INTERNAL (oral) use** → **plain bottle + WHITE label**.
* ⭐ Taken in ⭐ **DIVIDED DOSES** — usually **10 mL or 15 mL** (child 5 mL), from a bottle of
  **100, 150, 200 or 300 mL**.
* ⭐ The vehicle is **AQUEOUS** (water, chloroform water, aromatic waters, syrups).
* ⭐ **Extemporaneous**, freshly prepared, with a **SHORT SHELF-LIFE** (⭐ usually labelled to be
  used within **7–14 days**; discard after that).
* ⭐ **No preservative is added deliberately in most cases** — ⭐ the **chloroform of chloroform
  water (0.25 % v/v) acts as the preservative & mild flavour**.

⚠️ **TRAP — MIXTURE vs DRAUGHT**
* ⭐ **Mixture = taken in DIVIDED doses** (several doses in one bottle).
* ⭐ **Draught (Haustus) = a SINGLE dose, usually 50 mL.**

## 12.2 ⭐⭐ CLASSIFICATION / TYPES OF MIXTURES

```
                              MIXTURES
                                  │
   ┌───────────────┬──────────────┼───────────────┬────────────────┐
① TRUE SOLUTION  ② SUSPENSION   ③ EMULSION     ④ MIXTURES with  ⑤ EFFERVESCENT
   (all soluble)      │           (oil + water)   colloids/gums     MIXTURES
                      │                            (mucilages)
            ┌─────────┴─────────┐
     (a) DIFFUSIBLE      (b) INDIFFUSIBLE
        suspension          suspension
     (light, disperse    (heavy, settle fast →
      easily, settle      MUST have a suspending
      slowly — NO         agent)
      suspending
      agent needed)
```

### ⭐⭐ THE MOST IMPORTANT DISTINCTION OF THE CHAPTER
### DIFFUSIBLE vs INDIFFUSIBLE POWDERS

📌 **DIFFUSIBLE (dispersible) powders** — ⭐ **light, bulky, insoluble powders which, on shaking,
disperse readily & evenly throughout the vehicle and remain in suspension long enough for an
accurate dose to be poured; they therefore need NO suspending agent.**

📌 **INDIFFUSIBLE powders** — ⭐ **heavy, dense, insoluble powders which do NOT disperse readily,
settle rapidly to the bottom (and often CAKE), and therefore MUST be dispensed with a SUSPENDING
AGENT** so that a uniform dose can be poured.

| ⭐ **DIFFUSIBLE powders (no suspending agent)** | ⭐ **INDIFFUSIBLE powders (suspending agent NEEDED)** |
|---|---|
| **Light & Heavy MAGNESIUM CARBONATE** | ⭐ **Aspirin (acetylsalicylic acid)** |
| **Light & Heavy MAGNESIUM OXIDE** | ⭐ **Phenobarbitone / Phenacetin** |
| ⭐ **MAGNESIUM TRISILICATE** | ⭐ **SULPHUR (sublimed/ precipitated)** |
| **CHALK (Prepared/ Precipitated calcium carbonate)** | ⭐ **CALAMINE** |
| ⭐ **LIGHT KAOLIN** *(heavy kaolin is indiffusible)* | ⭐ **ZINC OXIDE** |
| **BISMUTH CARBONATE / Bismuth subnitrate** | ⭐ **HEAVY KAOLIN** |
| **Bismuth subgallate** | **Salol (phenyl salicylate) / Sulphonamides** |
| **Precipitated calcium phosphate** | **Resins & resinous matter (from tinctures)** |
| **Cinchona/ Rhubarb powder (light vegetable powders)** | **Iodoform, Bismuth subcarbonate (some texts)** |
| **Activated charcoal (light)** | **Barium sulphate** |

🧠 **MEMORY HOOK**
> ⭐ **DIFFUSIBLE = "the MAGNESIUMS + CHALK + LIGHT KAOLIN + BISMUTHS."**
> ⭐ **INDIFFUSIBLE = "ASPIRIN, SULPHUR, CALAMINE, ZINC OXIDE, PHENOBARBITONE, HEAVY KAOLIN."**
> 🧠 *"**A-S-C-Z-P-H** needs help to stay up."*

⚠️ **TRAP — LIGHT kaolin is DIFFUSIBLE, but HEAVY kaolin is INDIFFUSIBLE.**
⚠️ **TRAP — Magnesium trisilicate is DIFFUSIBLE (light & bulky)** — yet the official **Magnesium
Trisilicate Mixture** does contain a suspending agent (**compound tragacanth powder**) for elegance
and to keep the peppermint oil dispersed; be guided by the official formula.

## 12.3 ⭐⭐ SUSPENDING AGENTS

📌 **DEFINITION** — A **suspending agent** is a substance added to a liquid preparation to
⭐ **increase the VISCOSITY of the vehicle**, thereby **slowing the rate of sedimentation** of the
dispersed particles (Stokes' law) and keeping the suspension uniform long enough for an accurate
dose to be measured.

🔢 **STOKES' LAW — the theory behind suspending agents**
```
        d² (ρs − ρl) g
v  =  ──────────────────
             18 η
```
⭐ Sedimentation velocity **v** is **decreased** by: ↓ **particle diameter (d)**,
↓ **density difference (ρs − ρl)**, and ⭐ ↑ **VISCOSITY (η) of the vehicle** — the last is what a
suspending agent does.

### 📋 THE SUSPENDING AGENTS ⭐

| Class | Examples | 🔢 Usual concentration |
|---|---|---|
| ⭐ **Natural gums & mucilages** | ⭐ **TRAGACANTH (the classic best), ACACIA, Compound Tragacanth Powder, Sterculia (karaya), Guar gum, Agar, Sodium alginate, Pectin, Gelatin, Starch, Isapghula** | ⭐ **Tragacanth mucilage 1.25 % · Compound tragacanth powder 2 % · Acacia mucilage 35 % (as an emulgent)** |
| ⭐ **Semi-synthetic cellulose derivatives** | ⭐ **Methylcellulose, Sodium carboxymethylcellulose (Na-CMC), Hydroxyethyl/ Hydroxypropyl methylcellulose (HPMC), Microcrystalline cellulose** | 0.5–2 % |
| ⭐ **Synthetic polymers** | **Carbomer (Carbopol), Polyvinylpyrrolidone (PVP), Polyvinyl alcohol, Poloxamer** | 0.1–1 % |
| ⭐ **Clays (inorganic)** | ⭐ **BENTONITE, Magnesium aluminium silicate (Veegum), Attapulgite, Colloidal silicon dioxide (Aerosil)** | 1–5 % |
| **Sugars & polyols** | **Syrup, sorbitol, glycerin** (increase viscosity & palatability) | — |
| **Surfactants (wetting agents)** | **Polysorbates (Tween), Sodium lauryl sulphate, Sorbitan esters** | 0.05–0.5 % |
| **Flocculating agents** | **Electrolytes (NaCl, KCl), surfactants, polymers** | Small |

⭐ **THE OFFICIAL CHOICE IN DISPENSING: ⭐ COMPOUND TRAGACANTH POWDER, 2 g per 100 mL (2 % w/v).**
🔢 **Compound Powder of Tragacanth = Tragacanth 15 g + Acacia 20 g + Starch 20 g + Sucrose 45 g.**

⭐ **IDEAL PROPERTIES OF A SUSPENDING AGENT**
1. Should produce a suspension that ⭐ **settles slowly and re-disperses easily on shaking**.
2. Should be **chemically inert & compatible** with the medicaments.
3. Should be **non-toxic, tasteless & odourless**.
4. Should give a ⭐ **pourable (not too viscous) product** and be **thixotropic** (thick at rest,
   flows on shaking).
5. Should be **stable** over the shelf-life and over a range of pH & temperature.
6. Should be **cheap & readily available**.

⭐ **FLOCCULATED vs DEFLOCCULATED SUSPENSIONS** (frequently examined)

| Feature | ⭐ **FLOCCULATED** | ⭐ **DEFLOCCULATED** |
|---|---|---|
| Particles | Form **loose aggregates (flocs)** | Exist as **separate individual particles** |
| Rate of settling | ⭐ **RAPID** | ⭐ **SLOW** |
| Sediment | ⭐ **Loose, fluffy, LARGE volume — EASILY RE-DISPERSED** | ⭐ **Hard, dense, closely packed CAKE — DIFFICULT/ impossible to re-disperse** |
| Supernatant | **Clear** | **Cloudy/ turbid for a long time** |
| ⭐ **CAKING** | ⭐ **Does NOT cake** | ⭐ **CAKES — the major fault of a suspension** |
| Appearance | Less elegant | More elegant |
| ⭐ Which is preferred? | ⭐ **A CONTROLLED FLOCCULATION (with a suspending agent to slow the settling) is the IDEAL** | — |

🧠 **MEMORY HOOK** — "**FLOC = FLUFFY sediment, shakes up easily (good). DEFLOC = DENSE cake, will
not shake up (bad).**"

📌 **Sedimentation volume (F)** = ⭐ **F = Vu ÷ Vo** (volume of the final sediment ÷ original volume of
the suspension). ⭐ **The nearer F is to 1 (or > 1), the better the suspension.**
📌 **Degree of flocculation (β)** = F (flocculated) ÷ F (deflocculated).

## 12.4 ⭐⭐ THE GENERAL RULES FOR THE PREPARATION OF MIXTURES
### ⭐ (this is the exact syllabus question — learn the numbered list)

### ⭐ THE ORDER OF ADDITION — THE "GOLDEN RULE"
> ⭐ **Ingredients are added to the vehicle in the ORDER OF THEIR SOLUBILITY & NATURE:
> SOLIDS FIRST (soluble solids dissolved in the vehicle), then LIQUIDS, then the SUSPENDING AGENT
> & INSOLUBLE POWDERS, then the tinctures & resinous/ alcoholic liquids (SLOWLY, LAST), then the
> vehicle to volume.**

### ⭐⭐ THE STANDARD SEQUENCE (memorise this list — it answers the question directly)

| # | ⭐ Step / Rule | ⭐ Reason |
|---|---|---|
| **1** | ⭐ **Do the CALCULATIONS first** for the total quantity, and check the **dose & maximum dose** and for **incompatibilities** | Accuracy & safety |
| **2** | ⭐ **Select a clean, dry, graduated bottle of the correct size** — the finished mixture should nearly fill it | Elegance; allows shaking |
| **3** | ⭐ **Dissolve the SOLUBLE SOLIDS** first, in a **portion (about half to two-thirds) of the vehicle**, in a mortar or beaker — ⭐ the **most soluble/ largest quantity first**, and each solid **separately** if they may react | Complete dissolution; prevents interaction |
| **4** | ⭐ **Powder & triturate any solid** that dissolves slowly, and use **gentle warming** if permitted (⚠️ **never heat volatile or thermolabile substances**) | Speeds dissolution |
| **5** | ⭐ **Add the LIQUID ingredients** next — **syrups, glycerin, liquid extracts, aromatic waters** — with stirring | They are miscible with water |
| **6** | ⭐ For a SUSPENSION: **triturate the INSOLUBLE (indiffusible) POWDER with the SUSPENDING AGENT** (compound tragacanth powder) in a **DRY mortar first**, then add a **small amount of the vehicle to make a SMOOTH PASTE (levigation)**, and then **dilute gradually with more vehicle**, transferring to the bottle with rinsings | ⚠️ ⭐ **If water is added to a gum all at once, the gum forms LUMPS/ balls that will not disperse** — dry mixing then gradual addition prevents lumping |
| **7** | ⭐ **DIFFUSIBLE powders** are simply **triturated with a little vehicle** and added — ⭐ **no suspending agent** | They disperse readily |
| **8** | ⭐ **Add TINCTURES & other ALCOHOLIC/ RESINOUS liquids LAST but ONE — SLOWLY, in a fine stream, with CONSTANT STIRRING, into the BULK of the aqueous vehicle** (⚠️ **never the reverse — never add water to the tincture**) | ⭐ Prevents the **resin from precipitating in large sticky lumps**; gives a **fine, evenly dispersed precipitate** instead |
| **9** | ⭐ Where a **potent tincture/ liquid** is prescribed in a small volume, measure it with a **calibrated dropper/ pipette** or by the **aliquot method** | Accuracy |
| **10** | ⭐ **Add VOLATILE substances & flavours near the END, at room temperature** | Prevents loss by evaporation |
| **11** | ⭐ **MAKE UP TO THE FINAL VOLUME with the vehicle** ("ad 100 mL"), **using the vehicle to RINSE the mortar/ measure and adding the rinsings** | ⚠️ Ensures **no drug is lost** on the apparatus & the volume/ dose is correct |
| **12** | ⭐ **SHAKE/ mix well**, then **cork/ cap the bottle** | Uniformity |
| **13** | ⭐ **LABEL** — patient's name, date, name of the mixture, **DOSE & directions**, and the auxiliary labels: ⭐ **"SHAKE THE BOTTLE WELL BEFORE USE"** (for **all suspensions & emulsions**), *"Store in a cool place"*, *"Keep out of the reach of children"*, expiry | Legal & safety requirement |
| **14** | ⭐ **Final check** against the prescription, then dispense with **counselling** | Good dispensing practice |

⚠️ ⭐ **THE FIVE "NEVERS" OF MIXTURE PREPARATION**
1. ⚠️ **Never add water to a gum/ mucilage all at once** → lumps.
2. ⚠️ **Never add the aqueous vehicle TO a tincture** → the resin precipitates in a sticky lump.
   **Always add the tincture slowly to the vehicle.**
3. ⚠️ **Never heat volatile, thermolabile or alcoholic ingredients.**
4. ⚠️ **Never make up to volume without rinsing the mortar & measure into the bottle.**
5. ⚠️ **Never omit "Shake well" from a suspension or emulsion label.**

### ⭐ THE VEHICLES USED IN MIXTURES

| Vehicle | ⭐ Note |
|---|---|
| ⭐ **CHLOROFORM WATER (single strength = 0.25 % v/v chloroform)** | ⭐ **The commonest vehicle** — chloroform acts as a **PRESERVATIVE (antimicrobial), a mild flavour/ sweetener and a carminative**. Prepared by diluting **Double-Strength Chloroform Water (0.5 %) 1 : 1 with water** |
| **Purified/ Potable water** | Simple vehicle |
| ⭐ **Aromatic waters** — **Peppermint, Anise, Dill, Fennel, Cinnamon, Camphor water** | **Carminative & flavouring** vehicles |
| ⭐ **Syrup (66.7 % w/w sucrose)** | **Sweetening & viscosity-increasing** vehicle; self-preserving |
| **Glycerin, Sorbitol solution, Propylene glycol** | Sweet, viscous, cosolvent, preservative |
| **Alcohol (as part of tinctures)** | Cosolvent & preservative |
| **Mucilages (tragacanth/ acacia)** | Suspending vehicles |

## 12.5 ⭐ WORKED EXAMPLES — THE FOUR CLASSES OF MIXTURE

### ⭐ (A) A TRUE-SOLUTION MIXTURE
```
℞  Ammonium chloride            6 g
   Sodium citrate               3 g
   Liquorice liquid extract     8 mL
   Chloroform water        ad 100 mL
   M. ft. mist.  Sig.: 10 mL t.d.s. p.c.
```
⭐ **Method:** Dissolve the **ammonium chloride** and then the **sodium citrate** in about **60 mL of
chloroform water** in a beaker/ mortar (stir; both are freely soluble) → add the **liquorice liquid
extract**, stirring → transfer to a 100 mL bottle, **rinsing** the beaker with chloroform water →
**make up to 100 mL** → shake.
⭐ **Labels:** white label; ⚠️ **"Shake well" is NOT required** (it is a clear solution).
⭐ **Analysis:** Basis = ammonium chloride; Adjuvant = sodium citrate; Corrective = liquorice
(masks the salty taste); Vehicle = chloroform water. **Number of doses = 100/10 = 10.**

### ⭐ (B) A DIFFUSIBLE-SUSPENSION MIXTURE (no suspending agent)
```
℞  Light magnesium carbonate    5 g
   Sodium bicarbonate           4 g
   Peppermint water        ad 100 mL
   M. ft. mist.  Sig.: 10 mL t.d.s.
```
⭐ **Method:** Dissolve the **sodium bicarbonate** (soluble) in part of the peppermint water →
in a mortar, **triturate the light magnesium carbonate with a small amount of the vehicle to a
smooth cream**, then dilute gradually → combine, transfer with rinsings, **make up to volume**.
⭐ **No suspending agent** — magnesium carbonate is **diffusible**.
⭐ **Label: "SHAKE THE BOTTLE WELL BEFORE USE."**

### ⭐ (C) AN INDIFFUSIBLE-SUSPENSION MIXTURE (suspending agent REQUIRED)
```
℞  Sulphur, precipitated        3 g
   Compound tragacanth powder   2 g
   Chloroform water        ad 100 mL
   M. ft. mist.  Sig.: 10 mL b.d.
```
⭐ **Method:**
1. ⭐ **Mix the precipitated sulphur and the compound tragacanth powder together DRY** in a mortar
   (⭐ this is the crucial step — dry mixing prevents the gum from lumping).
2. Add a **small quantity of chloroform water** and triturate to a **smooth, lump-free paste
   (levigate)**.
3. **Gradually dilute** with more vehicle, triturating continuously.
4. Transfer to the bottle, **rinse the mortar** with the vehicle, add the rinsings, and
   **make up to 100 mL**. Shake well.
⭐ **Label: "SHAKE THE BOTTLE WELL BEFORE USE."**
⭐ **Why sulphur needs the suspending agent:** it is **heavy, hydrophobic (poorly wetted) &
indiffusible** — without it, the sulphur would **float or cake** and the doses would be grossly
uneven.

### ⭐ (D) A MIXTURE CONTAINING A TINCTURE (resinous precipitation)
```
℞  Compound tincture of benzoin  5 mL
   Syrup                        10 mL
   Chloroform water         ad 100 mL
```
⭐ **Method:** Place the **syrup and most of the chloroform water in the bottle/ beaker**, then
⭐ **add the compound tincture of benzoin SLOWLY, in a thin stream, with CONSTANT STIRRING/ shaking**
→ a **fine, milky, evenly dispersed** precipitate of resin forms → make up to volume.
⚠️ **If the water were added to the tincture, the resin would separate as sticky lumps that adhere
to the bottle.** Add a **suspending agent** if the precipitate settles too fast.
⭐ **Label: "Shake well before use."**

### ⭐ (E) AN EMULSION MIXTURE
```
℞  Liquid paraffin              30 mL
   Acacia (powdered)            q.s.
   Water                   ad 100 mL
```
⭐ **Method (dry gum/ Continental method):** liquid paraffin is a **MINERAL oil** →
⭐ **Oil : Water : Gum = 3 : 2 : 1** → for 30 mL of oil, use **10 g of acacia and 20 mL of water**.
Triturate the **acacia with the oil** in a **dry mortar**, add the **20 mL of water ALL AT ONCE**,
and triturate **rapidly & continuously in one direction** until a ⭐ **"crackling"/ clicking sound**
is heard and a **thick white PRIMARY EMULSION** is formed; then **dilute gradually** with water to
100 mL.
⭐ **Label: "Shake well before use."**

### ⭐ (F) AN EFFERVESCENT MIXTURE
📌 The **acid** and the **carbonate/ bicarbonate** are dispensed **SEPARATELY (in two bottles/
powders)** and **mixed by the patient immediately before drinking**, while effervescing.
⚠️ ⭐ **Never stopper an effervescing mixture tightly** — CO₂ pressure may **burst the bottle**.
⭐ Label: *"Mix the two solutions and drink while effervescing"*, *"Do not stopper tightly"*,
*"Keep in a cool place"*.

## 12.7 ⚠️ FAULTS IN MIXTURES & THEIR PREVENTION

| Fault | Cause | ⭐ Prevention |
|---|---|---|
| ⭐ **Caking of the sediment** | Deflocculated, indiffusible powder without a suspending agent | ⭐ **Add a suspending agent; aim for CONTROLLED FLOCCULATION** |
| ⭐ **Lumps of undispersed gum** | Water added to the gum all at once | ⭐ **Mix the gum DRY with the powder first, then add the vehicle GRADUALLY** |
| ⭐ **Sticky resinous lumps** | Vehicle added to the tincture, or the tincture added too fast | ⭐ **Add the tincture SLOWLY to the BULK of the vehicle with constant stirring** |
| **Gritty/ coarse suspension** | Powder not finely enough divided | **Reduce to a fine powder & levigate** |
| **Under-dose/ short volume** | Mortar & measure not rinsed into the bottle | ⭐ **Always rinse & add the rinsings; make up to volume** |
| **Uneven dosing** | Rapid settling; patient does not shake | ⭐ **Suspending agent + "SHAKE WELL" label + counselling** |
| **Cracked/ separated emulsion** | Wrong oil : water : gum ratio; alcohol/ electrolyte added; heat | ⭐ **Correct ratio (4:2:1 / 3:2:1 / 2:2:1 / 1:2:1); add alcohol last, diluted; avoid heat** |
| **Microbial spoilage/ mould** | Aqueous vehicle, no preservative, long storage | ⭐ **Chloroform water/ preservative; short expiry (7–14 days); cool storage** |
| **Bottle bursting** | Effervescence in a tightly stoppered bottle | ⭐ **Do not stopper tightly; dispense the acid & carbonate separately** |
| **Colour/ odour change, precipitate on standing** | Chemical incompatibility (Ch. 8) | ⭐ **Detect before compounding; correct or refer to the prescriber** |
| **Crystal growth on storage** | Temperature cycling of a nearly saturated solution | Avoid saturation; store at a steady cool temperature |

## 12.8 CHAPTER 12 — RAPID REVISION SHEET

* ⭐ **MIXTURE = liquid ORAL preparation, drug dissolved/ suspended/ dispersed in an AQUEOUS vehicle,
  taken in DIVIDED DOSES** (10–15 mL); **plain bottle + WHITE label**;
  shelf-life **7–14 days**.
* ⭐ **DRAUGHT = a SINGLE 50 mL dose.**
* ⭐ **Types: true solution · diffusible suspension · indiffusible suspension · emulsion ·
  effervescent · colloidal/mucilage.**
* ⭐ **DIFFUSIBLE (no suspending agent): light & heavy MAGNESIUM CARBONATE, MAGNESIUM OXIDE,
  MAGNESIUM TRISILICATE, CHALK, LIGHT KAOLIN, BISMUTH carbonate/ subnitrate/ subgallate.**
* ⭐ **INDIFFUSIBLE (suspending agent NEEDED): ASPIRIN, PHENOBARBITONE, SULPHUR, CALAMINE,
  ZINC OXIDE, HEAVY KAOLIN, salol, sulphonamides, resins.**
* ⚠️ ⭐ **LIGHT kaolin = diffusible; HEAVY kaolin = indiffusible.**
* ⭐ **The official suspending agent in dispensing = COMPOUND TRAGACANTH POWDER, 2 g per 100 mL
  (2 %)** = Tragacanth 15 + Acacia 20 + Starch 20 + Sucrose 45.
* ⭐ **A suspending agent works by INCREASING the VISCOSITY of the vehicle (Stokes' law).**
* ⭐ **FLOCCULATED = fluffy, large-volume sediment, settles fast, re-disperses easily, NO caking
  (desirable). DEFLOCCULATED = slow settling but a HARD CAKE that will not re-disperse
  (undesirable).** **Sedimentation volume F = Vu/Vo; the closer to 1, the better.**
* ⭐⭐ **THE GENERAL RULES / ORDER OF PREPARATION:**
  **(1) calculate & check the dose · (2) dissolve SOLUBLE SOLIDS in part of the vehicle ·
  (3) add the miscible LIQUIDS (syrups, glycerin, liquid extracts) ·
  (4) mix the INDIFFUSIBLE POWDER with the SUSPENDING AGENT DRY, levigate to a smooth paste, then
  dilute GRADUALLY · (5) add TINCTURES & resinous/ alcoholic liquids SLOWLY, LAST, into the BULK of
  the vehicle with constant stirring · (6) add volatiles & flavours near the end ·
  (7) MAKE UP TO VOLUME, rinsing the mortar & measure into the bottle · (8) shake, label
  ("SHAKE WELL"), final check.**
* ⚠️ ⭐ **NEVER add water to a gum all at once (lumps); NEVER add the vehicle to a tincture
  (sticky resin lumps); NEVER heat volatiles; NEVER forget the rinsings; NEVER omit "Shake well"
  from a suspension/ emulsion.**
* ⭐ **CHLOROFORM WATER (0.25 % v/v) is the commonest vehicle — it is the PRESERVATIVE and a mild
  flavour/ carminative.** Double-strength = 0.5 %, diluted 1 : 1.
* ⭐ **Emulsion ratios (oil : water : gum): fixed oil 4 : 2 : 1 · mineral oil 3 : 2 : 1 ·
  volatile oil 2 : 2 : 1 · oleoresin 1 : 2 : 1.** ⭐ The **primary emulsion is complete when a
  "crackling"/ clicking sound** is heard (dry gum/ Continental method).
* ⭐ **Effervescent mixtures: dispense the acid & the carbonate SEPARATELY; "mix and drink while
  effervescing"; DO NOT STOPPER TIGHTLY.**
* ⭐ **Chalk Mixture = the classic DIFFUSIBLE suspension; Magnesium Trisilicate Mixture & Kaolin
  Mixture use compound tragacanth powder; Liquid Paraffin Emulsion = the classic emulsion mixture.**
* ⭐ **"Shake well before use" goes on EVERY suspension & emulsion — but NOT on a true solution.**
