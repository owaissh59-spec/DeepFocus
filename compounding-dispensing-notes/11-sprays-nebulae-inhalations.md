## 11.2 ⭐⭐ SPRAYS (NEBULAE)

### 📌 Definition (repeat)
> **A nebula (spray) is a liquid preparation intended to be applied to the nose, throat or
> respiratory tract as a fine spray, by means of an atomiser or nebuliser.**

### ⭐ TYPES OF SPRAY (by vehicle)

| Type | Vehicle | ⭐ Notes |
|---|---|---|
| ⭐ **Aqueous sprays** | Water (isotonic, buffered) | ⭐ **The PREFERRED type** — physiological, do not interfere with ciliary movement |
| ⭐ **Oily sprays** | **Liquid paraffin, arachis oil** | ⚠️ ⭐ **DANGEROUS — droplets may reach the lungs & cause LIPOID (oil) PNEUMONIA, and they PARALYSE the CILIA of the nasal epithelium** → ⭐ **oily nasal sprays/ drops are now AVOIDED, especially in infants & the elderly** |
| **Alcoholic/ hydro-alcoholic sprays** | Alcohol, propylene glycol, glycerin | May be irritant/ drying |
| **Suspension sprays** | Aqueous with a suspending agent | Shake well |
| ⭐ **Pressurised (aerosol) sprays** | Propellant | Metered dose |

### ⭐ VEHICLE REQUIREMENTS FOR A NASAL PREPARATION (very examinable)
1. ⭐ **Isotonic** with nasal secretions — ⭐ **equivalent to 0.9 % w/v sodium chloride**
   (⚠️ hypertonic or hypotonic solutions damage the mucosa & impair ciliary action).
2. ⭐ **pH close to that of nasal fluid — about 5.5 to 6.5 (acceptable range 5.5–7.5)**;
   buffered where necessary. ⚠️ **Ciliary movement is inhibited below pH 5 and above pH 8.**
3. ⭐ **Should NOT inhibit ciliary movement** (⚠️ oils, high concentrations of preservatives,
   surfactants, cocaine & vasoconstrictors in excess all impair the cilia).
4. **Non-irritant, non-sensitising, of low viscosity** so it can be atomised.
5. **Should contain a PRESERVATIVE** (chlorbutol 0.5 %, benzalkonium chloride 0.01 %,
   phenylmercuric acetate/ nitrate 0.002 %, chlorhexidine).
6. **Should be free from suspended particles** (or well suspended & shaken).
7. **Compatible with the container & the atomiser**.

### ⭐ THE ATOMISER / NEBULISER — how a spray is produced

📌 An **atomiser** breaks a liquid into fine droplets by means of a **jet of air (the Venturi/
Bernoulli principle)** — air forced through a narrow orifice creates a **negative pressure** which
draws the liquid up a **dip tube**, and the air stream then **shatters** the liquid into a mist.

| Device | Principle |
|---|---|
| ⭐ **Squeeze-bulb atomiser (insufflator/ nasal spray)** | Hand-squeezed bulb forces air across the liquid → **Venturi effect** |
| ⭐ **Jet (air-jet/ pneumatic) nebuliser** | Compressed air/ oxygen breaks the liquid into an aerosol; **baffles** remove the coarse droplets |
| ⭐ **Ultrasonic nebuliser** | **High-frequency piezoelectric vibration** creates the mist; **no gas needed**, gives a dense fine mist |
| ⭐ **Mesh nebuliser** | Liquid forced through a vibrating fine mesh |
| ⭐ **Pump/ metered spray (mechanical)** | Finger-pump gives a **metered dose**, no propellant |
| ⭐ **Pressurised MDI** | Liquefied propellant expels a metered dose |
| **Spinhaler/ Rotahaler/ Diskhaler/ Turbohaler (DPI)** | Breath-actuated **dry powder** |

🔢 ⭐⭐ **PARTICLE SIZE & DEPOSITION — the most important numbers of this chapter**

| 🔢 Droplet/ particle size | ⭐ Where it deposits |
|---|---|
| **> 20 µm (and > 10 µm)** | ⭐ Impact in the **nose, mouth & throat (oropharynx)** — do not reach the lungs |
| **10 – 5 µm** | **Trachea & large bronchi** |
| ⭐ **5 – 1 µm** | ⭐ **THE OPTIMUM RANGE for deposition in the BRONCHIOLES & ALVEOLI** (the "respirable fraction"; often quoted as **1–5 µm**, best **2–5 µm** or **0.5–5 µm**) |
| **< 1 µm (0.5 µm)** | ⭐ Too fine — remain suspended and are **EXHALED again** without depositing |

🧠 **MEMORY HOOK — "1 to 5 for the lung; over 10 stays in the throat; under 1 is breathed back
out."**
⭐ **Mechanisms of deposition: inertial IMPACTION (large particles), SEDIMENTATION/ gravitational
settling (medium), and DIFFUSION/ Brownian motion (very small).**

### ⭐ TYPICAL SPRAY / NEBULA FORMULATIONS

| Spray | Composition | Use |
|---|---|---|
| **Ephedrine Nasal Drops/ Spray** | Ephedrine hydrochloride **0.5 % or 1 %** in sodium chloride solution (isotonic) with chlorbutol | ⭐ **Nasal decongestant (vasoconstrictor)** ⚠️ rebound congestion (rhinitis medicamentosa) on prolonged use |
| **Xylometazoline/ Oxymetazoline Nasal Spray** | 0.05 % (child) / 0.1 % (adult) | Decongestant |
| **Salbutamol Inhalation/ Nebuliser Solution** | 100 µg per metered dose (MDI); nebuliser 2.5 / 5 mg | ⭐ **Bronchodilator — asthma** |
| **Beclomethasone/ Budesonide MDI** | 50–250 µg per dose | ⭐ **Inhaled corticosteroid (prophylaxis of asthma)**; ⚠️ label **"rinse the mouth after use"** (oral candidiasis) |
| **Ipratropium bromide Inhaler** | 20–40 µg | Anticholinergic bronchodilator |
| **Sodium cromoglycate** | 20 mg (Spinhaler) | Mast-cell stabiliser (prophylaxis) |
| **Adrenaline (Epinephrine) Spray** | 1 : 1000 | Local vasoconstrictor/ haemostatic |
| **Lignocaine (Lidocaine) Topical Spray** | **4 % / 10 %** | ⭐ **Surface anaesthesia of the throat & larynx** before endoscopy |
| **Povidone-iodine/ Chlorhexidine Throat Spray** | 0.5–1 % | Antiseptic |
| **Benzocaine/ Menthol Throat Spray** | — | Local anaesthetic, soothing |
| **Saline nasal spray** | 0.65–0.9 % NaCl | Moisturising, decongestant |
| **Beclometasone + Formoterol / Salmeterol + Fluticasone** | Combination MDI | Asthma/ COPD |

### ⭐ AEROSOLS — the pressurised sprays

📌 **PROPELLANTS**

| Class | Examples | ⭐ Note |
|---|---|---|
| ⭐ **Liquefied gases — chlorofluorocarbons (CFCs)** | **Freon 11 (trichlorofluoromethane), Freon 12 (dichlorodifluoromethane), Freon 114** | ⚠️ ⭐ **BANNED/ phased out** under the **Montreal Protocol** because they **deplete the OZONE layer** |
| ⭐ **Liquefied gases — hydrofluoroalkanes (HFA)** | ⭐ **HFA-134a (norflurane), HFA-227** | ⭐ **The modern replacement for CFCs** |
| **Liquefied hydrocarbons** | **Propane, butane, isobutane** | Cheap, ⚠️ **inflammable** |
| **Compressed gases** | **Nitrogen, carbon dioxide, nitrous oxide** | ⚠️ Pressure **falls** as the container empties; used for foams/ sprays |

⭐ **COMPONENTS OF AN AEROSOL:** **container (aluminium/ tinplate/ glass)**, ⭐ **valve (metering
valve for inhalers)**, **actuator (mouthpiece/ nozzle)**, **dip tube**, **propellant**, and the
**product concentrate** (drug + cosolvent + surfactant/ suspending agent).
⭐ **Types of system:** **two-phase (solution) system** (drug + propellant vapour) and
**three-phase (suspension/ emulsion) system**; **foam aerosols**; **barrier/ bag-in-can systems**.
⭐ **Advantages:** metered accurate dose, protection from air & moisture, sterility maintained, rapid
onset, no contamination of the contents, convenient.
⚠️ **Disadvantages:** expensive, ⚠️ **pressurised → explosion/ fire risk if heated or punctured**,
**poor patient technique** (⭐ hence the **SPACER/ large-volume holding chamber** is used, especially
for children & the elderly), cold "freon effect", oropharyngeal deposition.
⭐ **Labels on an aerosol:** *"Shake well before use"*, *"Do not puncture or incinerate"*,
*"Store below 30 °C"*, *"Keep away from fire & heat"*, *"Rinse the mouth after use"* (steroids).

⭐ **Labels on a nasal/ throat spray:** *"For nasal use only"* / *"For use in the throat"*,
⚠️ *"NOT TO BE SWALLOWED"* (throat sprays), *"Shake well"* (suspensions),
*"Do not use for more than 5–7 days"* (decongestants — rebound congestion),
*"Discard 4 weeks after opening"*.

## 11.3 ⭐⭐ INHALATIONS (VAPOURS)

### 📌 Definition (repeat)
> **An inhalation is a liquid preparation containing one or more volatile substances which are
> vaporised — usually by adding the preparation to hot water — and the vapour is inhaled to give
> relief in congestion & inflammation of the respiratory passages.**

### ⭐ HOW AN INHALATION WORKS
* The **volatile substance** (a volatile oil, menthol, camphor, thymol, eucalyptol, benzoin) is
  carried in the **steam** to the mucous membranes of the nose, throat & bronchi.
* ⭐ The **STEAM (moist heat)** itself does most of the work — it **warms & moistens the mucosa,
  liquefies tenacious mucus, promotes drainage, soothes irritated mucosa and relieves congestion**;
  the volatile drug adds a **mild antiseptic, decongestant & counter-irritant** effect and a
  subjective feeling of easier breathing.

### ⭐⭐ THE CLASSICAL METHOD OF USE — *the standard label*
> ⭐ **"Add ONE TEASPOONFUL (about 5 mL — for Compound Benzoin Tincture, 1 mL) of the inhalation to
> a PINT (about 500 mL) of water at about 65 °C (NOT boiling) in a suitable vessel, and inhale the
> vapour for 5–10 minutes, with a towel over the head."**

⚠️ ⭐ **WHY NOT BOILING WATER?**
1. ⚠️ **Risk of SCALDING** the patient's face & respiratory mucosa (this is the main reason).
2. ⚠️ **Too-rapid, wasteful volatilisation** of the drug — the volatile substance is lost all at
   once instead of being released steadily.
3. ⚠️ In the case of **Compound Tincture of Benzoin, boiling water makes the resin coagulate into a
   sticky mass** that clings to the vessel.
🔢 **The recommended temperature is about 65 °C** ("hot, but not boiling").

### ⭐ FORMULATION POINTS
* ⭐ Many inhalations are ⭐ **"light magnesium carbonate" preparations** — the **volatile oil is
  triturated with LIGHT MAGNESIUM CARBONATE**, which acts as a ⭐ **DISPERSING/ DISTRIBUTING AGENT**,
  so that when the inhalation is added to hot water the oil is **finely & evenly dispersed** instead
  of floating in a single layer on the surface. *(Talc is used similarly for aromatic waters.)*
* Volatile ingredients must be added **last** and the container kept **tightly closed**.
* ⭐ Dispensed in a **well-closed, amber bottle**; **RED label — "NOT TO BE TAKEN"/ "For inhalation
  only"**.

### ⭐⭐ OFFICIAL / CLASSICAL INHALATIONS — THE FORMULA TABLE

| Inhalation | 🔢 Composition | ⭐ Dose / use |
|---|---|---|
| ⭐ **Compound Tincture of Benzoin (Friar's Balsam)** *— used as an inhalation* | ⭐ **Benzoin 10 g, Prepared storax 7.5 g, Tolu balsam 2.5 g, Aloes 2.5 g, Alcohol (90 %) to 100 mL** | ⭐ **1 mL (up to 5 mL) added to 500 mL (1 pint) of hot water; inhale the vapour** — laryngitis, bronchitis, catarrh |
| ⭐ **Menthol & Benzoin Inhalation BPC** | ⭐ **Menthol 2 g, Compound tincture of benzoin (to) 100 mL** | 5 mL to 500 mL hot water |
| ⭐ **Menthol Inhalation BPC** | ⭐ **Menthol 2 g, Light magnesium carbonate 1 g, Water to 100 mL** | 5 mL to 500 mL hot water — nasal congestion |
| ⭐ **Menthol & Eucalyptus Inhalation BP** | ⭐ **Menthol 2 g, Eucalyptus oil 10 mL, Light magnesium carbonate 7 g, Water to 100 mL** | ⭐ **5 mL (1 teaspoonful) to 500 mL of hot water** — the classic decongestant inhalation |
| ⭐ **Eucalyptus Inhalation / Oil** | Eucalyptus oil 10 %, light magnesium carbonate, water | Decongestant, antiseptic |
| ⭐ **Creosote Inhalation** | Creosote 10 mL, light magnesium carbonate 7 g, water to 100 mL | Antiseptic expectorant (obsolete) |
| **Pine Oil/ Terebene Inhalation** | Volatile oil + light magnesium carbonate + water | Decongestant |

| ⭐ **Amyl Nitrite Inhalation** | Amyl nitrite **0.2 mL in a crushable glass "vitrellae"/ ampoule** | ⭐ **Crushed & inhaled — vasodilator (angina); antidote in CYANIDE poisoning** |
| **Ammonia (Aromatic Spirit of Ammonia) Inhalation** | — | ⭐ **Inhaled as a reflex respiratory stimulant in FAINTING** ("smelling salts") |
| **Chloroform & Morphine Inhalation** | — | Antitussive (obsolete) |
| ⭐ **Oxygen inhalation / Nitrous oxide** | Medicinal gases | Hypoxia / anaesthesia & analgesia (**Entonox = 50 % N₂O + 50 % O₂**) |
| **Anaesthetic inhalations** | Ether, halothane, isoflurane, sevoflurane | General anaesthesia |

### ⭐ MODERN INHALATION DEVICES (worth a mention)

| Device | Principle | ⭐ Note |
|---|---|---|
| ⭐ **pMDI (pressurised metered-dose inhaler)** | Propellant-driven metered dose | Needs **coordination**; use a **SPACER** |
| ⭐ **DPI (dry-powder inhaler)** — Rotahaler, Spinhaler, Turbohaler, Diskhaler, Accuhaler | **Breath-actuated dry powder**, drug blended with **lactose carrier** | ⭐ **No propellant, no coordination needed**; ⚠️ needs an adequate inspiratory flow; ⚠️ moisture-sensitive |
| ⭐ **Nebuliser** (jet/ ultrasonic/ mesh) | Continuous mist from a solution | ⭐ **For infants, the very ill & acute severe asthma** — needs no coordination, delivers a large dose |
| ⭐ **Spacer/ holding chamber** | Holds the aerosol cloud so it can be inhaled slowly | ⭐ **Reduces oropharyngeal deposition & candidiasis; essential for children & the elderly** |
| **Steam inhaler/ vaporiser** | Classical steam inhalation | Domestic use |
| **Nasal inhaler stick** | Menthol/ camphor impregnated wick | Convenient decongestant |

### ⭐ ADVANTAGES & DISADVANTAGES OF THE INHALATION ROUTE
✔ ⭐ **Direct delivery to the target organ → rapid onset, small dose, minimal systemic side-effects
(e.g. inhaled steroids), avoids first-pass metabolism, huge alveolar absorbing surface (~ 70–100 m²)
and rich blood supply.**
⚠️ **Dose is difficult to control precisely; depends heavily on the patient's technique & inspiratory
effort; most of the dose is lost in the oropharynx & device; may irritate/ cause bronchospasm &
cough; expensive devices; unsuitable for irritant drugs.**

## 11.4 INSUFFLATIONS

📌 **DEFINITION** — **An insufflation is a finely divided, medicated powder intended to be blown
into a body cavity — the nose, throat, ear, or the respiratory tract — by means of an
INSUFFLATOR.**

⭐ **Points**
* The powder must be ⭐ **very fine (pass a no. 120–180 sieve, ideally 1–5 µm for the lung)**, dry &
  free-flowing.
* Delivered by an **insufflator** (a squeeze-bulb powder blower) or, in modern practice, by a
  ⭐ **dry-powder inhaler (DPI)** in which the micronised drug is blended with a ⭐ **coarse LACTOSE
  carrier** (the lactose improves flow & metering and gives the patient a "taste" confirming the
  dose).
* ⚠️ **Disadvantages:** the powder may **irritate** the mucosa & provoke coughing/ bronchospasm;
  dosing is inaccurate; ⚠️ **hygroscopic powders cake**.
* ⭐ **Examples:** **Sodium cromoglycate (Spinhaler), salbutamol & beclomethasone DPI,
  antibiotic/ sulphonamide throat & ear insufflations (classical), amphotericin, tobramycin**.
* ⭐ Store in an **air-tight, moisture-proof** container; label **"For insufflation — not to be
  taken"**.

## 11.5 GARGLES, MOUTHWASHES & THROAT PAINTS ⭐

### ⭐ GARGLES

📌 **DEFINITION** — **An aqueous solution used to treat infection or inflammation of the THROAT
(pharynx), usually dispensed in a CONCENTRATED form to be DILUTED with warm water before use.**

⭐ **Points**
* ⭐ Dispensed **concentrated** (economy of packing & transport) — ⭐ the **label must state the
  dilution** (e.g. *"Dilute one part with 10 parts of warm water"*).
* ⭐ Used **warm** (comfortable & more effective); brought into contact with the throat by
  exhaling through the liquid.
* ⭐ **LABELS: "NOT TO BE SWALLOWED", "Dilute before use", "For gargling only" — on a RED
  (external) label.**

| Gargle | Composition | Use |
|---|---|---|
| ⭐ **Potassium Chlorate & Phenol Gargle BPC** | Potassium chlorate, phenol, water (concentrated) | Antiseptic, astringent |
| ⭐ **Sodium Chloride Gargle / Compound Sodium Chloride Mouthwash** | Sodium chloride + sodium bicarbonate (isotonic saline) | ⭐ **Simple soothing, cleansing** |
| **Phenol Gargle** | Liquefied phenol 1.5 % + glycerin | Antiseptic, analgesic |
| **Povidone-iodine Gargle** | 1 % (diluted) | Antiseptic |
| **Chlorhexidine Gluconate Mouthwash/ Gargle** | 0.2 % | ⭐ Antiseptic, gingivitis; ⚠️ stains teeth |
| **Hydrogen Peroxide Mouthwash** | 1.5–3 % (6 % diluted) | Oxidising, deodorant, ⭐ anaerobic infection |
| **Potassium Permanganate Gargle** | 1 : 5000 – 1 : 10 000 | Antiseptic, deodorant |
| **Tincture of Myrrh Gargle** | Diluted tincture | Astringent |
| **Benzydamine Mouthwash/ Spray** | 0.15 % | Analgesic, anti-inflammatory |
| **Thymol/ Compound Thymol Glycerin** | Thymol, glycerin, alcohol, colour | Deodorant mouthwash |

### ⭐ MOUTHWASHES
📌 **Aqueous solutions with a deodorant, antiseptic, astringent or soothing action, used for
rinsing the MOUTH.** ⭐ Also often dispensed concentrated. ⭐ Label **"Not to be swallowed"**.
⭐ Contain: **antiseptics (chlorhexidine, cetylpyridinium, hexetidine, povidone-iodine)**,
**astringents (zinc chloride, alum, tannic acid)**, **deodorants (thymol, menthol, eucalyptol)**,
**alcohol & glycerin (vehicle)**, **sweeteners, flavours & colour**, ⭐ often **fluoride** (0.05 %
sodium fluoride) for caries prevention.

### ⭐ THROAT PAINTS (PIGMENTA)
📌 **Viscous liquid preparations, usually with GLYCERIN as the vehicle, applied to the throat or
oral mucosa with a BRUSH or swab.**
⭐ **Why glycerin?** — ⭐ It is **viscous & adhesive, so it CLINGS to the mucous membrane and
prolongs the contact/ action of the drug**; it is also **hygroscopic (so it draws out inflammatory
fluid, giving a decongestant & soothing effect)**, **sweet**, and **mildly antiseptic/ preservative**.

| Throat paint | Composition | Use |
|---|---|---|
| ⭐ **Compound Glycerin of Thymol (Mandl's Paint type)** | ⭐ **Iodine, potassium iodide, glycerin, peppermint oil, water** *(Mandl's paint)*; Compound Thymol Glycerin = thymol, glycerin, alcohol, colour | ⭐ **Antiseptic & astringent throat paint — pharyngitis, tonsillitis** |
| **Glycerin of Tannic Acid** | Tannic acid 10 % in glycerin | Astringent |
| ⭐ **Glycerin of Borax (Honey of Borax)** | Borax, glycerin, honey/ purified water | ⭐ **ORAL THRUSH (candidiasis) in infants**, sore mouth |
| **Glycerin of Ichthammol** | Ichthammol 10 % in glycerin | Mild antiseptic |
| **Gentian Violet (Crystal Violet) Paint 0.5–1 %** | Aqueous/ alcoholic | Antifungal, antiseptic (thrush) |
| **Magenta (Castellani's) Paint** | Basic fuchsin, phenol, resorcinol, acetone, boric acid, alcohol | Antifungal |
| **Compound Podophyllum Paint 15–25 %** | Podophyllum resin in compound tincture of benzoin | ⭐ **Genital & plantar warts** |
| **Salicylic Acid Collodion/ Paint** | 12–20 % | Corns, warts |
| **Iodine Paint / Tincture of Iodine** | Iodine 2.5 % + KI 2.5 % in alcohol | Skin antiseptic |

## 11.6 NASAL & EAR DROPS ⭐

### ⭐ NASAL DROPS
* ⭐ **Vehicle: AQUEOUS, isotonic (≈ 0.9 % NaCl), pH 5.5–7.5, with a preservative** —
  ⚠️ ⭐ **oily vehicles are avoided (LIPOID PNEUMONIA & ciliary paralysis)**.
* Contents: **decongestants (ephedrine 0.5–1 %, xylometazoline 0.05–0.1 %)**,
  **antihistamines**, **corticosteroids (beclomethasone, fluticasone)**,
  **antibiotics**, **saline**, **sodium bicarbonate (to soften crusts)**.
* ⭐ **Technique:** the patient should **lie supine with the head tilted back/ over the edge of the
  bed** so the drops reach the whole nasal cavity, and remain so for a minute.
* ⭐ **Labels:** *"For nasal use only"*, *"Do not use for more than 5–7 days"* (⚠️ **rebound
  congestion — rhinitis medicamentosa**), *"Discard 4 weeks after opening"*, *"Shake well"*
  (suspensions).

### ⭐ EAR (AURAL) DROPS
* ⭐ **Vehicles: water, GLYCERIN, PROPYLENE GLYCOL, alcohol, and (for wax) oils** —
  ⭐ **glycerin & propylene glycol are viscous & hygroscopic → they cling and are dehydrating/
  soothing.**
* Contents: **antibiotics (chloramphenicol, gentamicin, neomycin)**, **corticosteroids**,
  **antifungals (clotrimazole)**, ⭐ **wax softeners (olive/ arachis oil, sodium bicarbonate 5 %,
  glycerin, docusate, urea-hydrogen peroxide)**, **local anaesthetics (benzocaine)**,
  **antiseptics (boric acid in alcohol, acetic acid 2 %)**, **spirit (drying) drops**.
* ⭐ **Technique:** ⭐ **warm the bottle to body temperature in the hand before use** (⚠️ cold drops
  cause **giddiness/ vertigo & pain**); lie with the affected ear uppermost, instil, and stay for
  a few minutes.
* ⚠️ ⭐ **Aminoglycoside ear drops must NOT be used when the ear drum is PERFORATED**
  (risk of **ototoxicity**).
* ⭐ **Labels:** *"For use in the ears only"*, *"Warm before use"*, *"Discard 4 weeks after
  opening"*, *"Not to be taken"*.
* ⚠️ Ear drops for use after **ear surgery or with a perforated drum must be STERILE**.

## 11.7 CHAPTER 11 — RAPID REVISION SHEET

**DEFINITIONS**
* ⭐ **SPRAY (NEBULA)** = liquid **broken into fine droplets** by an **atomiser/ nebuliser** for the
  nose, throat or respiratory tract. (*Nebula = mist/cloud*)
* ⭐ **INHALATION** = liquid containing **volatile** substances, **vaporised (usually in hot water)**
  and the **vapour inhaled**.
* ⭐ **INSUFFLATION** = medicated **fine POWDER blown** into a cavity with an **insufflator**.
* ⭐ **AEROSOL/ MDI** = drug + **propellant** in a **pressurised** container, metered valve.
* ⭐ **GARGLE** = for the **THROAT**, dispensed **CONCENTRATED, to be diluted with warm water**.
* ⭐ **MOUTHWASH** = for **rinsing the MOUTH**. ⭐ Both are labelled **"NOT TO BE SWALLOWED"**.
* ⭐ **THROAT PAINT (Pigmentum)** = **viscous, GLYCERIN-based**, applied with a **BRUSH** —
  glycerin **clings & prolongs action, is hygroscopic (decongestant) and antiseptic**.

**PARTICLE SIZE (the key numbers)** 🔢
* ⭐ **> 10–20 µm → nose, mouth & throat.**
* ⭐ **5–10 µm → trachea & large bronchi.**
* ⭐ **1–5 µm → OPTIMUM: bronchioles & alveoli (the "respirable fraction").**
* ⭐ **< 1 µm → exhaled again.**
* Deposition by **impaction, sedimentation & diffusion**.

**NASAL PREPARATION REQUIREMENTS**
* ⭐ **Isotonic (≈ 0.9 % NaCl) · pH 5.5–6.5 (5.5–7.5) · must NOT inhibit ciliary movement ·
  preservative · non-irritant · AQUEOUS (⚠️ NOT oily).**
* ⚠️ ⭐ **Oily nasal drops/ sprays cause LIPOID PNEUMONIA and PARALYSE the CILIA → avoided.**
* ⚠️ **Decongestants for > 5–7 days → REBOUND congestion (rhinitis medicamentosa).**

**INHALATIONS**
* ⭐ **Standard direction: 1 teaspoonful (5 mL) — or 1 mL of Compound Benzoin Tincture — added to
  500 mL (1 pint) of hot water at about 65 °C; inhale the vapour.**
* ⚠️ ⭐ **NOT boiling water** — risk of **scalding**, **too-rapid loss of the volatile drug**, and
  (with benzoin) **coagulation of the resin into a sticky mass**.
* ⭐ **LIGHT MAGNESIUM CARBONATE is added as a DISPERSING/ DISTRIBUTING AGENT** so the volatile oil
  spreads evenly in the hot water.
* ⭐ **Menthol & Eucalyptus Inhalation BP = Menthol 2 g + Eucalyptus oil 10 mL + Light magnesium
  carbonate 7 g + Water to 100 mL.**
* ⭐ **Menthol Inhalation = Menthol 2 g + Light magnesium carbonate 1 g + water to 100 mL.**
* ⭐ **Compound Tincture of Benzoin (Friar's Balsam) = Benzoin 10 + Storax 7.5 + Tolu 2.5 +
  Aloes 2.5 + alcohol to 100.**
* ⭐ **Amyl nitrite** is inhaled from a **crushable ampoule (vitrellae)** — angina & **cyanide
  poisoning**; ⭐ **Aromatic spirit of ammonia** is inhaled in **fainting**.
* ⭐ Advantages of the inhalation route: **direct delivery, rapid onset, small dose, few systemic
  effects, avoids first pass, huge alveolar surface (70–100 m²)**.

**AEROSOLS**
* ⭐ **CFC propellants (Freon 11, 12, 114) are BANNED (Montreal Protocol — ozone depletion) and are
  replaced by HFA-134a.** Hydrocarbons (propane, butane) are **inflammable**; compressed gases
  (N₂, CO₂) lose pressure.
* ⭐ **Spacers reduce oropharyngeal deposition & candidiasis, and remove the need for coordination.**
* ⭐ **DPIs use a coarse LACTOSE carrier**, are breath-actuated, and need no propellant.
* ⭐ **Nebulisers** (jet/ ultrasonic) are for **infants, the very ill & acute severe asthma**.
* ⭐ Labels: *"Shake well"*, *"Do not puncture or incinerate"*, *"Rinse the mouth after use"*
  (steroids).

**EAR & NOSE DROPS**
* ⭐ **Ear drops: glycerin/ propylene glycol vehicles; WARM before use (cold → vertigo);
  aminoglycosides contraindicated with a PERFORATED drum (ototoxicity); sterile if the drum is
  perforated or after surgery.**
* ⭐ **Both ear & nasal drops: discard 4 weeks after opening.**

**KEY PAINTS/ GARGLES**
* ⭐ **Mandl's paint (compound glycerin of thymol/ iodine-KI-glycerin) — throat antiseptic.**
* ⭐ **Honey/ Glycerin of Borax — oral thrush in infants.**
* ⭐ **Compound Podophyllum Paint 15–25 % in compound benzoin tincture — warts.**
* ⭐ **Chlorhexidine 0.2 % mouthwash — gingivitis (⚠️ stains teeth).**
* ⭐ **Potassium permanganate gargle 1 : 5000 – 1 : 10 000.**
