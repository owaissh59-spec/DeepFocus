# ⚗️ INCOMPATIBILITY
### Physical · Chemical · Physiological (Therapeutical)

## 8.1 DEFINITION & IMPORTANCE

📌 **DEFINITION** — **Incompatibility** is an **undesirable change** that occurs when **two or more
substances (drugs, excipients or the container) are brought together**, resulting in a product that
is **physically unacceptable, chemically altered, therapeutically ineffective, or harmful to the
patient**.

📌 **In simple exam language:** *"Incompatibility is the phenomenon in which two or more ingredients
of a prescription react with each other, producing a change in the physical, chemical or therapeutic
properties of the preparation."*

⭐ **WHY IT MATTERS TO THE PHARMACIST**
* An incompatible mixture may be **inelegant** (unsightly precipitate), **inaccurate in dose**
  (the drug settles or decomposes), **therapeutically inactive**, or **actually toxic**.
* ⭐ Detecting an incompatibility **before compounding** is a **professional and legal duty** of the
  dispensing pharmacist.
* ⚠️ **The pharmacist must never dispense a knowingly incompatible mixture, and must never alter a
  prescription without the prescriber's consent.**

## 8.2 ⭐⭐ CLASSIFICATION OF INCOMPATIBILITY

```
                          INCOMPATIBILITY
                                 │
        ┌────────────────────────┼────────────────────────┐
        │                        │                        │
   ① PHYSICAL             ② CHEMICAL            ③ THERAPEUTIC
   (Pharmaceutical)                             (Physiological /
        │                        │               Pharmacological)
   No chemical            A chemical             No physical or
   reaction;              reaction occurs        chemical change;
   only physical          → new substance        the EFFECT in the
   change                 formed                 BODY is altered
        │                        │
   • Insolubility         Divided into:
   • Immiscibility        (a) IMMEDIATE (visible at once)
   • Liquefaction         (b) DELAYED  (develops on standing)
     (eutexia)            (c) TOLERATED / ADJUSTED
   • Precipitation            & UNADJUSTED / UNTOLERATED
     (physical)
```

⭐ **THE THREE TYPES — one-line discriminators (memorise!)**

| Type | What changes | Is a new compound formed? | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| ⭐ **PHYSICAL** | Only the **physical state/ appearance** | ❌ **No** | Camphor + menthol → liquefies |
| ⭐ **CHEMICAL** | **Chemical composition** — a **new substance** is formed | ✔ **Yes** | Tannic acid + alkaloid → precipitate |
| ⭐ **THERAPEUTIC** | The **pharmacological effect in the body** | ❌ No (in the bottle) | Morphine + strychnine (opposite actions) |

🧠 **MEMORY HOOK** — "**P**hysical = you can **see** it but nothing has **reacted**;
**C**hemical = something has **reacted**; **T**herapeutic = the **patient** is the one affected."

## 8.3 ① PHYSICAL (PHARMACEUTICAL) INCOMPATIBILITY ⭐⭐

📌 **DEFINITION** — Physical incompatibility occurs when the ingredients of a prescription **fail to
form a uniform, elegant, physically acceptable product**, **without any chemical reaction taking
place**. It is also called **pharmaceutical incompatibility**.

### ⭐ THE FIVE TYPES OF PHYSICAL INCOMPATIBILITY

### (A) INSOLUBILITY — *"a solid will not dissolve in the prescribed vehicle"*

| Situation | Example | ⭐ Correction |
|---|---|---|
| A resinous/ alcoholic tincture is added to an aqueous vehicle → **resin precipitates ("milkiness")** | ⭐ **Tincture of Benzoin, Tincture of Myrrh, Compound Tincture of Benzoin, Tincture of Tolu added to water** | Add a **suspending agent (tragacanth/ acacia mucilage)** and label **"Shake well"**; or add the tincture **slowly, with constant stirring, into the whole vehicle** (never the reverse) |
| A substance is insoluble in water | **Sulphur, calamine, zinc oxide, magnesium trisilicate, kaolin, chalk, aspirin, phenobarbitone** | Dispense as a **suspension** with a suspending agent + **"Shake well"** |
| Alkaloidal base is insoluble | Quinine base, morphine base | Use the **salt** (sulphate/ hydrochloride) instead — with the prescriber's consent |
| Volatile oil in water | Peppermint oil, clove oil | ⭐ Use an **aromatic water**, or triturate with **TALC/ light magnesium carbonate as a distributing agent** and filter, or add a **solubilising agent/ alcohol** |
| Substance soluble only in excess of another | Iodine (soluble in KI solution), calcium hydroxide | Add the **solubiliser** |

⭐ **The classical solubilisation tricks**
* **Alcohol/ glycerin/ propylene glycol** as a **cosolvent**.
* **Complexation:** ⭐ **Iodine dissolves in water in the presence of potassium iodide** (forms
  KI₃) — the basis of **Lugol's solution & Weak Iodine Solution**.
* **Caffeine dissolves in water in the presence of sodium benzoate/ citrate** (soluble complex).
* **Surfactant (Tween 80, polysorbate)** micellar solubilisation.
* **Salt formation** (converting a base into its hydrochloride/ sulphate).

### (B) IMMISCIBILITY — *"two liquids will not mix"*

| Example | ⭐ Correction |
|---|---|
| ⭐ **Fixed oil + water** (castor oil, cod liver oil, liquid paraffin with water) | ⭐ **Emulsify** — add an **emulsifying agent (acacia, tragacanth, gelatin, soap)** using the correct **oil : water : gum ratio** (fixed **4 : 2 : 1**, mineral **3 : 2 : 1**, volatile **2 : 2 : 1**, oleoresin **1 : 2 : 1**), and label **"Shake well"** |
| **Volatile oil (turpentine) + water** | Emulsify or use an aromatic water |
| **Oil + water in a liniment** | Nascent soap method (⭐ **olive oil + lime water = Carron oil/ Lime liniment**, emulgent = calcium oleate formed *in situ*) |
| **Chloroform/ ether + water** | Use as **chloroform water**, or emulsify |

### (C) LIQUEFACTION / DELIQUESCENCE ⭐ (the **EUTECTIC** problem)

📌 ⭐ **EUTECTIC MIXTURE** — a mixture of two or more solids which, when **triturated/ mixed
together**, **liquefies or softens** because the **melting point of the mixture is LOWER than the
melting point of either individual component**.

⭐⭐ **THE CLASSIC EUTECTIC SUBSTANCES — memorise this list!**
**Camphor · Menthol · Thymol · Phenol · Chloral hydrate · Betanaphthol · Resorcinol · Salol
(phenyl salicylate) · Antipyrine · Phenacetin · Acetanilide · Aspirin · Urethane · Pyrocatechol ·
Chloral + camphor ("chloral camphor")**

🧠 **MEMORY HOOK — "CAMP-MEN-THY-PHE-CHLO"** →
**Camp**hor, **Men**thol, **Thy**mol, **Phe**nol, **Chlo**ral hydrate — the five that turn up in
almost every question.

⭐ **CORRECTION of a eutectic (liquefaction) incompatibility**

| Method | How |
|---|---|
| ⭐ **1. Mix with the LEAST possible pressure, on a TILE with a SPATULA (spatulation)** | Never triturate hard in a mortar — pressure & friction cause liquefaction |
| ⭐ **2. Add an ABSORBENT / inert diluent (the commonest method)** | Add **light magnesium carbonate, light kaolin, magnesium oxide, starch, lactose, talc or bentonite** — each drug is **triturated separately with a portion of the absorbent** and the two are then **mixed gently** |
| ⭐ **3. Allow the liquefaction to occur deliberately, then absorb it** | Let the mixture form a liquid, then **incorporate an absorbent** to make a dry powder |
| **4. Dispense the components SEPARATELY** | In separate powder papers, to be mixed by the patient |
| ⭐ **5. Dispense in a CACHET or CAPSULE, or use double-wrapped WAXED powder papers** | Prevents moisture & leakage |
| **6. Convert into a liquid/ semisolid form** | Dissolve in a suitable solvent, or dispense as an ointment/ paste |
| **7. Use the eutectic deliberately** | ⭐ Sometimes desirable: **EMLA cream = eutectic mixture of lidocaine + prilocaine** (a therapeutic use!); a eutectic also helps in **fusing suppository bases** |

⚠️ **TRAP** — *Liquefaction of a eutectic is a **PHYSICAL** incompatibility, not chemical* —
no new compound is formed; the drugs are simply **mutually soluble in each other's melt**.

⭐ **DELIQUESCENT & HYGROSCOPIC substances also cause "physical" dampness in powders:**
**Calcium chloride, zinc chloride, potassium/ sodium hydroxide, ferric chloride, ammonium nitrate,
pepsin, trypsin, sodium iodide, potassium citrate, ammonium chloride, glycerin, sorbitol.**
⭐ **Correction:** add an absorbent (light magnesium carbonate/ starch), dispense in
**air-tight containers** with a **desiccant**, or use **double-waxed powder papers**.

⭐ **EFFLORESCENT substances** (they *give out* water of crystallisation and make a powder damp):
**Sodium carbonate (Na₂CO₃·10H₂O), ferrous sulphate, magnesium sulphate, sodium sulphate,
alum, atropine sulphate, codeine phosphate, citric acid, quinine bisulphate, caffeine.**
⭐ **Correction:** ⭐ use the **exsiccated (anhydrous/ dried) form** — e.g. **exsiccated ferrous
sulphate, exsiccated sodium carbonate, exsiccated alum** — **and adjust the quantity** for the
change in molecular weight; or add an absorbent.

🔢 **WORKED EXAMPLE (efflorescence correction)**
*A prescription calls for 1 g of ferrous sulphate (FeSO₄·7H₂O, mol. wt 278). If exsiccated ferrous
sulphate (FeSO₄·H₂O, mol. wt 178, or the IP dried form containing ~ 80 % FeSO₄) is substituted, what
quantity is required?*
`Quantity = 1 g × (178/278) = 0.64 g` (i.e. about **⅔ of the hydrated weight**) ✔
⭐ **Rule: substituting the exsiccated salt requires a SMALLER weight; substituting the hydrated
salt for the anhydrous requires a LARGER weight.**

### (D) PRECIPITATION (physical, i.e. **not** due to a chemical reaction)

| Situation | Example | ⭐ Correction |
|---|---|---|
| **Change of solvent ("solvent shift")** — a substance dissolved in alcohol precipitates when water is added | ⭐ **Resins from tinctures; camphor from spirit of camphor added to water; volatile oils** | Add the alcoholic solution **slowly to the whole of the aqueous vehicle with constant stirring**; or add a suspending agent; or reduce the water content |
| **Salting out** — a large amount of an electrolyte reduces the solubility of another substance | Volatile oil precipitated from an aromatic water by a strong salt solution; **soap precipitated by salts** | Reduce the salt concentration; add alcohol/ solubiliser |
| **Common-ion effect** | A chloride reduces the solubility of another chloride | Substitute a different salt |
| **Cracking/ breaking of an emulsion** | Adding alcohol/ electrolyte/ excess phase to an emulsion | Add the alcohol **last, well diluted**; do not exceed the emulgent's capacity |
| **Adsorption** | ⭐ **Alkaloids & antibiotics adsorbed onto kaolin, charcoal, talc, bentonite, magnesium trisilicate** → reduced availability | Avoid the combination; give at different times |

### (E) OTHER PHYSICAL PROBLEMS
* ⭐ **Sedimentation/ caking** of an indiffusible powder → add a **suspending agent** & label
  **"Shake well"** (Ch. 12).
* **Colour change** (physical staining) — e.g. iodine staining.
* **Loss of volatile constituents** — use **tightly closed containers**.
* ⭐ **Container/ closure incompatibility (sorption & leaching):**
  * **Rubber closures adsorb preservatives** (phenylmercuric nitrate, benzalkonium) and
    **leach zinc & sulphur** into injections.
  * **Plastic (PVC) containers adsorb** insulin, GTN, diazepam, and **leach plasticisers (DEHP)**.
  * **Alkaline glass** raises the pH of injections → use **Type I borosilicate glass**.
  * **Metal (iron/copper) traces** catalyse oxidation of ascorbic acid & adrenaline.
* **Cellulose filter paper adsorbs** benzalkonium & some antibiotics.

## 8.4 ② CHEMICAL INCOMPATIBILITY ⭐⭐

📌 **DEFINITION** — Chemical incompatibility occurs when the ingredients of a prescription
**react chemically with one another**, so that **a new chemical substance is formed** and the
therapeutic value of the preparation is reduced, destroyed or made harmful.

### ⭐ SUB-CLASSIFICATION OF CHEMICAL INCOMPATIBILITY

| Sub-type | 📌 Definition | Example |
|---|---|---|
| ⭐ **(a) IMMEDIATE (visible) incompatibility** | The reaction occurs **at once** on mixing, and is **visible** as a precipitate, colour change, effervescence or heat | Tannic acid + alkaloid → precipitate |
| ⭐ **(b) DELAYED incompatibility** | The reaction is **slow** and becomes apparent only **after some time (on standing/ storage)** | Aspirin + sodium bicarbonate in a moist mixture → slow hydrolysis; chloral hydrate + alkali |
| ⭐ **(c) TOLERATED / ADJUSTED incompatibility** | The reaction **can be prevented or minimised** by an **alteration in the order or the technique of mixing, or by dilution**, **without changing the therapeutic effect** — the pharmacist may correct it himself | Sodium bicarbonate + acid: mix in **dilute solution, add slowly** to avoid effervescence loss |
| ⭐ **(d) UNTOLERATED / UNADJUSTED incompatibility** | The reaction **cannot be avoided** by any pharmaceutical device; correction would **change the therapeutic effect** → ⭐ **the pharmacist MUST refer the prescription back to the prescriber** | An alkaloid prescribed with tannic acid at therapeutic strengths |

🧠 **MEMORY HOOK** — "**Adjusted = I can fix it at the bench. Unadjusted = I must phone the
doctor.**"

### ⭐⭐ THE MECHANISMS OF CHEMICAL INCOMPATIBILITY — with the classic examples

### (1) PRECIPITATION by chemical reaction ⭐ (the biggest group)

| Reaction | Example | Result |
|---|---|---|
| ⭐ **Alkaloidal salt + ALKALI** | Quinine sulphate/ morphine/ atropine salts **+ sodium bicarbonate, borax, alkaline substances** | ⭐ **Free alkaloidal BASE precipitates** (bases are water-insoluble) |
| ⭐ **Alkaloidal salt + TANNIN** | Alkaloid + **tannic acid, catechu, kino, gall, tea, myrobalan, cinchona, krameria** | **Insoluble alkaloidal tannate** precipitates |
| ⭐ **Alkaloidal salt + IODIDES/ heavy metal salts** | Alkaloid + **potassium iodide, mercuric chloride, silver nitrate, picric acid** | Insoluble complex |
| ⭐ **Salicylates/ Phenols + IRON salts** | Sodium salicylate/ phenol/ tannin **+ ferric salts** | ⭐ **Violet/ purple/ blue-black colour** |
| ⭐ **Soluble sulphate + soluble Ca/ Ba/ Pb salt** | Magnesium sulphate + calcium chloride | **Insoluble CaSO₄** |
| ⭐ **Soluble carbonate/ bicarbonate + Ca/ Mg/ Fe/ Zn salt** | Sodium bicarbonate + calcium chloride | Insoluble carbonate |
| ⭐ **Soluble chloride/ bromide/ iodide + SILVER NITRATE** | NaCl + AgNO₃ | White AgCl precipitate |
| ⭐ **Acid + soluble salicylate/ benzoate/ borate** | Sodium salicylate + an acid | Free **salicylic/ benzoic acid** precipitates |
| ⭐ **Sulphonamide sodium salt + acid** | Sulphadiazine sodium + acidic syrup | Free sulphonamide precipitates |
| ⭐ **Barbiturate sodium salt + acid** | Phenobarbitone sodium + acid | Free barbituric acid precipitates |
| ⭐ **Soap + acid or Ca/ Mg (hard water)** | Soap liniment + acid/ hard water | Free fatty acid/ insoluble Ca soap |
| **Gum/ mucilage + alcohol or heavy metals** | Acacia mucilage + strong alcohol/ lead acetate | Gum precipitated |
| **Protein/ gelatin + tannin, alcohol, heavy metals, acid** | Gelatin + tannic acid | Protein precipitated |

⭐ **CORRECTION of chemical precipitation**
1. **Substitute a compatible salt or form** (e.g. use an **alkaloidal salt that resists the alkali**,
   or replace tannin-containing drug with a non-tannin astringent).
2. **Increase the dilution** so that the product remains in solution.
3. **Add a solubilising/ complexing agent** (e.g. KI for iodine, sodium benzoate for caffeine).
4. **Change the order of mixing** and add slowly with stirring.
5. **Dispense as a suspension** with a suspending agent + "Shake well" — ⭐ **only if the
   precipitate is therapeutically harmless and the dose remains uniform**.
6. **Dispense the two ingredients separately** (two bottles).
7. ⭐ **Refer to the prescriber** if the therapeutic effect would be altered (unadjusted type).

### (2) OXIDATION–REDUCTION (REDOX) ⭐

| Oxidising agents | Reducing/ oxidisable substances |
|---|---|
| ⭐ **Potassium permanganate, hydrogen peroxide, chlorates, nitrates, nitrites, iodine, silver nitrate, sodium hypochlorite, bleaching powder, chromates, persulphates** | ⭐ **Glycerin, alcohol, sugars, tannins, phenols, organic matter, ferrous salts, iodides, sulphites, hypophosphites, adrenaline, ascorbic acid, morphine, easily oxidised alkaloids, essential oils, charcoal, sulphur** |

⚠️ ⭐ **THE CLASSIC DANGER** — **potassium permanganate/ chlorate + glycerin, sugar, charcoal,
sulphur or any organic matter** → **violent reaction, EXPLOSION or FIRE on trituration**.
⭐ **NEVER TRITURATE an oxidising agent with an organic/ reducing substance in a mortar.**
Mix, if unavoidable, **very gently on paper, in small quantities, with no pressure**, or **dispense
separately**.

* ⭐ **Antioxidants used to prevent oxidative incompatibility:**
  * Water-soluble: **sodium metabisulphite, sodium bisulphite, sodium sulphite, ascorbic acid,
    thiourea, sodium formaldehyde sulphoxylate**.
  * Oil-soluble: **BHA, BHT, tocopherols (vit. E), propyl/ octyl/ dodecyl gallate,
    ascorbyl palmitate, lecithin**.
  * **Chelating agents (synergists): EDTA (disodium edetate), citric acid, tartaric acid.**
  * ⚠️ **Acacia's oxidase enzyme oxidises easily oxidisable drugs** → heat the mucilage to
    100 °C for 30 min to destroy the enzyme, or choose another emulgent (Ch. 2).

### (3) HYDROLYSIS ⭐
* ⭐ **Aspirin → salicylic acid + acetic acid** (smells of vinegar) — accelerated by **moisture,
  heat & alkali**.
* **Esters:** procaine, atropine, benzocaine, methyl salicylate, physostigmine.
* **Amides & β-lactams:** ⭐ **penicillins & cephalosporins** (very unstable in aqueous solution →
  ⭐ **reconstituted antibiotic syrups keep only 7 days at 2–8 °C, or as labelled**).
* **Glycosides:** digoxin, sennosides (acid hydrolysis).
* ⭐ **Correction:** keep **anhydrous/ dry**, adjust to the **pH of maximum stability**, use a
  **buffer**, use **non-aqueous or cosolvent vehicles**, store **cool**, dispense as a
  **powder for reconstitution**, and give a **short expiry**.

### (4) EFFERVESCENCE & GAS EVOLUTION ⭐
* ⭐ **Carbonate or bicarbonate + acid → CO₂** (citric/ tartaric acid, acid salts, aspirin) —
  the medicine may **froth over or burst the bottle**.
* **Nitrite + acid → nitrous fumes**; **sulphide + acid → H₂S**;
  **ammonium salt + strong alkali → ammonia gas**;
  **hypochlorite + acid → chlorine**.
* ⭐ **Correction:** ⭐ **do NOT cork the bottle tightly**; add the acid **last, slowly, in dilute
  solution**; **leave head-space**; label **"Keep in a cool place; do not stopper tightly"**; or
  dispense the acid & the carbonate **separately** (as in an **effervescent draught**, where the
  patient mixes them just before drinking).

### (5) COLOUR CHANGE & COMPLEX FORMATION
* ⭐ **Salicylates/ phenols + ferric salts → violet**.
* **Tannins + iron → blue-black (the basis of ink)**.
* **Amines + sugars (Maillard reaction) → brown discolouration** (e.g. **aminophylline/ amines +
  lactose**).
* ⭐ **Tetracycline + Ca²⁺/ Mg²⁺/ Al³⁺/ Fe²⁺ → insoluble chelate → NOT absorbed**
  (⚠️ **do not give with milk, antacids or iron**).
* **Fluoroquinolones + antacids/ iron/ zinc → chelation.**

### (6) EVOLUTION OF HEAT / EXPLOSION
* **Glycerin + potassium permanganate**; **chlorates + sulphur/ charcoal**;
  **concentrated acid + water**; **iodine + ammonia (→ explosive nitrogen tri-iodide)**.
* ⭐ **Correction:** never triturate; mix separately; refer back.

### (7) RACEMISATION, POLYMERISATION, DECARBOXYLATION, PHOTOLYSIS
* **Racemisation** — active *l*-isomer converts to the inactive form (adrenaline).
* **Polymerisation** — formaldehyde, ampicillin sodium concentrates.
* **Decarboxylation** — p-aminosalicylic acid → *m*-aminophenol + CO₂.
* ⭐ **Photolysis** — **sodium nitroprusside, chlorpromazine, riboflavin, silver salts, phenothiazines**
  → dispense in **amber, light-resistant containers**.

## 8.5 ③ THERAPEUTIC (PHYSIOLOGICAL / PHARMACOLOGICAL) INCOMPATIBILITY ⭐⭐

📌 **DEFINITION** — **Therapeutic incompatibility** occurs when **two or more drugs, given together,
produce an effect in the body that is different from the intended one** — the action of one drug is
**opposed, exaggerated or altered** by the other, **without any physical or chemical change in the
preparation itself**.

📌 It is also called **physiological, pharmacological or clinical incompatibility**, and is
essentially the same phenomenon as a **drug interaction**.

⚠️ **KEY POINT** — In therapeutic incompatibility the **mixture in the bottle looks perfectly
normal**; the incompatibility appears only **inside the patient**. ⭐ Therefore it **cannot be
detected by inspection** — only by **pharmacological knowledge**.

### ⭐ THE FOUR CAUSES / TYPES OF THERAPEUTIC INCOMPATIBILITY

| Type | 📌 Explanation | ⭐ Classic examples |
|---|---|---|
| ⭐ **(1) OPPOSITE (antagonistic) ACTIONS prescribed together** | Two drugs with **directly opposing pharmacological effects** | **Morphine (CNS depressant) + Strychnine/ caffeine/ amphetamine (CNS stimulant)** · **Atropine (mydriatic) + Pilocarpine (miotic)** · **Adrenaline (vasoconstrictor) + Nitrite (vasodilator)** · **A purgative + an antidiarrhoeal (kaolin/ opium)** · **A sedative + a stimulant** · **Insulin + a corticosteroid/ adrenaline (hyperglycaemic)** · **A diuretic + an antidiuretic** |
| ⭐ **(2) OVER-DOSE / DUPLICATION (excessive same-direction effect)** | Two drugs of the **same class** giving a **dangerously additive** effect, or a dose above the maximum | **Two sedatives together (barbiturate + antihistamine + alcohol)** · **Aspirin + another salicylate** · **Two NSAIDs** · **Digitalis + a thiazide diuretic (hypokalaemia → digoxin toxicity)** · **Warfarin + aspirin (bleeding)** |
| ⭐ **(3) ALTERED ABSORPTION, DISTRIBUTION, METABOLISM or EXCRETION** | One drug changes the fate of the other in the body | ⭐ **Tetracycline + milk/ antacid/ iron (chelation → not absorbed)** · **Kaolin/ charcoal + any drug (adsorption)** · **Liquid paraffin + fat-soluble vitamins A, D, E, K (dissolved & lost in faeces)** · ⭐ **Rifampicin/ phenobarbitone (enzyme inducers) + oral contraceptives/ warfarin → failure** · ⭐ **Cimetidine/ erythromycin/ ketoconazole (enzyme inhibitors) + theophylline/ warfarin → toxicity** · **Probenecid + penicillin (blocks excretion — deliberately!)** · **Aspirin displaces warfarin/ sulphonylurea from plasma protein** |
| ⭐ **(4) SYNERGISM used incorrectly / potentiation of toxicity** | The combined effect is dangerously greater | **Aminoglycoside + frusemide (ototoxicity)** · **Aminoglycoside + a muscle relaxant (apnoea)** · **MAO inhibitor + tyramine-rich food/ pethidine (hypertensive crisis)** · **Alcohol + a sedative/ metronidazole** · **Sulphonamide + procaine (procaine's PABA antagonises the sulphonamide)** |

⭐ **A FEW MORE HIGH-YIELD THERAPEUTIC INCOMPATIBILITIES**

| Combination | Why it is incompatible |
|---|---|
| ⭐ **Male fern + castor oil (or fat/ alcohol)** | Increases the absorption of filicin → **toxicity (blindness)**; a **saline purgative** must be used instead |
| ⭐ **Sulphonamide + PABA/ procaine/ benzocaine** | PABA **antagonises** the antibacterial action |
| **Penicillin (bactericidal) + tetracycline (bacteriostatic)** | The bacteriostatic stops cell division, so the bactericidal cannot act |
| **Antacid + enteric-coated tablet** | Raised gastric pH → the coating dissolves too early in the stomach |
| **Antacid + acid-requiring drug (e.g. ketoconazole, iron)** | Reduced dissolution & absorption |
| **Levodopa + pyridoxine (vitamin B6)** | B6 increases peripheral decarboxylation → **less levodopa reaches the brain** |
| **Levodopa + a phenothiazine/ metoclopramide** | Dopamine blockade **negates** levodopa |
| **Corticosteroid + NSAID** | Additive **peptic ulceration** |
| **ACE inhibitor + potassium-sparing diuretic/ potassium salt** | **Hyperkalaemia** |
| **Adrenaline + a β-blocker (propranolol)** | Unopposed α-effect → **severe hypertension** |
| **Digoxin + calcium injection** | Increased cardiac toxicity |
| **Oral hypoglycaemic + a β-blocker** | Masks the warning signs of hypoglycaemia |
| **Alcohol + metronidazole/ disulfiram/ chlorpropamide** | **Disulfiram-like (antabuse) reaction** |
| **Vitamin K + warfarin** | Direct **antagonism** |
| **Suxamethonium + neostigmine** | Prolonged apnoea |

### ⭐ Is therapeutic incompatibility always undesirable?
❌ **No.** Sometimes it is **deliberately exploited** — this is the basis of **antidote therapy** and
of several useful combinations:

| Deliberate ("useful") incompatibility | Purpose |
|---|---|
| **Naloxone + morphine** | Antidote (competitive antagonism) |
| **Atropine + organophosphate/ neostigmine** | Antidote |
| **Vitamin K + warfarin overdose** | Antidote |
| **Protamine sulphate + heparin** | Antidote (chemical + therapeutic) |
| **Dimercaprol (BAL)/ EDTA/ penicillamine + heavy metals** | Chelation antidote |
| **Desferrioxamine + iron** | Chelation antidote |
| **Activated charcoal + an ingested poison** | Adsorption (physical antidote) |
| **Probenecid + penicillin** | Prolongs the penicillin level |
| **Carbidopa + levodopa** | Blocks peripheral decarboxylation |
| **Adrenaline + a local anaesthetic** | Vasoconstriction prolongs anaesthesia |
| **Trimethoprim + sulphamethoxazole** | Sequential blockade (true synergism) |
| **Clavulanic acid + amoxicillin** | β-lactamase inhibition |

⭐ **THE UNIVERSAL ANTIDOTE (classical, worth memorising)** —
🔢 **Activated charcoal 2 parts + Magnesium oxide 1 part + Tannic acid 1 part**
(charcoal **adsorbs**, magnesium oxide **neutralises acids**, tannic acid **precipitates alkaloids
& metals**). ⚠️ Now considered of limited value — **activated charcoal alone is preferred**.

## 8.6 ⭐⭐ MASTER TABLE — INCOMPATIBLE PAIRS & THEIR CORRECTION

| # | Incompatible combination | Type | What happens | ⭐ Correction |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Camphor + Menthol / Thymol / Phenol / Chloral hydrate** | Physical (eutectic) | Liquefies | **Spatulation + absorbent** (light MgCO₃/ light kaolin/ starch); or dispense separately; or in a capsule |
| 2 | **Tincture of Benzoin/ Myrrh/ Tolu + water** | Physical (insolubility) | Resin precipitates, milky | Add tincture **slowly to the whole vehicle with stirring** + **suspending agent** + "Shake well" |
| 3 | **Fixed oil + water** | Physical (immiscibility) | Two layers | **Emulsify** (acacia 4 : 2 : 1) + "Shake well" |
| 4 | **Volatile oil + water** | Physical | Oil floats/ not dissolved | Use an **aromatic water**, or triturate with **talc/ light MgCO₃** & filter; or emulsify |
| 5 | **Sulphur / calamine / ZnO / kaolin / mag. trisilicate + water** | Physical | Settles as a cake | **Suspending agent** (tragacanth/ Comp. tragacanth powder) + "Shake well" |
| 6 | ⭐ **Alkaloidal salt (quinine, morphine, atropine) + alkali (NaHCO₃, borax)** | **Chemical** | **Free alkaloidal base precipitates** | Avoid the alkali; **dilute greatly**; substitute a compatible salt; suspend if harmless; **else refer to prescriber** |
| 7 | ⭐ **Alkaloid + tannic acid/ catechu/ kino/ tea/ gall** | **Chemical** | Insoluble **alkaloidal tannate** | ⭐ **Unadjusted → refer to the prescriber**; or dispense separately |
| 8 | ⭐ **Sodium salicylate / phenol / tannin + ferric salt** | **Chemical** | **Violet/ blue-black colour** | Substitute; separate; refer |
| 9 | ⭐ **Potassium permanganate / chlorate + glycerin, sugar, charcoal, sulphur, organic matter** | **Chemical (redox)** | ⚠️ **Heat, fire, EXPLOSION** | ⭐ **NEVER triturate**; mix on paper with no pressure; **dispense separately** |
| 10 | ⭐ **Carbonate/ bicarbonate + acid (citric, tartaric, aspirin)** | Chemical (effervescence) | CO₂ evolved, bottle may burst | ⭐ **Do not stopper tightly**; add acid last, dilute, slowly; leave head-space; or **dispense separately** |
| 11 | ⭐ **Aspirin + sodium bicarbonate/ moisture** | Chemical (hydrolysis, delayed) | Smells of **vinegar**; loses potency | Keep **dry**; dispense as **separate/ dry powders**; short expiry |
| 12 | ⭐ **Tetracycline + milk / antacid / iron / calcium** | **Therapeutic** (chelation) | **Not absorbed** | Give **2 h apart**; counsel the patient |
| 13 | ⭐ **Liquid paraffin + fat-soluble vitamins (A, D, E, K)** | **Therapeutic** | Vitamins dissolved in the oil & lost | Separate the times of administration |
| 14 | ⭐ **Male fern + castor oil / alcohol / fat** | **Therapeutic** | Toxicity (optic damage) | Use a **saline purgative (magnesium sulphate)** |
| 15 | ⭐ **Morphine + strychnine / caffeine** | **Therapeutic** (antagonism) | Opposite CNS effects | ⭐ **Refer to prescriber** |
| 16 | ⭐ **Sulphonamide + procaine/ benzocaine (PABA)** | **Therapeutic** | Antibacterial action antagonised | Refer / substitute |
| 17 | **Silver nitrate + chloride/ bromide/ iodide** | Chemical | White/ yellow precipitate | Substitute; separate |
| 18 | **Soluble sulphate + calcium/ barium/ lead salt** | Chemical | Insoluble sulphate | Substitute |
| 19 | **Soap liniment + acid / hard water** | Chemical | Free fatty acid separates | Avoid acid; use distilled water |
| 20 | **Gelatin/ protein + tannin/ alcohol/ heavy metal** | Chemical | Coagulation | Avoid |
| 21 | **Acacia mucilage + alcohol (> ~ 20 %)/ heavy metals/ borax** | Physical–chemical | Gum precipitates | Use tragacanth, or reduce alcohol |
| 22 | ⭐ **Acacia (oxidase) + easily oxidised drug (adrenaline, phenol, thymol, tannin, vitamins)** | Chemical (oxidation) | Drug destroyed | ⭐ **Heat the mucilage at 100 °C for 30 min** to destroy the enzyme, or use another emulgent |
| 23 | **Chloral hydrate + alkali/ cyanide** | Chemical | Decomposes → chloroform/ toxic products | Avoid |
| 24 | **Iodine + ammonia solution** | Chemical | ⚠️ **Explosive nitrogen tri-iodide** | Never mix |
| 25 | **Iodine + starch** | Chemical | Blue colour, iodine inactivated | Avoid (this is also the **antidote for iodine poisoning**) |
| 26 | **Iodine + alkaloids/ metals** | Chemical | Precipitates | Use as **Lugol's solution (with KI)** |
| 27 | ⭐ **Efflorescent salts (Na₂CO₃·10H₂O, FeSO₄, MgSO₄, alum, citric acid) in a powder** | Physical | Powder becomes **damp/ pasty** | ⭐ Use the **EXSICCATED (dried) salt** with the weight adjusted, or add an **absorbent** |
| 28 | ⭐ **Deliquescent/ hygroscopic salts (CaCl₂, ZnCl₂, NH₄Cl, KI, pepsin) in a powder** | Physical | Powder liquefies | ⭐ **Absorbent + air-tight container + double-waxed papers/ capsules** |
| 29 | **Alkaloid/ antibiotic + kaolin, charcoal, bentonite, magnesium trisilicate** | Physical (adsorption) → therapeutic | Reduced absorption | Separate the doses |
| 30 | **Preservative (benzalkonium/ phenylmercuric nitrate) + rubber closure or plastic** | Physical (sorption) | Preservative lost → contamination | Use **Type I glass**, coated closures, higher preservative allowance |
| 31 | **Insulin/ GTN/ diazepam + PVC infusion set** | Physical (sorption) | Drug lost | Use glass/ polyolefin sets |
| 32 | **Penicillin/ ampicillin in aqueous solution** | Chemical (hydrolysis) | Loses potency | **Reconstitute fresh; store 2–8 °C; discard after 7 days** |
| 33 | **Adrenaline / ascorbic acid + trace copper or iron, air, light, alkali** | Chemical (oxidation) | Colour change, loss of potency | **Antioxidant + EDTA + amber container + acid pH** |
| 34 | **Chlorpromazine, riboflavin, sodium nitroprusside, silver salts in light** | Chemical (photolysis) | Discolouration/ decomposition | **Amber, light-resistant container** |
| 35 | **Alkaline glass + an injection of an acid-labile drug** | Physical–chemical | pH rises, drug degrades | **Type I borosilicate glass** |

## 8.7 ⭐ HOW A PHARMACIST HANDLES AN INCOMPATIBILITY — THE DECISION FLOW

```
             Incompatibility detected on reading the prescription
                                   │
             ┌─────────────────────┴────────────────────┐
             │                                          │
    Is it PHYSICAL or an ADJUSTED                Is it a CHEMICAL
    (tolerated) CHEMICAL type?                   UNADJUSTED type, or a
             │                                   THERAPEUTIC type?
             ▼                                          ▼
  ⭐ The pharmacist MAY correct it himself      ⭐ The pharmacist MUST NOT
     by a pharmaceutical device:                 alter the prescription
     • change the ORDER of mixing                    │
     • add a SUSPENDING/ EMULSIFYING agent           ▼
     • add an ABSORBENT / use spatulation      CONSULT THE PRESCRIBER
     • add a SOLUBILISER / cosolvent           • explain the problem
     • increase the DILUTION                   • suggest an alternative
     • substitute an equivalent FORM of the      (with justification)
       same drug (exsiccated salt etc.)        • RECORD the agreed change
     • dispense in a suitable CONTAINER          on the prescription,
     • add "SHAKE WELL"/ cautionary label        with the DATE & INITIALS
             │
             ▼
       Dispense + label + counsel
```

⭐ **THE SEVEN GENERAL METHODS OF CORRECTING AN INCOMPATIBILITY** (learn as a list)
1. **Change the order/ technique of mixing** (add slowly, with stirring, into the whole vehicle).
2. **Add a suspending, emulsifying, solubilising or wetting agent.**
3. **Add an absorbent / adsorbent** (for liquefaction & dampness).
4. **Increase the dilution / volume of the vehicle.**
5. **Substitute a chemically equivalent salt or form** of the same drug (with consent).
6. **Omit or replace the offending ingredient** — ⚠️ **only with the prescriber's consent**.
7. **Dispense the incompatible ingredients separately** (two containers, mixed or taken
   separately by the patient).
*(+ 8. **Refer back to the prescriber** — always the correct answer for unadjusted & therapeutic
types.)*

## 8.8 ⭐ INTRAVENOUS / PARENTERAL INCOMPATIBILITY (a modern high-yield addition)

📌 **IV incompatibility** — an undesirable reaction occurring when **two or more injectable drugs
are mixed in the same syringe, infusion bottle or line**.

| Cause | Example |
|---|---|
| ⭐ **pH mismatch** | ⭐ **Alkaline sodium bicarbonate/ aminophylline/ phenytoin + acidic drugs (adrenaline, insulin, vitamin C, most antibiotics)** → precipitation |
| ⭐ **Calcium + phosphate/ bicarbonate** | ⭐ **Calcium gluconate + sodium bicarbonate → insoluble CaCO₃**; calcium + phosphate in TPN → **calcium phosphate precipitate** (⚠️ fatal emboli) |
| ⭐ **Penicillin/ β-lactam + aminoglycoside in the same syringe** | Mutual **inactivation** |
| **Heparin + aminoglycosides/ vancomycin/ many antibiotics** | Precipitation/ inactivation |
| **Diazepam, phenytoin + aqueous infusions** | Precipitate (non-aqueous vehicle) |
| **Amphotericin B + saline** | Precipitates (must use **5 % dextrose only**) |
| **Blood/ blood products + any drug or dextrose** | ⚠️ Haemolysis/ clotting — ⭐ **nothing may be added to blood** |
| **Thiopentone + suxamethonium** | Precipitate |
| **Sorption to the bag/ set** | Insulin, GTN, diazepam onto PVC |

⭐ **GOLDEN RULES OF IV ADMIXTURE**
* ⭐ **Never mix drugs in an infusion unless compatibility is documented.**
* Add **one drug at a time**, mix thoroughly, and **inspect against light & dark backgrounds**
  for **haze, precipitate, colour change or gas**.
* **Flush the line** between two incompatible drugs.
* ⭐ **Nothing is ever added to blood, blood products, mannitol solutions or fat emulsions.**
* Prepare **immediately before use** in a **laminar air flow** cabinet; label with the time of
  preparation & expiry.

## 8.9 CHAPTER 8 — RAPID REVISION SHEET

* **Incompatibility** = undesirable change on mixing two or more substances.
* ⭐ **Three types: PHYSICAL (no reaction) · CHEMICAL (new substance formed) · THERAPEUTIC (effect
  in the body altered).**
* **PHYSICAL types (5): insolubility · immiscibility · liquefaction (eutexia) · precipitation
  (solvent shift/ salting out) · adsorption & container effects.**
* ⭐ **EUTECTIC MIXTURE** = mixture whose melting point is **lower than either component** →
  liquefies.
  ⭐ Classic eutectics: **camphor, menthol, thymol, phenol, chloral hydrate, betanaphthol,
  resorcinol, salol, antipyrine, phenacetin, acetanilide, aspirin.**
  ⭐ Corrected by: **spatulation with minimum pressure + an ABSORBENT (light magnesium carbonate,
  light kaolin, starch, lactose)**, separate dispensing, or capsules/ waxed papers.
* ⭐ **Efflorescent salts** (Na₂CO₃·10H₂O, FeSO₄, MgSO₄, alum, citric acid, atropine sulphate,
  codeine phosphate) → make powders damp → use the **EXSICCATED salt with the weight adjusted**.
  ⭐ **Deliquescent** (CaCl₂, ZnCl₂, KOH, NaOH, FeCl₃, NH₄Cl, pepsin) → **absorbent + air-tight
  container**.
* **Insoluble resinous tinctures (benzoin, myrrh, tolu) in water** → add the tincture **slowly to
  the whole vehicle with stirring** + suspending agent + **"Shake well"**.
* **Oil + water** → **emulsify** (fixed 4:2:1, mineral 3:2:1, volatile 2:2:1, oleoresin 1:2:1).
* **CHEMICAL sub-types: immediate · delayed · tolerated (ADJUSTED — pharmacist may correct) ·
  untolerated (UNADJUSTED — must refer to the prescriber).**
* ⭐ **Alkaloidal salt + alkali → free base precipitates.**
  ⭐ **Alkaloid + tannin → insoluble tannate.**
  ⭐ **Salicylate/ phenol + ferric salt → violet colour.**
  ⭐ **Carbonate + acid → effervescence (do not stopper tightly).**
  ⭐ **Permanganate/ chlorate + glycerin, sugar, charcoal, sulphur → EXPLOSION (never triturate).**
  ⭐ **Aspirin + moisture/ alkali → hydrolysis, smells of vinegar.**
* **Antioxidants:** water-soluble — **sodium metabisulphite, sulphite, ascorbic acid**;
  oil-soluble — **BHA, BHT, tocopherol, gallates**; synergist/ chelator — **EDTA, citric acid**.
* ⭐ **Acacia contains oxidase** → destroys oxidisable drugs → **heat mucilage 100 °C/ 30 min**.
* **THERAPEUTIC types (4): opposite actions · overdose/ duplication · altered ADME ·
  dangerous synergism.**
* ⭐ **Therapeutic classics: morphine + strychnine · atropine + pilocarpine ·
  tetracycline + milk/ antacid/ iron · liquid paraffin + vitamins A D E K ·
  male fern + castor oil · sulphonamide + procaine (PABA) · rifampicin + oral contraceptive ·
  levodopa + pyridoxine · warfarin + aspirin · MAOI + tyramine.**
* **Antagonism types: chemical (tannic acid + alkaloid; chelators) · physical (charcoal) ·
  physiological (adrenaline vs histamine) · pharmacological/ competitive (naloxone vs morphine).**
* ⭐ **Universal antidote = Activated charcoal 2 : Magnesium oxide 1 : Tannic acid 1.**
* ⭐ **Correction methods (7): change order of mixing · add suspending/emulsifying/solubilising
  agent · add absorbent · increase dilution · substitute an equivalent form · omit/ replace with
  consent · dispense separately — and REFER TO THE PRESCRIBER for unadjusted & therapeutic types.**
* ⭐ **IV incompatibility: pH mismatch · calcium + bicarbonate/ phosphate · penicillin +
  aminoglycoside · amphotericin B needs 5 % dextrose only · NOTHING is added to blood.**
