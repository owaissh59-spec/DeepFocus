## 9.1 SUPPOSITORIES — DEFINITION & TERMINOLOGY

📌 **DEFINITION (learn word-for-word)**
> **A suppository is a SOLID, single-dose medicated dosage form, of a shape and size suitable for
> INSERTION INTO THE RECTUM, which either MELTS at body temperature or DISSOLVES/ DISPERSES in the
> rectal fluid, thereby releasing the medicament for a LOCAL or SYSTEMIC effect.**

📌 The whole family of "solid dosage forms for insertion into body cavities":

| Form | Cavity of insertion | Usual shape | 🔢 Usual weight/ size |
|---|---|---|---|
| ⭐ **SUPPOSITORY** | ⭐ **RECTUM** | Torpedo/ bullet/ cone/ conical, pointed at one end | ⭐ **Adult 1 g (2 g also official) · Child 0.5–1 g · Infant 0.25 g** (weights refer to the theobroma-oil mould size) |
| ⭐ **PESSARY (vaginal suppository)** | ⭐ **VAGINA** | Globular/ oviform/ ovoid/ cone-shaped; also compressed vaginal tablets | ⭐ **3 g – 5 g** (commonly **4 g** for theobroma oil; glycero-gelatin pessaries **5 g**) |
| ⭐ **BOUGIE (urethral suppository)** | ⭐ **URETHRA** (also nose & ear) | **Long, thin, pencil-shaped/ cylindrical, tapering** | ⭐ **Male urethral: 4 g, about 100–150 mm long** · **Female urethral: 2 g, about 60–75 mm long** · **Nasal & aural bougies: about 1 g, 2 g** |
| **NASAL BOUGIE / Rhinal cone** | Nose | Pencil-shaped, small | ~ 1 g |
| **AURAL BOUGIE** | Ear | Very thin rod | ~ 1 g |
| **IMPLANT/ PELLET** | Subcutaneous tissue | Small sterile cylinder | mg |

🧠 **MEMORY HOOK — "R-V-U": Suppository = Rectum (1 g) · Pessary = Vagina (4 g) ·
Bougie = Urethra (4 g male / 2 g female)."**

⚠️ **TRAP** — Examiners commonly ask *"Suppository for the vagina is called…?"* → **Pessary**;
*"…for the urethra?"* → **Bougie**. And the weight pairs (**1 g / 4 g / 4 g & 2 g**) are asked
directly.

📌 **Torpedo shape** — the classical rectal suppository shape; ⭐ it is inserted **blunt (broad) end
first**, so that the **anal sphincter closes behind it and drives it inwards** (the pointed end
would allow it to be expelled).

## 9.2 ⭐ ADVANTAGES & DISADVANTAGES OF THE RECTAL ROUTE

### ⭐ ADVANTAGES (a favourite written-answer question)
1. ⭐ Useful when the patient is **UNCONSCIOUS, VOMITING, uncooperative, or unable to swallow**
   (post-operative, convulsions).
2. ⭐ Suitable for **INFANTS, small children and the very old**.
3. ⭐ **Avoids gastric irritation** by irritant drugs (aspirin, NSAIDs, aminophylline).
4. ⭐ **Drugs destroyed by gastric acid or by digestive enzymes are protected**
   (e.g. insulin-type, erythromycin, penicillin — classically).
5. ⭐ **Partially avoids HEPATIC FIRST-PASS METABOLISM** — the **lower & middle rectal veins drain
   into the inferior vena cava (systemic circulation) directly**, bypassing the liver
   (only the **superior rectal vein** goes to the portal system) → **higher bioavailability** for
   drugs with a high first-pass effect.
6. ⭐ Provides a **LOCAL effect** where it is needed: **haemorrhoids, anal fissure, proctitis,
   constipation, local anaesthesia**.
7. Useful for drugs with an **unpleasant taste or odour**.
8. Gives a **prolonged/ sustained** release; convenient **once-a-night** administration.
9. No injection needed → **no pain, no sterility requirement** (⚠️ except for surgical/ post-op use).

### ⚠️ DISADVANTAGES
1. ⭐ **Patient acceptability is poor** (cultural embarrassment) — the main practical limitation.
2. ⭐ **Absorption is often SLOW, ERRATIC & INCOMPLETE**, and varies with the presence of faeces.
3. **The suppository may be expelled**, especially if the rectum is full or the patient is
   diarrhoeic.
4. ⭐ **Local irritation** of the rectal mucosa is possible.
5. **Not suitable for drugs needing very rapid or precisely controlled plasma levels.**
6. Preparation is **more skilled & time-consuming**; the product needs **cool storage**.
7. Some drugs are **unstable** in the base, or **degraded by rectal micro-organisms**.
8. Limited **volume/ dose capacity**.

## 9.3 ⭐⭐ SUPPOSITORY BASES

📌 **DEFINITION** — A **suppository base** is the **inert vehicle** in which the medicament is
incorporated; it must **melt at body temperature or dissolve in the body fluid**, and release the
drug.

### ⭐ IDEAL PROPERTIES OF A SUPPOSITORY BASE (learn as a list)
1. ⭐ Should **melt at or just below body temperature (37 °C), or dissolve in the body fluids**.
2. ⭐ Should be **solid at room temperature (below 25 °C)** and **remain firm on handling**.
3. Should be **non-toxic, non-irritant, non-sensitising** to the mucous membrane.
4. Should be **chemically & physically stable** on storage (no rancidity, no polymorphic change).
5. Should be **compatible with a wide range of medicaments**.
6. Should **release the medicament readily** (good drug release/ spreading).
7. Should **contract slightly on cooling** so that it **releases easily from the mould**
   (⭐ *hence a lubricant may be unnecessary for cocoa butter*).
8. Should be **easy to mould/ handle**, of good **appearance & elegance**, and not adhere to the
   mould.
9. Should be **stable to heating above its melting point** (so the melt can be re-used).
10. Should have a **wide range between its melting point and its solidifying point** (so it can be
    poured), and should **not have a large expansion/ contraction problem**.
11. Should be **cheap and readily available**.

### 📋 CLASSIFICATION OF SUPPOSITORY BASES ⭐⭐

```
                        SUPPOSITORY BASES
                                │
    ┌───────────────────────────┼───────────────────────────┐
① FATTY / OLEAGINOUS      ② WATER-SOLUBLE &         ③ EMULSIFYING
   (MELT at body temp)       WATER-MISCIBLE            (mixed/ hydrophilic)
   • Theobroma oil           (DISSOLVE in fluids)      • Witepsol
     (cocoa butter)          • Glycero-gelatin         • Massa esterinum
   • Hydrogenated            • PEG (macrogols)         • Massuppol
     vegetable oils          • Polyoxyl-40 stearate    • Anhydrous wool fat
     (Witepsol, Massa                                    + fatty base
     estarinum, Suppocire,
     Cotmar, Wecobee)
   • Wool fat/ beeswax +
     fatty additives
```

### 📋 THE MASTER BASE TABLE ⭐⭐

| Base | Composition / nature | 🔢 Melting point | ⭐ Advantages | ⚠️ Disadvantages |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| ⭐⭐ **THEOBROMA OIL (Cocoa butter, Oleum theobromatis)** | The **fixed oil expressed from the roasted seeds of *Theobroma cacao* (Sterculiaceae)**; a mixture of **triglycerides of oleic, stearic, palmitic & lauric acids**; a **yellowish-white brittle solid** with a faint chocolate odour | ⭐ **30–35 °C** (melts at body temp; solidifies at 22–27 °C) | ⭐ **The traditional & official base**; melts at body temperature; **bland & non-irritant**; **contracts on cooling → no mould lubricant strictly needed**; easily moulded | ⚠️ ⭐ **POLYMORPHISM — if overheated above ~ 36 °C it forms the unstable α & γ forms with a LOW melting point (α ≈ 24 °C, γ ≈ 18 °C) which will NOT set properly** → ⭐ **NEVER heat above 36 °C; melt only two-thirds and let the rest melt by residual heat.** Also: **becomes rancid** on storage; **leaks from the rectum** (oily); **low water-absorbing capacity**; **melting point lowered by phenol, chloral hydrate & volatile oils** (needs beeswax/ white wax **to raise the m.p.**); adheres to the mould in humid weather |
| ⭐ **GLYCERO-GELATIN (Glycerinated gelatin) base** | ⭐ **Gelatin 14 g (or 20 g), Glycerin 70 g, Water to 100 g** — the **official BP "Glycerol Suppository" base is Gelatin 14 %, Glycerin 70 %, Water 16 %** | Does not melt — **DISSOLVES/ dissolves slowly in the mucous secretions** | ⭐ **Prolonged, sustained local action** — the classic base for **PESSARIES** and for **glycerin suppositories**; **water-miscible**; can hold aqueous solutions | ⚠️ **HYGROSCOPIC** → must be kept in an **air-tight container** (dehydrates or absorbs moisture); ⚠️ **supports MICROBIAL GROWTH → needs a preservative** (methyl/ propyl paraben, chlorocresol); **slow release**; **osmotic/ dehydrating irritation** of the mucosa (which is what makes glycerin suppositories laxative); ⭐ **must be MOISTENED WITH WATER before insertion**; mould **must be lubricated** (it does not contract) |
| ⭐ **POLYETHYLENE GLYCOLS (PEG/ Macrogols)** | Mixtures of PEG 300/ 400 (liquid), 1000, 1500, 4000, 6000 (solid) blended to the required consistency; e.g. **PEG 4000 : PEG 400 = 25 : 75 or 33 : 67**; **PEG 1540 + PEG 6000** | Do **not melt** at body temp (m.p. 40–60 °C) — ⭐ **DISSOLVE in the rectal fluid** | ⭐ **Do NOT melt at room temperature → no refrigeration needed; ideal for hot climates**; **no leakage**; **stable, no rancidity, no polymorphism**; **mould release good**; long shelf-life; **drug release controlled by the PEG grade** | ⚠️ ⭐ **HYGROSCOPIC & DEHYDRATING → causes a stinging/ irritant sensation** — ⭐ **moisten with water before insertion**; ⚠️ **brittle if cooled too rapidly**; ⚠️ **INCOMPATIBLE with many drugs** — **benzocaine, aspirin, phenol (softening), sulphonamides, tannic acid, silver salts, quinine, ichthammol, and it reacts with plastic/ polystyrene containers**; may crystallise |
| **HYDROGENATED VEGETABLE OILS (Witepsol H/ W/ S, Massa Estarinum, Suppocire, Wecobee, Cotmar, Massuppol)** | Semi-synthetic **tri-, di- & mono-glycerides of saturated C₁₂–C₁₈ fatty acids** (from coconut/ palm kernel oil) | ⭐ **Sharp melting points, 33–37 °C** (grades available) | ⭐ ⭐ **The MODERN base of choice** — **no polymorphism, resistant to rancidity (saturated), sharp m.p., narrow melting range, good water-absorbing capacity (mono/di-glyceride content), contracts on cooling, no lubricant needed** | ⚠️ **Brittle if cooled too fast (thermal shock) → cool slowly**; low viscosity when molten → **drug may sediment** (needs stirring while pouring); slight "shattering" |
| **WOOL FAT / Anhydrous lanolin + fatty base** | Emulsifying additive | — | Increases **water-absorbing capacity** of a fatty base | Greasy, allergenic |
| **BEESWAX / WHITE WAX / Paraffin / Spermaceti / Cetyl esters wax** | **Hardening agents added to theobroma oil** | Raises m.p. | ⭐ **Used to RAISE the melting point** of a base softened by a drug (chloral hydrate, phenol, volatile oil) or for **hot climates** — usually **2–10 %** | Too much → will not melt in the rectum |
| **Soap base (sodium stearate)** | Sodium stearate + glycerin | Dissolves | Laxative (osmotic/ irritant) action | Irritant |
| **Polyoxyl 40 stearate / non-ionic surfactant bases** | Water-soluble | ~ 39–45 °C | Water-soluble, self-emulsifying | Irritant potential |

⭐⭐ **THE POLYMORPHISM OF THEOBROMA OIL — the single most-asked "why" of this chapter**

| Form | 🔢 Melting point | Note |
|---|---|---|
| **γ (gamma) — unstable** | ⭐ **≈ 18 °C** | Formed on rapid, excessive heating & quick chilling |
| **α (alpha) — metastable** | ⭐ **≈ 22–24 °C** | Will **not set** at room temperature |
| **β′ (beta prime)** | ≈ 28 °C | Intermediate |
| ⭐ **β (beta) — STABLE** | ⭐ **34–35 °C** | ⭐ **The desired form** |

⭐ **THE RULE:** **Melt theobroma oil GENTLY on a WATER BATH, never above ~ 36 °C; melt only about
two-thirds of it and remove from the heat, allowing the residual heat to melt the remainder.**
This keeps the **stable β form** intact (it acts as a "seed") and the suppositories will set
properly. ⚠️ *If overheated, the mass takes hours or days to set, or never sets.*

## 9.4 ⭐⭐ DISPLACEMENT VALUE (DV) — THE CALCULATION SECTION

📌 **DEFINITION (learn word-for-word)**
> **The DISPLACEMENT VALUE of a medicament is the NUMBER OF PARTS BY WEIGHT of that medicament
> which DISPLACES ONE PART BY WEIGHT of the suppository base (theobroma oil).**

⭐ **WHY IT IS NEEDED:** the mould is calibrated **by volume**, but ingredients are weighed **by
weight**. Because a drug has a **different density from the base**, the drug **displaces** a certain
weight of base. If this were ignored, the suppositories would be **over-weight and the dose would be
wrong**.

🔢 **THE FORMULAE**

```
                            Weight of the base displaced by the drug ×  ... no:
① DV  =  Weight of the medicament ÷ Weight of the base it displaces
```
More usefully, in dispensing:
```
                                                  Total weight of drug
② Weight of base required = (n × mould size) − ──────────────────────────
                                                  Displacement value
```
where **n = number of suppositories to be prepared (⭐ always calculate for 1 or 2 EXTRA)** and
**mould size = nominal calibrated weight of the mould in grams (usually 1 g)**.

🔢 **DV FROM AN EXPERIMENT (the "how is DV determined" question)**
```
                  100 (a − b)
DV  =  ──────────────────────────────
                      c
```
where
**a** = weight of 6 (or n) **plain/ blank** suppositories of pure base,
**b** = weight of 6 (or n) **medicated** suppositories containing **c %** of the drug,
**c** = percentage strength of the drug.
*(Some texts write it as: DV = c × a ÷ [100(a − b) ] etc.; the version above is the standard.)*

⭐ **Equivalent simple experimental method:**
1. Prepare **6 blank suppositories** of pure base → weigh → **average weight = a**.
2. Prepare **6 suppositories containing a known % (c) of the drug** → weigh → **average = b**.
3. Weight of base in the medicated ones = b − (b × c/100); weight of drug = b × c/100.
4. Weight of base displaced = a − [b − (bc/100)] ; **DV = (weight of drug) ÷ (weight of base
   displaced)**.

🧠 **MEMORY HOOK for the "big four" numbers:**
**Zinc oxide 4.7 · Bismuth subgallate 2.7 · Aspirin 1.1 · default 1.5.**
⚠️ **A DV greater than 1 means the drug is DENSER than the base** (so it displaces less than its own
weight of base). ⭐ **Camphor & menthol (DV 0.7) are LESS dense → they displace MORE than their own
weight.**

### ⭐ WORKED EXAMPLES — DISPLACEMENT VALUE

🔢 **EXAMPLE 1 (the classic)**
*Prepare **12** suppositories, each **1 g**, containing **300 mg of zinc oxide** (DV = 4.7).*
```
Number to calculate for       : 12 + 2 extra = 14  (always make extras!)
Total drug required           : 14 × 0.3 g   = 4.2 g
Base displaced by the drug    : 4.2 ÷ 4.7    = 0.894 g
Total mould capacity          : 14 × 1 g     = 14 g
⭐ Base required               : 14 − 0.894   = 13.106 ≈ 13.1 g
```
**Answer:** Zinc oxide **4.2 g** + Theobroma oil **13.1 g** ✔

🔢 **EXAMPLE 2 (percentage strength)**
*Prepare **10** suppositories of **1 g** containing **10 % w/w bismuth subgallate** (DV = 2.7).*
```
Calculate for                 : 12
Drug per suppository          : 10 % of 1 g = 0.1 g
Total drug                    : 12 × 0.1 = 1.2 g
Base displaced                : 1.2 ÷ 2.7 = 0.444 g
Total mould capacity          : 12 × 1 = 12 g
⭐ Base required               : 12 − 0.444 = 11.556 ≈ 11.56 g
```
**Answer:** Bismuth subgallate **1.2 g** + Theobroma oil **11.56 g** ✔

🔢 **EXAMPLE 3 (two drugs)**
*Prepare **6** suppositories of **1 g**, each containing **200 mg zinc oxide (DV 4.7)** and
**100 mg bismuth subgallate (DV 2.7)**.*
```
Calculate for 8 :
Zinc oxide            : 8 × 0.2 = 1.6 g  → displaces 1.6 ÷ 4.7 = 0.340 g
Bismuth subgallate    : 8 × 0.1 = 0.8 g  → displaces 0.8 ÷ 2.7 = 0.296 g
Total base displaced  : 0.340 + 0.296     = 0.636 g
Total mould capacity  : 8 × 1             = 8 g
⭐ Base required       : 8 − 0.636         = 7.364 ≈ 7.36 g
```
**Answer:** ZnO **1.6 g**, Bi subgallate **0.8 g**, Theobroma oil **7.36 g** ✔

🔢 **EXAMPLE 4 (2 g mould)**
*Prepare **10** suppositories in a **2 g** mould, each containing **250 mg aspirin** (DV = 1.1).*
```
Calculate for 12 :
Total drug            : 12 × 0.25 = 3 g
Base displaced        : 3 ÷ 1.1   = 2.727 g
Total mould capacity  : 12 × 2    = 24 g
⭐ Base required       : 24 − 2.727 = 21.273 ≈ 21.27 g
```
✔

🔢 **EXAMPLE 5 (calculating the DV itself)**
*6 blank suppositories of theobroma oil weigh **6.0 g**. 6 suppositories containing **10 % w/w** of
a drug weigh **6.3 g**. Find the DV of the drug.*
```
a (blank, per supp.)              = 6.0 ÷ 6 = 1.0 g
b (medicated, per supp.)          = 6.3 ÷ 6 = 1.05 g
Drug in each medicated supp.      = 10 % of 1.05 = 0.105 g
Base in each medicated supp.      = 1.05 − 0.105 = 0.945 g
Base displaced by the drug        = 1.0 − 0.945  = 0.055 g
⭐ DV = weight of drug ÷ weight of base displaced = 0.105 ÷ 0.055 = 1.9
```
✔ (≈ **1.9**)

🔢 **EXAMPLE 6 (a liquid — DV ≈ 1)**
*Prepare 12 suppositories of 1 g containing **0.1 mL of castor oil** each (DV = 1.0, sp. gr. 0.95).*
Weight of oil per supp. = 0.1 × 0.95 = 0.095 g; for 14 → 1.33 g;
base displaced = 1.33 ÷ 1.0 = 1.33 g; base = 14 − 1.33 = **12.67 g** ✔

⭐ **RULES FOR THE DV CALCULATION — the four things students forget**
1. ⭐ **Always calculate for 1–2 EXTRA suppositories** (2 extra for 6–12, more for larger batches)
   because some mass is always lost in the mould, on the spatula & in trimming.
2. ⭐ **Use the CALIBRATED weight of your own mould**, not the nominal size, for accurate work
   (see §9.5).
3. ⭐ **A DV is only valid for the base for which it was determined** (theobroma oil unless stated).
4. ⭐ If the DV is **not given, assume 1.5** for a solid; **1.0** for a liquid or an oil.

📌 ⭐ **CALIBRATION OF A SUPPOSITORY MOULD** — *"How do you calibrate a mould?"*
1. **Melt pure theobroma oil** (or the base to be used) gently.
2. Pour it into the **clean, lubricated mould**; let it set; **trim** the surface level.
3. **Remove, weigh** all the suppositories, and calculate the **average weight** — this is the
   ⭐ **calibrated value of the mould for that base**.
4. Repeat for each base used, because ⭐ **the "size" of a mould is a VOLUME, and different bases of
   different density give different weights** (e.g. a "1 g" mould holds ~1 g of theobroma oil but
   about **1.25 g of glycero-gelatin** and about **1.1–1.2 g of PEG**).

## 9.5 ⭐⭐ METHODS OF PREPARING SUPPOSITORIES

📌 **THREE METHODS** (learn the names, then the details):

| Method | Principle | Scale | Note |
|---|---|---|---|
| ⭐ **1. MOULDING (fusion) method** | The base is **melted**, the drug incorporated, and the melt **poured into a mould** and allowed to set | ⭐ **The standard extemporaneous & industrial method** | Used for **all bases** |
| **2. COLD COMPRESSION method** | The base & drug are **mixed cold** and **compressed** in a special suppository-making machine | Industrial | ⭐ For **heat-labile drugs**; no heat, no sedimentation |
| **3. HAND-ROLLING (hand-shaping/ conical rolling) method** | The mass is **kneaded & rolled by hand** into a cylinder and cut/shaped | ⭐ **Oldest; no mould needed; obsolete** | Requires great skill; poor uniformity; used only when a mould is unavailable |

### ⭐⭐ THE MOULDING (FUSION) METHOD — STEP BY STEP
*(the answer to "How are suppositories prepared?")*

```
 ① CALCULATE — number (+ 2 extra) × mould size, and subtract the base displaced
              by the drug (displacement value)
 ② PREPARE THE MOULD — clean, dry, correctly assembled; LUBRICATE if required
 ③ WEIGH the drug and the base accurately
 ④ MELT the base GENTLY on a WATER BATH  ⚠️ (theobroma oil: melt only 2/3, NEVER above 36 °C)
 ⑤ INCORPORATE the drug — by the appropriate route (see the table below)
 ⑥ POUR into the mould, slightly OVERFILLING each cavity, while the melt is
   just beginning to thicken, stirring gently and continuously
 ⑦ COOL — allow to set (⚠️ slowly for hydrogenated bases; a refrigerator may be
   used for theobroma oil, but avoid thermal shock)
 ⑧ TRIM (scrape) the excess level with a warm knife/ spatula when fully set
 ⑨ REMOVE from the mould by opening it; handle as little as possible
 ⑩ WRAP each suppository in metal foil/ waxed paper, PACK in a cardboard box,
   LABEL, and store in a COOL place
```

⭐ **KEY POINTS AT EACH STEP (the "why" questions)**

| Step | ⭐ Rule & reason |
|---|---|
| **Melting** | ⭐ **Water bath only, gentle heat.** ⚠️ Theobroma oil above 36 °C → **polymorphism → will not set** |
| **Overfilling & pouring** | ⭐ **Pour while thickening & stir continuously**, so the **drug does not sediment** to the tip; ⭐ **overfill** because the mass **CONTRACTS on cooling** and would give a **cavitated/ hollow top** and an under-weight suppository |
| ⭐ **Lubrication of the mould** | ⭐ **Theobroma oil & hydrogenated bases CONTRACT on cooling → a lubricant is USUALLY NOT NEEDED.** ⭐ **Glycero-gelatin and PEG DO NOT contract → the mould MUST be lubricated.** ⭐ **Lubricant rule: use a lubricant that is IMMISCIBLE with the base** — for a **fatty base** use an **aqueous lubricant (soft soap + glycerin + alcohol, or "mould lubricant BP")**; for a **water-soluble base (glycero-gelatin/ PEG)** use **liquid paraffin or arachis oil** |
| **Cooling** | ⭐ Cool **slowly** for hydrogenated/ synthetic bases (they become **brittle & crack on thermal shock**); PEG cooled too fast becomes **brittle** |
| **Trimming** | With a **warm spatula/ knife**, only when **completely set** |
| **Mould** | Made of **stainless steel, brass (nickel-plated), aluminium or plastic**; two halves held by screws; sizes **1 g, 2 g, 4 g, 8 g** (nominal, for theobroma oil); ⚠️ **never scratch the cavity**; **clean with a dry cloth**, not with detergent & water for metal moulds |

### ⭐⭐ "HOW TO PREPARE SUPPOSITORIES OF SPECIAL MEDICINES"
### — INCORPORATING DIFFERENT KINDS OF MEDICAMENTS ⭐⭐
*(this is the exact wording of the syllabus — expect a direct question)*

| Nature of the medicament | ⭐ HOW IT IS INCORPORATED | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| ⭐ **1. INSOLUBLE SOLID (in the base)** | ⭐ Reduce to a **VERY FINE POWDER** (pass through a **no. 85–120 sieve**), then **LEVIGATE** it with a **small quantity of the MELTED base** (or a few drops of a suitable liquid) on a tile/ in a mortar to a **smooth, grit-free paste**, and **stir this into the remaining melted base**. ⚠️ **Pour while thickening & keep stirring so it does not sediment** | **Zinc oxide, bismuth subgallate, calamine, sulphur, hydrocortisone, aspirin, kaolin, boric acid, metronidazole** |
| ⭐ **2. SOLID SOLUBLE IN THE BASE (fat-soluble)** | ⭐ **Dissolve it directly in the melted base** — no levigation needed. ⚠️ **Do not use more heat than necessary**; ⚠️ if it is a substance that **LOWERS the melting point** (see 5), add a **hardening agent** | **Camphor, menthol, thymol, phenol, volatile oils, chloral hydrate, benzocaine** |
| ⭐ **3. SOLID SOLUBLE IN WATER** | ⭐ **Two choices:** (a) **dissolve in the MINIMUM quantity of water** and incorporate the aqueous solution into the melted fatty base **with the aid of wool fat/ anhydrous lanolin (about 10 % as an emulsifier)** — because a fatty base has a **low water-absorbing capacity**; **or** (b) *(preferred)* ⭐ **use the drug as a FINE POWDER and incorporate it as an insoluble solid (method 1)** — this avoids water in the base; **or** (c) use a **water-soluble base (glycero-gelatin/ PEG)** in which it will simply dissolve | **Morphine hydrochloride, codeine phosphate, zinc sulphate, sodium salts, procaine hydrochloride, chloral hydrate, potassium iodide** |
| ⭐ **4. LIQUIDS & SOFT EXTRACTS** | ⭐ **Small quantities are stirred directly into the melted base.** For **aqueous liquids/ tinctures**, ⭐ **first EVAPORATE the alcohol/ water to a soft-extract consistency on a water bath** (alcohol & water do not mix with a fatty base and cause **leakage/ non-setting**), then incorporate; or ⭐ **add wool fat (~10 %) to help absorb the aqueous liquid**; or ⭐ **use a water-soluble base** | **Ichthammol, balsam of Peru, castor oil, liquid extracts, hamamelis liquid extract, tinctures, coal tar solution** |
| ⭐ **5. SUBSTANCES THAT LOWER THE MELTING POINT of the base** | ⭐ **Add a HARDENING AGENT — BEESWAX, WHITE WAX, spermaceti, cetyl alcohol or paraffin wax (about 2–10 %)** — to raise the melting point back, **or** use a **higher-melting hydrogenated base**. ⭐ Also relevant **in hot climates** | ⭐ **Chloral hydrate, phenol, camphor, menthol, thymol, volatile oils, creosote, benzocaine** |
| ⭐ **6. EUTECTIC MIXTURES** *(e.g. camphor + menthol)* | ⭐ **Allow the eutectic to form deliberately**, then incorporate it into the base with a **hardening agent (beeswax)**; or **absorb** it on an absorbent first | Camphor + menthol; menthol + thymol |
| ⭐ **7. VOLATILE SUBSTANCES** | ⭐ **Add LAST, at the LOWEST possible temperature**, just before pouring, and **cover the container** to prevent loss by evaporation | **Volatile oils, camphor, menthol, chloroform** |
| ⭐ **8. THERMOLABILE / heat-sensitive drugs** | ⭐ Add at the **lowest workable temperature, last of all**; or use the ⭐ **COLD COMPRESSION method** | **Antibiotics, enzymes, vaccines, some hormones** |
| ⭐ **9. Drugs of very SMALL DOSE (potent)** | ⭐ Use a **trituration (a 1 in 10 dilution with lactose)** or the **aliquot method** (Ch. 4) to weigh accurately | Atropine, hyoscine, potent alkaloids |
| **10. Coarse/ gritty powders** | ⭐ **Must be levigated** — grittiness causes **irritation of the mucosa** | Any solid |
| **11. Surface-active/ irritant drugs** | Use a bland base; add a soothing agent (wool fat, zinc oxide) | Phenol |

🧠 **THE FOUR-WAY RULE for incorporating a drug — memorise:**
> **Soluble in the base → DISSOLVE it.**
> **Insoluble in the base → LEVIGATE it to a fine paste.**
> **Water-soluble → powder it (or use minimum water + wool fat, or a water-soluble base).**
> **Volatile/ heat-labile → add LAST at the LOWEST temperature.**

### ⭐ OFFICIAL / TYPICAL SUPPOSITORY FORMULAE worth knowing

| Suppository | Formula / strength | Use |
|---|---|---|
| ⭐ **Glycerin Suppositories (Glycerol Suppositories BP)** | ⭐ **Gelatin 14 %, Glycerin 70 %, Water to 100 %** (adult 4 g, child 2 g, infant 1 g) | ⭐ **Laxative** — acts by the **osmotic/ hygroscopic irritant action of glycerin** on the rectal mucosa |
| **Bisacodyl Suppositories** | 5 mg (child) / 10 mg (adult) | Laxative |
| **Zinc Oxide & Bismuth Subgallate ("Anusol-type") Suppositories** | ZnO, bismuth subgallate, bismuth oxide, balsam of Peru in a fatty base | **Haemorrhoids** — astringent, protective |
| **Hamamelis Suppositories** | Hamamelis dry extract 200 mg | Haemorrhoids |
| **Aspirin Suppositories** | 150 mg/ 300 mg | Analgesic/ antipyretic |
| **Paracetamol Suppositories** | 125 mg/ 250 mg/ 500 mg | Antipyretic (children, vomiting) |
| **Indomethacin Suppositories** | 100 mg | Anti-inflammatory |
| **Diazepam Rectal Solution/ Suppository** | 5 mg/ 10 mg | ⭐ **Convulsions in children** |
| **Aminophylline Suppositories** | 360 mg | Bronchodilator |
| **Hydrocortisone acetate Suppositories** | 25 mg | Proctitis, haemorrhoids |

⭐ **EVALUATION / QUALITY CONTROL OF SUPPOSITORIES**
1. ⭐ **Appearance & uniformity** — smooth, no cracks, no fissures, no cavitation, no mottling,
   uniform colour.
2. ⭐ **Weight variation / uniformity of weight** (weigh 20; calculate the average & deviation).
3. ⭐ **Melting-point/ macro-melting range** — the temperature at which the suppository melts
   completely in a water bath.
4. ⭐ **Liquefaction (softening) time** — the time required for the suppository to soften/ melt in a
   constant-temperature water bath at 37 °C.
5. ⭐ **Disintegration time** — ⭐ **fatty base: within 30 min; water-soluble base: within 60 min**.
6. ⭐ **Dissolution & drug release** (rotating-basket/ flow-through apparatus).
7. **Content uniformity/ assay** of the active drug.
8. **Breaking (crushing) strength / brittleness** test.
9. **Microbial limits/ sterility** if required.

⭐ **PACKING, STORAGE & LABELLING OF SUPPOSITORIES**
* ⭐ Wrap **individually in metal (aluminium) foil or waxed paper**; pack in a **cardboard box/
  plastic strip (blister) of PVC-aluminium**.
* ⭐ **Store in a COOL place, below 25 °C, preferably 2–8 °C (refrigerator)** —
  ⚠️ **theobroma-oil suppositories melt in hot climates**.
* ⭐ **LABELS:** *"**FOR RECTAL USE ONLY**"* · *"**Insert one suppository into the rectum**"* ·
  ⚠️ *"**NOT TO BE TAKEN BY MOUTH**"* · *"**Store in a cool place**"* ·
  *(glycero-gelatin & PEG: **"Moisten with water before insertion"**)*.
* ⭐ **Remove the wrapper before insertion**; insert the **blunt end first**.

## 9.6 ⭐ PESSARIES (VAGINAL SUPPOSITORIES)

📌 **DEFINITION** — **Pessaries are solid, single-dose preparations of a shape & size suitable for
insertion into the VAGINA**, where they melt or dissolve to exert a **local** (occasionally systemic)
action.

⚠️ **TRAP** — Do not confuse the **medicated pessary** (a dosage form) with the **mechanical/ ring
pessary** (a rubber or plastic **device** used to support a prolapsed uterus).

⭐ **TYPES OF PESSARY**

| Type | Description | 🔢 Weight |
|---|---|---|
| ⭐ **Moulded (fatty/ theobroma) pessary** | Made exactly like a suppository, in a **4 g mould**; **conical/ ovoid** | **4 g** |
| ⭐ **Glycero-gelatin pessary** | The **preferred classical base** — gives a **prolonged local action** and holds aqueous solutions | **5 g** (a 4 g mould holds ≈ 5 g of glycero-gelatin) |
| ⭐ **Compressed (vaginal) tablet** | ⭐ The **modern & commonest form** — made by **compression**, with a disintegrant & effervescent couple, inserted with an applicator | Varies |
| **Vaginal capsule** | Soft gelatin ovule | — |

⭐ **PREPARATION** — identical to suppositories (moulding/ fusion), except:
* ⭐ **Larger mould (4 g)** and a **larger dose of drug**.
* ⭐ **Glycero-gelatin base must be lubricated in the mould** (no contraction), needs a
  **preservative**, and the pessary must be **moistened with water before insertion**.
* Displacement-value calculations are done exactly as for suppositories, using the **calibrated
  weight of the 4 g mould for that base**.

⭐ **USES & typical medicaments**

| Purpose | Drug |
|---|---|
| **Antifungal (candidiasis/ thrush)** | ⭐ **Nystatin, clotrimazole, miconazole, boric acid** |
| **Antiprotozoal (trichomoniasis)** | **Metronidazole** |
| **Antibacterial** | Furazolidone, povidone-iodine, sulphonamides, acetarsol |
| **Astringent/ antiseptic** | **Zinc sulphate, alum, tannic acid, ichthammol** |
| **Contraceptive (spermicide)** | **Nonoxynol-9** |
| **Hormonal** | **Oestriol/ oestradiol, progesterone, dinoprostone (labour induction)** |
| **Cleansing/ deodorant** | Lactic acid, boric acid |

⭐ **LABELS:** *"**FOR VAGINAL USE ONLY**"* · *"**Insert one pessary high into the vagina at
night**"* · ⚠️ *"**NOT TO BE TAKEN BY MOUTH**"* · *"Moisten with water before use"* ·
*"Store in a cool place"* · *(often: "use the applicator provided")*.

## 9.7 ⭐ BOUGIES (URETHRAL, NASAL & AURAL SUPPOSITORIES)

📌 **DEFINITION** — **Bougies are solid, single-dose, PENCIL-SHAPED (long, thin, cylindrical,
tapering) medicated preparations intended for insertion into the URETHRA, NOSE or EAR**, where they
melt or dissolve and act locally.

🔢 **DIMENSIONS & WEIGHTS — memorise**

| Bougie | ⭐ Weight | ⭐ Length | Diameter |
|---|---|---|---|
| ⭐ **Male urethral** | ⭐ **4 g** | ⭐ **100 – 150 mm** | ≈ 3–6 mm |
| ⭐ **Female urethral** | ⭐ **2 g** | ⭐ **60 – 75 mm** | ≈ 3–6 mm |
| **Nasal bougie (rhinal cone)** | ≈ **1 g** | ≈ 20–30 mm | Small |
| **Aural bougie** | ≈ **1 g** | Very thin | Very small |

⭐ **BASE** — ⭐ **Glycero-gelatin (with a HIGHER proportion of GELATIN so that the bougie is tough,
flexible & does not break on insertion)** — this is the key point. Theobroma oil is too brittle;
PEG may also be used.
🧠 **"A bougie needs GELATIN for toughness."**

⭐ **PREPARATION** — by the **moulding method** in a **special long, narrow, tapering bougie mould**
(or classically by **rolling into a thin cylinder on a tile and cutting to length**); the mould
**must be lubricated** (glycero-gelatin does not contract).

⭐ **USES**
* **Urethral:** antiseptic/ antibacterial (**silver nitrate, silver protein, sulphonamides,
  acetarsol, furazolidone**), local anaesthetic (**benzocaine, lignocaine**), astringent
  (**zinc sulphate, alum**), for **urethritis & gonorrhoea** (classically).
* **Nasal:** decongestant & antiseptic.
* **Aural:** antiseptic, wax-softening.

⭐ **LABELS:** *"For urethral use only"* · *"Moisten with water before insertion"* ·
⚠️ *"Not to be taken by mouth"* · *"Store in a cool place, in an air-tight container"*.

## 9.8 ⭐ PLASTERS (EMPLASTRA)

📌 **DEFINITION** — **A plaster is a SOLID or SEMI-SOLID ADHESIVE MASS, spread on a suitable
BACKING material (cloth, paper, plastic film or moleskin), which is applied to the skin and
ADHERES to it on WARMING to body temperature.** It may be **medicated** (for a therapeutic effect)
or **unmedicated** (purely for support & protection).

⭐ **PURPOSES / USES OF PLASTERS**
1. ⭐ **Protection** of the skin & of wounds (protective plaster).
2. ⭐ **Support** — to give mechanical support to a joint, muscle or a fracture strapping.
3. ⭐ **Adhesion** — to hold a dressing, tube, splint or bandage in place (adhesive plaster).
4. ⭐ **Therapeutic (medicated) action** — the drug is held in **prolonged, occlusive contact** with
   the skin:
   * **Counter-irritant/ rubefacient** — **Belladonna Plaster, Capsicum Plaster, Mustard Plaster
     (sinapism)**.
   * **Keratolytic/ corn & wart removal** — ⭐ **Salicylic Acid Plaster (10–40 %)**.
   * **Astringent/ protective** — **Zinc Oxide Plaster, Lead (Diachylon) Plaster**.
   * **Antiseptic** — **Iodine, Boric acid plaster**.
5. ⭐ **Transdermal drug delivery (the modern descendant)** — **glyceryl trinitrate, nicotine,
   hyoscine, fentanyl, oestradiol, clonidine patches** — for a **systemic** effect.
6. **Corn caps & medicated dressings.**

### ⭐ COMPOSITION OF A PLASTER MASS

| Component | Function | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| ⭐ **Adhesive base** | Sticks to the skin | ⭐ **Rubber (masticated crepe rubber) or resin/ rosin; lead soap (lead oleate/ oleo-palmitate)** |
| ⭐ **Resin / tackifier** | ⭐ Provides **tack (stickiness)** | ⭐ **COLOPHONY (rosin)**, gum dammar, coumarone resin, ester gum |
| **Plasticiser/ softener** | Gives flexibility | **Wool fat, soft paraffin, lanolin, liquid paraffin, castor oil, dibutyl phthalate** |
| **Filler** | Bulk & body | **Zinc oxide, starch, chalk, talc, titanium dioxide** |
| **Antioxidant** | Prevents rubber degradation | Hydroquinone derivatives |
| ⭐ **Medicament** | The therapeutic agent | Salicylic acid, belladonna extract, capsicum |
| ⭐ **Backing** | Carries the mass | ⭐ **Cotton cloth, moleskin, flannel, paper, plastic film (PVC/ polyethylene)** |

⭐ **TYPES OF PLASTER**

| Type | Description |
|---|---|
| ⭐ **Rubber-based (adhesive) plaster** | Masticated rubber + colophony + fillers; e.g. **Zinc Oxide Adhesive Plaster (Zinc Oxide Plaster BP), adhesive tape/ strapping** |
| ⭐ **Resin-based plaster** | Colophony/ resins + wax + oil; e.g. **Resin Plaster** |
| ⭐ **Lead (Diachylon) plaster (Emplastrum Plumbi)** | ⭐ **Made by boiling LEAD OXIDE (litharge) with OLIVE OIL/ arachis oil and water → forms LEAD OLEO-PALMITATE (a lead soap)** — the classical base of many plasters | 
| **Salicylic Acid Plaster** | Salicylic acid **10 %, 20 % or 40 %** in a suitable base | ⭐ **Keratolytic — corns, warts, callosities** |
| **Belladonna Plaster** | Belladonna extract in a rubber/ resin base | Counter-irritant, analgesic |
| **Capsicum Plaster** | Oleoresin of capsicum | Counter-irritant |
| **Mustard Plaster (Sinapism)** | ⭐ Black/ white mustard powder spread on paper; ⭐ **moistened with LUKEWARM water (⚠️ NOT hot — hot water destroys the enzyme MYROSIN/ myrosinase, so no allyl isothiocyanate is liberated and there is no rubefacient action)** | ⭐ **Counter-irritant/ rubefacient — a classic exam point** |
| **Corn Cap** | A felt ring + salicylic-acid plaster mass | Corns |
| **Transdermal patch** | Reservoir/ matrix system with a rate-controlling membrane | Systemic delivery |

⭐ **METHOD OF PREPARATION (classical)**
1. **Prepare the adhesive mass** — masticate the rubber, add the resin, plasticiser & fillers, and
   mix in a **sigma-blade/ Z-blade mixer** with a **solvent (petroleum spirit/ benzene)** to a
   dough; **or** for lead plaster, **boil lead oxide + olive oil + water with constant stirring**
   until a soap is formed.
2. **Incorporate the medicament** at the lowest workable temperature.
3. **SPREAD the mass evenly on the backing** by a **spreading (coating) machine/ calender roll**.
4. **Dry** off the solvent in a **drying tunnel**.
5. **Cut into the required sizes**, cover with a **protective release liner (silicone paper)**.
6. **Pack** in an air-tight container/ roll & label.

⭐ **CATAPLASM (POULTICE) — do not confuse with a plaster**

| Feature | ⭐ **PLASTER** | ⭐ **POULTICE (Cataplasm)** |
|---|---|---|
| Consistency | **Solid/ semi-solid adhesive mass** | ⭐ **Soft, moist, paste-like** |
| Spread on | A **backing (cloth/ plastic)** | A **piece of cloth/ gauze** |
| Applied | **Cold**, adheres on warming to body temperature | ⭐ **Applied as HOT as the patient can bear** |
| Purpose | Adhesion, support, protection, prolonged medication | ⭐ **To RETAIN HEAT & moisture** — relieves pain, inflammation, "draws" boils |
| Examples | Zinc oxide plaster, salicylic acid plaster, belladonna plaster | ⭐ **Kaolin Poultice (kaolin + glycerin + boric acid + methyl salicylate + thymol + peppermint oil), Linseed (crushed) Poultice, Mustard Poultice, Bread poultice** |

⭐ **Kaolin Poultice BP (classical formula worth knowing):**
**Heavy kaolin 52.7 %, Glycerin 44.5 %, Boric acid 1.5 %, Methyl salicylate 0.2 %, Thymol 0.05 %,
Peppermint oil 0.05 %** — ⭐ heated in the tin (in hot water), spread on gauze, applied hot.

## 9.9 CHAPTER 9 — RAPID REVISION SHEET

**FORMS & SIZES**
* ⭐ **Suppository → RECTUM, torpedo-shaped, 1 g (adult), 0.5–1 g child, 0.25 g infant; insert
  BLUNT end first.**
* ⭐ **Pessary → VAGINA, ovoid/ conical, 4 g (theobroma) / 5 g (glycero-gelatin).**
* ⭐ **Bougie → URETHRA (also nose/ ear), pencil-shaped; MALE 4 g × 100–150 mm, FEMALE 2 g ×
  60–75 mm; base = glycero-gelatin with EXTRA GELATIN for toughness.**

**ADVANTAGES OF THE RECTAL ROUTE**
* Unconscious/ vomiting patient · infants & elderly · avoids gastric irritation · avoids gastric
  acid & enzymes · ⭐ **partly avoids hepatic first-pass metabolism (lower & middle rectal veins →
  inferior vena cava)** · local action for haemorrhoids · masks bad taste.
* ⚠️ Disadvantages: poor patient acceptance, erratic & incomplete absorption, may be expelled,
  local irritation, needs cool storage.

**BASES**
* ⭐ **THEOBROMA OIL (cocoa butter)** — from ***Theobroma cacao* (Sterculiaceae)**; m.p. **30–35 °C**;
  ⭐ **POLYMORPHIC: γ 18 °C, α 22–24 °C, β′ 28 °C, β (stable) 34–35 °C** →
  ⭐ **NEVER heat above 36 °C; melt only two-thirds.** Contracts on cooling → **no lubricant needed**.
  Disadvantages: **rancidity, leakage, polymorphism, m.p. lowered by phenol/ chloral hydrate/
  volatile oils.**
* ⭐ **GLYCERO-GELATIN = Gelatin 14 % + Glycerin 70 % + Water to 100 %** — **dissolves** (does not
  melt); **prolonged action**; base of **glycerin suppositories & pessaries**;
  ⚠️ **hygroscopic, supports microbial growth (needs preservative), must be moistened before
  insertion, mould MUST be lubricated.**
* ⭐ **PEG (macrogol)** — **dissolves**, **no refrigeration needed (good for hot climates)**,
  no rancidity/ polymorphism; ⚠️ **hygroscopic → stinging (moisten before use)**, **brittle**,
  **incompatible with benzocaine, aspirin, phenol, sulphonamides, tannic acid, silver salts,
  plastics.**
* ⭐ **Hydrogenated vegetable oils (Witepsol, Massa Estarinum, Suppocire)** = the modern base —
  **sharp m.p. 33–37 °C, no polymorphism, no rancidity**; ⚠️ **brittle on thermal shock, drug may
  sediment.**
* ⭐ **Beeswax/ white wax 2–10 % is added to RAISE the melting point.**

**DISPLACEMENT VALUE**
* ⭐ **DV = the number of parts by weight of a drug that displaces 1 part by weight of the base.**
* ⭐ **Base = (n × mould size) − (total weight of drug ÷ DV)**; ⭐ **always calculate for 2 EXTRA**.
* ⭐ **DV values: theobroma oil 1 · default for solids 1.5 · zinc oxide 4.7 · bismuth subgallate
  2.7 · bismuth subnitrate 6 · bismuth carbonate 4.5 · aspirin 1.1 · ichthammol 1.0 ·
  castor oil 1.0 · balsam of Peru 1.1 · morphine HCl 1.6 · codeine phosphate 1.1 ·
  phenobarbitone 1.2 · zinc sulphate 2.4 · tannic acid 1.6 · camphor & menthol 0.7.**
* ⭐ **Calibration of the mould:** pour pure base, set, trim, weigh, average — because the mould is
  calibrated by **volume**, and each base has a different density (a 1 g mould holds ~1.25 g of
  glycero-gelatin).

**PREPARATION**
* ⭐ **Three methods: MOULDING (fusion) — standard · COLD COMPRESSION — for heat-labile drugs ·
  HAND-ROLLING — obsolete.**
* ⭐ **Overfill the mould (the mass contracts on cooling) and pour while thickening, with constant
  stirring (to prevent sedimentation of the drug).**
* ⭐ **Lubricant rule: the lubricant must be IMMISCIBLE with the base** — aqueous (soap-glycerin-
  alcohol) for **fatty** bases; **liquid paraffin/ arachis oil** for **water-soluble** bases.
  Fatty & hydrogenated bases contract → usually need **no** lubricant.

**SPECIAL MEDICINES (the syllabus phrase)**
* ⭐ **Insoluble solid → fine powder + LEVIGATE with melted base.**
* ⭐ **Fat-soluble solid → DISSOLVE in the melted base.**
* ⭐ **Water-soluble solid → powder it, or dissolve in minimum water + ~10 % WOOL FAT, or use a
  water-soluble base.**
* ⭐ **Liquids/ tinctures → evaporate the alcohol/ water to a soft extract first, or add wool fat.**
* ⭐ **Substances lowering the m.p. (chloral hydrate, phenol, camphor, menthol, volatile oils) →
  add BEESWAX/ white wax to raise the m.p.**
* ⭐ **Volatile & thermolabile → add LAST, at the LOWEST temperature.**

**EVALUATION** — appearance, weight uniformity, melting range, **liquefaction/ softening time**,
⭐ **disintegration: fatty base 30 min, water-soluble base 60 min**, dissolution, content uniformity,
breaking strength.

**LABELS** — ⭐ *"For rectal/ vaginal/ urethral use only"*, *"NOT to be taken by mouth"*,
*"Store in a cool place"*, *(glycero-gelatin & PEG: "Moisten with water before insertion")*;
wrapped in **foil/ waxed paper**, stored **below 25 °C or at 2–8 °C**.

**PLASTERS**
* ⭐ **Plaster = solid/ semi-solid adhesive mass on a backing, adheres on warming.**
* Uses: **protection, support, adhesion, medication (counter-irritant/ keratolytic), transdermal
  delivery.**
* Components: **adhesive (rubber/ lead soap) + tackifier (⭐ COLOPHONY) + plasticiser (wool fat) +
  filler (zinc oxide) + medicament + backing.**
* ⭐ **Lead (Diachylon) plaster = lead oxide (litharge) + olive oil + water → lead oleo-palmitate.**
* ⭐ **Salicylic acid plaster 10–40 % = keratolytic for corns & warts.**
* ⭐ **Mustard plaster must be moistened with LUKEWARM water — hot water destroys the enzyme
  MYROSIN and there is then no counter-irritant action.**
* ⭐ **POULTICE (cataplasm) = soft, moist paste applied AS HOT AS BEARABLE to retain heat**
  (**Kaolin poultice, linseed poultice**) — *not* the same as a plaster.
